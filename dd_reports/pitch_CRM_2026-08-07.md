# PITCH — Salesforce (CRM) — VERDICT: Ownable only if the earnings hold up. And we own none of it — a records error we caught.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at $190. Challenge bench 7/10 (reject the edge, call it fairly priced at $169–190); review bench weakened that case but cut its own conviction to 5/10 after seeing the historical base rates. Confidence 6/10. Print: August 26, company-confirmed.*

**Say the awkward part first.** Our records said we held Salesforce at 2.9% of the book. We hold **zero** — the 2.9% was a software-sector bucket mislabeled as a position, and the one resting buy order (53 shares at $175) shows a broker status suggesting it may no longer exist. The first action from this court was correcting our own books. Verify the $175 order is actually working.

## What we believe
Salesforce is a roughly 7–9% grower trading at 12–13.5× earnings — a price that, run backwards through a cash-flow model, assumes only ~2% growth forever. The verifiable majority of the business (Sales +9.3%, Service +8.8%) is holding and organic growth *accelerated* last quarter (6.9%→7.4%). The AI product line ($1.2B ARR, up 50% in one quarter, $2.3B including data) is carried at roughly zero in the price. If earnings prove durable, the market is paying bear-case pricing for a base-case business.

## What the market believes
That AI agents erode seat-based software and Salesforce is a giant seat-seller whose weakest 30% of revenue (marketing, commerce, analytics — growing 1.5–3.7%) drags the whole to low-single-digit growth as the strong parts tire. The CFO's own words concede "ongoing weakness in marketing and commerce and increased softness in Tableau."

## Why we might have edge
Salesforce collapsed its revenue disclosure from five product lines to two in a single quarter — right after two of the five stopped growing — and named the surviving bucket after its AI product. We reconstructed the quarter that was published both ways: the new lines are **not a re-partition of the old ones** — $555M (6% of subscription revenue) moved into the AI-named bucket with no bridge, and which products moved is unverifiable. Zero analysts or journalists have flagged any of this (we checked coverage exhaustively — a clean negative). The disaggregation change was filed in the one quarter the CFO personally held the accounting-officer seat. That information — and the discipline of watching the metric that still can't hide (organic contracted revenue vs organic revenue) — is not in the price.

## Why it might be priced in
The base rates are brutal: of ten episodes of mature software at ≤14× forward earnings with slowing growth, one paid (IBM 2021 — which required an actual growth inflection), four were traps, and the closest live comparable (Adobe) sliced straight through its "floor" while raising guidance. CTSH re-rated its multiple and still lost 33 points to the index because the earnings eroded underneath. Cheap-versus-history is not a floor; only earnings durability is. Also: the EPS raise this year is largely mechanical from a $25B debt-funded buyback, and cash-flow growth guidance was *cut* to pay for it.

## The plan
Keep the $175 resting buy through the August 26 print **if it exists** (pulling it early forfeits ~10 points of fill probability for no information). Grade the print on earnings, not multiple: operating margin ≥33.5%, cash-flow growth not guided negative excluding interest, and **organic contracted-revenue growth ≥ organic revenue growth — with the deflator restored; if the deflator stays withdrawn, that test is failed, not passed.** Any further unbridged reclassification into the AI-named bucket is strike two: adopt the fairly-priced verdict and stand down. Do not add Alphabet-correlated mega-cap exposure beyond this per household policy.
