# PITCH — Sportradar (SRAD) — VERDICT: Watch. The edge we thought we had was an artifact of the company's own contract structure.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at ~$12.70. Challenge bench REJECT 8/10; review bench weakened it to a fair-price verdict and killed our own best argument in the process. Probe declined pending November. Confidence 6/10.*

**Say the awkward part first.** Our triage's prize finding — "US revenue grew 10% while betting volume fell 4%, so the market has mispriced a data vendor as a bookmaker" — died in court. Sportradar's own annual filing says its revenue is **67% fixed-fee and 33% revenue-share, zero percent volume-linked**. Revenue diverging from volume proves nothing when the contracts never referenced volume. We also mis-graded the quarter twice: the guide was **cut** (~8% at the EBITDA midpoint, fourteen weeks after being reiterated), not raised, and "net cash" ignores ~€80M a year of very real sports-rights payments that sit outside the profit measure.

## What we believe
A genuinely channel-hedged B2B franchise — it sells data and odds feeds to whoever takes the bets, and has signed both Kalshi and Polymarket as customers — trading at a fair, not cheap, multiple (roughly 6.4–11.1× depending on how honestly you load the rights obligations), with one live credibility problem: half the margin promise for this year disappeared in one quarter, and a second cut in November is structurally possible.

## What the market believes
That anything touching US sports betting deserves the sector's derate — SRAD fell 15% on a quarter that beat — plus an unquantified grey-market compliance tail from two short reports (Muddy Waters, which covered its own short on publication day; Callisto).

## Why we might have edge (what survived)
The channel-indifference is real even though our volume framing wasn't: fixed-fee contracts insulate revenue from the handle contraction, and the prediction-market entrants are *customers*, not competitors. US revenue is growing organically (+17% in dollars). If the OSB fear is a sector brush, a fee-based data vendor painted with it is the wrong target.

## Why it might be priced in
Three things the court upheld: the guide cut is real and basis-consistent (the four innocent explanations all failed); the FY2025 annual report **deleted** the long-standing risk factor about revenue from unlicensed jurisdictions — and the company's rebuttal to the short sellers truncates one sentence before its own filing admits "the scope of that disclosure has evolved"; and a securities class action (S.D.N.Y., 2026) pleads the CEO's own "we only work with licensed operators" statement as the misstatement. Net retention also fell 127% → 109% — a bear datum neither bench had until the blue found it.

## The plan
No position. Watch with two dated tests: the **November 5 print** must deliver the implied 27.4–27.9% second-half margin with **no third guide revision** (a third revision terminates coverage), and the call must show the growth is IMG-ARENA absorption rather than core deceleration. Both pass → a quarter-size probe activates. The next annual filing restoring and quantifying the deleted disclosure would upgrade the honesty grade; any ruling sustaining the class action's misstatement claim is an exclusion-grade finding.

## What remains unverified
The Q2 call transcript (the absorption-vs-deceleration decomposition lives there); the 2025-09-30 and 2026-03-25 drawdown catalysts; whether exchange volume over-indexes to the tax-shock states — still the cleanest discriminator between tax damage and substitution, still unobtained.
