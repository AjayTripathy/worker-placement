# PITCH — GitLab (GTLB) — VERDICT: Wait. Not impaired, not yet a buy. Alert at $31.50, decision at the September print.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at $36.62. A split court: challenge bench REJECT 7/10; review bench BUY-a-starter 6/10. Adjudicated to a price-gated wait. Confidence 6/10.*

**Say the awkward part first.** Our two benches disagreed, and both were partly right: the challenge bench's headline math was wrong (its "0.48% true cash margin" used a stale year and over-charged stock compensation by about a sixth), and the review bench's "the crash never round-tripped" was right — but the one metric that broke this stock is still falling, and that is the difference between GitLab and the Bentley position we did buy.

## What we believe
A profitable, net-cash ($1.36B, zero debt) developer-platform company whose guidance sits 6–8 points *below* what its own disclosed metrics arithmetically produce, whose operating-income guide was just raised, and whose share count actually fell last quarter. The market prices roughly a 1-in-5 chance of the good outcome; the payoff is +54% against −38%.

## What the market believes
That AI code generation makes per-seat developer tools structurally smaller — fewer developers, fewer seats — and GitLab's own numbers agree so far: net revenue retention has declined a point a quarter, monotonically, to 117, and growth in customers over $5k halved. The company cut 14% of its own workforce.

## Why we might have edge
The bear case leans on numbers that don't survive checking: the "no free cash flow" claim double-counts dilution the share count already reflects; the "+95% off the low" anchor measures from a one-week liquidation print (the stock is *down* 25% from November); the "restated consumption metric" honesty flag died on inspection — GitLab volunteered the restatement in a signed filing, named the prior figure, and gave reasons. Disclosed is clean. What's left is a valuation debate about a stabilizing business, and the market is pricing the debate as if it were resolved against.

## Why it might be priced in
The named break metric has not turned. Bentley's break-cause reversed to a series high before we bought; GitLab's retention is still declining a point a quarter, flat and steady. Duo (the AI product) is bundled into the seat price, and no seat count is disclosed anywhere — the one number that could justify the multiple cannot be built from primary sources. Until retention stabilizes or the consumption line grows to matter, the derate is a fair price for an unresolved question.

## The plan
No position at $36.62 and **no resting orders into the unconfirmed September print**. An alert at $31.50 (the review bench's first rung) triggers a cause-check, not an automatic buy. The print decides: consumption revenue ≥$35M **and** retention ≥114 activates the starter case; retention ≤114 or a guide cut hardens the reject. Below ~$26 the math is compelling even on today's disclosure.

## What remains unverified
The Q2 print date itself (both benches exhausted their search budgets); Duo monetization at any scale; whether the guide gap is sandbagging or deterioration foreknowledge — that is precisely what the print resolves.
