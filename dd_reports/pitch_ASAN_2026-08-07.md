# PITCH — Asana (ASAN) — VERDICT: DECLINE. No position at $8.90, and no resting buy order either.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at $8.90, twenty-seven days before second-quarter results on 2026-09-03. Two benches sat. The challenge bench filed REJECT at 9/10 conviction on eleven separate kill findings. The review bench filed RED CASE WEAKENED at 7/10 — it overturned one of those findings outright as an arithmetic error, downgraded two of the three the challenge bench had marked fatal, and then made the single worst finding in the file worse than it had been filed. Both benches reached the same action. They disagreed about nearly every reason.*

**Say the awkward part first.** Our own bear case was wrong three times, and each error ran in the same direction — it made the business look sicker than the filings support. We compared a backlog number to the wrong quarter and reported a decline where there was a 23% increase. We called a seasonal pattern that has repeated for three straight years a break in the trend. And we graded a management claim "refuted" using a metric that, by the company's own written definition, is mathematically incapable of refuting it. None of those errors changed the answer. All three would have changed the answer on a closer name, and that is why they are printed here rather than quietly fixed.

The reason we are declining is not that the business is collapsing. It isn't. The reason is the entry price and the calendar.

---

## What we believe

That Asana is a slow-growing but stabilizing software business whose installed customers have stopped leaving — and whose actual problem is that it can no longer win new ones fast enough to matter.

Revenue grew 9.5% last quarter. The customers it already has are behaving better than they have in two years: the retention measure bottomed out five quarters ago and has ticked up a point on both the overall and the large-customer cut. Revenue from its core customer base grew 10% while the *count* of those customers grew only 7% — meaning existing accounts are spending more per account, which is the healthy half of the equation. Contracted future revenue is up 23.1% year over year, growing roughly 2.4 times as fast as revenue itself. Profitability is genuinely repairing: the operating loss margin went from −23.4% to −7.4%, research spending is down 12%, stock compensation is down 24.6%, and the share count actually *shrank* 2.05% over the year. There is $424.6M of cash and securities and no borrowings.

What is broken is new-customer acquisition. Large customers spending over $100k grew 20% year over year five quarters ago; that rate is now 12.2%. Net additions to the core customer base, compared like for like against the same quarter last year, went from 235 to 175 — down 26%. This is not churn. It is a demand-generation failure, and it is exactly the kind of problem a new chief executive is hired to fix, and exactly the kind that has not yet shown up in any number.

## What the market believes

That work-management software is what artificial intelligence eats first, and that Asana specifically is losing share inside a category that is otherwise fine.

The stock is down 35.1% this year and trades at 2.01 times trailing revenue after subtracting cash. But the "AI kills seat-based software" story does not survive contact with Asana's two closest comparables, both of which reported inside the last ninety days: monday.com grew 24% with 110% net retention and stable enterprise retention across five quarters; Atlassian's cloud revenue grew 31% with subscription bookings up 23% and *accelerating* seat expansion. The category is not being eaten. Asana's gap to it is specific to Asana.

The market has also already located the floor. One times revenue after cash prices this stock at $5.35. The 52-week low, set in April 2026, is $5.385. The market marked Asana at that level four months ago and bounced. At $8.90 we would be buying 66% above a floor that has already been tested and held.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly — not much here, and that is the finding.

The review bench did establish that the business is in better shape than the consensus shorthand suggests: backlog outgrowing revenue by 2.4 times, retention troughed and turned, cash flow before the seasonal receivables swing flipping from −$12.0M to +$3.8M, and a *raised* profit-margin guide delivered while absorbing a $75M acquisition. If the market is pricing terminal decline, the filings do not yet show terminal decline.

But an edge has to be actionable, and this one isn't at this price. The multiple that would express it — around $6.50, or 1.33 times revenue — is 27% below where the stock trades today, and the path from here to there runs directly through a scheduled earnings date. More on that below, because it is the whole decision.

## Why it might be priced in

Because the two things that would make us buy are unresolved, and the calendar resolves them for us in twenty-seven days at no cost.

The revenue guidance raise was almost entirely purchased. The full-year guide went from $850–858M to $855.5–863.5M, of which roughly 50 basis points of growth is the StackAI acquisition. The organic raise is 0.2 of a percentage point, and the organic full-year growth guide of 7.7–8.7% sits *below* the 9.5% the company actually did last quarter. Management is guiding to deceleration on the underlying business. Meanwhile the quarter beat the top of its own guide by $0.6M — a 0.29% beat, which is thin.

And the oversight layer keeps emptying. Seven separate executive- and board-departure filings since July 2025: chief executive, chief operating officer, general counsel, chief financial officer, lead independent director, and two seventeen-year founder-era directors. The board leg of that is defensible — the successor lead director was named the same day, the departures were framed as planned refreshment, and the finance seat was filled immediately with a named internal successor. The accounting leg is not defensible, and it got worse under review. See the table.

## The adjudication: why there is no resting order either

The review bench recommended a good-till-cancelled buy ladder at $6.50 down to $5.75, sized in thirds. **We are overruling that.**

Here is the plain version. A resting order at $6.50 is 27% below where the stock trades today. Asana reports on 2026-09-03. For the stock to travel 27% down inside the next four weeks, something has to go wrong — and by far the most likely something is the print itself, or an accounting filing from a company that currently has nobody in the chief accounting officer's chair. Which means the order does not fill on a quiet drift lower. It fills on the day the news is bad, and on essentially no other day.

That is the trade backwards. We would be committing capital, at a fixed price set today, to be executed by whoever knows the answer before we do. A limit order below the market on a name with a hard catalyst date is not patience — it is a standing offer to be adversely selected. The correct move on a name with a scheduled resolution twenty-seven days out is to let the resolution happen and re-derive the entry against the new quarter. It costs us nothing but the chance to buy before the news, which is precisely the chance we do not want.

The $6.50 level is not wrong as *analysis* — it is 1.33 times revenue, a defensible asymmetry point above the tested $5.35 floor. It is wrong as an *instruction*.

## Reopen conditions

1. **Contracted future revenue of $560M or more at the July-31 balance sheet date, reported 2026-09-03.** This is the cleanest single falsifier available on the tape. The July-2025 figure of $507.3M was a step-change that gets lapped this quarter, so growth decelerates by construction; clearing $560M means the step was a new level and not a one-off. Below it, the backlog story we just corrected in the company's favor stops being true.
2. **After the print, re-derive the entry against the $5.35 floor on the new quarter's numbers.** Not before. The floor is one times revenue after cash, and revenue will have moved. Everything else — whether the retention number finally appears in the release itself and reconciles, whether large-customer additions resume in what is a seasonally *strong* quarter, whether the organic guide moves without an acquisition attached, whether a chief accounting officer has been named — is input to that re-derivation, not a separate gate.

## Tripwires

**Any change-of-auditor filing or any non-reliance filing between now and 2026-09-03 converts this from decline to permanent avoid.** No re-court, no price gate, no discussion. A company whose chief accounting officer took the job in March, signed the annual and quarterly reports, resigned in July after four months, and has not been replaced is a company where that filing would not be a surprise — and it is the one outcome from which no entry price recovers.

Standing watch on the regulator's filing index for this company through the print. Everything else waits for 2026-09-03.
