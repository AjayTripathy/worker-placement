# PITCH — Bentley Systems (BSY) — VERDICT: BUY a starter. One third of a normal position, at the market.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at $35.36 (the August 6 close). Two benches: the challenge bench filed REJECT at 8/10 conviction; the review bench filed "case weakened" at 7/10 and overturned five of the thirteen kills outright. Adjudication: STARTER APPROVED — the only buy to come out of four software courts. Confidence 7/10.*

**Say the awkward part first.** This is the smallest kind of yes. Four of the five kills we overturned failed the same way — the challenge bench read one side of a two-sided disclosure — and a pattern like that is what breaks an 8/10, not brilliance on our part. What survived the review is real and we are carrying it: the second-half margin promise is undischarged and gets tested in November, and the company has almost no contracted backlog, which is why the position is capped at a third of normal size rather than sized to the conviction. We are buying a business whose reason for falling has already reversed, at a price that has not noticed. We are not buying a bargain.

---

## What we believe

Bentley broke on a specific day for a specific reason, that reason has since reversed, and the price has not.

The break was November 5, 2025 — the Q3 print, where constant-currency recurring-revenue growth came in at 10.5%, down from 11.5%. The stock fell 8.1% and then 7.6% in the two weeks that followed, against a software index down 5.2% and 1.9%. That was genuinely Bentley's own problem, and we concede the point to the bear side without argument.

Then we tested the cause. The same metric has printed 11.5%, 11.5%, and now **12.0%** — the highest figure in the disclosed series and 150 basis points above the number that broke the stock. Revenue per quarter went from $375.5M to $410.7M. Account retention held at 99% and net revenue retention at 109% the entire way through. Revenue per share of the business is larger and growing faster than it was before the break.

The stock is **32% below its pre-break level** of $52.35.

That is the whole thesis in one sentence: a dated, identifiable cause of a drawdown, which has since gone away, on a price that still reflects it. Everything else in this document is either defending that sentence or bounding what happens if we are wrong.

## What the market believes

That software companies selling seats to engineers are about to lose those seats to AI, and that Bentley — infrastructure design software, priced on how much engineers use it — sits directly in the path.

The bear version of this is sharper than the generic version, and it deserves stating properly. Bentley's largest product line is not a seat licence at all; it is a meter that charges based on how many distinct users open the applications each day. If engineers stop working, a seat licence survives until renewal but a daily meter falls immediately. On that reading Bentley is *more* exposed to engineering headcount than a normal software company, not less.

We think the mechanism is right and the reading of it is wrong, for two reasons given below. But the belief itself is coherent, widely held, and is what is keeping the price where it is.

## Why we might have edge

Because the instrument that would detect the feared damage is published every quarter, almost nobody reads it, and it currently says the damage is not happening.

The metric everyone watches is net revenue retention — 109%. That number covers a rolling twelve months. Management describes customers as only "beginning to apply" AI capabilities on live projects. So a twelve-month backward-looking average is structurally incapable of detecting a hazard that started recently. On this the challenge bench was completely right, and we upheld it.

But the same filing that creates the hazard also publishes the sensor. The usage meter is recognised on actual daily user access within the quarter, and its revenue is disclosed in the quarterly revenue breakdown. That line has essentially no reporting lag — one quarter at most. It reads **+12.4% year over year in the quarter ended June 30, 2026**, against total revenue up 12.8%, on a product line that is 46% of all recurring revenue.

Four-plus quarters into the AI-displacement story, the metric that would collapse first is growing roughly in line with the company. The gap between what the market watches (a twelve-month lag) and what actually measures the risk (one quarter) is about **three quarters of lead time**. That is the edge, and it is also the exit: the same line tells us first if we are wrong.

## Why it might be priced in

Because this is a reasonably-priced compounder whose derate reversed, not a distressed asset, and two real problems remain open.

At $35.36 the enterprise is worth $11.74B against free cash flow guided to $500–570M — about 22× at the midpoint, a 5.0% cash-flow yield on the equity, roughly 26× adjusted earnings. Against 12% growth, 99% retention, 93% recurring revenue and a 31% cash-flow margin, that is cheap relative to Bentley's own history and to the reversal in its growth. It is not cheap in absolute terms, and it is not a dislocation. If we are simply wrong about the reversal being unpriced, we have bought a fairly-valued business at a fair price.

The two open problems are named in the plan below: the second-half margin swing the company has promised but not demonstrated, and the near-total absence of contracted backlog.

### The finding the review bench made against its own side

The review bench was arguing *for* the stock and reported this anyway, which is why it is load-bearing for the sizing.

**Bentley has almost no contracted backlog.** Committed future revenue is **$280.6M — about 19% of a single year's revenue — and 94% of it lands inside twelve months.** It also *declined* sequentially, from $296.7M, while revenue grew. Contract terms align to calendar quarters, revenue is recognised on usage rather than ratably, and the customer deposits we cited approvingly above can roll forward *or be refunded*.

So the durability of this revenue is **behavioural, not contractual.** It rests on 99% account retention, 109% net retention, 93% recurring revenue and a $496M prepaid balance — not on signed multi-year commitments. That is a legitimate reason to own less of this than of a comparably-growing company with a committed book. It is a sizing input, not a rejection: the quarterly floors bound the damage in any single quarter, and 99% retention has now held through two full cycles of this same worry. It is the single clearest reason the position is capped at one third.

## The plan

**Starter now: about $35.40, at the market.** Roughly 450 shares, about $16,000, around 35 basis points of the book — one third of a normal position. The limit sits at the tape for spread protection only; we are explicitly **not** fishing for weakness. The whole thesis is that the cause of the drawdown already reversed and the price has not marked it, so waiting for the price to fall is waiting for the thesis to get worse.

**Add the second third at $31–32, and only after November.** That is the consolidation shelf from June and July. The condition is not the price alone — it is the price *plus* the November quarter delivering the second-half margin inflection it has promised. If the margin gate fails, this becomes an exit, not an add. A cheaper price on a failed gate is a different security.

**A final third below $30** if it gets there, approaching the June low where flat cash flow is fully paid for. Do not chase above $38 before the November print.

**Tripwire: usage revenue growing slower than total revenue.** It is +12.4% against +12.8% today — already marginal, which is the honest way to hold it. Below roughly +9% is thesis damage and we cut. This line appears in the quarterly revenue breakdown and carries at most one quarter of lag, so it fires roughly **three quarters before the headline retention metrics could possibly show the same thing**. That lead time is the entire reason we are willing to own a behaviourally-durable revenue stream at all.

Secondary tripwires, in order of how early they fire: customer deposit inflows turning negative (enterprises stop pre-funding the meter before they stop using it — currently +$37.3M against +$27.4M); constant-currency growth back below 11.0%, which would re-establish the November 2025 cause; and the 2027 notes refinancing above roughly SOFR+200, which re-cuts forward cash flow.

**No premium selling against this position** — taxable book, non-deferrable ordinary income. Use resting limit orders at $32 and $30 instead.

## What remains unverified

One hole, reported rather than inferred around. We could not complete independent labour-market work on infrastructure engineering employment — payroll data, industry headcount, the direction of headcount at the large engineering firms. The steelman that infrastructure engineers are not easily displaced therefore rests on Bentley's own usage meter and on management's framing, **not** on outside employment data. That is a coverage gap, not a clean bill of health, and it is the first thing to fill if the usage line ever wobbles.
