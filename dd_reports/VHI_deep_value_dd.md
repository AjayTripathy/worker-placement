# Valhi, Inc. (VHI) — Deep-Value Diligence

**Rating:** REAL-CHEAP (qualified) · **Price $14.49 (2026-06-04)** · **Look-through NAV $22–$23/sh (base, at market)** · **Discount ~35%**

Valhi is a Contran/Simmons-controlled (~91%) holding company whose value is essentially a leveraged-by-structure, *un*-leveraged-by-recourse claim on titanium-dioxide pigment maker Kronos Worldwide (KRO), plus a controlling 83% stake in NL Industries (NL), which itself owns CompX (security products) and a piece of the Kronos chain and Nevada real estate. A naive consolidated screen reads VHI as a $2.6B-asset, $625M-debt, loss-making industrial; the *parent* reality is ~$11.9M of recourse debt and a sum-of-parts that, marked entirely at the public market prices of its own listed subsidiaries, is worth ~$22.2/sh against a $14.49 quote — a ~35% holdco discount struck at a cyclical trough in TiO2. The thesis is real but bounded: the discount is structural (permanent controlled-company governance penalty) and the underlying asset (Kronos) is itself at trough earnings, so the margin of safety is "buy the trough at a discount-to-a-discount," not "buy a mispriced compounder." This is a deep-value basket position, not a concentrated bet.

## 1. Business & structure (ownership chain)

Per the Q1-2026 10-Q (filed 2026-05-07) and Kronos' 2025 10-K, the chain is:

| Entity | What it is | VHI economic/control position |
|---|---|---|
| **Contran Corp** (private, Simmons family) | Ultimate parent | owns **~91%** of VHI |
| **Valhi, Inc. (VHI)** | The holdco being valued | ~28.5M shares out; ~91% Contran-held → **~9% true float** |
| **Kronos Worldwide (KRO)** | TiO2 pigment (5 plants, EU/NA), the crown jewel | **~50% held directly by VHI** (~57.5M of 115.05M sh) **+ ~31% held by NL** (35.2M sh) → VHI **controls ~81%** |
| **NL Industries (NL)** | Sub-holdco: 35.2M KRO shares + 87% of CompX + real-estate interests | VHI owns **~83%** of NL |
| **CompX International (CIX)** | Security products / marine components | held **through NL** (~87% controlled) |
| **BMI / LandWell** | Henderson, NV real-estate development (former magnesium site) | VHI **~63% of BMI**; LandWell = ~27% direct + 50% via BMI |

The 10-Q states it directly: *"Our control of Kronos includes approximately 50% we hold directly and approximately 31% held directly by NL. We own approximately 83% of NL."* Note the user's "~58M shares held directly" premise is the **~50% direct stake** (≈57.5M sh); the remaining ~31% (35.2M sh) sits inside NL — this distinction is the single most important thing to get right in the NAV (see §3, double-count trap).

## 2. Why the screen mis-fired (holdco distortion)

A consolidated screen sees the *whole* Kronos/NL/CompX/LandWell stack rolled into VHI:

- **Consolidated balance sheet (3/31/26):** total assets $2,610.0M; total debt $625.3M; total equity $1,370.7M, **of which $344.9M is noncontrolling interest** (the ~19% of Kronos + ~17% of NL/CompX + RE minorities VHI does *not* own). A screen that uses total equity or total EV over-weights value that doesn't accrue to VHI holders.
- **Consolidated EV/EBIT looks ugly and levered.** Q1-26 income before tax fell to $14.1M (from $30.8M) and net income attributable to Valhi was just **$2.0M ($0.07/sh)**; TTM is a loss. On consolidated numbers VHI screens as a leveraged, money-losing chemical company.
- **The debt is almost entirely non-recourse subsidiary debt.** From the 10-Q debt note (3/31/26): of the $625.3M total, **Valhi parent-level debt is only $11.9M** (the Contran revolving credit facility, 7.75%, $113.1M still available). The remaining $613.4M is *subsidiary* debt — Kronos $602.7M (9.50% Senior Secured Notes €426.2M ≈ $490.9M + $53.7M Contran subordinated term loan + $58.1M revolver) and LandWell $10.7M (Western Alliance note). **None of the Kronos or LandWell debt is recourse to Valhi.** A consolidated leverage/coverage screen therefore charges VHI for ~$613M of debt that can never reach the parent.

Net: the screen conflates (a) minority-owned equity and (b) ring-fenced subsidiary leverage with VHI's actual economics. Strip both, and what's left is a clean look-through claim on two liquid, publicly-priced stakes minus ~$12M of parent debt.

## 3. Sum-of-parts / look-through NAV

**The double-count trap:** NL's market cap *already embeds* the 35.2M Kronos shares NL owns. So the correct build is **(VHI's direct KRO stake at market) + (83% × NL's whole market cap)** — and you do **not** separately add NL's KRO shares. Adding them again would over-count ~$200M.

All marks at **2026-06-04** closing prices: VHI $14.49, KRO $6.86, NL $6.06. KRO 115.05M sh, NL 48.9M sh, VHI 28.5M sh.

|---|---|---|---|

| VHI stake in NL (embeds NL's KRO + 87% CompX + RE + cash) | 83% × (48.9M × $6.06 = $296.3M) | **246.0** | $8.63 |
| **Gross listed value** | | **640.6** | **$22.48** |

| **Conservative look-through NAV** | | **633.7** | **$22.23** |

**Discount to price:** $14.49 / $22.23 → **~35% discount** (reconciles to the prior ~$22.29 NAV reference).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Two embedded conservatisms worth flagging as *free* optionality: (1) NL itself trades at a holdco discount — NL's $296M cap is barely above its $241M KRO stake alone, before crediting 87% of CompX (~$260M look-through) and NL's cash — so marking VHI's NL stake at NL's *market* price applies a **discount-on-a-discount**; (2) LandWell/BMI Nevada land and the $111.9M "OPA" note receivable carry at book and are not separately credited at market. A "NL-at-its-own-NAV" build would push base NAV toward ~$25–26/sh, but I deliberately hold the headline at market-to-market (~$22) for honesty.

## 4. Underlying business: TiO2 / Kronos cycle

Kronos is the whole ballgame (Chemicals = $509.8M of $560.1M Q1-26 sales). TiO2 is a deep cyclical commodity (pigment for paint/coatings/plastics/paper), and Kronos is squarely at/near a **trough**:

- **Earnings path:** 2020 NI $63.9M → **2021 peak $112.9M** ($0.98/sh) → 2023 **loss $(49.1)M** → 2024 recovery **+$86.2M** → **2025 trough loss $(110.9)M** ($0.96 loss/sh) → Q1-2026 loss $(4.8)M.
- **Operating signal:** plant utilization fell to **77% in 2025 from 96% in 2024**; gross margin **11% vs 19%**. In the VHI segment view, Chemicals operating income collapsed to **$14.5M in Q1-26 from $41.2M a year earlier** — a textbook trough print (low price × low volume × under-absorbed fixed cost).
- **Normalized mid-cycle earning power:** the 5-yr NI average is only ~$21M (dragged by two loss years), which *understates* mid-cycle because it averages a destock-driven 2023 and a price-collapse 2025. A reasonable mid-cycle for Kronos is **$80–120M NI** (it printed $86M in a merely-okay 2024 and $113M at the 2021 peak). At ~10× mid-cycle that's a **$0.8–1.2B** Kronos equity value, i.e. **$7–10.5/sh** vs $6.86 today — so KRO is priced near trough, not mid-cycle. VHI's NAV inherits all of this beta.

## 5. Why it's cheap

1. **Controlled-company discount (structural, permanent).** ~91% Contran ownership → ~9% float, S&P-index-ineligible scale, no takeover put, no activist path. Governance/control discounts of 20–40% are normal and *do not fully close*.
2. **Cyclicality.** TiO2 is at a trough; the market refuses to pay NAV when the look-through asset is loss-making and the cycle bottom is unconfirmed.
3. **Complexity / double-holdco.** VHI → NL → (KRO + CompX + RE) is a two-layer holdco. Each layer adds a discount and a modeling barrier; most screeners can't even compute the look-through correctly (the double-count trap).
4. **Litigation memory.** NL's lead-pigment history is a permanent scarlet letter even though the acute exposure is largely behind it (§6).
5. **Related-party optics.** Contran term loan to Kronos, shared management, intercompany credit facilities — value-extraction *perception* depresses the multiple.

## 6. Hidden-liabilities check

- **Lead-pigment litigation (NL).** Materially de-risked vs the historical tail. The Q1-26 10-Q states NL *"currently has no pending lead paint class action cases or pending lead paint cases brought by housing authorities, school districts or other government entities,"* with only the possibility of new individual filings. The large California (Santa Clara/People) public-nuisance settlement that drove the prior ~$101.7M scheduled-payment liability has been the governing resolution; there is no new probable/estimable accrual in this 10-Q. **Verdict: capped/settled as premised — a known, scheduled, shrinking obligation, not an open-ended tail.** Confirm the residual payment schedule in NL's own 10-K/10-Q before sizing.
- **Environmental.** Accrued environmental remediation $14.6M (noncurrent) — modest and reserved.
- **Pension.** Accrued pension costs $76.4M (noncurrent), partly offset by a $18.2M pension asset elsewhere; manageable, not a crater. There is also a German tax assessment (2019–2021) under appeal — monitor but not sized as material.
- **Related-party / Contran.** Contran revolving facility ($11.9M drawn, $113.1M available, 7.75%) and a $53.7M Contran subordinated term loan *into Kronos*. These are the channel for any controller value-extraction; terms look market-ish but warrant ongoing scrutiny. A "Contran takes it private cheap at the trough" scenario is the real governance risk (see §8).
- **Governance discount.** Real and permanent; embedded in the rating, not a hidden surprise.

## 7. Bull case

- **~35% discount to a market-marked NAV**, struck when the core asset (Kronos) is *itself* at trough — i.e., **discount × cyclical-low**, a double-compression that mean-reverts hard if either leg normalizes.
- **Recourse leverage is trivial ($11.9M)** with $113M undrawn; the parent cannot be forced; bankruptcy risk at the VHI level is near-nil even though consolidated leverage looks scary.
- **Operating leverage to TiO2 recovery is enormous** — Kronos swung +$197M in NI from 2023→2024 on modest volume/cost moves; a normal mid-cycle ($80–120M KRO NI) plus any discount-narrowing could drive VHI toward the high-$20s (upside case $29.5/sh).
- **Hidden conservatism:** NL stake marked at NL's own discounted price; LandWell Nevada land + $111.9M OPA note at book — real "stuff" not credited at market.
- **Dividend** (~$0.32/yr, ~2.2% yield) pays you to wait; Contran's own need for VHI/KRO/NL dividend upstreaming aligns toward continued distributions.

## 8. Bear case / what breaks the thesis

- **Deeper / longer TiO2 trough.** Chinese TiO2 over-capacity and weak coatings demand could keep Kronos loss-making into 2027; KRO to $5 and NL to $4.50 takes NAV to **~$15.7/sh** — i.e., the discount *evaporates at lower asset prices* and there is no cushion. The margin of safety is in the discount, and the discount is measured against marks that can fall.
- **Controller value-extraction.** Contran controls dividend policy, intercompany loan terms, and any take-private. At a trough, a low-ball Contran buyout of the ~9% float (or further intercompany loans on favorable-to-Contran terms) is the asymmetric governance risk. Minorities have little recourse.
- **The discount never closes.** This is the base-rate outcome for a 91%-controlled, two-layer, index-ineligible holdco. You may collect the dividend and ride the cycle but realize the gap only via *KRO/NL* appreciation, not via discount-narrowing. **Reflect this in MoS: assume the ~35% gap compresses to perhaps 20–25% over a cycle, not to zero — most of your return must come from the underlying assets, not the re-rate.**

## 9. Catalysts (what actually closes the gap)

- **TiO2 cycle recovery** — the dominant lever; raises KRO/NL marks directly (NAV is ~95% Kronos-beta).
- **Buybacks / simplification** — a VHI or NL repurchase, or a collapse of the VHI→NL layer (NL merged up), would mechanically remove one discount layer. No announced plan; treat as optionality, not base case.
- **Kronos balance-sheet de-risking** (2029 notes refi at lower rates as cycle turns) lifts KRO equity.
- **LandWell land monetization** — lumpy Nevada land sales (real-estate op income jumped to $11.3M in Q1-26) provide episodic NAV proof-points.
- **Controller liquidity event** — a clean take-private *at or above NAV* would close the gap instantly but is the low-probability/controller-discretion tail.

## 10. Risks

1. TiO2 price/volume deeper or longer trough (primary; ~95% of the variance).
2. Controller (Contran) value-extraction or trough take-under of the minority float.
3. Permanent/structural discount — re-rate may never arrive; returns tied to underlying assets.
4. Liquidity: ~9% float, thin volume; hard to build/exit size without impact.
5. Litigation re-emergence (new individual lead cases) or German tax appeal loss — lower-probability, sized small.
6. Currency (Kronos is heavily EUR; €426M notes, EU plants) — FX swings move both Kronos earnings and the look-through.

## 11. Position sizing & portfolio fit

- **Own it as a deep-value-basket position, not a single-name concentrated bet.** The thesis is statistically sound (buy a real ~35% discount at a real trough) but every dollar of upside ultimately rides on the *same* asset (Kronos) and is gated by a *permanent* controlled-company discount. Size for the basket, ~1–3% type weight, alongside other independent discount/cyclical names.
- **Liquidity-constrained:** ~9% float and modest dollar-volume mean this is for patient capital that can scale in over time and tolerate wide bid/ask; not for tactical sizing.
- **VHI vs owning KRO directly — the key choice.** You buy VHI for (a) the ~35% NAV discount and (b) the embedded NL/CompX/LandWell extras at book. You *pay* for it with (a) the controlled-co governance penalty, (b) a second holdco layer, and (c) worse liquidity than KRO itself. **If your view is purely "TiO2 cycle turns," own KRO directly** — cleaner, more liquid, more torque per dollar of conviction, no holdco discount risk. **Own VHI instead only if you specifically want the discount-to-NAV closing as a *second* source of return** and are comfortable that, at a 91%-controlled holdco, that gap narrows but realistically never fully closes. A reasonable construction: core cyclical exposure via KRO, plus a smaller VHI "discount-capture" sleeve.

**Bottom-line MoS judgment:** REAL-CHEAP is the right tag — the discount is genuine and primary-source-verified, and parent recourse risk is negligible. But it is *conditionally* cheap: the ~$22 NAV is a market mark on a trough asset, so the true margin of safety is the **spread between the holdco discount and the permanent-discount floor** (~10–15 pts of compressible discount) **plus** the cyclical recovery in Kronos — not the full 35%. Underwrite to ~20–25% realized discount-closure and let the TiO2 cycle do the rest.
