# PITCH — Datadog (DDOG) — VERDICT: DECLINE. No position, no short.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at $231.33, the session after a −19.03% print-day drop ($283.17 → $229.29). Two benches: the challenge bench filed REJECT 8/10; the review bench filed DECLINE 7/10 and broke a large share of the challenge bench's reasoning while reaching the same action. Confidence 8/10. This is a crowding-and-price decline, not a business decline.*

**Say the awkward part first.** We came into this expecting to find a great business unfairly punished, and instead the first bench built a bear case that the second bench substantially dismantled: the "the whale drove all the growth" claim is causally backwards, the forward-book claim was an arithmetic error, and the insider-selling claim was overturned outright. What survived was narrower and duller than what we started with — dilution economics, flat-to-falling GAAP profit on a 74% larger revenue base, and an entry price 136% above February's low at the exact multiple where the last 52% drawdown began. We are declining on those grounds, not on the ones we first wrote down.

---

## What we believe

Datadog is a good business that just delivered its best quarter in two years, and the stock is nonetheless a poor entry today.

The quarter was strong on its face: revenue $1,121.5M, up 35.7% year over year — an acceleration from 32.1% the prior quarter. Net revenue retention improved to the low-120s. Management raised the full-year outlook by $140M, and the beat itself was only $46.5M of that, meaning they added roughly $93.5M to the second-half path — on the same day, in the same document, in which they disclosed that their largest customer began reducing usage. Companies bracing for a shock do not do that.

But three things make the entry bad rather than the business bad:

1. **Shareholders are being diluted at a real rate with nothing offsetting it.** Diluted shares went from 358.7M to 371.0M year over year — 3.43% annually — and there is no buyback at all; the repurchase line is simply absent from the financial data. At today's price that is roughly $2.8B a year of economic dilution against $915M of stated stock-compensation expense, which means the accounting understates the cost, because most of those grants were struck far below where the stock trades now.
2. **GAAP profit is going the wrong way.** Operating income was $12.6M on $645.3M of revenue two years ago and $5.5M on $1,121.5M today. Revenue grew 74%; reported operating profit shrank. Full-year 2025 was a $44.4M operating loss. On reported free cash flow the stock is ~62×; adjusted for stock compensation it is ~219× — and the dilution evidence says the adjusted number is the honest one.
3. **We would be buying 136% above this year's low into a name that just made an all-time high the prior session.** The last time Datadog traded at roughly this valuation (about 16.6× forward revenue, December 2024), it fell 52% over the following four months.

## What the market believes

That the largest customer's pullback is optimization, not departure — a pause, then a re-ramp.

We can price that belief. Building three honest two-year scenarios (whale optimizes and regrows / whale halves and growth settles near 22% / structural migration and growth falls to ~15%) and discounting them back, today's $231 implies roughly 69% confidence in the benign case. The payoff from here is about +19% in the good case against −48% in the bad — roughly 2.5 to 1 against us — and even the good case only compounds around 9% a year. The options market agrees the next print is genuinely unknowable: the November straddle prices a 27% move, on 91% implied volatility.

The market is not wrong. It is just fully paid for being right.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: we don't have much, and the review bench is why.

The candidate edge was that the market had misread the disclosure — that it punished $19.3B of market value for a customer problem it had already been told about and that management had already embedded in guidance. That part is real and defensible. Guidance was set on August 6, five weeks into the quarter, with the reduction already observed. Sized from the guide gap itself, the drag is roughly $6–11M a quarter, $25–45M a year — worth something like $0.44–0.80B of enterprise value at the multiple the market pays. The market destroyed 24 to 44 times that.

And the underlying business is genuinely better than the bear read. Stripping out the AI-native cohort entirely, the rest of Datadog accelerated from 18.1% to 27.1% growth over five quarters. The forward contracted book grew 43.1% year over year, faster than revenue. Insiders sold nothing they hadn't programmed months earlier.

But "the market overreacted by $18B" is not the same as "the stock is cheap." It only means the stock was expensive before and is somewhat less expensive now. That is not an edge we can underwrite.

## Why it might be priced in

Because the good outcome is already the base case in the price, and the un-sized risk is genuinely un-sizable.

The company disclosed that its largest customer is reducing usage and did not disclose what that customer is worth. Not its share of recurring revenue, not the cohort's share, not the name. We have five weeks of observation on an unnamed counterparty. There is no external channel that resolves it — software leaves no shipping manifest — so this is a hole we should report, not paper over.

To be long here we must be more than 69% confident in something we cannot measure. To be short here we would be fighting an accelerating base business, a 43% forward-book build, clean insider records, a raised second-half path, a 27% implied move, and a stock fresh off a 199% run with plenty of squeeze fuel. This name is crowded and heavily followed, not overlooked. Neither side pays. So we sit.

## Reopen conditions

Either one puts Datadog back in front of the court. Neither is a standing order.

1. **Information gate — the Q3 print, early November 2026.** Reopen if revenue lands at or above **$1,190M** (+34% year over year) **and** the AI-native cohort still contributes at least 7 points of growth **and** management actually sizes the largest customer as a share of recurring revenue. That combination proves the base absorbed the pullback and converts an unbounded unknown into a bounded one. Any two of three is not enough; the sizing disclosure is the one that matters most, because it is the thing we cannot get ourselves.
2. **Price gate — $155**, roughly 11× next year's revenue. Below that, the bad-case value of ~$121 is within one standard error and the upside is effectively free. Set this as an **alert, not a resting order** — a limit sitting 33% below market on a name with a live, un-sized customer risk is exactly the setup where the only fills you get are the ones you don't want.

No premium selling despite 91% implied volatility: in a taxable account that income is non-deferrable and taxed at the top ordinary rate. If we ever want the exposure, we ladder in with limits.

**Failure mode we're logging up front:** if Q3 prints above $1,190M and the stock runs from here without either gate tripping, that is a missed entry attributable to the *price* gate, not to the customer analysis. Grade it that way — against the $155 level specifically — so we learn the right lesson.

## What remains unverified

Three holes. We are reporting them rather than inferring around them.

- **Who the largest customer is.** Not named in any filing. There is no third-party channel that resolves a software customer relationship the way shipping records resolve a physical one. Search budget was exhausted mid-review without a defensible answer.
- **What that customer is worth.** Neither its share of recurring revenue nor the AI-native cohort's share is disclosed. Our whole sizing of the risk is inferred from the guidance gap, which is a floor, not a measurement. If the customer is 8% of recurring revenue rather than 3%, the bad case gets materially worse and we would not know until November.
- **Why the stock gapped 42% in the week of April 27, 2026** ($140.53 → $200.16), with no earnings in the window — the Q1 report came a week later, on May 7. That leg is positioning or narrative, and we could not source it. It matters because it is the part of the 199% run with no fundamental event underneath it, and therefore the part most likely to unwind.
