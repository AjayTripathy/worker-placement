# PITCH — monday.com (MNDY) — VERDICT: HOLD THE STARTER, ADD NOTHING BEFORE MONDAY'S PRINT. Capped at 1.5% until the billing change clears two quarters.

*Decided 2026-08-07 at $88.74, three days before the second-quarter results land on Monday 2026-08-10 at 8:30am Eastern. We own 140 shares at an average cost of $77.10 — $12,424, or 0.38% of deployable capital, up 15.1%. Two benches sat: the challenge bench filed REJECT at 8/10 conviction; the review bench filed RED CASE WEAKENED at 6/10, overturning both of the challenge bench's headline accusations while making its best surviving finding worse. Both benches agreed on the action. They disagreed almost entirely on the reason.*

**Say the awkward part first.** Our own bear case got its two loudest arguments wrong, and our own file missed two things it had explicitly written itself instructions to catch. The challenge bench's designated best finding — that monday.com told its regulator one thing and its investors another — compared a number measured in December 2024 against a number announced in August 2025, seven months apart, in a product line growing about 80% a year. The gap it called a lie is what compounding looks like. Its second headline finding, that the "record profit" quarter is an accounting artifact about to break, is arithmetically correct about the mechanism and then benchmarks the prediction against the wrong quarter: the outcome it forecasts as a disaster would be a $10–20M improvement over the same quarter last year.

Meanwhile, the things that *are* wrong with this position were mostly found by the bench arguing the other way, and two of them fired months ago while our record sat frozen. That is the honest shape of this court, and it is why the position stays small rather than either doubling or leaving.

---

## What we believe

That this is a decelerating but genuinely growing software business, priced at a level that assumes decline, holding a quarter of its market value in cash — and that it is walking into its first results under a new way of charging customers, which is exactly the configuration that has recently blown up its closest comparable.

The valuation is the reason we own any of it. About 42.3M shares at $88.74 is a $3.75B market value against roughly $0.93B of net cash and no debt, so the business itself is priced at about $2.82B. On this year's guided revenue that is **1.9 times sales**, about **10 times free cash flow**, and about **13 times operating profit before stock compensation** — for a company still growing revenue around 21% with forward bookings growing faster than revenue (contracted future revenue up 33%, the portion due within a year up 26%, both ahead of revenue's 24.5%). The large-customer business is not just intact but accelerating: customers spending over $100k are up 39% in count and their share of total recurring revenue went from 24% to 29%, which means their dollars grew about 50% — spend per large customer up roughly 8%. Customers over $500k are up 74%.

What is genuinely deteriorating is the self-service funnel, and the company has stopped feeding it: search traffic declined, management explicitly rebalanced away from online marketing, total paying customers went from >225,000 to ~245,000 to >250,000 (+9%, then +2%), and sales-and-marketing spend per new logo rose 52%. Management's own words on the last call: the top of the funnel "remains soft," guidance "doesn't assume any rebound," and self-service returns are "below historical levels" and "it just continues." That is a real problem, honestly stated, and it is narrower than "customer acquisition is broken" — the salesforce-led business is accelerating.

## What the market believes

That seat-based software is what artificial intelligence eats first, and that work management is a feature rather than a category — so it prices roughly 58% of outcomes as terminal decline and values the upside at about zero.

The stock is down about 66% from its 52-week high of $264. It trades at 1.9 times sales against a median near 4.6 for companies growing at its rate. Option pricing implies a single-day move of ±18–19% on Monday's print, with implied volatility at 95.9% annually — the market is not merely bearish, it is genuinely uncertain. Even the most pessimistic published analyst target sits above today's price, which tells you the pessimism lives in the multiple rather than in anyone's forecast.

## Why we might have edge

We do not claim better information. What we claim is a different weight on shared evidence, plus one exclusion the market's framing does not price: **being wrong slowly here is cheap**, because a quarter of the market value is cash and there is no debt.

The court also produced two corrections to the market's shorthand that we think are real. First, the completed buyback is an **asset arriving**, not a support that vanished: the company retired 10,486,207 shares — about 20% of the company — at a blended $82.97, below today's price, and the exit share count of ~42.3M is roughly 12% below the average count used in the most recent reported per-share figures. That is a ~13% per-share lift that has not yet appeared in any printed number. Second, the deceleration is flattening rather than steepening: the year-over-year slowdown ran −3.0, −1.0, −1.4 and then **−0.2** percentage points, and the largest revenue beat in the company's history (+3.6%) landed in the most recent quarter.

## Why it might be priced in

Because the seller may have seen the same thing we did and simply weighted it more heavily — and because two dated events already told us the company is under more strain than our file believed.

The first is the buyback. It is fully exhausted, tied out to the dollar from primary documents: $870M authorized in September 2025, $135.0M spent by year-end with $735.0M remaining, and the proxy in July confirming the full $870M and 20% of shares retired. There is no new authorization on file as of today, including in the annual-meeting filing furnished yesterday. And the review bench found the fact that makes this worse rather than better: Israeli law requires a **30-day creditor-objection period** before a company may begin repurchasing. That is why a September 2025 board approval produced no buying until November. **Even the single cleanest positive available on Monday — a new authorization — cannot put a bid under this stock until roughly mid-September.**

The second is the layoff. On 2026-07-22 the company cut about 20% of its workforce — roughly 630–660 people off a 3,155 base — impaired office space, and named "marketing and go-to-market" as the target, while leaving revenue guidance unchanged and raising only margin. Our file had called this company damage-absent **two days earlier**, on a document set frozen at 2026-07-20. The margin raise (about 13% to about 15% operating margin, roughly $30–35M) is offset in-year by roughly $30–35M of cash severance flowing through cash flow, so this year is close to a wash; next year inherits the benefit clean.

The third is the guide itself. The second half of this year is guided at 1.081 times the first half, against 1.12 last year and 1.15 the year before — **the weakest second-half guide in the company's history**. The review bench correctly notes this is partly a tautology (a decelerating grower posts its "weakest ever" ratio every year) and that applying the company's own six-for-six beat history lands the realized ratio near 1.093, a normal-sized step. It is still the softest guide monday.com has ever set.

## The kill neither bench argued

The review bench, prosecuting the case *for* this position, entered the best finding in the court against its own side. We are recording it as such.

On 2026-05-11 the company launched its artificial-intelligence platform alongside "a new seats-plus-credits pricing model," described by the co-chief executives as "our shift to consumption-based pricing." Our own file, written earlier, contains this instruction verbatim: a self-elected pricing or packaging change from seats to credits or consumption, announced alongside full-year guidance, is to be treated as the air-pocket branch **regardless of how clean the demand metrics look**.

That instruction exists because we watched it happen at the closest comparable in this sector two days ago: a full-year constant-currency guide cut from 17.5% to 14.6%, with currency refuted as the cause and demand demonstrably intact — logos up 14%, margins fine, a $1B buyback running. The cause was the company's own monetization reset. And the retention and product metrics were **withdrawn**, which left the rubric we grade that name against ungradeable. An ungradeable rubric is a finding, not a clean pass.

Our instruction fired on 2026-05-11 and nothing caught it. It also explains the soft second-half guide better than anything the challenge bench filed, and it is the reason the largest beat in company history landed in exactly the quarter a management resetting its pricing model would widen its cushion.

**This is why the size cap stays at 1.5% and does not move to the 3–5% our original work contemplated.** A business-risk finding cuts size. It does not set a price gate, and it does not close the position.
