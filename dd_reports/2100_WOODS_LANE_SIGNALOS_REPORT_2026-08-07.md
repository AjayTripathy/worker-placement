# SignalOS Analyst Report — 2100 Woods Lane, Los Altos, CA
### 8.96 acres, two parcels, one creek, and eleven years of trying

*Prepared 2026-08-07 from the seller's own data room (293 files, 673MB). Method: every load-bearing claim traced to the primary document inside the room (Mode A), plus an independent disconfirming pass on what the room implies but does not say (Mode B). ## The one-paragraph read

This is not a house. It is an **entitlement lottery ticket** on 8.96 gated acres (APNs 342-04-078, 1.08 ac + 342-04-089, 7.88 ac) currently occupied by a private school, with a jurisdictional creek running through the development math. The seller's paperwork shows a theoretical 28–31 dwelling PUD yield and drawn schemes for 23–24 lots — and also, read closely, shows **at least three distinct development concepts attempted since ~2015 (subdivision, 146-unit assisted living, and now riparian restoration), none of which produced a recorded approval that appears anywhere in this data room.** The decisive documents are the newest and the oldest: a 2023 habitat letter that puts federal wildlife law between the owner and the bulldozers, and a 2003 geotech whose creek plan ("remove culverts, divert the creek") is from a regulatory world that no longer exists. Whether this is a good purchase depends almost entirely on price relative to the *unentitled* value — because the room, honestly read, prices the entitlement as an option, not an asset.

## What the room says when read against itself (Mode B — the disconfirming pass)

**1. The creek is the whole case, and the room's documents disagree about it across twenty years.** The 2003 geotech's plan for the creek: remove the weak soils, remove "all pipes, dams, culverts and any other structures," divert the flow through temporary pipes, fill and compact. The 2023 Live Oak Associates constraints letter describes the same water as **jurisdictional waters of the U.S. and state**, where impacts are CEQA-significant and require permits from the Army Corps, the Regional Water Board, and California Fish & Wildlife, with habitat mitigation at equal-or-better ratios — plus a recommendation for **protocol-level U.S. Fish & Wildlife surveys for the California red-legged frog**, a federally listed species with recorded occurrences upstream. If the frog is found, development requires a federal take permit. Every lot-yield number in this room was drawn as if the 2003 rules still applied. They don't.

**2. The eleven-year record is the base rate.** Tree report 2015 → soils draft 2018 (never finalized — it remains stamped draft) → "Option 12-24" plans January 2019 → a riparian restoration plan 2019 (revised) → assisted-living concept 2021–22 → new civil sheets 2023 → habitat constraints letter 2023 → **riparian restoration plan March 2025**. A fire-department review in the room is stamped **"Plans NOT APPROVED"** (access, turnarounds, hydrant spacing — Woods Lane is a narrow private lane that is also the school's only access). Three concepts, multiple engineering cycles, one visible agency rejection, zero visible approvals. When a seller's data room documents effort this thoroughly and outcomes not at all, the absence is the finding.

**3. The 2025 riparian plan is the most important ambiguous document.** Prepared for Blue Creek Capital LLC, it commits to invasive removal, native planting, multi-year monitoring, and adaptive management. Two readings: (a) *goodwill/mitigation banking* — the owner restoring the corridor to earn regulatory credit for a future application; or (b) *an obligation* — required restoration arising from a violation or a permit condition (one plan file is named "shared with CDFW," implying active negotiation with Fish & Wildlife). Which reading is true changes the buyer's inherited liability materially. **The room does not say. Ask, in writing, before any offer.**

**4. The seller is a professional who couldn't finish.** The "DB - AL" folders document the sponsor's other assisted-living ventures (Emory/San Jose, Belmont, Stevens Creek; purchase agreements, proformas, comps, 2014–2020). This is not a naive family selling grandma's land — it is an experienced developer marketing the project's *inputs* after a decade of working its *outputs*. Price accordingly.

**5. What genuinely cuts the buyer's way.** The Phase 1 is clean. The geotech, though 23 years old, found no landslides, no mapped faults, low liquefaction — the dirt itself is workable with standard (if costlier) foundations. The parcel is gated, private, adjacent to open-space preserve land, in one of the most supply-starved land markets in America. A paying school tenant occupies it today (lease terms not in the room — obtain them; this is the carry while any entitlement runs). And the strongest new fact since this seller's failures: **California housing law (2019–2026) has shifted power toward applicants** — housing-element pressure, streamlining statutes, and builder's-remedy dynamics that did not exist when the 2016–2019 schemes died. The next attempt happens on a friendlier field than the last one. That, not the lot math, is the honest bull case.

## The decision framework

The value is a probability tree, not a comp:

- **Entitled-someday value:** even a constrained 10–15 lots (the realistic creek-setback yield, not the marketed 23–31) in this location carries residual land value far above any school-campus valuation — but net of multi-year carry, mitigation, horizontal costs, and permit risk across four agencies (Town, USACE, RWQCB, CDFW ± USFWS).
- **Never-entitled value:** a gated 9-acre campus leased to a school, plus one existing-envelope estate play. This is the floor, and the school lease is its yield.
- **The price tells you which you're buying.** Near the never-entitled value, the entitlement option comes free and the eleven-year failure record is the *seller's* sunk cost, not yours. Priced for the lot math, you are paying today for approvals that three concepts and eleven years did not produce, against a federally-listed-species survey that has not been run.

### The ask arrived: $12M cash (informal, 2026-08-07)

**The per-unit arithmetic:** $12M ÷ 390,446 sq ft = **~$31/sq ft of land** — against Los Altos retail small-lot land that trades in the low hundreds per square foot (estimate, comps unverified this session). Per gross acre: $1.34M. Per *seller's* theoretical lot (24): $500k raw. Per *realistic* creek-constrained lot (10–15): $0.8–1.2M raw.

**The three-scenario frame (all lot values are desk estimates pending comps — treat ±30%):**

| Scenario | Math | Outcome on $12M |
|---|---|---|
| Entitled, ~12 lots, ~5 yrs | 12 finished lots × ~$2.5–3M = $30–36M gross, less horizontal + creek mitigation (~$6–10M), less entitlement/CEQA/carry (~$3–4M) | Residual ~**$18–24M** → roughly a double, mid-teens IRR — good, not extraordinary, for the risk carried |
| Never entitled, hold | School lease (terms unknown) + eventual as-is resale; the 1.08-acre parcel may be separable as an estate lot (verify access/frontage) | Recovery **$8–12M?** — the least-verified number in this table and the one the deal actually hinges on |
| Never entitled, forced resale | Thin buyer pool for a creek-burdened campus; discount for the next buyer's same diligence | **$7–9M?** — the true downside |

**The honest verdict at $12M:** the price sits at or near the plausible *as-is* value — meaning the entitlement option comes roughly free **if and only if the as-is floor is real**. That inverts the burden of diligence: the decisive work is no longer handicapping the entitlement (a coin-flip improved by post-2019 housing law) but **verifying the floor** — the school lease economics, the separability of the small parcel, and what an as-is buyer pool looks like. A ~$16M probability-weighted value against $12M cost is a positive but not compelling edge *before* those verifications; it becomes compelling only if the floor verifies near $11–12M, and it dies if the floor is $8M.

**Two things the structure itself signals:** "cash" asked informally means the seller wants speed and no entitlement contingency — consistent with a planning file the seller would rather you didn't condition on. And at $12M this is not a portfolio position for this household — it is a majority-of-visible-net-worth allocation unless syndicated; if pursued, it should be priced as a lead-sponsor deal with partners, not a personal purchase.

**Negotiation read:** the eleven-year record is the buyer's leverage. Every year of failure the seller documented is an argument that the *unconditional* cash value is the floor scenario, not the lot math. An offer anchored at the verified floor (post lease/assessor/planning-file diligence) with the entitlement upside kept by the buyer is the correct shape; $12M unconditional pays the seller today for an option they proved unable to exercise.

### The conservation-easement idea (principal, 2026-08-07)

Two distinct plays hide in one word, with opposite grades. **As a tax play — donate the corridor easement, harvest the deduction — weak and audit-prone:** the deduction is a before-minus-after appraisal, and the room's own 2023 habitat letter establishes the "before" development value of the corridor is already ~nil (jurisdictional waters, CEQA, listed-species risk). Tax Court requires the before-value's highest-and-best use to be legally permissible and reasonably probable; the seller's own documents refute it. Honest appraisal = modest deduction; aggressive appraisal = the IRS's favorite target class. **As an entitlement play — spend the easement as permit currency — the strongest idea on this site, and the seller appears halfway through building it:** the 2025 restoration plan + "plan 9 shared with CDFW" read as the predicate for a conservation subdivision — permanently protected, restored corridor (natural holders: MidPen, adjacent at Rancho San Antonio; or POST) buying the CDFW agreement, CEQA mitigation, possible CRLF safe-harbor, and the city-politics story, with clustered upland lots. Moving p(entitlement) ~50%→~65-75% is worth several million of expected value — an order of magnitude above any deduction. **Sequencing rules:** (1) never record the easement before it is embedded in an approved map — it is permanent and, spent early, buys nothing; (2) verify access geometry first (a badly drawn easement can landlock upland lots if a crossing is needed); (3) the seller minted this currency unspent — the offer should not pay for it; the project should spend it.

### Access geometry — verified by satellite + hydrography (2026-08-07)

Three independent sources agree. **NHD hydrography (live API):** an unnamed intermittent stream (fcode 46007) runs south→north through the parcel at ~lon −122.0778, terminating at the north edge in a **connector segment (fcode 33400) — an already-culverted reach**. **Sub-meter aerial (Esri ortho):** the parcel is the wooded N-S riparian band with the school cluster on the northeast upland and open oak slope east of the corridor; the access lane enters from the north without touching the canyon. **The seller's own plan-8 topo:** all 21 northern lots sit on the wide northern head around a loop road; the southern ~two-thirds is a steep-walled creek canyon with zero lots ever drawn in it; two SE lots have separate street access.

Consequences for the easement strategy: (1) a conservation easement over the **southern canyon costs zero yield** — the seller's maximum plan never used it — while contributing most of the restored-corridor acreage as permit currency; (2) the easement boundary must **stop south of the northern head**, where the creek's final (already-culverted) reach threads between the lot clusters — that reach belongs in the negotiable streambed agreement, never the permanent easement; (3) landlock risk **cleared** — the buildable head fronts the northern access with no canyon crossing required. Caveats: NHD alignment is mapped (~5m class), not surveyed; the ortho date is unstamped; the upper reach remains jurisdictional, which is why the realistic yield stays ~12–15 lots, not 23.

**Do not proceed without:** the asking price and any offer history; the Town of Los Altos planning file (the room's conspicuous absence — request every application, resolution, and denial since 2015); written clarification of whether the 2025 riparian plan is voluntary or compelled; the CRLF protocol-survey status; the school lease; a current title report with easements (the lane serves the school); and a refreshed geotech — the working one is from 2003 and the 2018 update was never finalized.
