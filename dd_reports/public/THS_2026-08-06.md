# Tharisa (LSE: THS) — paying for a South African mine, getting a Zimbabwean lottery ticket priced at zero

*Position disclosed: we are buyers around 116p (roughly 0.35% of our book — deliberately small, adds blocked until a specific condition clears, below). Written 6 August 2026. Not investment advice; every number is sourced — verify them.*

## What we believe
At ~118p ($444M), the market values Tharisa's Zimbabwe project (Karo, 70%-owned, part-built, ~$300M still needed) at roughly nothing — while the funded South African business alone plausibly covers the whole price. The SA operation co-produces platinum-group metals and chrome from one Bushveld open pit, is annualising ~$209M EBITDA after its metals basket averaged 85% higher year-on-year, has its underground extension financed ($179M facility), just had reserves reassessed from 74.5Mt to 109.8Mt (killing the "8 years left" fear), and its chrome price has risen five consecutive quarters. Cleaned of a one-off royalty credit, the trailing multiple is ~7.4x operating profit at ~0.55x book. We are paid to hold the floor; Karo is a free option we deliberately value at zero.

## What the market believes
Zimbabwe eats the terminal value, and the metal rally is over. Much of this is *true and quantifiable*: miners there must surrender 30% of export dollars for local currency the state has lately not been paying (unpaid balances hit ~US$228M industry-wide by June; one peer shelved a $400M expansion over it); Tharisa's own small Zimbabwean chrome asset has been idled ~3 years by an export ban; the country just abolished its 2028 elections. Meanwhile the PGM basket has fallen ~21% from its peak over two quarters, ~46% of basket value is rhodium+iridium (thin, unhedgeable dealer markets), and a 62% family bloc controls the register with ~$300k/day of London liquidity as your exit.

## Why we might have an edge
Two decompositions nobody covering this (which is almost nobody — ~2 analysts) has published. First, **floor vs option**: the deferral branch of the Karo fork *preserves* value — commitments were already halved to ~$28M, and walking away costs shareholders nothing the price hasn't charged. The bear case treats both branches as value-destroying; the documents don't support that. Second, **Karo isn't a mini-Tharisa**: its ore mix is heavily palladium-weighted (the weakest metal in the complex) — a *bearish* fact we found in the company's own disclosures that mainstream bulls miss, and which paradoxically supports our approach of valuing Karo at zero rather than half-built cost.

## Why it might already be priced in (the honest counter)
The discount may be exactly correct: an unhedgeable minor-metals book, a controlling family, a jurisdiction that has already idled one of the company's assets, and a falling metal tape can deserve 0.55x book indefinitely. The stock sat flat through a +472% earnings print — the market demanding proof cash reaches shareholders is a rational stance. That's why we sized at a third of a percent and blocked adds behind a metal-price gate.

## What would make us sell (specific, dated)
- The company issues **its own shares** to fund Karo → exit, full stop.
- The achieved basket prints **below $2,400/oz for two consecutive quarters** (first reading: the mid-October production report) → exit. Until that reading we are not adding.
- A Karo financing **without** an explicit, enforceable dollar-repatriation right → worse than deferral; no adds.
- Underground first ore slipping past this fiscal year, or net cash going negative.
