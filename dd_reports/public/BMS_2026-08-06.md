# Braemar (LSE: BMS) — a £73M shipbroker whose audited year ended the day the tanker market went vertical

*Position disclosed: we are buyers around 220p (roughly 0.4% of our book — sized small on purpose, reasons below). Written 6 August 2026. Nothing here is investment advice; check every number yourself — we cite where each one comes from.*

## What we believe
Braemar's fiscal year ended 28 February 2026 — the same week the Strait of Hormuz disruption began — so the audited accounts everyone screens contain **none** of the extraordinary tanker market that has existed since. Braemar is the world's #2 listed shipbroker (~£135M revenue, paid in commissions). Since its year-end: the Baltic Dirty Tanker Index is up ~156% year-on-year, a supertanker on the benchmark Middle East–China run earns ~$423,000/day versus ~$22,000 a year ago, and the only listed peer (Clarkson, 10x the size) just reported a record half, +56% profit, "materially ahead of market expectations." Commission accounting lags loading and discharge by weeks-to-months, so the March–June rate spike lands in the half-year now underway. The forward order book already inflected: $72.5M at year-end → $77.9M two months later.

## What the market believes
Mostly nothing — Braemar has essentially no distributed research coverage; the one published forecast (~£14.2M operating profit) was set on 2 July, almost exactly the local low in tanker rates, before the July re-escalation. The informed skeptic's case — which we built ourselves and take seriously — is: the stock isn't as cheap as screens claim (properly adjusted for leases and regulated cash it's ~7.4x operating profit, ~5% free-cash-flow yield); broker bonuses are uncapped and take the windfall first (staff costs were 74% of revenue last year; shareholders historically keep only ~a fifth of each incremental revenue pound); and management's disclosure record deserves a discount (details in the table below).

## Why we might have an edge
Not valuation — information timing plus absence of an audience. The audited base year is structurally unrepresentative of the current business, the only forecast was struck at the bottom tick, and nobody is paid to update either. There is also a dated event that resolves the question quickly: Braemar's half-year trading update, due around 25 September on its established pattern. Uncovered companies can stay mispriced precisely because no one's job is to fix it.

## Why it might already be priced in (the honest counter)
Braemar is +6% this year — maybe that IS the market quietly pricing some of the tape. And even if revenue explodes, the uncapped bonus pool means the equity's share of the upside is structurally limited; a windfall that lands mostly in broker pockets doesn't re-rate the stock. That, plus the disclosure history, is why this is 0.4% of the book and not 2%.

## What would make us sell
- The September update reads "in line" while Clarkson runs ahead → exit (that's a verdict on the franchise, not the cycle).
- Staff costs above ~74% of revenue in a windfall half (November interims) → sell.
- Any further gap between what the accounts imply and what announcements later reveal → exit the same week, regardless of price.
