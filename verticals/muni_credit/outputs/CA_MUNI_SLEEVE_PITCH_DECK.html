The Thesis in One Statement

Buy the pledges that keep paying when tech-driven state revenue collapses.

California's General Fund leans on personal income tax, and the most volatile slice of that is capital-gains realizations on tech equity. A severe AI de-rating would collapse those realizations — the dot-com bust cut California capital-gains revenue ~71% and pressured state-General-Fund-backed credits by an estimated 4–5 notches.

What kept paying

Bonds secured by a statutory property-tax lien (school general obligation) or by essential-service utility revenue were untouched through the dot-com bust. No California school GO has missed a debt-service payment through Loma Prieta, Northridge, or the Camp Fire.

What we exclude entirely

The state-General-Fund channel — income-tax-backed state GO and appropriation debt — is the transmission path for an AI shock. This sleeve holds no state GO or state-appropriation debt; its only state-linked exposure is two University of California names (8% of par) whose pledge includes some state educational appropriations — scored accordingly at the sleeve's lowest insulation tier.

The yield is not a risk premium for reaching down the quality curve. It is compensation for illiquidity and obscurity inside verified-safe credits — small, unrated-on-the-public-feed, lightly-traded issuers sitting behind a statutory first lien.

Why The Yield Exists

An obscurity-and-illiquidity premium, not a credit premium.

Small & unrated on the public feed

EMMA returns no third-party agency rating for most of these issuers — the agencies do not publish them on the public feed. A buyer leaning on agency letters is effectively flying blind, so the names trade cheap to their structure.

Lightly traded

Municipals trade by appointment with no consolidated quote. Thin tapes — some names print a handful of times a month — keep institutional buyers away and widen the spread the seller must give up.

Strong statutory credit underneath

Behind the obscurity sits a CA Gov. Code §53515 first lien and an unlimited ad-valorem levy that floats up to cover debt service regardless of the economy. The structure is strong; only the visibility and liquidity are weak.

The distinction that matters — liquidity is not credit

Reaching for yield normally means accepting weaker credit. Here the extra yield is paid for how hard the bond is to find and trade — a cost borne once, on a hold-to-maturity ladder, against a pledge our own pledge-level read scores as Strong. Liquidity measures how easily you can sell; default risk measures whether the issuer pays — and the §53515 levy pays the same whether a bond trades five times a year or five hundred. The most liquid municipals have defaulted (Puerto Rico, Detroit GOs); CA school GOs under this lien essentially never have. Thin trading is why these bonds are cheap — never why they would be unsafe. We are paid for patience, not for risk.

Anatomy of a Cheap Bond

Why one bond costs 79 cents — and another 102.

Worked example — Turlock USD 3% of 2043 at 79.2 (8.61% after-tax). The price looks alarming until you decompose it. None of the three drivers is credit:

1 · Bond math, not distress

These are 3% coupons issued at the 2020–21 rate floor. With long tax-exempt yields near 4.5%, a 17–19-year 3% coupon must price near 78–80 to equate yields. Every 2020-vintage California school 3s-of-'43–'45 looks like this — the price is arithmetic about a coupon, not an opinion about a district.

2 · The de-minimis cliff overshoot

Below the de-minimis threshold, discount accretion is taxed as ordinary income — so retail buyers and advisors reflexively avoid these bonds. The market's concession overshoots the actual tax cost: Turlock yields ~4.79% vs ~4.09% for a comparable par-coupon name — a ~70bp concession against a true tax drag of ~30bp. The ~40bp net is harvestable only by a top-bracket, hold-to-maturity buyer who does the after-tax math per name. Buyers who can't, stay away — which is exactly why the concession persists.

3 · Orphan coupon, orphan issuer

The institutional muni standard is a 5% coupon near par; low-coupon discounts are an unloved structure with no natural constituency. Stack that on small, agency-unrated issuers — Emery USD serves a few hundred students (atop Emeryville's dense commercial tax base) — and the bonds are orphaned twice over, inside the same statutory first-lien pledge as everything else in the book.

The lemons test — structure, not information

If one district yielded 8.6% while its peers sat at 7.5%, someone would know something. Instead all fifteen discount 3s in the book cluster at 8.0–8.7% after-tax, ordered almost perfectly by maturity — a whole coupon class being repriced, not name-specific information. And every name-specific channel we can check — fiscal-stress rosters, pledge text, tax status, two-sided tape — came back clean.

The mirror image — why the book also pays 102 for UC Regents at "only" 6.3%

The anchors are the discounts inverted: a 5% UC coupon at 101.7, callable May 2027 at par, is an 11-month bond wearing a 2035 costume — 6.32% after-tax to the near-certain call, ~2× a T-bill's after-tax equivalent. It does two jobs the discounts can't: the front rung of the ladder (insulated short paper is structurally scarce) and the deepest liquidity in the book (~100 trades/yr) — the seat that lets thin names be held without forced selling. Call asymmetry favors the holder (no refund → 5% to 2035 ≈ 9.6% tax-equivalent). Cost of the seat: ~$700 of forgone yield until the call recycles the cash.

Tax Treatment — Worked Example

How the after-tax yield is actually computed.

The headline metric is an after-tax tax-equivalent yield — not a gross coupon. Every discount bond's yield splits into two pieces taxed differently. Worked on Turlock USD 3% of 2043 at 79.23 (YTW 4.785%):

1 · Split the yield by tax character

Coupon (current yield) = 3.00 ÷ 79.23 = 3.79% — federally tax-free.
Accretion to par = YTW − current = 4.785 − 3.79 = 1.00% — ordinary income.

Why ordinary? The de-minimis cutoff = par − 0.25 × years ≈ 95.75 (the bond was issued at 97.80; either way far above 79). Buying below the cutoff makes the entire pull-to-par ordinary income, not tax-exempt — the de-minimis rule.

2 · The formula

After-tax cash you keep = coupon (tax-free) + accretion × (1 − t).
Tax-equivalent yield divides that by (1 − t):

TEY = coupon ÷ (1 − t)  +  accretion

The coupon gets the full gross-up; the accretion enters at 1.0× — no muni benefit, because in tax-equivalent terms ordinary income maps 1:1 to a taxable bond. That 1.0× is the de-minimis penalty, made explicit.

Why the levy survives a quake

The unlimited ad-valorem levy floats up on surviving assessed value to cover debt service; Prop 13 keeps assessed value well below market, a buffer against decline; county Teeter plans advance delinquent collections to the district. No CA school GO defaulted through Loma Prieta, Northridge, or the Camp Fire. The 25% per-zone cap reflects that San Andreas North (1906) and South (1857) rupture independently.

What could still hurt

Rate shock: all munis can fall together — AI-insulation rates credit, not price. Fill slippage: thin names give back some yield in spread on the buy. Community-college names: AB 1200 certification does not apply (CCCO monitors); their fiscal status is unverified, not clean. Concentration: Bay + LA Basin together are the bulk of the ladder.

AB 1200 fiscal-certification gate

Every K-12 district in the basket is absent from the state's Negative and Qualified rosters (FY24-25 interims). The publicly stressed districts — SFUSD & Plumas (Negative), Oakland & Hayward (Qualified) — hold no place in this ladder. Community-college districts are outside this regime: treated as unverified, not clean.

The channel is insurance, not flame

No CA school GO defaulted through Loma Prieta, Northridge, or the Camp Fire — Paradise's bonds paid while the town burned. The live risk is slower: insurer withdrawal makes property unmarketable, which drags the assessed-value base the levy rides on over years, with no covenant trigger. That benign historical record predates this channel — which is exactly why we measure exposure instead of citing history.

Why the two perils share a page

Steering the book away from fault zones steers it toward the hills — the first draft of this portfolio concentrated 21% of names in high-fire districts because of its seismic discipline. Three were swapped out at zero yield cost once fire was scored. Earthquake is capped per fault zone (ruptures are local: 1906 vs 1857); fire is capped in aggregate (sieges are statewide: 2017, 2020). One peril per slide, one joint constraint set per book.

Honest Caveats — What Could Make This Wrong

Read these before the headline yield.

Marks are EMMA last-trade prices. Municipals trade by appointment, so a given bond's last print may be days old even for liquid names. The sleeve's value is an appointment-market estimate, not a continuous quote.
Thin names give back some yield in spread on fill. The realized after-tax TEY on the yield-core depends on execution; the dealer markup (0.1–0.5pt) on the buy is a real reduction to the headline figure for the L4 names.
AI-insulation rates credit, not price. It is a statement about which pledges keep paying in a tech-driven state-revenue collapse — not a forecast of mark-to-market in a pure interest-rate shock, where all munis can fall together.
The agency column is a class benchmark, not an issue rating. EMMA carries no issue-level third-party rating for most of these names; the column shows the typical rating for each security class for context only. Issue-level ratings can be pulled from a paid feed on request.
Community-college districts are unverified-not-clean. AB 1200 interim certification does not apply to them (the Chancellor's Office monitors separately); we have not independently cleared their fiscal status, and we do not treat that absence as a clean bill.
Eleven of twenty-four bonds breach the de-minimis threshold. Their discount accretion is taxed as ordinary income. The after-tax TEY already reflects this drag, but it makes the tax outcome sensitive to the buyer's actual bracket and holding behavior.
