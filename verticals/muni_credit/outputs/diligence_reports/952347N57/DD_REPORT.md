## (a) Verdict & Thesis

**Verdict: ADD — but as a TAX-BASE / SEISMIC bond, not a clean operator. Size it modestly and price the operating-distress and Chevron-concentration tails honestly.**

This is a genuinely insulated *bond* attached to a genuinely troubled *district*. The two
caveats the screen flagged — Hayward-fault seismic and enrollment decline — are real but are
**not** the bond's first-order risk. The honest decisive findings are:

1. **The operating district is in a multi-year fiscal crisis** ($127M three-year structural
   deficit; reserves projected to deplete ~2027; $13M/yr borrowed from the retiree-health fund;
   county oversight; board dysfunction; and a historical 1991 *state receivership* — WCCUSD was
   California's first). This has already cost the GO **two rating notches** (AA-/Aa3/AA+ at our
   2016 issuance → **A+ negative** at both S&P (Sep-2024) and Fitch (Dec-2025)).

2. **Why it is still an ADD, not a decline:** the operating distress does **not** reach this
   bond's source of repayment. The pledge is an *unlimited ad-valorem property-tax levy* that the
   County Board of Supervisors is statutorily **obligated** to levy and collect on every parcel in
   the district — legally separate from the General Fund that is in deficit. A school district's
   General-Fund insolvency does not default its voter-approved GO bonds; the levy floats on top of
   the tax roll, not the operating budget. The takeaway-vs-data test passes only because the
   operating distress is **fully and publicly disclosed** (qualified/positive-with-conditions AB-1200
   filings, FCMAT reports, rating actions) — this is honestly-disclosed bad news on the operating
   side that does **not** impair the secured GO claim. That is exactly the "impaired-business-that-
   discloses-is-clean" pattern; the bond is the insulated layer.

3. **Where the bond *is* exposed:** the GO rating channel (already repriced two notches and on
   negative watch — a third notch is plausible and would widen spread) and **tax-base
   concentration** — the Chevron Richmond refinery is **12.02% of the district's secured assessed
   value** and a chronic property-tax appellant. A refinery wind-down or a large successful Chevron
   appeal is the most credible path to *levy-rate* (not default) pressure.

**Seismic & enrollment, up front:** The Hayward fault runs directly through the district (Ss 2.15
is correctly HIGH). But seismic risk to a *diversified, parcel-wide* GO tax base is a levy-rate /
recovery-lag risk, not a default mechanism — a quake damages buildings, not the County's
obligation to levy; AV recovers and Prop-8 reductions are temporary. Enrollment decline
(~-2.3%/yr) is an *operating-revenue* problem (LCFF is per-ADA) and is the proximate cause of the
deficit — but it does **not** touch the GO levy, which is sized to debt service on a fixed
authorization, not to enrollment. Both caveats are real and are priced into the A+/neg rating;
neither is a bond-default channel.

**Mechanism (why there is edge):** masking channel = *headline conflation* (a district that is
"running out of money," in county oversight, with a receivership history, reads as a credit to
avoid — when the secured GO claim is unaffected). Signal channel = the statutory unlimited-levy
pledge + AB-1200/FCMAT disclosure that lets you confirm the operating distress is ring-fenced.
Signal-to-price latency: the rating agencies have already moved (Sep-24, Dec-25), so the easy
spread is partly captured; remaining edge is the gap between "scary district name" retail
perception and the legally-insulated levy, plus the ~25-30bps after-tax pickup vs a clean A+ CA
school GO.

## (c) Issuer, Tax Base, Debt Load & Enrollment

**Tax base (2016 OS, FY2015-16):** Total AV **$26,700,078,330**. Decade of AV swings shows real
cyclicality (–12.3% FY2009-10, –7.7% FY2010-11 post-GFC; then recovery). County-wide AV has since
risen to ~$278.8B (FY2024-25) / ~$290.7B (FY2025-26) — WCCUSD's parcel-level AV has grown
materially in tandem, which mechanically improves debt-to-AV vs the 2016 snapshot below even as
nominal debt rose.

**Top-taxpayer concentration — MATERIAL.** Chevron USA's Richmond refinery = **12.02% of total
local secured AV** (FY2015-16), the single largest taxpayer, with a long history of property-tax
appeals and settlements that have repeatedly *reduced* the roll (a $581.7M settlement reduction; a
$915.8M double-reporting correction). This is the most concrete tax-base risk: a refinery
wind-down or a large successful appeal compresses the levy base and forces the levy *rate* up (GO
debt service is fixed, so a smaller base = higher rate per $100k AV, which is legal under the
unlimited pledge but politically/credit-relevant).

**Debt load — HIGH (verify carefully, as instructed).** From the 2016 OS direct & overlapping
table (as of 02/01/2016, AV $26.7B):

| Metric | Amount | Ratio to AV |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct GO debt** (District) | $967,491,483 | **3.62%** |
| Total direct + overlapping tax & assessment debt | — | 4.29% |
| Gross combined total debt (incl. overlapping GF/pension) | $1,825,484,599 | 6.84% |
| **Net combined total debt** | $1,772,877,445 | **6.64%** |
| GO authorized since 1998 (six measures) | $1.63 billion | — |
| GO then-outstanding | ~$968.2 million | — |

A **3.62% direct-debt-to-AV** is high for a CA school district (typical < 2%; > 2.5% is elevated).
WCCUSD has a documented history of aggressive GO issuance and capital-appreciation-bond (CAB) use,
and is a frequently-cited "high debt burden" district. **Since 2016** the district added **Measure
R ($575M, approved Mar-2020, ~$60 per $100k AV)** plus the **Oct-2024 GO/refunding issuance
(~$352M)**, so *nominal* direct debt is now well above the 2016 ~$968M — but AV has roughly
doubled, so the *ratio* is likely in a broadly similar 3-4% direct range. **The precise current
debt-to-AV (FY2024-25) could not be pinned to a primary figure this session — flagged
UNVERIFIABLE; the OS-anchored 3.62% direct / 6.64% net combined (FY2015-16) is the verified
number.** Direction of travel on the rating (two notches down) is consistent with a still-elevated,
not improving, burden.

**Enrollment — declining, as flagged.** ~28,247 (FY2019-20) → ~24,792–25,000 (FY2025-26) ≈
**-2.3%/yr** (screen's -1.85%/yr is in the same range, modestly understated). This is the proximate
driver of the operating deficit (LCFF funds per-ADA). **It does not touch the GO levy.**

**Operating-fiscal / governance context (the decisive Mode-B finding):**
- **$127M three-year structural deficit**; reserves projected to deplete ~FY2026-27; $13M/yr
  borrowed from the retiree-health benefits fund (Fund 71) to balance.
- Budget certification cycle: **qualified → "positive with conditions"** (May-2025), under **Contra
  Costa County Office of Education oversight**; AB-1200 disclosures filed.
- Governance dysfunction: board **rejected the required budget-cuts resolution 3-2 (Oct-2025)**,
  risking appointment of a county fiscal advisor with power to halt/rescind board decisions.
- ~10% workforce reduction planned (4,000→3,600 positions); layoff-notice deadlines; possible
  school mergers.
- **Historical tail: WCCUSD (then Richmond USD) was California's FIRST district to enter STATE
  RECEIVERSHIP (1991)** after a 1990 bankruptcy. This is a real governance tail, but a state
  receiver historically *protects* debt service (the state stepped in to keep the district whole);
  a school GO has never defaulted through CA fiscal-takeover.
