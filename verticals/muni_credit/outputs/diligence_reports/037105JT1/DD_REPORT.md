## (a) Verdict & thesis

**HOLD — do not add; not a REJECT.** The security itself is sound: this is a tax-exempt, voter-approved, **unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** bond whose debt service is paid from a dedicated county property-tax levy, collected under the **Teeter Plan** (the County advances the District 100% of the levy regardless of delinquency). That payment channel is legally and mechanically **insulated** from the District's general-fund operating budget. On the bond's own cash-flow source, this is a continue-to-hold.

**But two screen inputs are wrong as of cutoff, and one of them is decisive — so this is not a clean add.**

1. **The screen says "AB-1200 POSITIVE." That is refuted.** The Contra Costa County Office of Education downgraded Antioch USD's interim certification to **QUALIFIED on 2026-02-02** and then to **NEGATIVE on 2026-04-17** — the most severe AB-1200 status, on a ~$31.5M budget shortfall, ~303 FTE layoff notices, a reserve-minimum breach, an FCMAT review, and explicit board discussion of receivership / state-takeover risk. The District is in genuine operating distress. (Verified against the County Office of Education's own action — see (d).)

2. **The screen describes an "SB-222 lien." That is not in this bond's documents.** SB-222 (the Government Code §53515 statutory lien) applies to GO bonds issued on or after 2016-01-01; this Series A was issued in 2013 and its Official Statement contains **no** statutory-lien language. The security is the unlimited ad-valorem levy plus a pledged Debt Service Fund — strong, but **not** the SB-222 statutory lien the screen asserts. (See (b).)

**Why HOLD and not REJECT:** the operating distress hits the *general fund*, while GO debt service is paid from a *segregated, off-general-fund property-tax levy* advanced in full by the County under Teeter. The Bond Interest & Redemption Fund actually **grew** to $9.5M at 6/30/2025. Moody's, in its **2026-06-03 downgrade**, cut the *issuer* rating to Baa1 but kept the *GO unlimited-tax* rating one notch higher at **A3** — the agency is making exactly this distinction. So the bond is not impaired, but the credit is deteriorating, the marketed "POSITIVE/clean" framing is stale, and the GOULT rating has fallen three notches since issuance (Aa3 → A3). Hold the existing lot; do not add at a near-par price that does not pay you for a NEGATIVE-certified, layoff-stage issuer.

**Honesty-alpha read:** the catch here is **takeaway-vs-data divergence**, not the underlying distress. A NEGATIVE-certified district that discloses its deficit honestly (and AUSD does — the FY24-25 annual report shows the $28M fund-balance drawdown plainly) would still be CLEAN. What is *not* clean is the **screen's own marketed takeaway** ("AB-1200 POSITIVE; AI insulation 99; clean") diverging from the District's current, publicly-certified NEGATIVE status. The buy-book label was last-cycle data presented as current.

## (e) The other screens (AI / fire / flood / earthquake)

**AI-crash insulation — score 99 (CONFIRMED, fully insulated).** This is a **local property-tax** GO. Its debt service has no dependence on the California State General Fund and therefore no exposure to the capital-gains/income-tax channel that drives our AI/tech-crash credit model for State GO. An AI/tech crash that craters state income-tax revenue does not reach a property-tax-secured school GO. Verified by pledge type. (Note: this insulation is about the AI/state-revenue channel only — it does **not** insulate the District's general fund from the *local* operating pressures in (d), which are LCFF/enrollment/cost-driven, not AI-driven.)

**Wildfire — ~4% tail (CONFIRMED low).** Antioch is Delta-edge/East-Bay flatland and terrace, not wildland-urban interface. Low fire exposure to the tax base; no insurance-withdrawal AV channel of concern.

**Flood — REVIEW (screen "45% tail"): real Delta-edge exposure, but NOT a 45%-of-AV impairment.** Antioch sits at the western edge of the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta on the San Joaquin River. Public flood data (ClimateCheck/FEMA) put **~17% of Antioch buildings at some flood risk** with **5 of ~85 census tracts** carrying significant building-level risk — concentrated along the shoreline (Smith Canal backs up in a San-Joaquin-River 100-year event). However, the bulk of the SFID No. 1 tax base sits on **elevated terrace land above the river**, not on the below-sea-level leveed Delta islands that carry catastrophic levee-failure risk. The "45%" is a screen *flag*, not a measured 45% of AV underwater; treat Delta-levee/sea-level-rise as a **long-horizon AV watch-item**, not a base-case impairment. The OS itself carries only generic "earthquake, flood or other natural disaster could reduce AV" boilerplate — **no specific Delta-levee disclosure** (UNVERIFIABLE at the OS level; characterized here from external flood-hazard sources).

**Earthquake — Ss ~1.82 (CONFIRMED moderate-to-high).** The **Concord–Green Valley** and **Greenville** faults run through Contra Costa and are M6.7-capable; Ss ≈ 1.82 is consistent with this East-Bay-fault setting. This is a tax-base (AV) seismic risk, not a debt-service risk — and against an unlimited-levy/Teeter GO it would have to destroy a large share of the base *and* defeat the County's obligation to raise the rate before it reached this bond. Real but secondary for the pledge.

## (g) Tax & yield — and the call/sinker mechanics

- **Yield:** At the recent ~99.35 dollar price, our bond math gives **YTM-to-2038 = 4.068%**, reproducing EMMA's reported 4.068% to the third decimal. Modified duration ≈ **9.4 years**. The screen's after-tax TEY ~8.1% is a **taxable-equivalent** figure (≈ ×2.01 CA top-bracket gross-up on a ~4.07% gross tax-free yield) — *taxable-equivalent, not gross or net-of-fee; do not conflate.* The bond is confirmed **tax-exempt both federally and in CA**, so the gross-up is legitimate (not a federally-taxable muni that the after-tax screen can mis-harvest).
- **Worst-case yield = to maturity.** Although the bond is *continuously callable at par since 2023*, it trades at a **discount** to par, so its yield-to-call exceeds its yield-to-maturity — i.e., maturity (2038) is the worst case and EMMA correctly reports ~4.02–4.07%. The par call is an *asymmetric benefit* to a discount holder: the issuer only calls if refinancing is cheaper (rates fall), which would be value-accretive to you anyway. The 2036/2037 sinking-fund redemptions at par shorten average life modestly.
- **De-minimis:** at 99.35 the 0.65-pt discount is far above the de-minimis threshold (~97.00 = par − 0.25% × ~12 yrs), so there is **no de-minimis breach**; secondary-market discount accretes as capital gain, not ordinary income. (Original-issue-discount nuance: the bond was *issued* at 96.307 OID; a secondary buyer above the issue price has accreted-OID treatment — consult tax counsel for the exact basis split, but it does not change the credit call.)
