# Dislocation-Buy Diligence — Oakland USD GO, CUSIP 672325ZG5

**Security:** Oakland Unified School District (County of Alameda, California), General Obligation Bonds (Election of 2006), **Series 2016A** — the **$20,170,000 3.000% Term Bond due August 1, 2041** (CUSIP 672325ZG5).
**Cutoff:** 2026-06-20 | **Analyst-facing dislocation diligence**

---

## a. Verdict — the dislocation call

**BUY-dislocation.**

Three sentences: The market has marked this bond to ~83.75 (yield-to-maturity ~4.49%, after-tax taxable-equivalent ~7.6–9.0%) on an **operating/general-fund** story — Oakland USD's chronic structural deficits, a 2025 Fitch downgrade and Moody's negative outlook, a qualified audit, FCMAT oversight and the legacy of the 2003 state takeover — none of which reaches the bond's cash flows. Debt service on this GO bond is paid from a **separate, mandatory, unlimited county ad-valorem levy** that California law keeps **at all times segregated from the District's operating revenues**, sits under a **statutory first lien (Government Code §53515)**, is held by the **County** (not the District) in an Interest-and-Sinking Fund used **solely** for the bonds, and is in fact **over-levied 18 months of debt service every year** — so the bonds kept paying right through the 2003 receivership and have never been impaired. The discount is a **rating/coupon/headline** artifact (low 3% coupon + a ~90–120 bp spread the operating headline has tacked on), not a cash-flow problem, which gives a money-good 15-year hold at a high tax-free carry **plus** ~5–10 points of price-recovery optionality if the operating outlook stabilizes.

**The single most important fact that confirms the thesis:** Both rating agencies rate the GO bond **above** the operating district, by their own published methodology — Moody's GO **A1** vs Issuer **A2** ("one notch higher due to California's lockbox security features"); Fitch GO **AA-** vs Issuer Default Rating **BBB** (a four-notch gap). The rating architecture itself prices the insulation. The thing that would break the thesis — operating distress reaching the levy — is contradicted by the agencies who are paid to find exactly that.

**One correction to the thesis as posed:** the insulation does **NOT** come from the Teeter Plan. Alameda County **expressly does not apply Teeter to school-district GO levies** (confirmed in two separate official statements). The insulation is real, but the load-bearing mechanism is the **§53515 statutory lien + county-held segregated I&S Fund + the County's standing practice of over-levying 18 months of coverage**, which is arguably *stronger* than a Teeter advance because it builds a structural surplus rather than just back-filling delinquencies.

## c. Is the debt-service levy actually segregated from the operating mess? (the core test)

This is where the thesis lives or dies. I tried hard to break it. The answer: **the levy is segregated, and the operating distress structurally cannot reach it.**

**The flow of money (from the OS diagram):** District taxpayers → County Treasurer (collects the dedicated debt-service tax) → Interest & Sinking Fund (held by the County, used solely for the bonds) → Paying Agent (U.S. Bank) → Bondholders. The District receives **account-balance information only** — "no money representing property taxes is received by the District." The District has no role in the taxation or payment process.

**The over-levy cushion (stronger than Teeter):** "the County has historically levied a tax in each year sufficient to cover debt service obligations of the District for the **succeeding eighteen months**... which has resulted in a **surplus balance in the Interest and Sinking Fund each year**." So delinquencies are absorbed by a built-in 1.5x timing cushion, not by district money.

**Did the bonds pay through the 2003 state takeover?** Yes. The 2003 emergency was a **General-Fund** liquidity crisis: the District drew a $100M State Emergency Apportionment Loan (S.B. 39) to meet **operating** costs, repaid from the **District's General Fund** over 20 years and intercepted from **State apportionments** — entirely on the operating side. The GO debt-service levy, run by the County, was never part of it and never missed. The same separation held through every FCMAT letter and the qualified audits since.

**The General-Fund distress is unambiguously operating, by its own description** (2021 OS FCMAT section): projected operating deficits, unsettled collective-bargaining agreements, LCFF revenue calculations, reserves, healthcare (HBGB), declining enrollment, school-closure fights. **None of these line items is the debt-service levy.**

**The two "qualified" items, disambiguated (they are different things, both operating/disclosure):**
- The **qualified audit opinion** in the OS is a narrow **GAAP** qualification — the District omitted Associated Student Body (student-activity site) funds from its financials. "Except for the omission of the ASB funds," the auditor opined the statements fairly present the District's position. This is a disclosure-completeness issue, not insolvency, and not the bond.
- The **AB-1200 / AB-1840 "qualified certification"** (the one flagged in the prompt) is a **fiscal-solvency** certification on the District's *operating* budget — the formal label for "this district may not meet its financial obligations." Again: the operating budget, not the segregated levy.

**Refutation attempts — every way I could find the distress reaching the bond, and why each fails:**
1. *Limited-tax / lien gap?* No — it's unlimited-tax with a §53515 first lien on the levy revenues. No gap.
2. *Teeter exclusion?* The Teeter exclusion is **real** and removes the delinquency-advance feature — but it is **replaced** by the County's 18-month over-levy, which produces a surplus every year. Net: not a hole.
3. *Bankruptcy?* The District **cannot voluntarily file** (state law bars it while the Infrastructure-Bank lease bonds were outstanding; and only the State Superintendent's administrator can file on its behalf). If a Chapter 9 ever occurred, the §53515 statutory lien survives into post-petition receipts; the automatic stay could **delay** (not erase) payments. The dedicated levy "may not lawfully be used for any other purpose," which a court would likely respect. This is a tail-of-tail risk, expressly disclosed, and has not occurred in 20+ years of distress.
4. *Diversion of the levy to plug the operating hole?* Prohibited — debt-service ad-valorem revenue "may not be lawfully used for any other purpose." The District *may* voluntarily top-up the I&S Fund but has **no obligation**, and the money it would use isn't in its hands anyway.

**Finding: VERIFIED.** The operating distress does not reach this bond's cash flows.

## d. The litigation flag — operating or security-reaching?

Investigated the master's issuer-litig-review flag against the OS. Two buckets, both resolved benign:

- **"No Litigation" certificate:** "**No** litigation is pending or threatened concerning or contesting the **validity of the Bonds** or the District's ability to receive ad valorem taxes... or to issue and retire the Bonds." So nothing reaches the security, the levy, or the lien. (CONSISTENT / no fire.)
- **Routine operating lawsuits:** the District discloses "several lawsuits... stemming from events that occurred between 1990 and 2018 which, if decided for the plaintiff, would materially adversely affect the District's **finances**" — i.e., the **General Fund**. The OS itself closes the loop: "The Bonds are payable from **dedicated unlimited ad valorem property taxes that may not be lawfully used for any other purpose**."

No SEC/disclosure/securities-fraud/proceeds-misuse action surfaced in the OS, and the litigation that exists hits the operating budget, not the bond. **Finding: operating-only — NOT thesis-breaking.** (A live federal-docket screen on the District is a reasonable add-on, but the OS's own validity certificate and the dedicated-levy ring-fence make this low-priority; the master flag appears to be the routine operating litigation, not a security-reaching matter.)

## f. Why is it at ~84? (the dislocation mechanism, decomposed)

**EMMA trade tape (authoritative, saved to `raw/emma_trade_tape.json`, 2,158 prints):** most recent sale-to-customer **2026-05-28 @ 83.751, yield 4.488%**; the tape is two-sided and reasonably liquid ($50k–$225k blocks across late-April/May 2026, prints clustered 83.5–85.0). This matches the prompt's ~83.75 / ~8.1% TEY.

**The discount is explained, in order of contribution, by:**
1. **Low coupon (3.00%) + higher-rate regime.** A 3% bond in a ~4.5% market trades at a discount mechanically — most of the gap to par is the coupon-vs-market-rate differential, *not* credit. Pull-to-par over 15 years (~1.08 pts/yr) is contractual.
2. **The operating-headline spread.** At 4.49% YTM vs an AAA 15-yr benchmark of ~3.3–3.6%, the bond carries roughly **+90 to +120 bps** of spread. For an **A1 (Moody's) / AA- (Fitch)** GO that's **wide** — a single-A/AA CA school GO would normally trade ~+40–60 bps. The extra ~50–60 bps is the market pricing the *operating* headline (negative outlook, deficit, downgrade) onto a security that doesn't bear that risk.
3. **No insurance** (priced on underlying, which is fine here).

**Peer/spread check:** the spread is consistent with the *operating* credit (BBB issuer, A2 issuer rating) bleeding into the *bond's* price even though the bond is rated AA-/A1. That gap between where it trades and where its bond rating says it should trade **is the dislocation.**

It is **not** something worse: no default, no missed payment, no security-reaching litigation, no AV collapse, no lien gap. YTW = YTM = 4.49% (the 8/1/2026 par call is far out of the money at an 84 price, so it does not truncate the bond).

## g. Seismic (Hayward fault) — sized, disclosed, diversifiable

Oakland sits on the **Hayward fault**; the OS discloses a **72% probability of a M6.7+ Bay Area quake before 2043** (USGS/2014 Working Group). This is a genuine tail risk to the **AV base** (a major quake could damage property and depress assessed values, pressuring the levy base). Two mitigants specific to *this security*:
- The levy is **unlimited as to rate** — if AV falls, the County raises the *rate* to cover the same dollar debt service (subject to collection). The dollar obligation is fixed; the rate floats.
- The base is huge and granular ($73B, top-20 = 6.7%), so even significant localized damage is a fraction of the levy base.

This is a real, honestly-disclosed risk that applies to **every** Bay Area GO — it is a **portfolio-level cap** consideration (don't stack Oakland on top of other Hayward/Bay-Area names), not a reason to avoid this specific bond. **Anti-masking note:** the OS discloses it plainly; this is disclosed bad-news, not a hidden risk — it does not change the BUY but should be position-sized.

## h. Rating-recovery optionality vs carry — sizing the upside

**Current ratings (2025 actions — the dislocation driver, saved to `raw/ratings_and_headline_evidence.md`):**
- **Moody's (2025-10-24):** Issuer **A2**; GO Bond **A1** ("one notch higher due to California's lockbox security features"); outlook **stable → negative**. Driver: general-fund flexibility; FY25 ~$43.4M deficit, ~7% reserves.
- **Fitch (2025-09-15):** Issuer Default Rating **BBB+ → BBB**; GO Bond **AA → AA-**; outlook negative. Driver: enrollment decline, failed consolidations, expiring pandemic funds, reserve pressure — **"not debt service specifically."**
- **At 2016 issue (uninsured):** Moody's Aa3 / S&P AA- / Fitch AAA.

**The carry while you wait:** current yield (coupon/price) = **3.58% tax-free**; YTM **4.49%**; after-tax TEY **~7.6%** (federal 40.8%) to **~9.0%** (federal + CA ~50.4% double-exempt); prompt's ~8.1% sits in this range. Plus contractual pull-to-par of ~1.08 pts/yr. **You are paid well to hold a money-good bond.**

**The recovery option (price sensitivity, computed):**

| Scenario | Yield | Price | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 4.49% | 83.75 | — |
| Outlook stabilizes, spread tightens ~50 bps | 4.00% | ~88.7 | **+5.0 pts (+5.9%)** |
| Outlook→stable + modest re-rate | 3.85% | ~90.3 | **+6.5 pts (+7.8%)** |
| Full normalization toward clean AA- school GO | 3.70% | ~91.9 | **+8.2 pts (+9.8%)** |

**Asymmetry:** the bond is **money-good to 2041 regardless** (insulated levy), so the downside on the *credit* leg is bounded — the realistic adverse path is mark-to-market (rates rising further or the spread widening on more operating headlines), not impairment. The upside is the **+5 to +10 points** of price recovery if/when the operating outlook stabilizes (Oakland keeps reserves above the 2% state floor, settles bargaining, executes consolidations) — **on top of** a ~7.6–9.0% TEY carry and contractual accretion. That is a favorable carry-plus-convexity profile.

**Catalyst watch (rating-recovery triggers):** removal of the Moody's/Fitch negative outlook; structural-deficit closure / reserves sustained above the state minimum; resolution of the AB-1840 qualified certification; successful school consolidation. Any of these tightens the spread toward where the *bond rating* (A1/AA-) says it should trade.
