## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**Verdict: BUY (high-grade essential-service water-revenue credit; no honesty red flag).**

**Resolved issuer (CONFIRMED from the on-file EMMA Official Statement).** The generic "City of Riverside
(water)" tag resolves to the **City of Riverside Water System**, a municipal retail water utility owned and
operated by the City through its Board of Public Utilities (Riverside Public Utilities). The specific security
is the **Water Revenue Bonds, Issue of 2019A** — a direct obligation of the City payable from Net Operating
Revenues of the Water System. This is NOT a conduit/COP/lease: the City itself is the obligor. (The
prominent "Build America Bonds" language in the document refers to the City's *prior* 2009B series being
refunded — it is not this bond; the 2019A is conventional tax-exempt.)

**Thesis.** Large, multiply-rated SoCal municipal water utility, true first-tier net-revenue pledge, coverage
~2.1x most recent year vs. an obligation to set cost-recovery rates, deeply diversified retail base (top-10 =
8.15% of retail revenue), and standalone **S&P "AAA" / Fitch "AA+"** ratings sold **uninsured** — the wrap is
absent because the underlying does not need one (a positive anti-masking signal). The single structural risk is
**water supply**: Riverside is unusual among SoCal utilities in that it owns substantial **local groundwater**
(Bunker Hill / Riverside-Arlington basins) and is far less import-dependent than peers — but it still draws on
adjudicated/SGMA-governed basins and supplements with imported water, so supply is the risk to monitor.

**Why BUY:** clean senior net-revenue pledge, coverage comfortably above a cost-recovery rate covenant on both
OS and current data, top-tier ratings, diversified base, double tax-exemption, and adequate secondary liquidity
(69 trades/yr, tight 3 bps two-sided yield spread). The marketed takeaway ("AAA water revenue, diversified")
does not diverge from the document data. No liar to exclude.

- **System type:** Retail municipal **water** utility (water only; the City's separate electric utility is a

- **Customer diversification — CONFIRMED diversified:** **Top-ten retail customers = 8.15% of total retail

### Rate covenant
- **Rate covenant (Resolution):** the City must set rates sufficient to pay O&M, debt service on all Bonds and
  Parity Debt, and all other charges on Net Operating Revenues, "with specified requirements as to priority and
  coverage." Rates are set by the Board of Public Utilities subject to City Council approval; **not** CPUC-regulated.
- **Additional-bonds capacity is open** (Additional Bonds + non-Bond parity obligations permitted) — the dilution
  channel to watch, though historical coverage leaves a cushion.

### WATER SUPPLY (the load-bearing risk — lead here)
- **Supply mix:** Riverside's distinguishing feature is heavy reliance on **local groundwater** pumped from
  wells in the Bunker Hill / Riverside-Arlington / San Bernardino-area basins, supplemented by surface and
  imported water. It is materially **less import-dependent than the typical MWD-fed SoCal retail utility**.
- **The risk (genuine but second-order vs. peers):**
  - Local basins are **adjudicated / SGMA-governed** (the City pumps under court-defined rights and basin
    management); groundwater levels fall in dry years. A multi-year drought reduces safe yield and raises
    pumping/treatment cost (some local supply requires treatment for historical contamination).
  - To the extent the City supplements with imported water, it inherits a slice of the regional Colorado River
    + State Water Project shortage exposure — but this is a smaller share of supply than at import-dependent peers.
  - The cached screen tagged supply "HIGH" on a Colorado-River/SGMA keyword match. **Calibrated finding:**
    supply risk is real (adjudicated/SGMA basins + drought) but Riverside's large owned-groundwater position
    makes it **lower-risk than the import-dependent names in this sleeve** (Palmdale, Azusa, EBMUD's import
    supplement). Treat as MODERATE for this issuer, not extreme.
- **Mitigant:** cost-recovery rate-setting (rates not CPUC-regulated; Board sets to recover O&M including
  higher supply cost), diversified local + imported portfolio, and a long operating history through prior droughts.

### Prop 218
- Water rates and charges are **Proposition 218 fees/charges** (majority-protest process; cost-of-service cap).
  The City adopted a multi-year rate schedule (cached mitigant). A 2013 lawsuit challenged the City's General
  Fund transfer on Prop 218 grounds (settled; the transfer is subordinated to debt service and excluded from the
  coverage calc) — disclosed, resolved, and structured so it does not reach bondholder coverage.

## (g) Risks (lead with supply, then concentration/structure)

1. **WATER SUPPLY (primary, structural — but lower than peers):** adjudicated/SGMA local groundwater + a
   minority imported-water supplement. Drought reduces safe yield and raises cost. **Lower import-dependence
   than the import-fed names in this sleeve** is the relative strength. Monitor multi-year drought + basin
   management. *Mitigant:* cost-recovery rates + diversified owned-groundwater portfolio.
2. **Customer concentration: LOW (top-10 = 8.15% of retail revenue).** No single-customer dependency.
3. **Open additional-bonds capacity:** City may issue Additional Bonds/parity obligations; coverage dilution
   over time is the structural watch. No funded debt-service reserve (offset by AAA/AA+ ratings).
4. **Revenue-quality:** coverage includes some cyclical wholesale-conveyance/connection-fee revenue; the
   recurring retail base is the durable support.
5. **Honesty assessment: CLEAN.** Marketed takeaway matches OS data; supply and Prop-218 history fully
   disclosed; uninsured AAA/AA+ credit needs no wrap (anti-masking positive). Nothing to exclude.
