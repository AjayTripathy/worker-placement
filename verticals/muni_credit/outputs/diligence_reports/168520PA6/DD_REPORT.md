# Diligence Report — CUSIP 168520PA6

**Chico Unified School District (Butte County, CA) — General Obligation Bonds, Election of 2016, Series B**
3.000% coupon, term bond due 2044-08-01 · Cutoff: 2026-06-18

---

## (a) Verdict & buy thesis

**VERDICT: CLEAR TO BUY** (high-quality unlimited-tax GO, deeply-discounted, ordinary-income-on-discount already priced into the after-tax yield).

This is a voter-approved **unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** bond of a fiscally-healthy
Central Valley unified school district. Butte County is legally **obligated to levy property taxes
without limitation as to rate or amount** to pay it, the levy is backed by a **statutory first lien**
(SB-222 / Gov. Code §53515), and the County runs a **Teeter Plan** so the District receives 100% of
the secured levy regardless of taxpayer delinquencies. Credit quality is investment-grade
(**Moody's Aa3** at issuance), debt burden is light (**direct GO debt = 0.96% of assessed value**),
and the District carries a **POSITIVE** state fiscal certification. The ~80.3 dollar price is a
function of the low 3% coupon in a higher-rate market, not credit distress — the trade tape shows an
orderly two-sided market. The discount is taxed as ordinary income (de-minimis breached), and the
quoted **after-tax taxable-equivalent yield (~8.56%)** already nets that drag.

The thesis is: buy a near-AA, statutorily-lien-secured, AI-/wildfire-/earthquake-insulated cash flow
at a deep discount where the only real "risk" is the ordinary-income tax treatment that is already
in the after-tax math.

## (b) Security & pledge — what legally compels payment

**Finding: UNLIMITED AD-VALOREM GENERAL OBLIGATION. VERIFIED against the Official Statement.**

- **Claim:** It is a true GO, not a COP / lease / certificate / special-tax (CFD) obligation.
  **Verified** by reading the Official Statement (`raw/official_statement.pdf`). EMMA "Source of
  Repayment" field = **General Obligation** (`raw/emma_security_details.html`).
- **The legal mechanism (quoted from the OS):** "The County Board of Supervisors is empowered and
  obligated to annually levy ad valorem taxes for the payment of interest on, and principal of, the
  Bonds upon all property subject to taxation by the District, **without limitation of rate or
  amount**." Taxes are levied by the District and **collected by Butte County**.
- **Statutory lien (SB-222 / Gov. Code §53515):** The OS confirms voter-approved GO bonds secured by
  ad-valorem tax collections "**are secured by a statutory lien on all revenues received pursuant to
  the levy and collection of the property tax** imposed to service those bonds … attaches
  automatically and is valid and binding from the time the bonds are executed and delivered …
  enforceable against the issuer, its successors, transferees, and creditors … irrespective of
  whether those parties have notice of the lien." This is the strongest form of muni security: a
  perfected first lien on the tax stream, ahead of general creditors.
- **Teeter Plan (credit positive):** Butte County has adopted the Teeter Plan (Cal. Rev. & Tax. Code
  §4701 et seq.). The District "receives 100% of secured property taxes levied in exchange for
  foregoing any interest and penalties collected on delinquent taxes." Result: **taxpayer
  delinquency does not reduce debt-service cash flow** — the County advances the full levy.
- **Tax status:** **Tax-exempt.** Bond Counsel (Jones Hall) opinion: interest is excluded from
  federal gross income, is **not** an AMT preference item, and is **exempt from California personal
  income tax**. EMMA "Tax Status" = Tax Exempt.

There is no insurance wrap; the credit rides on the District's own unlimited-tax pledge and the
statutory lien. (No wrap = no rating-conditional spread compression to model here.)

## (f) Tax & yield

**Finding: De-minimis breached → discount accretes as ORDINARY income. The ~8.56% after-tax TEY
already nets this; a naive exempt-only gross-up would overstate it.**

- **Coupon is fully tax-exempt** (federal + CA), per Bond Counsel opinion (§b).
- **The discount is NOT.** Purchase price ~**80.34** vs par 100. The de-minimis threshold from the
  purchase date to the 2044 maturity (~18.2 years × 0.25/yr below par = ~**95.46**) is far above the
  purchase price. Because the **market discount exceeds the de-minimis amount**, the ~**19.7-point**
  accretion of the discount toward par is taxed as **ordinary income** (taxed annually if a
  constant-yield election is made, or as ordinary income at sale/maturity otherwise) — **not** as a
  long-term capital gain.
- **TEY math (as screened):** gross yield-to-worst ≈ **4.61%** (EMMA tape, YX = MSRB yield-to-worst,
  i.e. priced to the worse of the 2027 par call vs 2044 maturity — labeled correctly here as YTW, not
  YTM). The **after-tax taxable-equivalent yield ≈ 8.56%** already reflects: (i) exempt coupon grossed
  up at the CA top combined bracket, and (ii) the ordinary-income tax **drag on the discount
  accretion** netted out. The de-minimis breach is a *known, priced* feature, not a hidden catch —
  it is the reason the headline TEY is stated after-tax rather than as a raw exempt gross-up.

*Self-discipline note (per house rule on TEY conflation): the ~8.56% is an **after-tax
taxable-equivalent** figure, not gross YTM and not net-of-fees. Gross YTW is ~4.61%.*

## (g) Risks / what could go wrong

1. **Interest-rate / price risk (the dominant risk).** This is a long-duration 3% coupon. The deep
   discount and ~80.3 price are *rate-driven*. If rates rise further the mark falls; this is a
   price/regime risk, **not** a credit-impairment risk. Hold-to-maturity neutralizes it (statutory
   lien + unlimited levy → principal at par at 2044, subject to the 2027 par call which is unlikely
   to be exercised on a below-market 3% coupon).
2. **Call (mild, in our favor).** Callable at par 2027-08-01. With the bond at ~80, a call would be a
   ~25% windfall to the buyer — the District has no economic incentive to call a deep-discount
   below-market coupon, so YTW≈YTM(2044) is the realistic case. Asymmetric upside, not a risk.
3. **AV-base erosion (low).** A severe, sustained property-value decline (e.g. wildfire insurer
   withdrawal) could pressure the levy *rate* needed — but the pledge is **unlimited as to rate**, so
   the County simply raises the rate to cover debt service. The wildfire screen is low.
4. **Operating-fund stress / enrollment decline (does NOT hit this bond).** CA-wide enrollment decline
   pressures LCFF operating revenue, but GO debt service is paid from a **separate dedicated property
   levy**, not operating funds. The POSITIVE AB-1200 cert indicates no near-term operating distress.
5. **Stale continuing disclosure (UNVERIFIABLE current AV/coverage — flagged honestly).** The
   continuing-disclosure documents posted on EMMA that I retrieved are the **FY2019 originals**
   (audited FY2019 financials, 2019 budget, FY2019 annual report). A **current-year (FY2024-25) AV,
   coverage, and audited-financials** package was **not** locatable in the posted EMMA disclosures at
   cutoff. The FY2024-25 POSITIVE state certification (§c) is the freshest independent fiscal
   data-point and is reassuring, but **current AV and current GO debt-service coverage are
   UNVERIFIABLE from primary disclosure** as of this report — treat as a monitoring item, not a clean
   confirmation.
6. **No distress events found — but absence ≠ exhaustive search.** No default, missed-payment,
   rating-withdrawal, or distress material-event notice surfaced. The EMMA material-events list could
   not be machine-parsed via WebFetch (Terms-of-Use wall); this is **"no distress found," not
   "distress affirmatively ruled out across every event filing."**
