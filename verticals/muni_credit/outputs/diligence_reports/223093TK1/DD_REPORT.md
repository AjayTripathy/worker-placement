## (a) Verdict & buy thesis

**VERDICT: BUY** (voter-approved unlimited-tax GO, near-AA, statutory-lien-secured, deeply discounted; the discount's ordinary-income tax treatment is already inside the after-tax yield). **Buy with two honest caveats** that separate it from our Central-Valley GO comps: (1) Los Angeles County does **not** run a Teeter Plan, so debt-service receipts are exposed to taxpayer delinquencies (partially backstopped by a delinquent-tax-purchase JPA), and (2) the tax base sits in a **moderate-to-high seismic** zone (Ss 1.87, San Gabriel Valley / LA Basin) rather than the low-seismic foothills.

This is a **true unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** of a fiscally-healthy LA-County unified district. The County is legally **obligated to levy property taxes without limitation as to rate or amount** to pay it, the levy is backed by a **statutory first lien** (SB-222 / Gov. Code §53515), the District carried **Moody's Aa3** at issuance, debt burden is light (**direct GO debt ≈ 1.73% of assessed value**), the tax base is large (**$8.30B AV**) and reasonably diversified, and the District holds a **POSITIVE** state fiscal certification (FY2024-25). The ~78.5 dollar price is a function of the low 3% coupon in a higher-rate market, **not credit distress** — the trade tape shows an orderly, genuinely two-sided customer market. The discount is taxed as ordinary income (de-minimis breached by a wide margin), and the quoted **after-tax taxable-equivalent yield (~8.5%)** already nets that drag.

The thesis: buy a near-AA, statutorily-lien-secured, AI-/wildfire-insulated property-tax cash flow at a deep discount where the dominant "risk" is rate-driven price (neutralized by hold-to-maturity) and the only credit nuances — no-Teeter delinquency exposure and moderate seismic — are real but well within an unlimited-tax-pledge, light-leverage, diversified-base profile.

## (b) Security & pledge — what legally compels payment

**Finding: UNLIMITED AD-VALOREM GENERAL OBLIGATION. VERIFIED against the Official Statement.**

- **Claim:** It is a true GO, not a COP / lease / certificate / special-tax (CFD) obligation.
  **Verified** by reading the Official Statement (`raw/official_statement.pdf`, $28,410,000 issue). The inside cover binds **CUSIP TK1** to the **$4,075,000 3.000% Term Bond due 2045-08-01, yield 3.050%**, under the "New Money Bonds" (Election of 2012, Series C). These are explicitly "**general obligations of the District payable solely from the proceeds of ad valorem property taxes**."
- **The legal mechanism (quoted from the OS):** "The Board of Supervisors of Los Angeles County … is empowered and **obligated to annually levy such ad valorem taxes without limitation as to rate or amount**, upon all property within the District subject to taxation … for the payment of principal of and interest on the New Money Bonds when due." This is the strongest pledge form — unlimited as to rate AND amount.
- **Statutory lien (SB-222 / Gov. Code §53515) — VERIFIED:** OS: the Bonds "**will be secured by a statutory lien on all revenues received pursuant to the levy and collection of ad valorem property taxes** … The lien automatically attaches, without further action … and is valid and binding from the time the Bonds are executed and delivered … enforceable against the District, its successor, transferees and creditors … irrespective of whether such parties have notice of the lien." A perfected first lien on the tax stream, ahead of general creditors.
- **NO Teeter Plan (HONEST CREDIT FLAG) — VERIFIED:** Unlike our Central-Valley comps, the OS states plainly: "**The County has not adopted the Teeter Plan**, and consequently the Teeter Plan is not available to … the District. The District's receipt of property taxes is therefore **subject to delinquencies**." Mitigant: the District is a member of the **California Statewide Delinquent Tax Finance Authority (CSDTFA)**, which purchases delinquent receivables at ~110% of face. Net effect: the *unlimited rate* pledge still guarantees the levy can be set high enough to cover debt service, but **cash-flow timing is exposed to delinquency** in a way a Teeter county's is not. This is a genuine (modest) differentiator and is flagged rather than glossed.
- **Tax status — VERIFIED tax-exempt:** Bond Counsel (Stradling Yocca Carlson & Rauth) opinion: interest **and original issue discount** are excluded from federal gross income, are **not** an AMT preference item, and are **exempt from California personal income tax**. (Note: this OID exemption is distinct from the *market-discount* tax treatment in §f, which is ordinary income.)

There is no insurance wrap; the credit rides on the District's own unlimited-tax pledge + statutory lien. (No wrap → no rating-conditional spread compression to model.)

## (f) Tax & yield — de-minimis math behind the ~8.5% TEY

**Finding: De-minimis breached by a wide margin → the discount accretes as ORDINARY income. The ~8.5% after-tax TEY already nets this; a naive exempt-only gross-up would overstate it.**

- **Coupon is fully tax-exempt** (federal + CA, non-AMT), per Bond Counsel (§b).
- **The discount is NOT.** Purchase price ~**78.5** vs par 100 → a **~21.5-point market discount**. The de-minimis threshold from purchase (~2026-06) to the 2045 final maturity is `100 − 0.25 × 19 full years = 95.25`. The purchase price (78.5) is **far below** 95.25, so the market discount **exceeds the de-minimis amount** and the ~21.5-point accretion toward par is taxed as **ordinary income** (annually under a constant-yield election, or at sale/maturity otherwise) — **not** as long-term capital gain.
- **Sinking-fund average life (refines the math):** the 2045 term bond is **not** a bullet — it carries a **mandatory sinking fund** redeeming ~$1.02M/yr at par on 2042 / 2043 / 2044 / 2045. Weighted average life ≈ **17.6 years** vs 19.1 years to final maturity. Effect: principal returns ~1.5 years earlier on average (mild positive for realized yield and for the de-minimis horizon), and the redemptions are at par — reinforcing the ordinary-income accretion conclusion.
- **TEY math (as screened):** gross **yield-to-worst ≈ 4.72%** (EMMA tape, YX = MSRB yield-to-**worst**, priced to the worse of the 2026-08 par call vs the 2045 maturity — labeled correctly here as YTW, not YTM). The **after-tax taxable-equivalent yield ≈ 8.5%** already reflects (i) the exempt coupon grossed up at the CA top combined bracket and (ii) the ordinary-income tax **drag on the discount accretion** netted out. The de-minimis breach is a **known, priced** feature — it is precisely *why* the headline is stated after-tax rather than as a raw exempt gross-up.

*Self-discipline note (house rule on TEY conflation): the ~8.5% is an **after-tax taxable-equivalent** figure, not gross YTM and not net-of-fees. Gross YTW ≈ 4.72%.*

## (g) Risks / what could go wrong

1. **Interest-rate / price risk (dominant).** A long-duration 3% coupon; the ~78.5 price is **rate-driven**, not credit-driven. Further rate rises lower the mark; this is a price/regime risk, not impairment. Hold-to-maturity neutralizes it (statutory lien + unlimited levy → par at the sinking-fund/2045 dates).
2. **No Teeter Plan → delinquency cash-flow exposure (modest, real).** LA County does not advance the full levy; debt-service receipts are subject to taxpayer delinquencies. CSDTFA's 110% delinquent-receivable purchases and the unlimited-rate pledge (the County can raise the rate to cover) are the buffers. In a severe local recession this could create *timing* stress on coverage, not a principal loss on a statutory-lien GO. **This is the main credit differentiator vs our Teeter-county comps.**
3. **Seismic (moderate, the one elevated physical channel).** Ss 1.87, Puente Hills / San Gabriel fault systems. A major quake could trigger base-wide Prop-8 AV reductions; the unlimited-rate pledge lets the County raise the levy rate on the reduced base, and AV recovers on rebuild. Sized moderate, not disqualifying; cap aggregate seismic exposure at the portfolio level rather than per-name.
4. **Call (mild, in our favor).** Optionally callable at par on any date on/after **2026-08-01**. With the bond at ~78, a call would be a ~28% windfall to the buyer — the District has no economic incentive to call a deep-discount below-market 3% coupon, so YTW ≈ yield-to-the-sinking-fund/2045 is the realistic case. Asymmetric upside.
5. **Enrollment decline (does NOT hit this bond).** Structural ADA decline pressures LCFF *operating* revenue, not the dedicated GO levy. The POSITIVE AB-1200 cert indicates no near-term operating distress.
6. **Stale primary disclosure (UNVERIFIABLE current AV/coverage — flagged honestly).** The AV ($8.30B), debt-to-AV (1.73%), and top-taxpayer (10.46%) figures are the **FY2015-16 issuance vintage** from the OS. The Series-C EMMA issue partial returned **zero** continuing-disclosure documents at cutoff, so **current AV and current GO debt-service coverage are UNVERIFIABLE from primary disclosure**. The FY2024-25 POSITIVE state certification is the freshest independent fiscal data point and is reassuring; treat current AV/coverage as a monitoring item, not a clean confirmation.
7. **No distress events found — but absence ≠ exhaustive search.** No default, missed-payment, rating-withdrawal, or distress material-event surfaced (and the trade tape shows no distressed-print pattern). The EMMA material-events list could not be machine-parsed via WebFetch (Terms-of-Use wall); this is **"no distress found," not "distress affirmatively ruled out across every event filing."**
