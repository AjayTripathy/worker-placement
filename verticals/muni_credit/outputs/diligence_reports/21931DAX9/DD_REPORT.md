## (a) Verdict, thesis & the true-issuer finding

**VERDICT: HOLD (qualified BUY held-to-maturity; do NOT add at size or chase here).**

This is a **sewer (wastewater) revenue** bond of a small Northern-California city, secured by a pledge
of **Net System Revenues** of the Sewer System and a **120% rate covenant**, **insured by Build
America Mutual (BAM)** to S&P "AA" over an **A-** underlying sewer credit. Applying the **Tulare
lesson** — for a water/sewer revenue bond the risk is **DSCR + customer concentration + supply**, not
a tax pledge — this name comes out the **opposite** of the feared profile on the dimension that
usually kills these bonds:

- **THE CUSTOMER-CONCENTRATION FINDING (the decisive Mode-B catch, and it INVERTS the hypothesis):**
  the Sewer System is **diversified**, not concentrated. The largest single ratepayer is **Blue
  Beacon (a truck stop) at 2.98% of gross sewer revenue**; the **top SEVEN ratepayers are only
  10.75%**. The one food/ag processor on the list — **Bell Carter Olive Company** — is just **0.65%**,
  and the OS explains why: Bell Carter **moved to its own on-site wastewater treatment in 2004**,
  shedding the system's only would-be dominant industrial load more than a decade before this bond.
  **There is no Tulare-style single-industrial-customer (food processor / dairy) exposure here.** The
  marketed worry is, on the document, **absent** — an anti-masking finding.

What keeps this a **HOLD and not a clean BUY** are three honest, document-backed watch-items the
"revenue/water track" framing obscured: (1) **thin coverage** — historic SEWER DSCR ran 105–127%,
including a **covenant breach in FY2013-14** (cured by a rate increase; debt service always timely
paid), and the **new 2017 covenant (120%) sits ABOVE several historical actuals**; (2) **a small,
A-/BAM-wrapped enterprise** where the wrap is doing real work; and (3) **execution** — this is a
**$970,000 term bond** that **has not traded since 2026-03-24** (~3 months stale at cutoff), so the
~98.22 mark is real but not live and the round-trip cost is high.

**The thesis (held to maturity):** own a small, **BAM-insured (AA-wrapped)**, **diversified-customer**
sewer-revenue cash flow at a modest discount to par for a **~4.15% tax-exempt YTM (~8.3% after-tax
TEY)**, where the genuine risks are coverage/rate-setting and liquidity, **not** the single-customer
concentration the asset class is usually caught on. It earns its yield; it does not warrant adding at
size, and the entry should be patient given the stale, wide retail tape.

## (b) Security & pledge — REVENUE, not a tax pledge

**Finding: NET SYSTEM REVENUES of the SEWER SYSTEM + 120% rate covenant + BAM wrap. VERIFIED against
the Official Statement (`raw/official_statement.pdf`, `raw/os.txt`).**

- **Claim — it is a revenue bond, not a GO/tax pledge.** **VERIFIED.** OS cover: "The Bonds are
  payable from and secured by the City's pledge of **Net System Revenues** … defined generally as
  System Revenues received from the City's … sewer system … **less maintenance and operation costs**."
  There is **no ad-valorem tax pledge** and **no general-fund obligation**. This is an **enterprise
  revenue** credit — its risk lives in rates, coverage, customers, and the plant.
- **Sewer-only recourse (important).** The combined 2017 OS issued **two separate series** —
  $3,460,000 **Water** and $5,160,000 **Sewer**. **Our bond is the SEWER series.** OS: "The Sewer
  Bonds are **repayable only** from certain money available to the City from its **Sewer System**."
  The Water System's troubles (drought, repeated water-covenant breaches — see §d) **do not** reach
  this bond's pledge except through shared City management.
- **Rate covenant — 120% (1.20x).** OS "Rate Covenants": the City covenants to set rates expected to
  yield **Net System Revenues equal to 120% of Debt Service** on the System Bonds and parity debt,
  plus reserve replenishment. If a year falls short, the City must hire an **Independent Financial
  Consultant** and adopt its rate recommendations; for the **Sewer System the hard floor is 100% of
  debt service at all times** (the consultant cure cannot drop net revenues below 1.00x).
- **Additional parity debt is permitted** (OS "Parity Debt") subject to a coverage test — a dilution
  channel to monitor on a thin-coverage system.
- **Reserve = BAM surety.** The Sewer reserve is funded by a **BAM debt-service-reserve insurance
  policy** (a surety, not cash) — standard, but note the reserve is itself insurer-dependent.
- **Insurance wrap — YES.** Concurrent **BAM municipal bond insurance policy** guarantees scheduled
  principal and interest. **Sewer insured rating S&P "AA"; sewer UNDERLYING S&P "A-".** BAM's own
  financial strength was **"AA/Stable" (S&P)** at issuance. This is the **rating-conditional wrap
  compression** mechanism: the AA wrap compresses spread over an A- underlying — verify the wrap is
  still in force and BAM's current rating before relying on it (see §g).
- **Tax status — VERIFIED.** Bond Counsel (Kronick Moskovitz): interest **excludable from federal
  gross income**, **not** a preference item for individual AMT (corporate ACE adjustment applies),
  and **exempt from California personal income tax**. Designated **"qualified tax-exempt obligations"
  (bank-qualified)**.

## (c) System & customers — concentration is the key risk, and it is LOW

**Finding: SEWER (wastewater) enterprise, ~2,287 connections, DIVERSIFIED ratepayer base, ample plant
headroom. The single-large-industrial-customer (Tulare) risk is ABSENT. VERIFIED — OS Tables 12 & 14.**

- **System type — SEWER (wastewater), not water.** OS "The Sewer System": the City has operated the
  Sewer System since 1940; it provides **secondary treatment** and **discharges to the Sacramento
  River**. Day-to-day management sits with the City's Public Works Department; sewer is billed monthly
  with water.
- **Size & footprint.** **2,269 in-City connections + 18 outside the City limits (~2,287 total)**;
  the service area is essentially **contiguous with the City**. Account count is **flat-to-stable**
  (2,274 → 2,269 over FY2011-16) — a mature, non-growth, non-shrinking base.
- **Plant capacity — ample headroom.** Treatment capacity **1.4 mgd**, **permitted to 1.75 mgd** by
  the Regional Water Quality Control Board, against average dry-weather flow of **~1.0 mgd**. The City
  states it has **no capital-improvement plan** to upgrade — i.e., no looming mandatory capex shock to
  coverage (a positive for a thin-coverage credit).
- **CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION — the Tulare check, and the decisive finding.** OS **Table 14, "Sewer
  System Largest Ratepayers (FY2015-16)"**:

  | Ratepayer | Business | % of gross sewer revenue |
  |---|---|---|
  | Blue Beacon | Truck stop | **2.98%** |
  | Petro | Traveler / trucking services | 2.06% |
  | Corning High School | Secondary education | 1.81% |
  | Travel Centers | Traveler / trucking services | 1.73% |
  | Corning Apartments | Apartments | 1.05% |
  | **Bell Carter** | **Preserved fruits & vegetables (olive processing)** | **0.65%** |
  | Holiday Inn | Motel | 0.47% |
  | **Top 7 total** | | **10.75%** |

  - **Read honestly:** this is a **genuinely diversified** base for a small system. The top user is
    under 3%; the top seven barely exceed 10%. The composition is highway/traveler-services-heavy
    (Corning sits on Interstate 5), **not** anchored to one industrial discharger.
  - **The would-be Tulare risk is gone — and the OS says when and why.** Corning is olive/food-
    processing country (Bell Carter is a major regional olive packer), so the *a priori* worry was a
    dominant food-processor discharge load. But Bell Carter **"began providing its own on-site
    wastewater treatment in the summer of 2004,"** which **cut average dry-weather flow from ~1.0 to
    ~0.875 mgd** and left it at **0.65%** of sewer revenue. **The single-large-industrial-customer
    risk that defines the Tulare-dairy failure mode is not present here.**

## (d) DSCR / coverage, rate covenant, supply & Prop 218

**Finding: THIN but historically-met coverage (one cured breach); rate-setting is the binding
constraint; supply/regulatory risk is LOW (it's a sewer, and the drought stress is on the WATER
series). VERIFIED — OS Sewer historic-coverage table + Rate Covenants + Risk Factors.**

- **Historic SEWER debt-service coverage (audited, OS):**

  | FY | 2011-12 | 2012-13 | 2013-14 | 2014-15 | 2015-16 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Net Revenues | $393,825 | $455,042 | $377,782 | $394,930 | $454,628 |
  | Debt Service | $358,937 | $358,958 | $359,775 | $359,428 | $359,439 |
  | **Coverage** | **110%** | **127%** | **105%** | **110%** | **126%** |

  - **Coverage is THIN** — most years sit right at or just above the old 110% covenant, and **FY2013-14
    fell to 105%**, a **covenant breach** of the then-110% covenant. The City **cured it** by adopting
    a sewer-rate ordinance on 2014-05-27 (a 3.5%/yr increase schedule through 2018) and **paid debt
    service on time in every year, including the breach year.**
  - **The forward bar is higher than the past.** The **2017 indenture covenant is 120%** — **above
    three of the five historical actuals** (110/105/110%). Holding 120% on a flat-connection, non-
    growth base depends on the City's willingness and ability to keep raising rates (see Prop 218).
- **Rate covenant mechanics:** 120% target; on a shortfall the City must engage an Independent
  Financial Consultant and adopt its rate recommendations; **Sewer hard floor = 100% of debt service
  at all times.** So a soft miss triggers a cure process, not an immediate default — but the floor is
  only 1.00x, leaving little error margin in a bad year.
- **Prop 218 constraint (the real bottleneck).** California **Proposition 218** subjects sewer rate
  increases to **notice + public hearing + majority-protest**. The City has used the process (2014/2018
  increases). **The credit's central dependency is the ability to keep pushing rates through Prop 218
  to hold 120% coverage** on a ~2,287-connection base — a small, captive, but rate-sensitive ratepayer
  pool. A successful majority protest against a needed increase is the most plausible path to coverage
  stress.
- **Supply & regulatory risk — LOW for the sewer pledge.** A **sewer** system has no raw-water supply
  exposure; the drought/SGMA narrative in the OS is concentrated on the **WATER** series (which
  **breached its covenant in FY2014-15 and FY2015-16** and leans on a Rate Stabilization Fund — **that
  is the other bond, not this one**). The relevant regulatory channel here is the **NPDES discharge
  permit** to the Sacramento River: tightening effluent/nutrient standards from the Central Valley
  RWQCB could force treatment-plant capex and pressure O&M/coverage over time. Today, capacity headroom
  is ample and no capex plan is pending.

## (f) Tax & yield

**Finding: ~4.15% tax-exempt YTM; the modest discount is WITHIN de-minimis (accretes as CAPITAL GAIN,
not ordinary income); after-tax TEY ~8.3%.**

- **Coupon fully tax-exempt** (federal non-AMT for individuals + CA), bank-qualified (§b).
- **Yield:** approx **YTM (to 2042-09-01) ≈ 4.15%** at 98.221 — matching the EMMA tape YX of **4.15%**
  on the 2026-03-24 print (clean cross-check). The **2027 par call is out-of-the-money** (YTC ≈ 5.86%
  > YTM on a sub-par bond), so **YTW = YTM ≈ 4.15%**; the City has no incentive to call a below-market
  4% coupon. The EMMA-reported trade yield (YX) is MSRB **yield-to-worst**, labeled as such — not YTM.
- **De-minimis — WITHIN cushion (capital-gain treatment).** Price 98.221 vs par 100, **16.20 years**
  to maturity → de-minimis threshold = 100 − 0.25 × 16.20 = **95.95**. The purchase **98.22 is ABOVE
  95.95**, so the small ~1.8-point discount accretes as a **capital gain**, **not** ordinary income.
  (Contrast our deep-discount names where the threshold is breached.) The drag is therefore minor.
- **After-tax TEY ≈ 8.3%.** The ~4.15% exempt coupon grossed up at the CA top combined bracket, with
  the small cap-gain accretion, supports a **~8.3% after-tax taxable-equivalent yield** — consistent
  with our book mark. *House-rule note: ~8.3% is an **after-tax taxable-equivalent** figure, **not**
  gross YTM (4.15%) and **not** net-of-fees.*

## (g) Risks / what could go wrong (lead = customer concentration — and it is LOW)

1. **Customer concentration (the asset-class headline risk) — LOW / not present.** Top sewer ratepayer
   2.98%, top 7 = 10.75%, only food/ag processor (Bell Carter) 0.65% and already on self-treatment
   since 2004. **No Tulare-style single-industrial-customer exposure.** This is the risk we lead with
   for sewer/water revenue, and here it is the *strength*, not the weakness. (Monitor: a current
   ratepayer table — the OS data is FY2015-16 — to confirm no new dominant discharger has appeared.)
2. **Thin coverage + a higher forward covenant (the real credit risk).** Historic DSCR 105–127% with a
   **cured FY2013-14 breach**; the **2017 covenant (120%) exceeds several historical actuals**. On a
   flat, non-growth base, holding 120% requires continued rate increases. The Sewer hard floor is only
   **100%** of debt service — little margin in a bad year.
3. **Prop 218 rate-setting risk.** The credit depends on pushing sewer-rate increases through Prop 218
   (notice/hearing/majority-protest). A successful protest against a needed increase is the most
   plausible coverage-stress path.
4. **Insurance-wrap dependence.** The investment-grade comfort leans on the **BAM "AA" wrap over an
   "A-" underlying.** Two checks before relying on it: (a) is the BAM policy still in force for this
   maturity, and (b) BAM's **current** S&P rating (was AA/Stable at issuance). If the wrap weakens, the
   bond reprices toward the A- (or worse, given thin coverage) standalone.
5. **Liquidity / staleness (execution risk).** $970k term, **no trade since 2026-03-24**, ~2.9-pt
   retail spread on the last two-sided day. Easy to mark, hard to exit at the mark. Size accordingly;
   treat as buy-and-hold-to-maturity only.
6. **Parity-debt dilution.** The City may issue additional sewer parity debt subject to a coverage
   test — a structural dilution channel on a thin-coverage system; monitor continuing disclosures.
7. **NPDES / regulatory capex.** Tightening Sacramento-River discharge standards could force
   treatment-plant capex and pressure O&M/coverage over the bond's 16-year life. No capex plan pending
   today; ample plant headroom.
8. **Stale primary disclosure (UNVERIFIABLE current data).** OS operating data (coverage, ratepayers,
   connections, rates) is the **FY2015-16 issuance vintage.** A **current** audited coverage / ratepayer
   package was **not** retrieved from posted EMMA continuing disclosures at cutoff — current DSCR,
   current concentration, and current BAM status are **UNVERIFIABLE from primary disclosure** as of this
   report. This is the single biggest open item and the reason for **HOLD not BUY**.
9. **No distress affirmatively ruled out.** No default/missed-payment surfaced, but the EMMA
   material-events feed was not machine-parsed (Terms-of-Use wall) — "**no distress found**," not
   "distress ruled out across every filing."
