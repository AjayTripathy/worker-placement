## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**RESOLVED ISSUER (from EMMA Official Statement):** **West Valley Water District**, an independent
water district headquartered in **Rialto, San Bernardino County (Inland Empire), California.** Base
CUSIP **956409** = West Valley Water District. The security is the **Water Revenue Refunding Bonds,
Series 2016A**, a $22,035,000 issue dated 12/22/2016. CUSIP suffix **AW6** is the **$3,910,000 term
bond maturing 10/01/2041, 4.000% coupon**, originally priced 99.063% to yield 4.060%. This is a
**WATER (retail water utility) revenue bond — NOT wastewater/sewer.**

**VERDICT: BUY (HOLD-quality credit; BUY at this dollar price/yield).** This is a clean,
honestly-disclosed essential-service water credit. It is the textbook *opposite* of the Tulare
single-dominant-customer trap: a diversified ~22,000-connection retail base (93% single-family
residential) with no meaningful customer concentration, diversified and largely **adjudicated**
local groundwater supply, a 1.20x rate covenant, and post-issuance coverage that ran **2.38x–7.05x**
through FY2019 — far above covenant even through the 2014–16 statewide drought. S&P rated it **AA-
underlying, uninsured.** The two honest risks (groundwater contamination requiring ongoing
bioremediation; uninsured + no reserve fund) are fully disclosed in the OS, so under the honesty
framework they do not impair the credit — they are priced, not hidden.

**Why BUY, not just HOLD:** the bond trades at a discount dollar price (~99.16) on a 4% coupon for a
AA- essential-service water credit, delivering ~8.2% after-tax TEY (CA top-bracket gross-up). The
discount is a function of the low 4% coupon vs. current rates, not credit deterioration. There is no
takeaway-vs-data divergence: the OS markets it as a strong essential-service credit and the data
confirms it.

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / water supply / Prop 218

### DSCR + rate covenant
- **Rate covenant:** net revenues ≥ **1.20x** annual debt service each fiscal year (no reserve fund, so coverage and rate-setting discipline are the primary protections).
- **Historic coverage (OS, FY2012–16):** 1.36x / 3.39x / 3.46x / 4.51x / **2.77x** (FY16 drought) — all above covenant; the only sub-2x year (FY12, 1.36x) predates the current debt structure.
- **Post-issuance coverage (FY2019 continuing disclosure, includes Series 2016A debt service):**
  FY2015 **3.88x** · FY2016 **2.38x** (drought trough) · FY2017 **4.54x** · FY2018 **4.14x** · FY2019 **7.05x.**
- **Finding:** coverage has held **2.38x–7.05x** post-issuance — comfortably above the 1.20x covenant even through the worst statewide drought. The drought-year trough (2.38x) is the relevant stress data point and it still clears covenant by ~2x. **VERIFIED strong.**

### Water supply — diversified and largely adjudicated (CLEAN, with one named risk)
Primary source is **local groundwater** (17 active District-owned wells, avg depth 475 ft). Supply
mix 2011–2015:
- ~**60% local groundwater** (Rialto-Colton, Lytle Creek, Bunker Hill basins / San Bernardino Basin Area)
- ~**17% Lytle Creek surface water** (District holds 8.09 cfs right)
- ~**17% purchases from San Bernardino Valley Municipal Water District ("Valley District")**
- ~**5% State Water Project** (untreated, via Silverwood Lake)

**Regulatory insulation:** the District's groundwater rights sit inside **adjudicated / stipulated
basins** — the **1969 Stipulations** (San Bernardino Basin Area, 167,238 AFY shared entitlement) and
the **Chino Basin Watermaster** (1975 adjudication). Adjudicated basins are largely *carved out of
SGMA curtailment risk*, which materially reduces the supply-shortage channel relative to an
unadjudicated overdrafted basin. Valley District is contractually obligated under the 1969
Stipulations to deliver supplemental/replenishment water to maintain safe yield. This is **NOT** an
MWD/Colorado-River/SWP import-dependent system exposed to allocation cuts.

**The one real supply-side risk — honestly disclosed:** the Rialto-Colton basin has documented
**perchlorate, arsenic, and VOC contamination** (discovered 2000; attributed to post-WWII defense
contractors and prior agricultural fumigants). The District operates a **bioremediation wellhead
treatment system** (built with the City of Rialto via state/regional grants) and budgets ~$1.2M/yr
in bioremediation O&M. This is a cost-and-operations risk, not a quantity/entitlement risk, and it
is fully disclosed — the OS even excludes the related capital contributions from the coverage table
so reported coverage is *conservative*. Under the honesty framework this is a CLEAN disclosed
impairment, not a hidden one.

### Prop 218 / Prop 26
Water rates are **property-related fees/charges under Article XIIID (Prop 218)** and subject to the
notice-hearing-majority-protest process. The District has demonstrated functioning rate authority:
it adopted five-year rate increases (Sept. 2011) in compliance with Prop 218. **Honesty tell (minor,
disclosed):** the Board *reversed the final year* of those increases in 2016 to give customers rate
relief — i.e., the Board has shown willingness to forgo a covenanted rate step for political reasons.
With coverage at 2.4x–7x this had no covenant consequence, but it is the one place to watch:
Prop 218's majority-protest mechanism plus board discretion is the structural ceiling on the
rate-covenant promise. No protest has defeated a needed increase to date.

## (g) Risks (ranked; honest, UNVERIFIABLE ≠ clean)

1. **Groundwater contamination / bioremediation dependence (disclosed, ongoing).** Perchlorate/
   arsenic/VOC in the Rialto-Colton basin requires perpetual treatment (~$1.2M/yr). A treatment
   failure or a tighter MCL could raise costs or temporarily reduce usable local supply, pushing the
   District toward more expensive purchased water. Disclosed and currently managed — monitor for any
   new regulatory action or treatment-plant capital call. **CLEAN-but-watch.**
2. **No reserve fund + uninsured.** Bondholders rely solely on net revenues and the 1.20x rate
   covenant; there is no DSRF cushion and no insurer backstop. Coverage history (2.4x–7x) makes this
   tolerable, but it removes the structural shock absorber. **Disclosed; priced.**
3. **Prop 218 rate-setting ceiling + demonstrated board willingness to forgo increases.** The 2016
   rollback shows political rate relief can override planned increases. Structural ceiling on the
   covenant if ever combined with a coverage squeeze. **Low probability given coverage; monitor.**
4. **Secondary-market liquidity UNVERIFIABLE this run.** EMMA trade tape 403-blocked; no IBKR live
   quote. Cannot confirm recent two-sided trading. Treat as a thin HTM name; **not graded clean.**
5. **Drought / climate.** FY2016 drought cut water sales sharply (revenue −~15%) yet coverage stayed
   2.38x. Adjudicated basins + diversified mix blunt the supply side. Residual but well-buffered.
6. **Single-maturity term-bond concentration (this CUSIP).** $3.91M term bond — fine for the credit,
   but position-sizing should reflect the thin float.
