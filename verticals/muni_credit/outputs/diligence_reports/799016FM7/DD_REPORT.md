## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**VERDICT: BUY / HOLD-QUALITY (own; add on weakness).**

**Resolved issuer (from EMMA Official Statement, CUSIP6 799016):** This is NOT a generic
"water/wastewater" credit. The true issuer is the **City of San Mateo, California**, and the exact
security is the **Sewer Revenue Bonds, 2014 Series A** — i.e. specifically a **WASTEWATER (sewer)
enterprise** revenue bond, payable solely from net revenues of the City's Sewer Enterprise. EMMA
issue EP365210; the 799016FM7 maturity is the **4.00% term bond due 08/01/2044, $9,705,000 original
par**. Dated 07/02/2014, competitively sold. Issuance ratings **Moody's Aa2 / S&P AA**, unenhanced
(no bond insurance).

**Thesis (Tulare lesson applied — graded on DSCR + customer concentration + supply/regulatory +
Prop 218, NOT a tax pledge):**

1. **Coverage is strong and got STRONGER through the leverage cycle.** FY2025 debt-service
   coverage = **3.80x** against a 1.20x rate covenant; the trailing-5-yr path was 3.00 / 2.17 /
   2.18 / 3.01 / 3.80x. The City issued the entire large capital program it warned about in 2014
   (2019 bonds, 2021A/B, ~$295M WIFIA loans, ~$79M SWRCB loan) and coverage still rose, because it
   raised rates and revenue grew from ~$25M (2014) to ~$86M (FY25). The 2014-OS risk that a
   regulator-mandated CIP would crush coverage **did not materialize.**

2. **Customer base is diversified retail/commercial/residential — the CLEAN end of the Tulare
   spectrum.** FY2025 single largest user = **1.72% of billings** (Hillsdale shopping property);
   top-10 = **9.27%**. There is no dominant single industrial/agricultural offtaker (the
   Tulare-dairy failure mode). This is a built-out, affluent Peninsula city, not a single-crop /
   single-plant economy.

3. **Risk is honestly disclosed and resolved, not hidden.** The bond carries a real regulatory
   driver — a 2009 Regional Board Cease-and-Desist Order on sanitary-sewer overflows — fully
   disclosed in the OS, and the City has since executed the mandated improvements and kept coverage
   high. Disclosed-and-managed risk on a high-grade essential-service monopoly = CLEAN under the
   honesty framework.

**Why HOLD-QUALITY rather than a screaming buy:** the credit is excellent but richly understood by
the market (Aa2/AA, ~4.1% gross / ~8.2% TEY is a fair high-grade level, not a dislocation). System
leverage is now heavy in absolute terms (~$647M total parity debt vs. our $24M slice), so future
coverage depends on continued Prop-218 rate execution. Own it; it is a core, defensible insulated
holding.

## (c) System and customers (CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION — Tulare test)

**System:** Combined wastewater collection system + Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP) serving the
City of San Mateo (service-area population ~160,000) plus, at the treatment plant only, four
**Subregional Partners** (Foster City, Hillsborough, Belmont/CSCSD, unincorporated County). San
Mateo accounts for ~74% of WWTP flow.

**Customer concentration — CLEAN (diversified):**

| Metric | FY2012-13 (OS) | FY2024-25 (current CD) |
|---|---|---|
| Largest single user, % of billings | 1.21% (Bohannon/Hillsdale Shopping Ctr) | **1.72%** (Hillsdale Property San Mateo LP) |
| Top-10 users, % of billings | 5.57% | **9.27%** |

Top-10 names are shopping centers, apartment REITs (Essex, AIMCO, BRE), the County, the community
college, and a hospital system — **no single industrial or agricultural offtaker dominates.** This
is the opposite of the Tulare single-dairy concentration risk. **Finding: customer concentration is
NOT a credit risk here.**

**Subregional-Partner concentration (the one wholesale angle):** Foster City is the largest partner
(~18% of WWTP O&M, ~18% of flow). However, Subregional Partners pay only their pro-rata share of
**treatment** O&M and capex — they are not retail ratepayers of the pledged revenue stream, and they
are *required by the State Water Resources Control Board* to use this single subregional facility
(captive). Their departure is legally and practically infeasible, so this is a low-risk dependency,
not a Tulare-style revenue cliff.

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / supply / Prop 218

**DSCR (authoritative, audited):**

| FY | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | $44.8M | $46.7M | $61.1M | $64.1M | **$69.3M** |
| DSCR | 3.00x | 2.17x | 2.18x | 3.01x | **3.80x** |

Covenant floor 1.20x; actual 3.80x = ~3.2x cushion. (OS-era history FY2009–13 was 2.00–3.09x.)
**Finding: VERIFIED strong; coverage rose through the full CIP-leverage cycle.**

**Rate covenant:** 120% of annual debt service (100% incl. subordinate). Verified in indenture
language (OS p. 15). Met with large margin.

**"Water supply" — reframed correctly for a WASTEWATER bond:** A sewer system has **no
water-supply/SGMA/imported-MWD/SWP shortage risk** — that channel applies to *water* utilities
(the Tulare-type framing). The analogous supply-side risk for a wastewater system is **treatment
capacity + discharge-permit (NPDES) regulatory risk**, and here it is real but managed:
- **2009 Cease-and-Desist Order** (Regional Board R2-2009-0020) on sanitary-sewer overflows (SSOs),
  driving a multi-hundred-million-dollar mandated CIP (20-yr program; City share est. ~$718M in
  2013 $). The City reports it has met all but one interim milestone (a multi-agency Hillsborough
  trunk-sewer project), accepted by the Regional Board.
- NPDES permit CA0038849 (mercury/PCB limits); four Class-2 chlorine-residual violations over five
  years, **no material fines**; a historical $950k administrative civil liability ($760k suspended
  via a Supplemental Environmental Project) tied to legacy SSOs.
- **Forward risk:** emerging total-nitrogen limits, biosolids-disposal restrictions, and emissions
  rules could raise future capex — the primary watch-item. This is the channel most likely to drive
  further Prop-218 rate increases. **Finding: regulatory/CIP risk is the real (and disclosed) risk;
  it has not impaired coverage to date.**

**Prop 218:** Applies. Sewer service-charge increases require the Article XIII C/D notice +
majority-protest hearing process. The City has demonstrably executed multiple rate increases through
this process (revenue grew ~3.4x since 2014), which is the affirmative evidence that Prop-218 is a
manageable procedural gate here, not a binding constraint. **Finding: Prop-218 applies but is being
navigated successfully; ongoing reliance on rate increases is the structural dependency.**

## (g) Risks (UNVERIFIABLE ≠ clean — stated honestly)

1. **System leverage is now heavy (PRIMARY).** ~$647M total parity obligations vs. ~$69M net
   revenues. Coverage is strong today (3.80x), but a large WIFIA/SWRCB amortization profile means
   future coverage depends on continued rate increases. If a future regulatory mandate forces
   another large CIP and Prop-218 rate execution stalls, coverage compresses. *Verified leverage;
   forward coverage is a projection.*
2. **Regulatory/NPDES escalation.** Total-nitrogen / biosolids / emissions rules could add capex.
   Disclosed, not yet quantified — **watch-item, treated as a real risk, not waved away.**
3. **Prop-218 dependency.** The credit's strength rests on repeated successful rate increases;
   political/affordability resistance in any cycle is the structural soft spot.
4. **No bond insurance.** Stands on Aa2/AA underlying — fine at this grade, but no wrap cushion if
   the underlying migrates.
5. **Rating currency UNVERIFIED to cutoff.** Issuance ratings were Moody's Aa2 / S&P AA (2014). I
   did **not** independently confirm a current (2026) rating from the agencies in this pass — the
   FY2025 continuing disclosure did not restate a rating. **This is UNVERIFIABLE, therefore flagged,
   not assumed clean.** Recommend a rating-confirmation pull before adding size.
6. **Single-slice subordination optics.** Our $24M is small within a $647M stack; on a *parity*
   lien this is fine, but confirm no senior carve-outs in any post-2014 indenture supplement before
   treating as pari-passu.
