## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**Verdict: HOLD (clean net-revenue electric pledge with strong coverage, but a real single-customer concentration
caveat that the cache understated).**

**Resolved issuer (CONFIRMED from on-file OS).** The obligor is the **Merced Irrigation District (MID) Electric
System** — a publicly-owned electric utility serving Merced, Livingston, Atwater and surrounding Central Valley
areas. The District also owns a separate Water System and a Hydroelectric System, but the OS is explicit that
**the revenues of the Water and Hydroelectric Systems are NOT pledged** to these bonds and these bonds are not
payable from them. This is a pure **Electric System net-revenue** bond. (Note: the cache mislabeled
system_type as "water" — corrected: this is the ELECTRIC system.)

**Thesis.** Strong disclosed coverage (~4.17x current vs. a self-tightened 120% rate covenant), a clean
net-revenue pledge, AGM bond insurance, and a power-supply portfolio anchored by a long-term wholesale
arrangement (no open-market exposure) make this an operationally sound credit. The reason it is **HOLD, not
BUY**, is **customer concentration**: per the OS, the Electric System's **two largest customers provided ~24% of
revenues and the top ten provided ~45% of revenue** (FY ended 3/31/2015) — a materially concentrated load base
the screen cache did not capture (it had top-customer null). That is the load-bearing risk to monitor.

## (c) System and customers (concentration test)

- **System type:** Publicly-owned retail **electric** utility (not CPUC-regulated). ~495,159 MWh delivered to
  customers in FY ended 3/31/2015. Service area: Merced, Livingston, Atwater + surroundings; ~85% of load base
  is larger commercial/industrial/agricultural.
- **Customer concentration — MATERIAL (the catch):**
  - **Two largest customers ≈ 24% of Electric System Revenues** (FY2015).
  - **Top ten ≈ 45% of Electric System Revenues** (FY2015).
  - No customer other than the top two exceeded 5%.
  - This is a genuine single-/few-customer dependency: loss or curtailment of one or two large
    commercial/agricultural offtakers would compress coverage. The 4.17x headline coverage gives a meaningful
    buffer, but the concentration is the reason this is HOLD not BUY.

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / POWER SUPPLY / Prop 218

### Debt-service coverage (DSCR)
- **Cached current continuing-disclosure coverage: 4.17x** (explicit coverage row, organic — no RSF transfer).
  Comfortably above covenant. This is the strongest coverage in the electric portion of the sleeve.

### Rate covenant
- **120% rate covenant:** the District covenants to set rates yielding Net Electric System Revenues equal to
  **120% of Debt Service**. Importantly, the covenant **caps Rate-Stabilization-Fund transfers at 20% of Debt
  Service** when computing coverage — a structural guardrail against coverage being manufactured by reserve
  draws (anti-engineering feature; positive).

### POWER SUPPLY (the load-bearing risk for an electric revenue bond — lead here)
- **Supply portfolio (OS "Power Supply"):** the District relies on power-supply **contracts**, principally:
  - an **all-requirements contract with Turlock Irrigation District (TID)** — the dominant supply source;
  - a contract with the **Western Area Power Administration (WAPA)** (federal hydro); and
  - a renewables contract with **Iberdrola Renewables**.
  - "Because of the contract with TID, the District does not engage in open market purchases" — so MID has
    **limited merchant/spot-price exposure**, but in exchange carries **supplier-concentration risk on TID**:
    the reliability and pricing of the entire electric system is tied to one wholesale counterparty.
  - All-in purchased power was ~6.0¢/kWh (FY2015).
- **The risk:** TID counterparty/contract-renewal risk + the usual wholesale-power and natural-gas cost pass-
  through. Lower spot-price volatility than a merchant utility, but concentrated in the TID relationship.

### Prop 218
- As a municipal electric utility, rate-setting follows the District's process (electric rates are generally
  treated under Prop 26 for cost-of-service; the District is not CPUC-regulated). Standard CA rate-setting
  friction, not a special weakness.

## (g) Risks (lead with supply, then concentration/structure)

1. **POWER SUPPLY / supplier concentration:** dependence on the **TID all-requirements contract** (single
   wholesale counterparty) plus WAPA + Iberdrola. Limited spot exposure but concentrated counterparty/contract-
   renewal risk. **Monitor the TID contract.**
2. **CUSTOMER concentration — MATERIAL:** top-2 ≈ 24%, top-10 ≈ 45% of revenue. The most concentrated load
   base in this sleeve's electric names. Buffered by 4.17x coverage and AGM insurance, but the reason for HOLD.
3. **Structure:** AGM-insured (insured rating; underlying weaker). Currently callable at par — limited call
   protection. Open additional-parity capacity.
4. **Honesty assessment: CLEAN disclosure, cache-understatement corrected.** The OS fully and plainly discloses
   the 24%/45% concentration; the marketed/cache takeaway ("diffuse customer base") **diverged from the OS data**,
   which is exactly the kind of divergence we grade on — corrected here. The credit itself is honestly disclosed;
   nothing to exclude, but the screen tag was wrong and the verdict is set on the OS, not the cache.
