## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**Verdict: HOLD (the actual credit is the take-or-pay obligations of two investment-grade municipal electric
utilities — Burbank and Glendale — which is sound; but the two-participant concentration, the thin secondary
liquidity, and a single small hydro asset keep it at HOLD, not BUY).**

**Resolved issuer / what you actually own (CONFIRMED from on-file OS).** The named issuer is the **Southern
California Public Power Authority (SCPPA)**, a joint-powers authority — but SCPPA is a financing shell. The
specific security is the **Tieton Hydropower Project, Revenue Refunding Bonds, 2020 Series A (Green Bonds)**, and
the credit you underwrite is **NOT "SCPPA" broadly** — it is the **take-or-pay payment obligations of the TWO
Project Participants: the City of Burbank (50.0%) and the City of Glendale (50.0%)**, payable from each city's
electric system revenues. (The cache's generic "Southern…" issuer and "22.6% take-or-pay member" are corrected:
this is a 50/50 two-city take-or-pay on the small Tieton hydro project in Washington State.)

**Thesis.** The take-or-pay structure is strong on paper: Burbank and Glendale are obligated to pay on a
**"hell-or-high-water" basis — "whether or not the Project … is functioning, producing, operating or operable,"
payable as operating expenses of their electric systems** — and a non-defaulting participant can be **stepped up
to 100% of its own share** (so each city backstops the other). Burbank Water & Power and Glendale Water & Power are
established investment-grade municipal utilities. Reasons for HOLD: (1) **two-name concentration** — your repayment
rests entirely on exactly two obligors; (2) **single small hydro asset** (Tieton) with operational/hydrology risk,
though take-or-pay insulates the *payment* from project performance; and (3) **thin liquidity** (20 trades/yr,
1 two-sided day).

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / POWER SUPPLY / Prop 218

### Debt-service coverage (DSCR)
- **Cached coverage: 3.69x (rate covenant / after-in-lieu, FY2019); current ~3.6x** — strong, organic (no RSF
  transfer). In a take-or-pay project, "coverage" is somewhat formulaic because the Participants are obligated to
  pay all costs including debt service regardless of output; the more decisive question is the **credit quality of
  Burbank and Glendale's electric systems** (their ability to set rates to cover their take-or-pay obligations).
- Cached series 4.68 / 7.95 / 2.49 / 3.78 / 3.6x — the variability reflects the formula/timing, not stress.

### Rate covenant
- **1.0x project-level covenant** (cache): the Authority sets charges to recover all costs including debt service;
  the binding rate discipline is at each *Participant city's* electric system (each must set rates to cover its
  take-or-pay payment as an operating expense).

### POWER SUPPLY (load-bearing for an electric/hydro project — lead here)
- **Asset: a single small hydroelectric facility (Tieton).** Hydrology/operational risk on one plant. **BUT** the
  take-or-pay structure means the *bond payment is insulated from project performance* — Burbank and Glendale must
  pay "whether or not the Project is operating." So project supply risk transfers to a **replacement-power / cost
  risk** borne by the two cities (if Tieton underperforms, the cities still pay debt service and must source
  replacement power), not to a bondholder shortfall.
- **The real supply/credit question is therefore the two participants' electric systems** — their own
  power-supply portfolios, fuel exposure, and rate-setting ability. Both are diversified SoCal municipal utilities;
  this is a manageable, investment-grade exposure.

### Prop 218 / Prop 26
- Each city's electric rates follow its own rate-setting process (Prop 26 cost-of-service). The take-or-pay payment
  is an operating expense senior in each city's flow of funds.
