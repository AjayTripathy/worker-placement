## (a) Verdict & Thesis

**VERDICT: DO NOT ADD to the top-buy set as briefed. The bond is MIS-CLASSIFIED in the screen, and on its true identity it carries a material, now-LIVE single-customer concentration risk.**

The screening brief describes 899124LL3 as a **Tulare City School District unlimited ad-valorem GO** (school-GO, AB-1200, SB-222 lien, ag-land assessed-value base, SGMA slow-AV-erosion tail). **That description is wrong.** The authoritative EMMA security record and the bond's own Official Statement show this CUSIP is:

> **$58,265,000 City of Tulare, California — Sewer Revenue Refunding Bonds, Series 2016** (our maturity = the $13,210,000 4.00% term bond due 11/15/2041, CUSIP 899124LL3).

This is a **wastewater (sewer) enterprise REVENUE bond** — a *special / limited obligation* of the **City of Tulare** payable solely from net sewer-system revenues. There is **no ad-valorem tax pledge, no full-faith-and-credit, no SB-222 lien, no school district, and no AB-1200 framework.** "Tulare City School District" actually appears in the OS as the system's **#6 sewer customer (0.7% of revenue)** — a customer of this enterprise, not the issuer.

Because the security is misidentified, the entire screen's risk overlay was applied to the wrong credit:
- **AB-1200 POSITIVE** — does not apply (no school district / no GO).
- **AI-insulation 99 / cap-gains channel** — irrelevant; this is not a state-aid or AV-levy credit.
- **"School-GO ad-valorem AV base"** — there is no AV pledge; repayment is sewer rates.
- **SGMA "slow ag-land-AV erosion to 2040"** — wrong transmission channel (see (g)). The *real* SGMA channel here runs through dairy-plant utilization, not assessed value, and it is much more direct.

On its true identity the bond is **not a clean low-risk diversifier**. The dominant credit fact is that **~49% of total system revenue comes from three industrial dairy processors (Saputo 22.8%, Land O'Lakes 16.6%, Kraft 9.4%)**, and the single largest — Saputo — has been **actively restructuring its Tulare footprint since 2024** (one plant closed Q1-2025, with offsetting new investment). The concentration risk the 2016 OS flagged as hypothetical is now partially realized with an uncertain net effect on wastewater load.

What IS true and good about the bond: it is **AGM-insured** (S&P AA insured / S&P A underlying), tax-exempt, low-seismic (no active faults in the five-county area), ~15-year duration (a genuine duration diversifier vs the long names), and the issuer honestly disclosed every one of these risks in 2016. But "honestly-disclosed concentration in a single industry whose anchor tenant is mid-restructuring" is a credit to *price*, not a clean buy to add on a mistaken GO thesis.

**Recommendation: REJECT from the top-buy add. If the desk still wants Central Valley / sewer-revenue / duration diversification, re-underwrite this as what it is — a dairy-concentrated, AGM-wrapped sewer revenue bond — and pull a CURRENT (post-2016) rating + coverage ratio + customer-concentration update before sizing.**

## (c) Issuer & Tax/Revenue Base  (the ag economy — and the concentration)

**This is a sewer enterprise, so the relevant "base" is sewer customers/revenue, not assessed value.** The City of Tulare (pop. ~63,500, Central San Joaquin Valley, Tulare County) is a regional **dairy-processing hub** — Tulare County is #1 in the U.S. for milk production. The wastewater system's revenue is therefore tied to industrial dairy/cheese plants, and the concentration is severe.

**10 Largest Sewer Customers by Service-Charge Revenue (FY2015-16 est., from OS p.28):**

| Customer | % of Gross System Revenue |
|---|---|
| **Saputo Cheese** | **22.8%** |
| **Land O'Lakes, Inc.** (Dairyman's; largest single-site dairy complex in the U.S.) | **16.6%** |
| **Kraft Foods Group** | **9.4%** |
| Nestlé / Dreyer's Ice Cream | 1.7% |
| Sunmet Juice | 1.1% |
| Tulare City School District (a *customer*) | 0.7% |
| Ruiz Foods | 0.5% |
| (others) | <0.5% each |
| **Top 10 total** | **53.2%** |
| **Top 3 dairy processors** | **~49%** of system gross revenue |
| **All industrial (13 accounts)** | **~57%** of total sewer revenue |

By contrast ~89.7% of *accounts* are single-family residential but they generate only ~30% of revenue. **The system is economically a dairy-industrial wastewater utility with a residential billing base attached.** If a top dairy processor relocates or sharply cuts production, the System loses up to ~17-23% of revenue from a single name, and the rate covenant forces the shortfall onto the (largely residential) remaining ratepayers under Prop 218.

**Debt-to-AV is N/A** for this credit — there is no ad-valorem pledge. The analogous leverage metric is debt-service coverage on net revenues (see (d)).

## (g) Risks

**1. Single-customer / single-industry concentration — DOMINANT, and now LIVE (post-cutoff verification).**
~49% of system revenue from 3 dairy processors; ~57% industrial from 13 accounts. The OS flags this as a named Risk Factor ("Concentration of Wastewater Accounts," p.61): "If one or more of these companies were to relocate or otherwise reduce wastewater flow, the finances of the System would be materially impacted."

This is **no longer hypothetical.** Public reporting (2024-2025) shows **Saputo — the #1 customer at 22.8% of system revenue — is restructuring its Tulare footprint**: it closed/converted its Tulare "Bardsley" cut-and-wrap facility (closed by Q1-2025) as part of a six-U.S.-plant consolidation, *while* announcing a ~CAD75M investment to convert a Tulare site into a string-cheese plant and to consolidate South Gate, CA production into Tulare. **Net effect on Tulare wastewater FLOW (which is what the System bills industrial customers on — flow + BOD/TSS strength) is genuinely ambiguous** and cannot be resolved from the 2016 OS. This is the single most important item to update before any purchase. The mitigant: under the rate covenant the City can raise rates to backfill lost flow — but doing so on a residential base under Prop 218 has political ceilings and erodes competitiveness for remaining industrial customers (a feedback loop the OS itself names).

**2. SGMA / groundwater — REAL, but via the DAIRY channel, not the AV channel the brief assumed.**
Tulare overlies the **critically-overdrafted Tulare Lake Basin** (Kings/Kaweah/Tule; DWR Bulletin 118). For a *school GO* the SGMA channel would be slow ag-land-AV reassessment to 2040. **For THIS sewer-revenue credit the channel is different and more direct:** SGMA pumping cuts → less local milk → dairy processors run less volume or close Tulare lines → lower wastewater flow → lower sewer revenue → rate-covenant pressure. The OS discloses exactly this (p.50-51): "any future restrictions on groundwater use could … result in reduced wastewater flows and sewer service charge revenues." Same 2040 horizon, but it transmits through the concentrated industrial customer base, compounding Risk #1. Still a slow tail, not a near-term default flag — but it is NOT insulated by anything in the screen.

**3. Subordinate lien + heavy, open-ended parity stack.** This CUSIP sits **behind the Senior 2012 Notes** (~$8.46M) and on parity with a large stack (2009/2010/2013 $32.855M/2015 $84.555M). The Indenture permits additional parity debt on a 125%-MADS test. Historical pledged-revenue coverage (FY2007-2015, OS p.161) ran ~1.18-1.51× on parity/subordinate debt — adequate but not a fortress, and FY2014 dipped to 1.18× on extraordinary O&M.

**4. AGM wrap is doing real work (wrap-compression mechanism).** Insured S&P AA vs **underlying S&P A / Moody's A3**. Per the rating-conditional wrap-compression framework, the AGM wrap compresses spread most where underlying credit is weakest; here the underlying is mid-A, so the wrap is a genuine credit enhancement, not cosmetic. **Holders are partly buying AGM, not just Tulare's sewer system.** Any deterioration in either AGM or the underlying would widen spread.

**5. No earthquake insurance on the system** (OS p.61) — physical-asset tail consistent with the LOW Ss but uninsured.

**6. Thin secondary liquidity** (see (e)); OS disclaims any secondary market.

**7. Continuing-disclosure late-filing history** (mild honesty tell; see (d)).

**VERIFICATION GAP (UNVERIFIABLE ≠ clean):** EMMA rate-limited (HTTP 403) repeated hits, so I could **not** pull (i) a CURRENT (post-2016) underlying/insured rating, (ii) the latest annual continuing-disclosure with current debt-service coverage, or (iii) a post-2024 customer-concentration update reflecting the Saputo restructuring. All financials cited here are from the **2016 OS / FY2015 CAFR and are ~10 years stale.** This gap must be closed (current EMMA continuing-disclosure + rating) before the bond is sized — do not treat the 2016 coverage and concentration figures as current.

899124LL3 is **not** the school-district GO the screen labeled it. It is a **City of Tulare sewer revenue bond** whose credit is ~half-dependent on three dairy processors, with the largest (Saputo) actively restructuring its Tulare plants right now. It is AGM-wrapped, tax-exempt, low-seismic, and honestly disclosed — but the GO/AB-1200/AI-insulation/ag-AV-SGMA framing that justified the "clean diversifier" verdict is misapplied. **Reject the add on the briefed thesis; re-underwrite as a dairy-concentrated sewer-revenue credit with a current rating + coverage + concentration update if still of interest.**
