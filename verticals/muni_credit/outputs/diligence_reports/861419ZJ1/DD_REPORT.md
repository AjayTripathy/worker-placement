## (a) Verdict & thesis

**BUY — cleared for the test fill (limit 99.62, target ~40,000 par).** This is a tax-exempt, voter-approved, **unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** bond of a California school district, secured by a **statutory first lien** (Government Code §53515) on property taxes the County is obligated to levy without limit as to rate or amount, and additionally **insured by Assured Guaranty** (now Assured Guaranty Inc., the successor to AGM). Every load-bearing fact below was verified against the bond's own Official Statement, the EMMA trade record, EMMA continuing-disclosure filings, and the California Department of Education's certification lists — not secondary summaries.

This is a calibration buy, so the bar is "no hidden liar, executable at our price, clean disclosure." It clears. The one item worth stating plainly up front: the **City of Stockton** went through a Chapter 9 municipal bankruptcy (2012–2015). The **School District is a different legal entity** with its own tax base and is **not** the City — the Official Statement says so in its own words, and the District itself was never a bankruptcy debtor. There is also a separate, real 2023 governance/fraud episode at the District's **operating** level (FCMAT audit) that does **not** touch this bond's dedicated tax-levy payment source. Both are addressed directly in sections (c) and (g).

A fill at or below our 99.62 limit yields roughly **4.10% tax-free** (yield-to-worst to the 08/01/2031 par call). Recent dealer-to-customer prints have executed between ~98.1 and ~99.4, so our limit is realistic and not chasing.

## (c) Issuer & tax base — and the City-bankruptcy vs. School-District distinction

**Tax base (the thing that actually pays this bond):**
- **Assessed valuation (FY 2021-22): $15,296,163,339** (~$15.3 billion) — OS Table No. 7.
- District **direct debt = 2.59%** of AV; **combined total overlapping debt = 3.98%** of AV — modest leverage against a large, diversified Central Valley tax base.
- **Enrollment is stable**: ~38.8K (2004-05) drifting to ~33.9K (2020-21, COVID trough), projected ~34.2K — a gradual, well-disclosed decline, not a collapse. ADA ~32,922 (2020-21). For a GO bond paid by a property-tax levy (not by per-pupil ADA funding), enrollment is a secondary, not primary, credit driver.

**THE CITY-OF-STOCKTON-BANKRUPTCY-vs-SCHOOL-DISTRICT DISTINCTION (addressed directly):**

The **City of Stockton** filed for **Chapter 9** municipal bankruptcy on **2012-07-28** and **exited on 2015-02-25** after restructuring ~$1.5B of liabilities. This is the well-known Stockton bankruptcy. **It is not this issuer.**

- The **Stockton Unified School District is a separate legal entity** from the City. The Official Statement states this explicitly: *"The Series B Bonds are not a debt of the County, the City, the State of California… and neither the County, the City, the State, nor any of the State's political subdivisions (other than the District) is liable for the Series B Bonds."* (OS, Appendix C cover language.) The City appears in the OS only as **community/economic background** and as **overlapping debt** of other agencies — never as an obligor.
- A California school district is an **independent local educational agency** that levies its **own** voter-approved ad-valorem GO tax through the County. It was **not** a debtor in the City's Chapter 9 case, and its tax base, governance, and debt are distinct from the City's general fund. The City's 2012 insolvency did **not** impair, restructure, or affect this District's GO bonds.
- For completeness: the City of Stockton itself is now financially healthier — its FY2023-24 audit was "clean," general fund up 13.4%, reserves above policy. Favorable, but, again, a different entity.

**A separate, real District-level governance flag (operating level, NOT this bond):** In **February 2023**, a **FCMAT (Fiscal Crisis and Management Assistance Team) audit** found "sufficient evidence to demonstrate that fraud, misappropriation of funds and/or assets, or other illegal fiscal practices may have occurred" — centered on **no-bid contracts and misused federal COVID-relief funds** (e.g., a $6.6M UV-disinfection contract largely undelivered). This is a genuine internal-controls / governance black mark and is flagged honestly in section (g). **Why it does not change the BUY:** (1) it is a **general-fund / operations** problem, while **GO debt service is paid from a legally segregated County ad-valorem property-tax levy**, off the general fund and protected by the §53515 statutory lien; (2) the bonds are **AGM/AG-insured**; and (3) the District has **not** been downgraded to a Qualified or Negative fiscal certification (see (d)) and there is **no** state trustee/receiver — the misconduct was identified, audited, and is being remediated (disclosed bad news that did not metastasize into bond-payment risk). Per our honesty framework, this is honestly-surfaced operational risk, not a masked solvency problem at the security level.

## (d) The three screens (AI / fire / earthquake) — and AB-1200 fiscal certification

**AB-1200 fiscal certification — VERIFIED POSITIVE (by exclusion against the State authority).**
California's Department of Education publishes the official lists of districts with **Negative** or **Qualified** interim certifications. For the most recent period (**FY 2024-25 Second Interim**), **Stockton Unified does not appear on either the Negative or the Qualified list** (no San Joaquin County district does). Under AB-1200, a district not flagged Qualified/Negative is **Positive** — i.e., it self-certifies it can meet its financial obligations for the current and two subsequent years. This independently confirms the screen's POSITIVE input. (Source: CDE FY2024-25 Second Interim status report.)

**AI-crash insulation — score 99 (fully insulated).** This is a **local-property-tax** GO, paid by the County ad-valorem levy. It has **no** dependence on the California State General Fund and therefore **no** exposure to the capital-gains/income-tax channel that drives the AI/tech-crash credit risk our framework models for State GO. An AI/tech-stock crash that craters State income-tax revenue does not reach a property-tax-secured school GO. Verified by pledge type.

**Wildfire — 0% high-fire tail.** District census tracts score "Relatively Moderate" at worst on the FEMA National Risk Index wildfire metric (district fire score ~30); **0%** of the tax base sits in a high-fire tail. Stockton is Central Valley flatland, not wildland-urban interface. No insurance-withdrawal / AV-erosion fire channel of concern.

**Earthquake — LOW.** USGS/ASCE 7-22 short-period spectral acceleration **Ss ≈ 0.86** at the district location — low for California (Central Valley / Sierra foothills, away from the major Bay Area/coastal faults). Not a material seismic-impairment risk to the tax base.

## (g) Risks (flagged honestly)

1. **District operating-level governance / 2023 FCMAT fraud findings.** Real and material at the operations level (no-bid contracts, misused COVID funds). **Mitigants:** GO debt service is paid from a segregated County ad-valorem levy (off the general fund) under a §53515 statutory lien; bonds are AGM/AG-insured; District is **not** Qualified/Negative-certified and is under no state trustee. Monitor future continuing-disclosure filings and interim certifications for any escalation.
2. **Enrollment drift.** Long, gradual enrollment decline (~38.8K → ~34K). Secondary for a property-tax-secured GO, but a slow erosion in the district's operating capacity worth watching; well disclosed.
3. **Call risk.** Callable at par on **08/01/2031**. The ~98.66 dollar price and the YTW-to-call mean the worst-case yield is the ~4.10% to the call; if not called, the bond runs to 2045. Priced as a to-call bond — appropriate.
4. **Single-county tax-base concentration / Prop 8 AV reductions.** AV can fall in a downturn (the OS flags Prop 8 temporary reductions), but the County is then obligated to raise the levy rate (unlimited tax) to cover debt service — the core GO protection.
5. **Insurer reliance is a backstop, not the thesis.** Underlying ratings (A+/Aa3 at issuance) carry the credit; AGM/AG insurance is the wrap. No present concern (insurer ratings AA+/AA/A1 stable), but the value of the wrap is conditional on the underlying.
