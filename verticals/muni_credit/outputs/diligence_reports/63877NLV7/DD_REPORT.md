## (a) Verdict & buy thesis

**VERDICT: CLEAR TO BUY — with the Natomas flood-basin/levee question resolved to a MANAGED, not dismissed, risk.**

This is a voter-approved **unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** bond of a growing north-Sacramento
unified school district, **insured by Assured Guaranty Municipal Corp (AGM)** and additionally secured by a
**statutory first lien** (SB-222 / Gov. Code §53515). Sacramento County is legally **obligated to levy
property taxes without limitation as to rate or amount** to pay it, runs a **Teeter Plan** (the District
receives 100% of the secured levy regardless of taxpayer delinquency), and the interest is **exempt from
both federal and California income tax**. The ~79 dollar price is a function of the low 3% coupon in a
higher-rate market, **not** credit distress — the trade tape shows a live, two-sided customer market on
2026-06-17. The discount is taxed as ordinary income (de-minimis breached); the quoted **after-tax
taxable-equivalent yield (~8.5–8.6%)** already nets that drag.

**The one thing to scrutinize — and we did:** Natomas sits in a deep Sacramento River flood basin behind
levees. Our NRI tail screen read 0%, but the Official Statement carries a **dedicated "Flood Zone" section**
with an explicit warning that pre-levee-completion flooding could cause **"substantial delinquencies in the
payment of ad valorem taxes."** That is the real risk channel and we did not let the 0% screen wave it
through (see §d). The finding: the basin's flood status has **improved materially** since the OS — FEMA
re-mapped it from an AE zone to an A99 zone in 2015 (building moratorium lifted), and the SAFCA/Army Corps
200-year levee upgrade has been in active construction and is at/near completion (target ~2025-26). It is
not a dismissed risk, but it is a **shrinking, insured, unlimited-tax-pledged** one.

The thesis: buy an AGM-wrapped, statutorily-lien-secured, AI-insulated, **growing-enrollment** district
cash flow at a deep discount, where the genuine idiosyncratic risk (flood basin) is disclosed, improving,
double-backstopped by insurance + an unlimited levy, and the tax treatment is already in the after-tax math.

## (b) Security & pledge — what legally compels payment

**Finding: UNLIMITED AD-VALOREM GENERAL OBLIGATION, AGM-INSURED, SB-222 statutory lien. VERIFIED against the Official Statement.**

- **Claim:** True GO, not a COP / lease / CFD special-tax obligation. **Verified** by reading the Official
  Statement (`raw/official_statement.pdf`, p.1 + "SECURITY AND SOURCE OF PAYMENT"). The OS cover states the
  Bonds are "payable from ad valorem property taxes levied and collected by Sacramento County… The Board of
  Supervisors of Sacramento County is empowered and **obligated to annually levy and collect ad valorem
  property taxes without limitation as to rate or amount** on all taxable property in the District… for the
  payment of principal of and interest on the Bonds." This is the strongest muni pledge form.
- **Statutory lien (SB-222 / Gov. Code §53515) — VERIFIED (quoted from OS):** the Bonds "are secured by a
  **statutory lien on all revenues received pursuant to the levy and collection of the tax.** The lien
  automatically arises without the need for any action… is valid and binding from the time the bonds are
  executed and delivered… enforceable against the local agency… its successors, transferees, and creditors…
  **irrespective of whether those parties have notice of the lien.**" A perfected first lien on the tax
  stream, ahead of general creditors.
- **Bond insurance (AGM) — VERIFIED.** "The scheduled payment of principal of and interest on the Bonds when
  due will be guaranteed under an insurance policy… issued concurrently… by **Assured Guaranty Municipal
  Corp. ('AGM')**" (OS "BOND INSURANCE"). Insured ratings **KBRA AA+ / Moody's A2 / S&P AA**; **underlying**
  (un-wrapped) ratings **Moody's A1 / S&P A**. Note: post-issuance AGM-affiliate downgrades have at times put
  the *insured* Moody's notch (A2) **below** the *underlying* (A1) — so for THIS credit the wrap adds default
  remoteness, not headline-rating uplift; underwrite to the **A1/A underlying** quality, which is solid
  investment grade on its own.
- **Teeter Plan (credit positive) — VERIFIED.** Sacramento County operates the Teeter Plan (Rev. & Tax. Code
  §4701 et seq.); the District "will be credited with the full" secured levy regardless of taxpayer
  delinquency. **This is the direct mitigant to the OS's flood-delinquency warning** (see §d): so long as the
  Teeter Plan stays in effect, taxpayer delinquency — including flood-driven delinquency — does **not** reduce
  the District's debt-service cash flow; the County advances it. (Caveat: the County may discontinue Teeter;
  the OS discloses this.)
- **Tax status — VERIFIED.** Bond Counsel (Quint & Thimmig LLP) opinion: interest excluded from federal gross
  income, **not** an AMT preference item, and **exempt from California personal income tax**.

## (d) The risk screens — LED by the Natomas flood-basin / levee question

### (d.1) Flood basin & levees — THE risk to scrutinize (we did not let the 0% NRI screen wave it through)

**Finding: REAL, DISCLOSED, and IMPROVING. The OS carries a dedicated Flood Zone section the NRI tail screen
missed; current levee status materially mitigates it; the unlimited-tax pledge + Teeter Plan + AGM wrap
backstop it three ways. Net: a managed idiosyncratic risk, not a dismissed one.**

What our screen said vs. what the primary source says — the honest reconciliation:

- **Our screen:** flood NRI tail = **0%** (low). **This was incomplete.** FEMA's NRI is a county/tract
  *expected-annual-loss* composite; it under-weights a **levee-protected deep basin** where the risk is a
  low-probability / high-severity *levee-failure* tail, not chronic riverine flooding. The screen is not the
  authority here — the OS is.
- **What the OS actually discloses (verified, OS "Flood Zone," pp.15–16):**
  - The **Natomas Basin** (within which the entire District sits) was **remapped by FEMA as an "AE" flood
    hazard zone in December 2008**, after the Army Corps found seepage and unstable slopes in two Sacramento
    River levee sections — creating a **de facto building moratorium** (new construction had to be elevated
    up to 20 feet).
  - **SAFCA** (Sacramento Area Flood Control Agency) + the **Army Corps** undertook a four-phase upgrade to
    **100-year, ultimately 200-year**, protection. SAFCA completed the first two phases (18 miles of the most
    vulnerable levees) in **2012**.
  - FEMA **re-mapped the basin from AE to "A99" in March 2015** (effective June 2015) — "indicative of
    substantial progress towards 100-year protection" — at which point **the building moratorium was lifted.**
  - The final phases (Corps responsibility) were authorized by **WRRDA (2014)**, moved into construction via
    an **August 2016 partnership agreement**, and **construction began in 2018, "expected to be completed in
    several years."**
  - **The load-bearing warning, quoted:** *"If substantial flooding occurs before the completion of levee
    improvements, there could be substantial delinquencies in the payment of ad valorem taxes within the
    District."* This is the exact channel — flood → AV/levy delinquency → debt-service stress — and the OS
    states it plainly.
- **Current status (verified beyond the 2019 OS):** Per SAFCA / Reclamation District 1000 project updates,
  the remaining ~24-mile, 200-year levee upgrade has been in active construction with **completion targeted
  ~2025–2026**. The basin is no longer under a moratorium and development has resumed (corroborated by the
  district's enrollment growth to ~14,900 and AV recovery). The OS's "before completion of levee
  improvements" window is therefore **largely closed or closing as of cutoff** — the risk the OS warned about
  is materially smaller now than when written.
- **Three-way backstop even in the residual tail:** (1) the pledge is **unlimited as to rate** — if AV/levy
  collections fall, the County raises the rate to cover debt service; (2) the **Teeter Plan** advances the
  full secured levy to the District regardless of delinquency (the direct answer to the OS's "substantial
  delinquencies" warning, so long as Teeter remains in effect); (3) **AGM insurance** guarantees scheduled
  P&I if all else fails.
- **Honest residual:** a catastrophic levee failure before final certification, *combined with* a County
  Teeter discontinuance *and* an AGM claims-paying failure, would be required to impair this bond — a remote,
  triple-conditional tail. The risk is **real and correctly elevated above the 0% screen**, but it is
  disclosed, shrinking, and triple-backstopped. **Not a reason to reject; a reason to size and monitor.**

### (d.2) AI / tech-crash insulation — **99 / 100 (fully insulated)**

This is a *local property-tax* credit. Debt service comes from the Sacramento County ad-valorem levy, **not**
the California General Fund. The State-GF capital-gains channel that crushed *State* GO ratings ~4-5 notches
in the dot-com bust does **not** touch a school-district unlimited-tax GO. (The only tech link is the #2
taxpayer, Ragingwire Data Centers, at 1.03% of AV — immaterial.) Credit insulation, not a price/rate
statement.

### (d.3) Wildfire — **0% high-fire tail (low)**

FEMA NRI × NCES tract crosswalk: the District's urban north-Sacramento tax-base parcels are not in
high-wildfire-risk tracts. The insurer-withdrawal / AV-erosion channel is not materially present. (Unlike
the flood screen, the wildfire 0% is reliable here — this is flat urban basin, not a wildland-urban
interface.)

### (d.4) Earthquake — **Ss ≈ 0.65 (low)**

Short-period spectral acceleration is low (Central Valley basin, no major active fault traversing the tax
base). Low correlated catastrophe risk to the AV base.

## (g) Tax & yield — the de-minimis math behind the ~8.6% TEY

**Finding: De-minimis breached → discount accretes as ORDINARY income. The ~8.5–8.6% after-tax TEY already nets this; a naive exempt-only gross-up would overstate it.**

- **Coupon is fully tax-exempt** (federal + CA, non-AMT), per Bond Counsel (§b).
- **The discount is NOT exempt.** Purchase price ~**79.16** vs par 100, with **~19.1 years** to the 2045-08-01
  maturity. The IRC de-minimis threshold = 0.25 pt/yr × 19.1 yrs = **4.78 pts below par = a 95.22 cutoff
  price.** Purchase at 79.16 is **far below 95.22**, so the **market discount exceeds the de-minimis amount**
  → the ~**20.8-point** accretion of the discount toward par is taxed as **ordinary income** (annually if a
  constant-yield election is made, else as ordinary income at sale/maturity) — **not** as a capital gain.
- **Computed analytics (my engine, validated vs EMMA on discounts):** at price 79.163, **YTM-to-maturity =
  4.658%** (exactly matches EMMA's reported trade yield on the same print — no call distortion), **modified
  duration ≈ 13.5 years.**
- **After-tax TEY (independently reconstructed):** decomposing the 4.658% YTM into a **~3.79% exempt
  current-coupon yield** + a **~0.87% ordinary-taxed accretion component**, then grossing the exempt portion
  to a taxable equivalent at the CA top combined bracket (~50.3% for the exempt comparison; the accretion
  carried as taxable-grade): **taxable-equivalent yield ≈ 8.5%**, consistent with the screened **~8.6%**. The
  headline is **honest** — it already reflects (i) the exempt coupon grossed up and (ii) the ordinary-income
  drag on the discount accretion netted out. The de-minimis breach is a **known, priced** feature, not a
  hidden catch — it is the reason the TEY is stated after-tax rather than as a raw exempt gross-up.

*Self-discipline note (house rule on TEY conflation): the ~8.5–8.6% is an **after-tax taxable-equivalent**
figure, not gross YTM and not net-of-fees. Gross YTM/YTW is **4.658%**.*
