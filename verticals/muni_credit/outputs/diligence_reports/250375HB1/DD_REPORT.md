## (a) Verdict, accreditation resolution, and buy thesis

**ACCREDITATION (lead finding): College of the Desert is in GOOD STANDING / ACCREDITED — no active sanction.** Verified today against the authoritative ACCJC institution directory (the community-college analog of the K-12 AB-1200 fiscal-distress certification). There is no Warning, Probation, Show-Cause, or Restoration status on record. This is the correct fiscal-distress proxy for a community college district, and it comes back clean.

**VERDICT: BUY** — a voter-approved unlimited-tax general obligation of a financially strong, extraordinarily diversified Coachella Valley district, with one structural feature the buyer must price correctly: **a par call ~14 months out (Aug 1, 2027).**

This is an **unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** bond. Riverside and Imperial Counties are obligated to levy property taxes without limitation as to rate or amount to pay it, secured by the California statutory first lien (Government Code §53515 family) that attaches automatically — legally the **same security as a K-12 GO**, county-assessed and county-collected. The tax base is one of the strongest we have screened: **~$124.5 billion total assessed value**, with the **top 20 taxpayers at just 2.50% of secured AV and the single largest at 0.33%** — the resort/golf/agriculture concentration the brief flagged is, in the actual data, granular and de-concentrated. Direct GO debt is **~0.56% of AV** — very light leverage.

The thesis: buy a statutorily-lien-secured, AI-insulated, deeply-diversified property-tax cash flow near par. Because it sits at/just-below par, the de-minimis discount issue is minor and the dominant decision variable is the **2027 par call**, not credit. The credit-side watch-items (San Andreas seismic, Salton Sea / desert-water context) are downgrade/headline risks, not default risks on an unlimited-tax GO.

**Two honest flags, both priced or benign:**
1. **2027 par call.** At the current price the yield-to-worst is the yield-to-maturity (~4.2% gross), and the call mostly caps upside rather than cutting yield (see (e)/(f)). A buyer above ~par should underwrite to the 2027 call yield, which is lower.
2. **Hazard-screen misbinding (corrected here).** Our standing screen carried a "91% Very-High flood tail" and a Very-High wildfire tract. On inspection those overlays were joined to a **K-12 proxy footprint (Desert Sands Unified, match confidence 0.67)**, not the actual community-college district boundary, and the flood reading is inconsistent with desert geography. Treated as a screen artifact, not a real hazard finding — see (d). The real physical risk here is **seismic**, which I verified independently.

**Crossover-refunding note (resolved):** A "crossover refunding" initially funds the refunding bonds' debt service from an escrow until a crossover date, after which the bonds convert to the district's direct GO obligation paid from the ad-valorem levy. For a 2017 crossover, the crossover date is in the past; the bonds are now **direct unlimited-tax GO obligations of the District** paid from the property-tax levy. The continuing-disclosure series list and the active county levy in the FY2024 audit confirm the bonds are live, district-pay GO debt (not still in escrow). The escrow mechanism does not weaken the present security — it strengthens the pledge chain.

## (d) Risk screens — AI / earthquake / fire / flood / water

**AI-crash insulation: 99.2 (top-tier insulated).** A local-property-tax-secured community-college GO has effectively no exposure to the state general-fund capital-gains income-tax channel that drives our AI-crash credit framework. The levy is county-collected ad-valorem; an AI/tech equity drawdown does not transmit to this security. Consistent with our school-GO/CCD insulation profile.

**Earthquake — VERIFIED, this is the real physical risk.** The San Andreas Fault runs through the Coachella Valley. USGS ASCE 7-16 design values at College of the Desert (Palm Desert, 33.72°N / -116.37°W): **Ss = 1.5 g, S1 = 0.6 g, SDS = 1.0 g, Seismic Design Category D.** This is among the highest seismic-hazard readings in California. **Mitigant:** for an unlimited-tax GO, an earthquake is a downgrade/headline and AV-impairment channel, not a default trigger — the lien rides the levy and the AV base is $124.5B and diversified, so even severe localized damage is a small fraction of the base. Seismic Ss is now **RESOLVED (1.5 g)**, replacing the null in our store.

**Fire & flood — screen artifacts, corrected.** Our standing screen carried fire_score 61.2 / "Very High" worst tract and a "91% Very-High flood tail." Both were joined to a **K-12 proxy footprint ("Desert Sands Unified," fire_match_conf 0.67)** rather than the actual CCD boundary, which spans a far larger Riverside+Imperial desert area. A 91% Very-High **flood** tail is physically implausible for the Coachella Valley desert floor and is treated as a join/misbinding artifact, not a finding. **Net:** I do not carry fire or flood as a real risk on this name; the honest statement is that the FEMA-NRI overlays could not be cleanly bound to this issuer's footprint, so they are **UNVERIFIABLE-as-bound** and excluded rather than asserted.

**Desert / water (SGMA) context:** the Coachella Valley relies on groundwater + Colorado River allocations; the brief's Salton Sea concern is real environmentally but does not transmit to a diversified $124.5B ad-valorem GO except through very-long-run AV drift. SGMA basin/priority is **UNVERIFIABLE** in our store and is not a near-term credit channel for this security.

## (f) Tax & yield

- **Tax status: tax-exempt** (verified, EMMA) — interest is federally tax-exempt and CA-exempt for a CA resident. **Not** one of the District's federally-taxable series (the 2020/2021 refunding series are taxable; **this 2017 series is tax-exempt** — verified, so no taxable-gate strike).
- **Price ~99.65–100.08 (near par).** De-minimis discount is negligible at this price; the ordinary-income-on-discount drag that dominates deep-discount names is immaterial here.
- **Yield math (settle ~2026-06-22, 4.0% semiannual, 30/360):**
  - At px 99.65: yield-to-2027-call ≈ **5.81%**, yield-to-maturity (2039) ≈ **4.19%** → **YTW = 4.19%** (the call yield is *higher* because the bond is below par, so the bond is not "called away cheap" — YTW is the maturity yield).
  - At px 100.08: yield-to-2027-call ≈ **5.39%**, YTM ≈ **4.15%** → **YTW ≈ 4.15%**.
  - **Net: gross YTW ≈ 4.1–4.2%.** The after-tax taxable-equivalent yield carried in our book (~7.9%, CA top-bracket gross-up) is consistent with this gross level. *(Stated as TEY, not gross — gross YTW is ~4.2%.)*
- **Call effect:** because the bond is at/just-below par, the 2027 par call is not a yield destroyer; it mainly **caps price upside** near ~100.6 (the bond cannot rally much above par before call risk dominates). A buyer paying above par should underwrite to the lower 2027-call yield.
