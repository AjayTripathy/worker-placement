## (a) Verdict & thesis

**Verdict: BUYABLE — add to the top-buy set as the highest-yield new pick — but accumulate patiently and size for the two honest caveats: (1) the highest seismic exposure in our entire book, on the Hayward fault, and (2) genuinely thin liquidity.** This is a high-credit-quality, Aa2, unlimited-tax school GO trading at a deep ~78.5 discount for ~8.7% after-tax tax-equivalent yield, and neither caveat is a credit-default risk — but both are real and are stated up front, not buried.

**Thesis.** This is a small (ADA ~3,700), affluent East Bay unified district whose bonds are an **unlimited ad-valorem general obligation** secured by an automatic **SB-222 / Government Code §53515 statutory lien** on the property-tax levy. Alameda County is *obligated* to levy the tax "without limitation as to rate or amount" to pay debt service. Credit quality is strong and honestly disclosed: Moody's Aa2, AV $2.46B with a steady ~6%/yr growth history, near-zero taxpayer concentration (largest taxpayer 2.11%), low leverage (direct debt 1.18% of AV), and a POSITIVE AB-1200 fiscal certification. The bond is cheap because (i) it is a long 2045 term bond bought at a deep discount in a higher-rate regime, and (ii) it is thinly traded, so the market demands a liquidity concession. The yield is real and the de-minimis tax drag is already correctly priced into our 8.7% after-tax number (section f).

**The two caveats, up front:**

1. **EARTHQUAKE — Hayward fault, the headline.** Our seismic intensity score for this district (Ss 2.40) is the **highest in the whole book**. The district sits essentially on top of the **Hayward fault**, one of the most dangerous urban faults in California and widely described as overdue for a full-rupture event. The Official Statement itself names the Hayward fault explicitly and cites the USGS 72% probability of a M6.7+ Bay Area quake before 2045 and a M7-Hayward scenario of ~$100B damage. **This is a downgrade-and-recovery risk, not a default risk** (mechanism in section d/g): because the levy is *unlimited*, a quake that shrinks the assessed-value base forces the tax *rate* up to cover the same debt service — the OS spells this exact mechanism out. The tail is a multi-year disruption to collections and AV, mitigated at the portfolio level by NOT stacking this with other Bay Area / Hayward-Calaveras names.

2. **LIQUIDITY — thin; patient accumulation only.** ~59 trades trailing 12 months, only ~25 distinct trading days, and just **1 strictly two-sided customer day** in the past year. There is no live two-sided dealer quote (IBKR carries no contract for this CUSIP). Clip sizes are small (median $20k, max $170k). This is acquirable in small lots over time, NOT in size at a single touch. Do not chase; work limit orders against the EMMA tape.

**The two items the work order flagged as open are now RESOLVED:** the call schedule (section b) and the issuer/pledge (section b) are both verified from the Official Statement. **Earthquake — Hayward fault (honest assessment).** The district's seismic intensity (Ss 2.40) is the **single highest in our portfolio**, and the fault in question is the **Hayward fault** — a right-lateral strike-slip fault running through the densely populated East Bay that the USGS characterizes as having a high probability of a major rupture and that is frequently cited as overdue relative to its ~150-year average recurrence (last major event 1868). The OS does **not** hide this: it names the Hayward fault explicitly, cites the USGS 72% probability of a M6.7+ Bay Area quake before 2045 (i.e., within this bond's life), and quotes the USGS M7-Hayward scenario of hundreds of deaths and ~$100B of damage.

Why this is a **downgrade-and-recover** risk, not a default risk, for *this* bond:
- The pledge is an **unlimited** ad-valorem levy. If a major quake destroys property and the assessed-value base shrinks, the County is still obligated to levy a tax sufficient to pay debt service — which means the tax *rate* rises to cover the same dollar amount on a smaller base. The OS states this mechanism in plain terms: "Lower assessed values could necessitate a corresponding increase in the annual tax rate to be levied to pay the principal of and interest on the Series 2016 Bonds." That is the structural shock-absorber that makes CA school GOs survive earthquakes as ratings events rather than payment defaults.
- The genuine tail is **multi-year**: a severe event can (i) disrupt the County's tax-collection cycle (and here, with no Teeter backstop on school GO, the District bears that timing risk — see section b), (ii) depress AV for years during rebuild, and (iii) coincide with broad regional economic stress. None of that is a same-day default, but it is real impairment risk over a long 2045 horizon, and it is the reason this name should be a **capped, diversified** position rather than an oversized one.
- **Portfolio rule:** cap aggregate Bay Area / Hayward-Calaveras-San Andreas-North exposure; do NOT stack this with other East Bay school GOs in the same rupture footprint. This is the same aggregate-not-per-zone discipline applied to fire/seismic elsewhere in the book.

## (f) Tax & yield — the de-minimis math behind ~8.7% after-tax TEY

- **Tax status:** tax-exempt (federal §103 + CA personal income tax; non-AMT), CONFIRMED (OS + EMMA). The coupon is a clean 3.000% — not the odd 3-decimal near-par signature of a federally-taxable issue; it **passes the taxable-gate**.
- **Gross yield:** at ~78.5, **YTW ~4.72%** to maturity (matches the EMMA print 4.719%; our internal mark uses 4.836% at the slightly lower 77.2 EMMA reference price). **YTW here is effectively yield-to-maturity**, because at a deep discount the issuer has no incentive to call at par (calling would mean paying 100 for a bond trading at ~78) — so the 8/1/2026 par call, while legally live, is economically irrelevant and does NOT cap the upside.
- **De-minimis is breached — the key honesty point.** The de-minimis threshold here is ~95.25 (par − 0.25 × ~19 years to maturity). Buying at ~78.5 is far below it, so the ~21.5-point market discount does **not** accrete as tax-exempt or as capital gain — it accretes as **taxable ordinary income** at sale or maturity. Only the **3% coupon** is tax-exempt.
- **After-tax TEY (de-minimis-aware) ~8.7%:** the correct gross-up applies the CA top-bracket muni multiplier (~2.01×) **only to the tax-exempt coupon-yield component** (≈ 3.00 / 78.5 ≈ 3.82% × 2.01 ≈ 7.7%) and adds the taxable discount-accretion component at 1.0× (≈ 4.84% YTW − 3.82% current = ~1.0%). Sum ≈ **8.7%** (our published 8.76% corresponds to the lower 77.2 EMMA reference price). **This is already the honest after-tax number — it is NOT a naive "YTW × 2.01," which would have overstated it to ~9.7% by wrongly treating the taxable ordinary-income discount as tax-exempt.** The 8.7% is genuine after the de-minimis drag, and it is our highest-yield new candidate on a like-for-like after-tax basis.
