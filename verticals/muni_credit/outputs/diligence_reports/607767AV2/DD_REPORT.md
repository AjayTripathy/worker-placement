## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**Verdict: HOLD (Electric System net-revenue pledge with the thinnest coverage of the sleeve's electric names,
material customer concentration, and natural-gas/wholesale-power cost exposure — monitorable, not disqualifying).**

**Resolved issuer (CONFIRMED from on-file OS).** Obligor is the **Modesto Irrigation District (MID) Electric
System**, a publicly-owned Central Valley electric utility (peak demand ~642 MW). The bonds are **Electric System
Revenue Bonds, Series 2015A** and **Electric System Refunding Revenue Bonds, Series 2015B**, issued by the
**Modesto Irrigation District Financing Authority**. **Structure nuance:** the legal mechanism is a **Lease
Agreement–2015** (the District leases the project to the Authority and leases it back, paying "Series 2015 Rental
Payments") — BUT the OS is explicit that those Rental Payments are **"payable from and secured solely by a pledge
of and lien on Electric System Revenues of the District."** So the *payment source* is a system-revenue pledge,
not a tax or appropriation; it is effectively a net-revenue bond wearing a lease wrapper. (The dominant "Lease"
language in the document reflects that wrapper, not a soft general-fund lease — see (b).)

**Thesis.** A revenue-backed essential-service electric credit. Three reasons for HOLD: (1) **coverage is the
thinnest of the electric names** at ~1.63x (vs Merced 4.17x, SCPPA 3.6x); (2) **customer concentration** — top-ten
customers ≈ 23% of kWh sales (16% of revenue), a real few-customer dependency; and (3) **power-supply cost
exposure** — MID owns hydro + gas turbines and buys gas-fired entitlement power (Lodi Energy Center), so
**natural-gas price risk** is the load-bearing supply risk.

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / POWER SUPPLY / Prop 218

### Debt-service coverage (DSCR)
- **Cached coverage: 1.63x** (2014 all-in "Adjusted Debt Service Coverage," organic — no RSF adjustment). This is
  the **thinnest coverage among the sleeve's electric names** (Merced 4.17x, SCPPA 3.6x). Above the covenant, but
  with a smaller cushion, and an electric utility's coverage is more sensitive to fuel/wholesale-power cost swings.

### Rate covenant
- **Rate covenant (Lease Agreement):** a two-part test — sufficient to pay M&O **plus 1.10x Debt Service and
  Obligation Service** for the Fiscal Year (OS p.868). Self-tightening at ~1.10x over the obligation stack.

### POWER SUPPLY (the load-bearing risk for an electric revenue bond — lead here)
- **Resource mix (OS resource table):** MID **owns generation** — **Don Pedro / Stone Drop hydro** (snowmelt-
  dependent), **McClure combustion turbines (2 units)** — plus **purchased power including a natural-gas
  entitlement share of the Lodi Energy Center** (a gas-fired plant). Peak demand ~642 MW.
- **The risk — NATURAL GAS + wholesale-power cost:** the OS's own forward-looking caveats flag "natural gas costs
  and availability, wholesale and retail electric energy and capacity prices." MID's gas-fired purchased power
  (Lodi Energy Center) and the hydrology of its Don Pedro hydro share mean coverage is exposed to **gas-price
  spikes and dry-year hydro shortfalls** — the classic municipal-electric fuel-cost squeeze. This is the
  load-bearing risk, and it intersects with the thin 1.63x coverage. (Cache tagged supply "low" on a water-supply
  keyword basis — for an *electric* system the relevant supply risk is fuel/power cost, which is real here.)
- **Mitigant:** owned hydro + gas turbines (partial self-supply), cost-recovery rate-setting (not CPUC-regulated),
  and a diversified own-plus-purchased portfolio.

### Prop 218 / Prop 26
- Electric rates follow MID's rate-setting process (Prop 26 cost-of-service framework for utility charges); not
  CPUC-regulated. Standard CA friction.

   thin-coverage + fuel + concentration combination.
