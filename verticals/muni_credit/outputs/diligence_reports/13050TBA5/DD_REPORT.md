## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**VERDICT: BUY** (essential-service wastewater revenue bond; diversified retail base; very strong coverage; honestly disclosed, now-resolved Chapter 9 history).

**Resolved issuer / true obligor.** The named issuer on the CUSIP (base 13050T = **California Municipal Finance Authority, "CMFA"**) is a **conduit / JPA shell with no taxing power and no operating role**. The conduit name tells you nothing about the credit. Reading the Official Statement resolves the **true obligor: the City of San Bernardino Municipal Water Department (the "Department"), Sewer Treatment Enterprise**, acting on behalf of the City of San Bernardino. The Department operates a 33 MGD regional secondary wastewater treatment plant (the "WRP"). CMFA loaned bond proceeds to the Department under an Installment Purchase Agreement; the bonds are repaid solely from the Department's sewer **Net Revenues**. This is the correct way to read a CMFA-style conduit: ignore the issuer name, find the underlying enterprise.

**Thesis.** This is a textbook *clean* essential-service wastewater revenue bond, and it is the **inverse of the Tulare-dairy reject risk**. The three things that actually matter for a water/wastewater revenue bond — debt-service coverage, customer concentration, and supply/regulatory (here NPDES treatment) risk — all read favorably:
- Coverage is enormous: **FY2024 DSCR = 6.10x** against a thin 1.10x rate covenant, and the 2016 Bonds are the *only* senior-lien debt.
- The customer base is **diversified retail/residential** (~64,000 connections). **No single customer exceeds ~1.4% of revenue**; top-ten = ~5.5%. There is no dominant industrial/ag offtaker whose loss would impair the pledge.
- It is a wastewater (sewer treatment) system with a long record of NPDES compliance; the "water supply" axis does not apply, and the analog (discharge-permit risk) is clean.

The one genuinely material item — the City of San Bernardino's Chapter 9 bankruptcy — was **fully and prominently disclosed on the OS cover** and has since been **resolved** (plan of adjustment confirmed Feb 2017, effective mid-2017). Critically, the Sewer Enterprise was never a debtor and the pledged sewer Net Revenues are statutory "special revenues" exempt from the automatic stay. This is honestly-disclosed, now-stale bad news, which under our framework is CLEAN, not a flag.

**Why it is in the book:** ~4.19% gross YTM at a 99.45 dollar price on a ~11-year-duration, AGM-wrapped, deeply-covered essential-service revenue credit; ~8.1% TEY at the CA top bracket.

## (c) System & customers

**System (wastewater).** The Department operates the **33 MGD Regional Secondary Treatment plant (the WRP)**. Secondary effluent (~27 MGD) is sent to the **RIX (Rapid Infiltration and Extraction) tertiary facility in Colton**, jointly owned by San Bernardino and Colton, which polishes to California Title 22 tertiary standards and discharges to the **Santa Ana River** under its own NPDES permit. The OS states RIX "consistently meets or exceeds required discharge standards." Service area ≈ **64,000 connections** across the City plus two partner agencies (East Valley Water District ~30% of flow; Loma Linda ~10%).

**Customer concentration — THE decisive Tulare-lesson test → CLEAN (diversified retail).**

| Metric | At issuance (FY2015-16) | Current (FY2024) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Largest single customer | School District, **1.03%** | County of San Bernardino, **1.37%** | OS Top-Ten table; FY2024 CD Top-Ten table |
| Top-ten customers, total | **5.35%** | **5.51%** | same |
| "No single customer >" | **2% of gross revenues** (OS explicit) | consistent (largest 1.37%) | OS p.~32 |
| Residential/multi-family share | ~63% of customers | stable | OS Table 2 |

The top-ten list is government, a school district, a hospital, housing/condo operators, and apartment owners — **no dominant industrial or agricultural offtaker**. This is exactly the diversified-retail profile that makes a water/wastewater revenue pledge clean. There is **no single-customer-reject risk** (the Tulare-dairy failure mode), because no customer is large enough to matter and the enterprise has statutory authority to terminate service to delinquents and to set rates without outside approval.

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / supply (NPDES) / Prop 218

**Debt-service coverage (DSCR) — very strong.**

| Fiscal Year | Net Revenues | Total Debt Service | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2020 | $13.12M | $2.27M | **5.79x** |
| FY2021 | $9.10M | $1.97M | **4.63x** |
| FY2022 | $12.96M | $1.97M | **6.59x** |
| FY2023 | $12.90M | $1.97M | **6.56x** |
| FY2024 | $11.98M | $1.96M | **6.10x** |

(Source: FY2024 Continuing Disclosure Annual Report, "Net Revenues & Debt Service Coverage.") At issuance, OS projections were 3.6x–4.1x on the new bonds after the 1998 COP was defeased; the system has comfortably beaten that. Pre-2016 historic coverage was thinner (1.16x–1.75x) only because it then carried the now-defeased COP.

**Rate covenant — 1.10x (thin on paper, immaterial in practice).** The City covenants to set rates yielding Net Revenues ≥ **110% of Debt Service**; the additional-bonds test is also 110% of Maximum Annual Debt Service. 1.10x is weak relative to the 1.20–1.25x typical of strong systems and is a genuine structural note — **but** actual coverage runs ~6x, so the headroom to the covenant is ~5.5x of debt service. The covenant is a floor the system is nowhere near.

**Supply / regulatory (wastewater analog) — CLEAN.** This is a sewer-treatment system, so the "imported-vs-groundwater / SGMA basin" supply axis does **not** apply. The analogous risk is **NPDES discharge-permit / treatment compliance**: WRP holds an NPDES permit; RIX holds its own NPDES permit and discharges to the Santa Ana River "in substantial compliance," "consistently meets or exceeds" standards. No disclosed permit violations or enforcement orders. **Forward capital risk:** the **Clean Water Factory** recycled-water program (a 5 MGD tertiary recharge/reuse build) is a multi-year CIP that will require future rate increases — properly disclosed as a risk factor, not a hidden liability.

**Proposition 218 — standard sewer-rate risk, mitigated.** Sewer charges are subject to Article XIIID majority-protest (but not the two-thirds-vote requirement). The OS discloses the risk that future increases could face a majority protest. Mitigants: the system already has large coverage headroom (it does not *need* near-term increases to service this debt), and the Department/Board sets rates without outside-body approval. The recent rate history (8.5%/8.5%/3.5% increases implemented) shows demonstrated rate-raising capacity.

**TEY discipline:** the **gross yield is ~4.19%**; the ~8.1% figure is the **full CA-top-bracket taxable-equivalent yield**, not the gross coupon/yield. Do not conflate the two.

**Yield-to-worst note:** the bond is a **discount** (99.45). Bonds maturing on/after 2027-08 are optionally callable at **par (100%)** on/after 2026-08-01. Because the bond trades below par, an early par call would *raise* the realized yield, so the investor's worst case (lowest yield) is running to the 2041 maturity / 2038–41 sinking fund. **YTW ≈ YTM here.** No premium-call give-up risk.

## (g) Risks (honest; UNVERIFIABLE ≠ clean)

1. **City of San Bernardino Chapter 9 history (RESOLVED, disclosed).** City filed Chapter 9 in 2012; was still in bankruptcy at 2016 issuance (disclosed prominently on the OS cover). Plan of adjustment confirmed Feb 2017, effective mid-2017. The Sewer Enterprise was **never a debtor**; pledged Net Revenues are statutory "special revenues" (Bankruptcy Code §§922/928) exempt from the automatic stay — which is why the underlying rating held at "A" through the bankruptcy. This is honestly-disclosed, now-stale bad news → **CLEAN under the honesty framework, not a flag.** *Residual:* a future municipal fiscal-stress recurrence is always possible for a city with SB's socioeconomic profile, but it would not reach the sewer Net Revenues pledge absent a special-revenues-impairing court ruling (an area of unsettled but generally bondholder-favorable law).

2. **Thin 1.10x rate covenant.** Weak covenant floor; mitigated by ~6x actual coverage and single-lien structure. Monitor that no large parity additional bonds are issued under the 110% MADS test that would compress coverage.

3. **Clean Water Factory CIP / future rate need.** Multi-year recycled-water capital program will require future rate increases and possibly additional bonds; disclosed. Watch for (a) parity debt issuance, (b) Prop 218 majority-protest on the rate case.

4. **Surety reserve (not cash).** Reserve Requirement satisfied by an AGM surety policy; reserve quality is tied to AGM, not cash on hand.

5. **AGM-wrap reliance / rating-conditional compression.** Bond is AGM-insured (S&P "AA" insured / "A" underlying at issuance). The wrap compresses spread conditional on the "A" underlying; in a stress the relevant credit is the **underlying "A"**, not the wrap. Re-pull current S&P underlying and AGM ratings before a large add (ratings not independently re-verified to today in this pass → treat as **needs-refresh, not assumed-unchanged**).

6. **Execution/liquidity feed gap.** IBKR has no live bid/ask for this CUSIP; work the order vs. EMMA tape and dealer runs.
