## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**Verdict: HOLD (clean first-lien net-revenue pledge and adequate coverage, but dual supply exposure
[adjudicated groundwater + Colorado/SWP imports] and coverage-quality volatility keep it off BUY).**

**Resolved issuer (CONFIRMED from on-file OS).** Obligor is the **City of Azusa Water System** ("Azusa Light &
Water" — the water utility), the largest municipal water utility in the San Gabriel Valley. The security is the
**Water System Refunding Revenue Bonds, Series 2015** (used to refinance the City's Azusa Public Financing
Authority Parity Revenue obligations). A direct City obligation — not a COP/lease/special tax.

**Thesis.** Clean **first-lien Net Revenues** pledge, BAM-insured (S&P "AA" insured rating), coverage ~2.6x most
recent OS year. The two reasons for HOLD rather than BUY: (1) **supply is doubly exposed** — Azusa draws on the
**adjudicated Main San Gabriel Basin groundwater** (Watermaster-managed, drought-sensitive) AND supplements with
**imported Colorado River + State Water Project water** via Upper San Gabriel Valley MWD; and (2) the OS
coverage history is **volatile** (1.31x in FY2011) and is partly supported by a swing-prone "water-rights lease
revenue" line. Both risks are honestly disclosed; this is a sound but watch-list essential-service credit.

### WATER SUPPLY (load-bearing — lead here)
- **Supply mix (OS "THE WATER SYSTEM"):** (i) **groundwater pumped from 11 deep wells** in the **Main / Intermediate
  / Upper (Canyon) San Gabriel Basins** (~20,118 gpm capacity); (ii) **surface water from the San Gabriel River**
  (treated); (iii) treated water from Upper San Gabriel Valley MWD; and (iv) **untreated State Water Project water**
  purchased from San Gabriel Valley MWD. The City's treatment plant is designed to treat **San Gabriel River
  water, imported Colorado River water, and State Water Project water**.
- **The risk (genuinely HIGH — dual exposure):**
  - The **Main San Gabriel Basin is ADJUDICATED** (Watermaster jurisdiction over pumping rights and surface
    diversions). Groundwater levels fall in dry years; pumping is rights-constrained, not freely expandable.
  - The imported supplement carries the **Colorado River (Lower Basin shortage / post-2026 interim guidelines)
    + State Water Project (Bay-Delta) allocation-cut** exposure shared across SoCal.
  - This is the cache's "supply_risk HIGH" flag, and it is **correctly HIGH** here (unlike Riverside, Azusa does
    not have a large independent owned-groundwater cushion — it is both adjudicated-basin AND import-dependent).
- **Mitigant:** cost-recovery rate-setting and a diversified four-source portfolio (groundwater + local surface
  + two import channels), so no single supply failure is total — but the floor under deliverable volume is set
  by basin adjudication + import allocations.

### Prop 218
- Water rates are **Prop 218 fees/charges** (majority-protest, cost-of-service cap). Standard CA friction.
