## (a) Verdict, thesis, and resolved issuer

**Verdict: BUY (HOLD-quality essential-service revenue credit; no honesty red flag).**

**Resolved issuer (CONFIRMED from the EMMA Official Statement we hold on file):** The water-track tag was
right. The obligor is the **Eastern Municipal Water District (EMWD)**, a Riverside County retail/wholesale
water and wastewater utility. The bonds are issued by a conduit — the **Eastern Municipal Water District
Financing Authority** — but are payable from **Net Revenues of EMWD's Water and Sewer System** via an
Installment Purchase Agreement (dated Sept. 1, 2016) between the District and the Authority. So the credit you
are underwriting is EMWD's system, not the shell Authority (the Authority "has no taxing power" and pledges
nothing of its own).

**Thesis.** This is exactly the kind of large, diversified, essential-service retail water/sewer revenue
credit the Tulare lesson tells us to underwrite on operations, not on a (non-existent) tax pledge. It clears
every operating gate: senior/parity debt-service coverage of ~2.9x (most recent year on file) vs a 1.10x rate
covenant; a deeply diversified customer base (146,225 domestic billing accounts; top-10 customers only 8.78%
of water-sales revenue, and the "largest customer" is a municipal wholesale buyer, City of Perris — NOT a
single private industrial credit); a rate covenant the District self-tightens to 115% in its own indenture;
and strong standalone ratings (S&P AA / Moody's Aa3 / Fitch AA+) with **no bond insurance** — the wrap is
absent because the underlying doesn't need one. The one genuine, structural risk is **water supply**: EMWD
imports ~48% of total supply (≈76% of its potable supply) entirely from MWD, sourced from the
Colorado River Aqueduct and the State Water Project — both under multi-year shortage/cut pressure.

**Why BUY and not just HOLD:** the supply risk is real but (i) honestly and extensively disclosed, (ii) a
shared regional risk priced into the whole SoCal water complex rather than an EMWD-specific defect, and (iii)
buffered by a pass-through rate mechanism and ~52% local supply (groundwater + desalter + 36% recycled). On
the SignalOS honesty test, the marketed takeaway ("AA essential-service water revenue, diversified") does
**not** diverge from the document data. No liar to exclude here.

## (b) Security — the revenue pledge (what actually backs the bond)

- **Pledge:** Special limited obligation of the Authority payable SOLELY from "Authority Revenues" =
  Installment Payments made by EMWD, which EMWD pays SOLELY from **Net Revenues of its Water and Sewer System**
  (gross water/sewer revenues less operation & maintenance costs and senior parity obligations). *(OS cover;
  "Installment Payments Payable From Net Revenues.")*
- **No tax pledge — confirmed (Tulare lesson satisfied):** The OS states in capital letters that neither the
  Authority nor the District has levied or pledged any form of taxation, and the Authority has no taxing power.
  This is a pure net-revenue bond. Underwrite DSCR / customers / supply / Prop 218 — not an AV/tax base.
- **Lien position:** The Installment Payments securing the 2016B Bonds are **subordinate** to ~$198.4M of
  senior Parity Obligations and **on parity** with ~$577.1M of other subordinate obligations (plus two
  interest-rate swaps), per the cover page. The District may issue additional senior parity debt — a
  structural subordination to watch, though coverage is computed all-in and remains strong (below).
- **No debt service reserve fund** for the 2016B Bonds, and **no acceleration** on default (bondholder remedy
  is limited). Both are disclosed; neither is unusual for a high-grade essential-service issuer.
- **Insurance: NONE.** Bonds were sold uninsured on the strength of the underlying ratings (see (g)).

## (c) System and customers (the Tulare concentration test)

- **System type:** Combined **water + wastewater (sewer) retail utility** with a wholesale water component and
  a large recycled-water operation. Retail service area covers Moreno Valley, Murrieta, Temecula and
  unincorporated Riverside County communities; wholesale water sold to the cities of Hemet, Perris, San
  Jacinto, Western MWD, LHMWD, Nuevo Mutual and DWR.
- **Customer diversification — CONFIRMED diversified retail, not single-industrial:**
  - **146,225 domestic active billing accounts** + 127 agricultural/irrigation accounts (FY2016).
  - **Ten largest customers (retail + wholesale) = ~8.78% of total water-sales revenue (FY2016).**
  - The largest individual customers are **public/municipal buyers** — City of Perris, Moreno Valley School
    District, Western MWD, Valley-Wide Recreation Park, City of Moreno Valley, Riverside County EDA — i.e.,
    governmental, recurring, essential demand. **There is no single private industrial customer whose loss
    would impair debt service.** This is the opposite of the Tulare single-industrial-offtaker failure mode.
- **Service-area growth:** Riverside County inland-empire footprint with long-run population/connection growth
  — supportive of the revenue base, and the reason the District runs a large CIP funded partly by connection
  fees (reported as volatile "non-operating" revenue — see DSCR caveat).

## (d) DSCR / rate covenant / WATER SUPPLY / Prop 218

### Debt-service coverage (DSCR)
From the OS historical operating-results table (Appendix A, Table 19):

| Metric (most recent year, FY2016 unaudited) | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **Senior / Parity Obligation debt-service coverage** | **~2.9x** |
| **All-in debt-service coverage** (total debt service incl. subordinate) | **~2.9x** |
| Prior years (FY2012–FY2015) parity coverage range | ~1.8x – 2.5x |
| Prior years all-in coverage range | ~1.8x – 2.3x |

- Coverage strengthened materially into FY2016 (a refunding year that lowered debt service) and sits well
  above covenant. Even the trough years (~1.8x all-in) cleared covenant.
- **DSCR caveat (honest):** EMWD's reported coverage includes connection/capacity fees and standby charges,
  which the OS itself flags as the most volatile, economy-driven revenue line. A development slowdown compresses
  coverage toward the rate-covenant floor faster than the headline 2.9x implies. Net-operating-revenue-only
  coverage is thinner than the all-in figure — but still designed by covenant to clear 1.10x–1.15x.

### Rate covenant
- **Master Resolution floor: 110%** — Net Water & Sewer Revenues must be ≥110% of (senior parity debt service +
  operating-reserve deposits + all subordinate debt service).
- **Installment Purchase Agreement / Indenture (the document that secures THESE bonds): 115%** — the District
  covenants to set rates expected to yield Net Revenues equal to **115%** of Debt Service each fiscal year.
- **Additional-bonds test:** senior parity long-term debt requires a 1.10x (historical) or 1.15x
  (pro-forma/projected) coverage certificate; additional subordinate parity bonds require a 115% Net-Revenues
  test. So the structure is self-limiting at roughly the covenant level even though new senior debt is allowed.

### WATER SUPPLY (the load-bearing risk — lead here)
- **Supply mix (OS, FY2016):** ~**52% local** (≈10% potable groundwater + ≈6% groundwater needing desalination
  + ≈36% recycled water) and ~**48% imported from MWD** (≈76% of *potable* supply). MWD's sole sources are the
  **Colorado River (via the Colorado River Aqueduct)** and the **State Water Project (via the California
  Aqueduct / Bay-Delta)**.
- **The risk:** EMWD's potable supply is structurally dependent on two stressed regional systems:
  - **Lower Colorado River shortage** — sustained Lake Mead/Powell drawdown and the post-2026 interim-guideline
    reductions raise the odds of MWD allocation cuts flowing through to member agencies including EMWD.
  - **State Water Project cuts** — Bay-Delta regulatory and hydrologic constraints have repeatedly cut SWP
    allocations; the OS explicitly discusses Bay-Delta, drought emergency orders, and the District's Water
    Shortage Contingency Plan (it was operating at Stage 3C of its WSCP during the drought).
  - **Local groundwater is constrained, not a free buffer:** the San Jacinto Basin is **partially adjudicated
    (2013 stipulated judgment) and in overdraft**, managed by a Watermaster with a defined safe yield; the
    **Soboba Settlement Act (2008)** imposes tribal groundwater-level obligations; **SGMA** governs the
    non-adjudicated portion. The District has voluntarily shut a well (No. 59) over a supply issue. So local
    groundwater cannot simply be ramped to offset an imported-water cut.
- **Mitigants (disclosed, real):** a pass-through rate policy (MWD cost increases are passed to customers,
  protecting *revenue* even when *supply cost* rises); heavy investment in recycled water (~36% of total
  supply) and desalination; and a self-certification that the District could withstand three additional severe
  drought years (its mandatory conservation target was eliminated on that basis). Net: supply is a genuine
  long-run credit pressure on *cost and deliverable volume*, but the revenue/coverage mechanism is built to
  absorb cost shocks. The bond risk is volume/curtailment + rate-fatigue, not near-term default.

### Prop 218 / Prop 26
- Water and sewer **rates and charges are "fees/charges" subject to Proposition 218** (majority-protest process;
  rates cannot exceed cost of service). The District's projected ~4.5%/yr water and ~5%/yr sewer increases are
  all subject to 218 notice/hearing/protest — "no assurance" they pass. This is the standard CA muni rate-setting
  friction, not a special weakness.
- **Standby charges are assessments under Prop 218.** Certain replenishment/regulatory fees may fall under
  **Prop 26**. The 218 majority-protest mechanism is the practical channel by which supply-cost-driven rate
  increases could be slowed — the intersection of supply risk and 218 is the real second-order risk (rate
  fatigue if MWD costs spike and customers protest).

  round coupon and tax-exempt opinion rule out the federally-taxable-muni trap (no odd 3-decimal near-par

- **Yield analytics (computed from verified structure; settle ~2026-06-22, clean px 99.787):**
  - **Yield to maturity (2035-07-01): ~4.03% gross.**
  - **Callable at par 2026-07-01.** Because the bond trades *below* par, the par call is NOT the worst case —
    a call returns par (>price), which is better for the holder. So **yield-to-worst = yield-to-maturity ≈ 4.03%.**
  - The mechanical "yield to the 2026-07-01 call" (~12.8%) is an **11-day artifact** of recovering the ~0.21pt
    discount to par over a tiny horizon — it is NOT an investable yield and must not be quoted as such.
- **Taxable-equivalent yield:** at the CA top combined bracket (≈2.01x gross-up), TEY ≈ **8.1%** on ~4.03%
  gross. **Label discipline:** this is a *TEY gross-up* of the gross YTM, not a net-of-fee after-tax realized
  yield. Net-of-fee TEY is modestly lower after the ~1–3 bps/yr liquidity cost. The brief's "~8.1% after-tax
  TEY" reconciles to the gross-up TEY, confirmed.

## (g) Risks (lead with supply, then concentration/structure)

1. **WATER SUPPLY (primary, structural):** ~76% of potable supply imported from MWD = Colorado River + SWP
   exposure. Lower-Colorado shortage + post-2026 interim-guideline cuts + Bay-Delta/SWP allocation cuts could
   curtail deliverable volume and raise wholesale water cost. Local groundwater is adjudicated/overdrafted
   (San Jacinto Basin, Soboba Settlement, SGMA) and cannot fully backfill. **This is the risk to monitor.**
   *Mitigant:* MWD-cost pass-through rate policy + 36% recycled + desalter + multi-year drought self-certification.
2. **Customer concentration: LOW / NOT A RISK (confirmed).** Top-10 = 8.78% of water-sales revenue; 146,225
   accounts; largest buyers are municipal/governmental. No single-industrial-offtaker failure mode (Tulare
   lesson cleared).
3. **Structural subordination + open additional-bonds capacity:** these bonds are subordinate to ~$198M senior
   parity debt and the District may issue more senior parity debt; the additional-bonds test only requires
   ~1.10–1.15x coverage. New senior leverage could dilute the cushion over time.
4. **No reserve fund + no acceleration:** limited bondholder protection if a stress event occurs (offset by the
   high-grade ratings and essential-service revenue stability).
5. **Rate-setting friction (Prop 218) intersecting supply-cost inflation:** if MWD costs spike, the District
   must push large 218-protestable rate increases; sustained rate fatigue is the channel by which supply cost
   could eventually pressure coverage. Connection/capacity-fee volatility (a development-cycle revenue line in
   the coverage calc) is a related cyclicality.
6. **Honesty assessment: CLEAN.** Marketed takeaway does not diverge from the OS data; supply risk is
   extensively and accurately disclosed; uninsured AA-category credit needs no wrap (anti-masking — the
   *absence* of insurance here is a positive signal, consistent with rating-conditional wrap economics, not a
   hidden defect). Nothing to exclude.
