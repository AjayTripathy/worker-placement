## (a) Verdict, thesis, resolved issuer

**RESOLVED ISSUER (from the Official Statement, not a database guess):**
**Santa Fe Springs Water Utility Authority** (the "Authority"), a joint-powers-style financing
authority whose obligor is the **City of Santa Fe Springs** water utility enterprise. The Authority
leases the water Enterprise from the City; the bonds are **special obligations payable solely from
"Net Revenues" of the water utility Enterprise** (gross operating revenue minus O&M). This is a
single-system **retail water utility** in southeastern Los Angeles County — NOT a groundwater
replenishment/assessment district (the underwriting tool's auto-guess of "groundwater_mgmt" was a
mislabel; see (g)).

- $6,890,000 total 2013 Water Revenue Bonds; base CUSIP 802199; this CUSIP =
  **$2,460,000 4.000% Term Bond due May 1, 2043** (verified against the OS inside cover).
- Indenture dated May 1, 2013; Trustee U.S. Bank N.A.; Bond Counsel Jones Hall (tax-exempt, fed + CA).
- At issuance: **Underlying "A" (S&P), Insured "AA-" (AGM wrap).**

**VERDICT: BUY / HOLD (qualified). Lean HOLD-to-add on weakness; not a REJECT.**

**Thesis.** This is a clean, diversified essential-service retail water credit, insured by a live
investment-grade monoline (AGM, now AA/A1). It passes the Tulare test on the dimension that matters:
**there is no dominant single customer.** Top-10 users = 27% of revenue and the largest single user
(a textile dyer) is only 5.5% — a diversified commercial/industrial base, the opposite of
Tulare-style single-obligor dairy dependency. The rate covenant is a strong 120% of debt service on
Net Revenues, and historical coverage ran 1.85x–5.38x. The bond trades at a discount (98.88), so the
par-call (continuous since 2023) creates no premium-call risk and yield-to-worst = yield-to-maturity
~4.09%, ≈8.2% TEY for a CA top-bracket holder.

**Why not an unqualified BUY:** (1) the only audited financials I could verify in-session are the
FY2011-12 issuance snapshot — EMMA's continuing-disclosure and trade-tape endpoints were rate-limited
this session, so **current DSCR and recent audit timeliness are UNVERIFIABLE (not "clean")**; (2)
~46% of supply is **imported MWD water (Colorado River + State Water Project)**, a real
drought/allocation tail; (3) the name is **illiquid** (no two-sided market on IBKR; close-only).

commercial/industrial; the customer base is correspondingly commercial/industrial-heavy, but
*diversified across many obligors*, not concentrated in one.

**Finding — CLEAN on the dimension that matters.** No single customer exceeds ~5.5% of revenue.
Top-10 at 27% is moderately concentrated *as a group*, but spread across textile, carpet, chemicals,
beverage, real-estate, and laundry — a heterogeneous industrial mix with no common cyclical driver
and no single counterparty whose departure would impair coverage. This is the **opposite of the
Tulare-dairy pattern** (one industrial/ag obligor dominating a thin revenue base). The OS does flag
industrial-use sensitivity as a risk factor (demand is ~82% consumption-based for a typical
residence, so revenue is volume-elastic), which is a real but ordinary water-utility risk.

**DSCR (historical, audited — Table 10):**

| FY | Net Revenues | Total Debt Service | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2007-08 | $1,648,012 | $538,398 | 3.06x |
| 2008-09 | $992,444 | $538,498 | 1.85x |
| 2009-10 | $1,259,411 | $542,923 | 2.32x |
| 2010-11 | $1,301,826 | $541,910 | 2.41x |
| 2011-12 | $2,904,414 | $540,260 | **5.38x** |

- Coverage was comfortably above the 1.20x covenant every year, with a soft trough of 1.85x in the
  GFE/drought year FY2008-09. The FY2011-12 jump reflects rate increases (3/2010, 3/2011) and the
  City moving to full-price internal water consumption.
- **Caveat (load-bearing):** these are the *old* (2003/2005 installment) debt-service figures. The
  2013 bonds layered on additional debt service; the OS projection (Table 11, FY2012-13→2016-17)
  shows projected Net Revenues of ~$2.18M–$2.66M against the new structure. I could **not** verify
  post-2013 actual coverage this session (EMMA continuing-disclosure endpoint blocked). Treat current
  DSCR as **UNVERIFIABLE**, not assumed-clean. The 120% covenant and AGM wrap are the backstops.

**Water supply (FY2011-12 mix):**
- **~46.3% imported MWD water (2,963 AF)** purchased via Central Basin MWD (CBMWD) — sourced from the
  **Colorado River Aqueduct and the State Water Project**. This is the genuine supply tail:
  Lower-Basin Colorado River shortage and post-2026 interim-guideline cuts, plus SWP allocation
  variability. No allocation limit was anticipated at issuance, but this is the channel to watch.
- **~21% Central Basin adjudicated groundwater (1,342 AF)** — the City holds an **adjudicated
  entitlement of 4,036 AF** in the Central Basin (watermaster = CA DWR / WRD). Adjudicated; the City
  was pumping below its right in FY2011-12 due to a single-well outage and was building a new 2,000-
  GPM well (online ~Oct 2013) to restore production.
- Remainder: recycled/reclaimed water from CBMWD + the Water Quality Protection Project; emergency
  interties with Cerritos and Downey.
- **Supply grade: MODERATE (not "high").** The imported leg carries real Colorado-River exposure, but
  the credit is *diversified across three sources* with an adjudicated groundwater entitlement and
  emergency interties — materially better than an import-only or single-source system.

**Prop 218 / rate-raising power:** Water charges are **property-related fees subject to Article XIIID
(Prop 218) majority-protest** procedure (notice + hearing; a majority written protest blocks an
increase). Historically the City has raised rates (2010, 2011) without obstruction. Prop 26 may also
bear on certain charges. The constraint is ordinary for a CA retail water system and has not impeded
covenant compliance. The OS flags that, because ~82% of the residential charge is consumption-based,
rate increases can suppress demand — the classic water-utility elasticity tension.

1. **Current DSCR UNVERIFIABLE.** Only the FY2011-12 issuance-era audit was readable; EMMA continuing
   disclosure was rate-limited. Post-2013 coverage against the larger debt stack is not independently
   confirmed. *Mitigant:* 1.20x covenant + funded DSRF + AGM wrap. *Action:* pull the latest ACFR /
   EMMA annual filing before adding materially.
2. **Imported-water / Colorado-River tail.** ~46% of supply is MWD (Colorado River + SWP). Lower-Basin
   shortage and post-2026 interim-guideline cuts raise cost and (tail) availability risk; cost
   increases pass through to rates, pressuring demand. *Mitigant:* adjudicated groundwater entitlement
   (4,036 AF) + recycled water + interties diversify the source mix.
3. **Demand elasticity / Prop 218.** ~82% consumption-based residential charge means conservation or
   recession cuts revenue; rate fixes require Prop 218 majority-protest clearance. Historically
   navigated, but it caps the speed of covenant repair.
4. **Liquidity.** No two-sided market; close-only on IBKR; small float. Exit at the screen price is
   not assured. Size HTM.
5. **Underwriting-tool mislabels (corrected here, flagged so the cache isn't trusted blindly):**
   `system_type` was auto-tagged "groundwater_mgmt" (it is a **retail water utility**), `rate_covenant`
   was recorded **1.0** (true covenant **1.20x**), and supply was graded "high / SGMA-critical." The
   Central Basin is an **adjudicated** basin — **exempt from SGMA** — so the "critically-overdrafted
   SGMA basin" framing does not apply; the real supply risk is the **imported MWD** leg, graded
   MODERATE. These do not change the BUY/HOLD verdict but the raw tool flags should not be cited.
6. **AGM wrap dependency.** Insured rating relies on AGM (now ~AA/A1, materially stronger than the
   2013 "AA-" snapshot). Underlying credit "A" stands on its own; wrap is incremental, not load-bearing
   to investment grade. (Per the rating-conditional wrap-compression mechanism, an "A" underlying gets
   modest, not decisive, spread benefit from the AGM wrap.)

### Re-adjudication (supply authority + coverage refresh) — 2026-06-21

**Verdict: BUY → HOLD (downgrade).** First-principles supply verification (DWR 2020 UWMP Table 6-8)
shows the actual portfolio is **85% purchased/imported water from MWD (Colorado River + SWP blend) +
15% recycled** — there is essentially no local supply. The original DD's "clean, diversified" framing
was wrong: this credit is heavily **import-dependent and therefore correlates with the very SoCal
Colorado/SWP water-supply tail the utility sleeve was meant to diversify away from.** Combined with the
29% single-customer concentration, that makes it a **poor diversifier** for this book even though it
remains money-good.

**Not a REJECT:** the FY2025 −6.68x continuing-disclosure coverage is a one-time artifact (the City's
billing system failed Aug 2024, understating revenue — the series is 4.84/9.14/6.47/7.92x in FY2021-24),
the bond is **AGM-insured (AA-)** over an **underlying "A"**, and the rate covenant is met historically.
**HOLD:** keep if held (insured, money-good), do not add, and do not count it toward diversification —
prefer EBMUD (62% own Mokelumne) or the irrigation districts (own senior Sierra rights) as the
genuine non-property-tax diversifiers. Re-confirm on the audited FY2025 (billing normalized) before any
change.
