## One line
A directed separately‑managed‑account strategy that earns a structural edge in California municipals by **(1) buying verified, agency‑unrated insulated credits the index won't hold, and (2) providing liquidity — being the standing bid for forced odd‑lot sellers — rather than paying the spread.** Tax‑efficient income with a built‑in hedge against a tech/AI‑driven state‑revenue shock.

## The opportunity
California top‑bracket taxpayers want tax‑exempt income, but the standard products leave edge on the table:
- **Muni ETFs / index SMAs hold rated, liquid, 5%‑coupon paper** — and they hold the **State‑GO / appropriation / lease** credits most exposed to a capital‑gains‑tax collapse in an AI/tech drawdown.
- **Managed muni SMAs (e.g., Parametric) explicitly won't buy deep‑discount or unrated paper** — exactly where the after‑tax yield and the liquidity premium live ("we don't buy deep discount paper," verbatim, from a recent desk note).

That self‑imposed exclusion is the opening. A buyer who **verifies the credit itself** (substituting independent diligence for the missing agency rating) and **provides rather than consumes liquidity** captures a premium the standard products structurally cannot.

## Why liquidity provision is the real story (and why it wants scale)
The illiquidity that excludes other buyers is the **source of the return, not an obstacle** — *if you provide the liquidity instead of paying for it.* A forced seller of a $40k odd‑lot insulated GO has almost no natural buyer; the patient standing bid **sets the price and collects the spread.** This is a **flow business**: returns scale with how much seller flow you absorb, so a bigger book — standing as the bid across hundreds of names and participating in bid‑wanteds (BWICs) — captures far more of the premium than a single account. **It also compounds the hedge:** in an AI‑driven sell‑off, the strategy is the standing bid *while others are forced sellers, in the assets that keep paying.*

## Honest caveats (lead with these — a sophisticated MFO rewards candor)
1. **Capacity is capped (~$20–50M).** This is a boutique, not a scalable fund; sizing past the ceiling kills the edge.
2. **The edge is partly a thesis, not yet a track record.** The ~100–150 bps and the spread‑capture are *modeled*; a $1M proof‑of‑concept (running now) is the honest first step before scaling client capital.
3. **Execution access is the gating dependency.** Without resting‑bid + BWIC access, the liquidity‑provision edge doesn't exist — this is the single thing [MFO] must confirm it can provide.
4. **AI‑insulation rates credit, not price.** In a pure rate shock all munis fall together; the hedge is about *which pledges keep paying* in a tech‑revenue collapse.
5. **Deployment is slow** (provide‑don't‑chase = fill only when sellers appear). Patience is the price of the premium.
6. **Fees are thin.** The value to [MFO] is client differentiation and retention (a strategy clients can't get from Parametric/an ETF), not fee revenue.
