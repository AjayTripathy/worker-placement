# California Municipal Bond ETF — Tearsheet

*AI-crash-insulated, primary-source-verified California municipal bonds for the high-bracket California investor*

Prepared 2026-06-09 · 24 holdings · $1,000,000 par · all issuers independently verified to EMMA

---

## 1. Executive summary

This is a 24-bond, $1,000,000-par basket of high-grade California municipal bonds, built to do one specific job: deliver a high tax-equivalent yield to a top-bracket California investor **while staying insulated from a sharp AI/tech-driven equity drawdown.**

The portfolio is a **barbell**. One end is a liquid, AA-level core of **University of California system revenue bonds** (17 of 24 bonds, $695,000 par) — the deepest, most-traded names in the California higher-ed complex. The other end is a smaller, deliberately **insulated sleeve** (7 bonds, $305,000 par) of California school-district and community-college general-obligation bonds plus essential-service wastewater revenue bonds, whose repayment is tied to local property-tax levies and ratepayer charges rather than to the state's volatile income-tax receipts.

On a par-weighted basis the basket yields **3.82% to worst**, which grosses up to a **7.65% tax-equivalent yield** for a California top-bracket investor (the 2.01× federal-plus-state gross-up, adjusted for the one bond that trips the de-minimis rule). Every coupon, maturity, price, and yield in this document was re-checked against MSRB/EMMA primary trade and security records by two independent reviewers — a process that **caught and corrected** several errors in an earlier draft, described in Section 5. We treat that as a feature, not a footnote.

The pitch in one line: **AA-level California munis, screened so that the cash flows that pay you are the ones least exposed to an AI-led collapse in capital-gains tax revenue, verified bond-by-bond to the source, at roughly a 7.65% taxable-equivalent yield.**

## 2. Methodology — two orthogonal screens

Each bond had to pass two independent tests. They are deliberately unrelated to each other: one is about *what kind of shock could impair the cash flow*, the other is about *whether the cash flow is creditworthy in the first place.*

### (a) AI-crash insulation — the capital-gains channel

California's state budget is unusually dependent on personal income tax, and within that, on **capital-gains realizations from a relatively small number of high earners.** That revenue is highly cyclical: in the dot-com unwind, state capital-gains-driven revenue fell on the order of 70%, and California **State general-obligation** credit quality moved down several notches relative to more insulated municipal sectors. A severe AI/tech equity drawdown would hit the same channel.

The insulation screen asks: *if that channel breaks, does this bond's repayment source break with it?* Bonds are scored on how far their pledged revenue sits from state capital-gains receipts:

- **School-district and community-college GO bonds (insulation ~99):** repaid from a dedicated **ad-valorem property-tax levy** on assessed value within the district. Property assessments are slow-moving and statutorily protected; they do not fall when capital-gains realizations evaporate. These are the most insulated holdings.
- **Essential-service wastewater revenue (insulation 100):** repaid from **mandatory ratepayer charges** for a service households cannot stop using. Demand is inelastic and unrelated to equity markets.
- **University of California system revenue (insulation ~87–93):** repaid from UC's own enterprise revenues — tuition, housing, facilities, and broad institutional pledges — **not** from the state general fund's tax receipts. Highly insulated, though one notch below the property-tax-backed names because UC enrollment and auxiliary revenues carry some indirect economic sensitivity.

Notably, the basket holds **zero State of California general-obligation bonds** — the single sector most exposed to the capital-gains channel. Par-weighted insulation score: **92 out of 100.**

### (b) Creditworthiness — verified to primary sources

The second screen is conventional credit quality, but with a SignalOS twist: rather than trusting a vendor's pre-chewed rating or a label on a screen, **each bond's security type and pledge were confirmed against the issuer's official EMMA security record**, and its market yield against EMMA's actual trade history. This matters because the *label* on a bond ("UC revenue") can hide a materially narrower pledge underneath (a single-project lease-revenue structure) — which changes both the credit and the yield it should command. Section 5 documents where this re-keying changed our numbers.

Par-weighted creditworthiness score: **82 out of 100.** The strongest credits are the property-tax-backed school and college GOs (90); the UC general-revenue bonds score 82; the narrower UC limited-project revenue bonds score 74; the wastewater revenue bonds 80.

## 3. Yield

| Metric | Par-weighted |
|---|---|
| **Yield to worst (YTW)** | **3.82%** |
| **Tax-equivalent yield (TEY), after-tax, de-minimis-adjusted** | **7.65%** |
| Yield to maturity (YTM, "if not called") | ~4.2% |

**How to read this.** Most of the basket is **5% premium coupons that are callable around 2027–2035.** The honest yield to quote is therefore the **yield to worst** — the return you earn if the issuer calls the bond at its first opportunity, which a premium 5% bond generally will. That is the **3.82% YTW / 7.65% TEY** headline.

The **YTM ("if not called") line sits above it**: if rates rise enough that issuers leave the bonds outstanding to maturity, the realized yield is higher (par-weighted YTM ~4.2%, with individual premium bonds showing TEYs in the high-8s to 9s if held to their 2042–2045 maturities). We lead with the lower, more conservative YTW number so the quoted yield is the one you are most likely to actually receive.

The **7.65% TEY** is the after-tax, de-minimis-adjusted figure: it applies the 2.01× California-top-bracket gross-up to the tax-exempt coupon, then docks the one bond (UC LPRB 91412GXY6, a 4.131% deep discount) whose discount is large enough to be taxed as ordinary income under the de-minimis rule. One bond in the basket breaches that threshold; the rest do not.

## 5. Verification & corrections — what was checked, and what we fixed

This basket was not assembled and shipped. After the first draft, it was handed to two independent reviewers — a quantitative-analyst pass and a valuation-review pass — who re-derived every figure from primary sources: **MSRB/EMMA per-CUSIP yield-to-worst, official issue descriptions, and security/pledge records.** They were instructed to find errors, not to confirm the thesis.

They found three classes of problem, all now corrected:

1. **Six UC bonds were mislabeled "General Revenue" when their security records show a narrower Limited Project Revenue pledge.** Re-keying them GRB → LPRB changed their creditworthiness input from **82 down to 74**, because a limited-project lease-revenue pledge is materially narrower than UC's broad general-revenue pledge. These six bonds now sit correctly in the LPRB group in Section 4, and the basket's par-weighted credit score reflects the lower input.

2. **A West Kern Certificate of Participation was dropped.** A COP is an appropriation-backed lease obligation, not a true revenue or GO pledge; it did not meet the insulation/credit bar and was removed.

3. **Two bonds carrying stale price marks were dropped.** Their marks could not be reconciled to recent EMMA trade activity, so we removed them rather than carry a yield we could not stand behind.

The headline numbers in this tearsheet — 3.82% YTW, 7.65% TEY, insulation 92, credit 82 — are the **post-correction** figures. The `all_issuers_verified` flag on the final basket is true; one bond (91412GXY6) carries a flagged de-minimis breach, which is reflected in the after-tax TEY rather than ignored.

## 6. What could make this wrong

We lead with the risks. None of these is hidden in the methodology; each is a live way the thesis could disappoint.

- **Rate-regime / duration risk — the biggest one.** This is a 2035–2045 ladder of long-dated bonds. The insulation screen protects the *credit* — the bonds' ability to keep paying — in an AI crash. It does **not** protect the *price* if interest rates rise. In a rising-rate regime these bonds will mark down like any long municipal, and the call-protected premiums extend in duration exactly when you'd least want them to. **Credit insulation is not price insulation.**

- **Single-credit UC concentration.** 17 of 24 bonds and ~70% of par are University of California system revenue. That is one obligor's enterprise credit. The UC names are the most liquid in the basket, but a UC-specific shock (enrollment, state-support politics, a system-wide event) hits the majority of the portfolio at once.

- **Thin liquidity in the sleeve.** Several insulated-sleeve and longer UC names trade rarely — some show only single-digit or low-double-digit EMMA trade counts (e.g., 5, 14, 36, 45 trades). Quoted YTWs on thin names are less firm, and exit in size could move the price against you.

- **LPRB narrower pledge.** The six limited-project revenue bonds are backed by specific project lease revenues, a narrower promise than UC's general revenue pledge. We've credited them accordingly (74), but in stress this is the part of the UC allocation with the least cushion.

- **De-minimis tax on the deep discount.** The 4.131% UC LPRB (91412GXY6) trades far enough below par to breach the de-minimis threshold, meaning its discount is taxed as ordinary income rather than capital gain. We've docked the after-tax TEY for it, but an investor in a different bracket should re-run that one bond's after-tax math themselves.

- **An appropriations clause still in the offering documents.** On at least one structure, an appropriations-in-pledge clause remains open in the official statement and has not been fully resolved. We flag it as pending diligence rather than treat it as clean; it is a reason to confirm the exact pledge language before sizing up.
