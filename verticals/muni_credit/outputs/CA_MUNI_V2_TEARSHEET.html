Executive summary

The problem. A high-bracket Californian — especially in tech — is already heavily exposed to the tech/AI cycle through salary, equity comp (RSUs/options), and portfolio. Yet the obvious tax shelter for their bond allocation, California munis, is at the state level the most tech-levered government revenue base in the country (capital-gains / stock-option income tax). Buy a generic CA muni fund and you may be doubling down on the very risk you're trying to diversify away from.

The product. A California tax-exempt income strategy selected on two independent, auditable screens: (1) creditworthiness, verified bond-by-bond against primary sources, and (2) tech/AI income-channel insulation, measuring exposure across nine distinct transmission channels at the bond-security level. The result: double-tax-exempt income (~4.4% YTW / 8.9% taxable-equivalent) from credits whose creditworthiness does not deteriorate when tech/AI wealth evaporates.

Why it's different — and defensible. No generic CA muni ETF offers income-channel diversification. Every score is transparent and every exclusion is auditable (see worked examples). And we are deliberately precise about what this is not: not a price hedge, not alpha, not a market forecast — just tax-exempt income, verified credit quality, and a diversification axis no one else screens for.

The pitch — two independent screens

Creditworthiness screen (verified diligence). Each bond's pledged security is verified against primary sources and the full trail is retained: the claim (the security/lien), how it was checked, the authority that confirms it (EMMA official statements, CA Gov Code, CDIAC default-draw, rating actions), and the verdict (Verified / Verified-wrap / Unverifiable). The diligence is auditable, not a black box — and Unverifiable ≠ clean: a bond whose security we have not actually read is excluded, not assumed good.

Tech/AI insulation screen (multi-channel). A tech/AI bust reaches California muni credit through nine distinct channels, not one. We score each separately and at the bond-security level (the pledged revenue), not the issuer's general economy — so a property-tax GO levy scores insulated even if the issuer's budget feels pension or sales-tax pressure. The composite score is insulation across all nine channels.

That is the defensible meaning of “insulated.” Note the budget-vs-security distinction: the pension channel can squeeze a school district's operating budget while its GO debt-service levy stays untouched — we score the pledge, not the issuer's general economy.

Creditworthiness Check (verified diligence)

Every credit claim is verified against a primary authority and documented end-to-end: the claim, how it was checked, the authority that confirms it, and the verdict (Verified / Verified-wrap / Unverifiable). “CORE” dimensions drive the v2 creditworthiness score; “EXTENDED” are sector-specific diligence layers from the detector library, applied where the pledge warrants.

What this IS — and is NOT

IS
  • Tax-exempt (fed+CA) income, primary-source credit-vetted
  • Income-channel diversification for tech-comp-concentrated CA holders — credit insulated from the tech/AI cycle across nine channels
  • Two transparent, auditable, orthogonal scores
IS NOT
  • A price hedge / return anti-correlation (that's a duration bet that inverted −30% in 2022)
  • Alpha — not return-backtested vs a CA muni ETF
  • A validated predictive rating — scores are STRUCTURAL classifications + diligence verdicts for selection, not forecasts
Honest limits. (1) 11 holdings, not 20 — the dual-axis gate clears only 11 of the 20-name research universe; a diversified 20 needs a broader CA screen (same archetypes, more names). (2) Long duration (10.1y to-worst) — production targets intermediate ~4–7y via maturity selection; this is income + credit quality, NOT crash protection. (3) Scores are structural, not validated betas. (4) Single-state (CA) concentration; some sectors trade thinly.
