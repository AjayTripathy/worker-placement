The Opportunity

The standard products leave the edge on the table.

California top-bracket taxpayers want tax-exempt income. But every off-the-shelf vehicle excludes itself, by policy, from exactly the paper where the after-tax yield and the liquidity premium live.

Muni ETFs / index SMAs

Hold rated, liquid, 5%-coupon paper — and, critically, they hold the State-GO / appropriation / lease credits most exposed to a capital-gains-tax collapse in an AI/tech drawdown. The index buys exactly the channel a California UHNW balance sheet should want to hedge.

Managed muni SMAs (e.g. Parametric)

Explicitly won't buy deep-discount or unrated paper. “We don't buy deep discount paper,” verbatim, from a recent desk note. That self-imposed exclusion is a hard house policy — and it is precisely where the after-tax yield and the liquidity premium sit.

The self-imposed exclusion is the opening

A buyer who verifies the credit itself — substituting independent, primary-source diligence for the missing agency rating — and who provides rather than consumes liquidity captures a premium the standard products structurally cannot. The gap is not a market mistake to be arbitraged away; it is a permanent feature of how the incumbents have chosen to operate.

What could make this wrong

If the MFO's own platform repeats the same “no deep-discount / no unrated” house limit as a hard rule rather than a default it can waive for a client-directed mandate, the entire edge is inaccessible through this channel and the strategy must be self-directed elsewhere. Confirming the waiver is Step 1 of the ask.

The Centerpiece

Liquidity provision is the real story — and it wants scale.

The illiquidity that excludes other buyers is the source of the return, not an obstacle — if you provide the liquidity instead of paying for it.

Be the standing bid

A forced seller of a $40k odd-lot insulated GO has almost no natural buyer. The patient standing bid sets the price and collects the spread — instead of paying it to a dealer on the way in.

It's a flow business

Returns scale with how much seller flow you absorb. A bigger book — standing as the bid across hundreds of names and participating in bid-wanteds (BWICs) — captures far more of the premium than a single account.

It compounds the hedge

In an AI-driven sell-off, the strategy is the standing bid while others are forced sellers — in the assets that keep paying. The crash that threatens the rest of the balance sheet is when this sleeve buys cheapest.

The honest distinction — what is live vs. what the proof-of-concept tests

Today the live $1M sleeve is still a patient buyer working bids — it pays a tight median round-trip spread (~0.36 pt) on a hold-to-maturity ladder. Turning that buyer into a standing bid that collects the spread requires the institutional execution access (resting bids + BWIC routing) that the MFO must supply. That is the single dependency that converts the modeled spread-capture into realized return — and it is what the proof-of-concept is designed to demonstrate.

Structure & Roles

A directed SMA — clean division of labor.

Vehicle: a directed / non-discretionary SMA. Each client holds the bonds directly, which sidesteps the redemption problem a pooled fund has with illiquid holdings.

Strategist (the engine)

  • The verification engine — primary-source diligence per CUSIP, standing in for the missing agency rating
  • The directed buy list — CUSIP, side, par, limit price, refreshed on cadence
  • Ongoing surveillance — continuing-disclosure monitoring, daily re-marks, hazard/tax screening
  • The liquidity-provision discipline — resting-bid sheets and BWIC selection

Multi-family office (the book & desk)

  • Clients — the top-bracket California taxpayers the strategy is built for
  • Custody & consolidated reporting alongside the rest of the client's assets
  • Execution access — the critical piece: posting resting bids and routing bid-wanteds; the institutional muni-desk flow a retail account simply cannot reach

The directed mechanic keeps roles clean

The strategist supplies a verified, limit-priced model and runs the surveillance; the MFO acts as executing broker and custodian, not as the discretionary manager or credit decision-maker for this sleeve. No substitution of any directed position without written authorization. That separation is what makes the deep-discount / unrated paper documentable as client-directed under the MFO's compliance.

The Caveats — Up Front, Not In Footnotes

Six limits we put on the table first.

A sophisticated MFO rewards candor — so here is what could go wrong, before the ask. Each is a boundary the strategy is already built to respect, not a risk we discovered late.

Capacity is capped (~$20–50M)

A boutique, not a scalable fund. Sizing past the ceiling kills the edge — the premium erodes the moment you become the market.

Partly thesis, not yet a track record

The ~100–150 bps and the spread-capture are modeled. The $1M proof-of-concept (running now) is the honest first step before scaling client capital.

Execution access is the gating dependency

Without resting-bid + BWIC access, the liquidity-provision edge does not exist. This is the single thing the MFO must confirm it can provide.

AI-insulation rates credit, not price

In a pure rate shock all munis fall together. The hedge is about which pledges keep paying in a tech-revenue collapse — not mark-to-market in a rate move.

Deployment is slow

Provide-don't-chase means fill only when sellers appear. Patience is the price of the premium — the build window runs weeks, not days.

Fees are thin

The value to the MFO is client differentiation and retention — a strategy clients can't get from Parametric or an ETF — not fee revenue. We don't pretend otherwise.
