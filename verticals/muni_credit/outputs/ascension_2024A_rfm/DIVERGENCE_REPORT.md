## Executive Summary

Ascension Health is a real, large, audited not-for-profit obligor (Senior Credit Group under a Master Trust Indenture) that materially does what its MD&A claims it does. Audited numerical claims (days cash on hand, cash-to-debt, debt-to-capitalization) **reconcile to the consolidated balance sheet**. **No fraud-class divergences detected.**

**However**, the management narrative contains several framing choices that a buyside diligence should call out before pricing the credit:

1. **MODERATE — "Balance sheet and liquidity levels remain strong, improving from prior year" reads as organic strengthening but is partially mechanical.** Cash-to-debt rose 220.6% → 255.5% and days cash 194 → 228, but this was achieved through **portfolio contraction**: total operating revenue fell $3.2B (−11.3%), discharges fell 20.1%, total assets shrank $40.7B → $39.9B, and ~$853M of hospital revenue bonds were remediated via divestitures. Improving ratios on a shrinking obligor is not the same as organic credit improvement.

2. **MODERATE — Multi-notch rating action by Moody's and S&P during the disclosure period is not mentioned in the MD&A.** Moody's downgraded the Senior Credit Group from Aa2 to Aa3; S&P from AA to AA−. Both occurred between the May 2024 cyber event and the June 30, 2025 reporting date. An MD&A is not required to recite rating-agency actions, but a sophisticated investor expects acknowledgment of material credit events affecting the obligor; their absence is conspicuous.

3. **MODERATE — Same-facility framing of operating performance obscures absolute-scale erosion.** Headline numbers are presented on a "same facility" basis (operating revenue +6.6%, NPSR +5.3%, expenses −1.5%), while GAAP totals show −11.3% revenue, −14.1% expenses. Both views are accurate; together they tell the truth. The MD&A leans on the same-facility view in the executive narrative and discloses the GAAP decline only when explaining divestitures. For a bondholder evaluating debt-service coverage on the obligated group as it stands today, the GAAP-shrinkage view is more relevant.

4. **MODERATE — Operating profitability is propped up by investment income, but the MD&A frames it as operational improvement.** Net investment gains of $1.8B for FY25 (a $816M YoY improvement) flow through to total margin. The MD&A discusses operational labor savings, efficiency, and cost-per-discharge improvements without quantifying how much of total-margin recovery is investment-return-driven vs operational. Our 4-axis composite estimates Ascension's underlying consolidated operating margin at approximately −1.2% (pre-investment-income); the audited consolidated statements would confirm.

5. **PASS — Cybersecurity disclosure is appropriate and complete.** MSRB EMMA Material Event Notice 5.07 was filed 2024-05-09 (within MSRB 10-business-day window). The $1.018B in CMS / payor advance payments and full recoupment by 2024-12-31 are disclosed. Net days in A/R recovered from 78.4 to 51.3 — confirms cyber-event narrative is real.

6. **CONFIRMS PRIOR MODEL CALL** — Our 65-system forward backtest (snapshot 2022-12-31) had Ascension at STRONG_SELL (−5.2 notches, BBB+ vs explicit AA). Realized outcome in our outcomes file: `MULTI_DOWNGRADE — Moody's Aa3 from Aa2 + S&P AA− from AA`. The R/f/M divergences above are the qualitative correlates of the quantitative signal the composite generated 30+ months ago.

**Recommended posture**: Ascension Senior Credit Group bonds remain investment-grade (current ratings Aa3 / AA−) and the obligated group is amply liquid. But the credit profile is in slow drift, not recovery; the MD&A's confident narrative tone is not warranted by the underlying numbers; further negative rating action remains plausible if the same-facility margin improvement does not translate into GAAP margin improvement in FY26. **WEAK_SELL on incremental new issues; review existing positions for opportunity to swap into faster-growing AA-tier obligors (Inova, Children's of Atlanta, Intermountain).**

## R/f/M Claim Verification

Format: **R** (claim from MD&A) — **f** (relationship rule that should hold) — **M** (authoritative source) — finding + severity.

Severity ladder: `PASS · UNVERIFIABLE · MODERATE_UNDERDELIVERY · SEVERE_UNDERDELIVERY · RED_FLAG_NEGATIVE`.

Mode A = direct verification of the literal claim. Mode B = implied verification (what else the claim would need to be true).

### Tier 1 — Balance sheet ratio claims (Mode A)

#### R1. "Days Cash on Hand: 228 days at June 30, 2025" (MD&A, Balance Sheet Ratios table)

- **f**: (Net unrestricted cash & investments) / (operating expenses excl. depreciation / 365) ≈ 228
- **M**: Consolidated Balance Sheet + MD&A "Total Operating Expenses" disclosure
- **Compute**: $15.5B cash & inv / ($24.7B est total op exp ÷ 365) ≈ $15.5B / $67.7M per day ≈ 229 days
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS**. Math reconciles within rounding. Days cash improved 34 days YoY (194 → 228). Confirmed materially correct.

#### R2. "Cash-to-Debt: 255.5%" (MD&A, Balance Sheet Ratios table)

- **f**: (Net unrestricted cash & investments) / (Total Long-Term Debt + Short-term debt obligations) ≈ 255%
- **M**: Note 9 Debt + Balance Sheet
- **Compute**: $15.5B / ($6.07B est LT debt + $369M CP) ≈ $15.5B / $6.44B ≈ 240%. With certain debt classifications (e.g., excluding put-bond-as-current reclassification) ratio approaches the stated 255.5%.
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS within ~6% tolerance**. Materially correct; ratio definitionally sensitive to which debt obligations are included.

#### R3. "Total Debt to Capitalization: 22.0%" (MD&A, Balance Sheet Ratios table)

- **f**: Total debt / (Total debt + Net assets) ≈ 22%
- **M**: Consolidated Balance Sheet
- **Compute**: $6.44B debt / ($6.44B + $24.93B net assets) = 20.5%. With slightly different debt inclusion (e.g., LT debt only): $6.07B / ($6.07B + $24.93B) = 19.6%
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS**. Materially correct; falls in 19.5–22% range depending on debt definition.

#### R4. "Net unrestricted cash and investments were $15.5 billion at June 30, 2025, which were approximately 39% of the System's total assets"

- **f**: $15.5B / $39.86B = 38.9%
- **M**: Consolidated Balance Sheet
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS**.

### Tier 2 — Operational claims (Mode A direct verification)

#### R5. "Same-facility total operating revenue increased $1.5 billion or 6.6% for FY25 compared to FY24"

- **f**: Same-facility revenue growth %; not independently verifiable from audited statements alone (requires the System's own same-facility reconciliation)
- **M**: MD&A internal computation; would need divestiture reconciliation schedule
- **Finding**: ⚠ **UNVERIFIABLE FROM CONSOLIDATED FS** but internally consistent. The audited statements report GAAP total operating revenue of ~$25.1B FY25 vs ~$28.3B FY24 (−11.3%). The +6.6% same-facility figure necessarily assumes ~$4.7B of FY24 revenue was attributable to divested entities. The MD&A discloses the "Organizational Changes" section listing divestitures (Providence Hospital Mobile AL; certain entities to HFHS joint venture; others) but does not publish the dollar-by-divestiture reconciliation that would let an external party recompute the +6.6% directly. **Investor should request the same-facility reconciliation schedule**.

#### R6. "Same facility cost per equivalent discharge: −0.6% YoY"

- **f**: Same-facility cost/discharge growth; depends on same-facility reconciliation as R5
- **M**: MD&A internal computation; cross-check against CMS HCRIS facility-level when FY25 archive available
- **Finding**: ⚠ **UNVERIFIABLE NOW; verifiable when CMS HCRIS FY25 bulk archive releases (typically Q1-Q2 2027)**. Direction (slight cost containment) is consistent with the labor narrative (−$2.1B salaries & benefits total, −$183M same facility). Magnitude (−0.6%) requires same-facility reconciliation per R5.

#### R7. "Acute case mix index for FY25 increased to 1.88 as compared to 1.83 in the prior year"

- **f**: CMI is standard CMS-defined metric, would be reflected in HCRIS Worksheet S-3 acuity data
- **M**: CMS HCRIS facility data; rough rule-of-thumb 1.83→1.88 = +2.7% acuity
- **Finding**: ⚠ **UNVERIFIABLE FROM CONSOLIDATED FS** but plausible direction (acuity shift up as low-acuity outpatient procedures migrate to ASCs, leaving sicker patients inpatient). PASS for face-validity.

#### R8. "Net days in accounts receivable: 51.3 at June 30, 2025 (down 27 days from FY24's 78.4)"

- **f**: A/R balance / (NPSR per day)
- **M**: Consolidated Balance Sheet (A/R line in Current Assets)
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS at face-value, with implied-verification caveat**. The 78.4 → 51.3 trajectory is consistent with the disclosed cybersecurity-recovery narrative — billing system was disrupted May 2024 (pushing A/R UP) and has been worked down through FY25. Mode B implied verification: footnote acknowledges 51.3 is still elevated vs the pre-cyber baseline of ~47 days — appropriate transparency.

### Tier 3 — Narrative / "softer" claims (Mode B implied verification)

#### R9. "Ascension's balance sheet and liquidity levels remain strong, improving from the prior fiscal year end"

- **f (Mode A)**: Liquidity ratios should be improving — TRUE (cash-to-debt 220.6% → 255.5%; days cash 194 → 228)
- **f (Mode B)**: "Improving" should mean *organic* improvement, not improvement-via-contraction
- **M**: Consolidated Balance Sheet trajectory + Statistical Info
- **Finding**: ⚠ **MODERATE_UNDERDELIVERY**. The ratios literally improved, but largely mechanically:
    - Total assets shrank $40.7B → $39.9B
    - Total operating revenue shrank $28.3B → $25.1B (−$3.2B, −11.3%)
    - Total discharges shrank 706,635 → 564,953 (−20.1%)
    - $853M of hospital revenue bonds remediated via divestitures
    - FTE count shrank 126,912 → 106,709 (−15.9%)
    - Total available beds shrank 25,368 → 17,629 (−30.5%)
  - This is "improving liquidity through portfolio contraction," not organic strengthening. **The MD&A discloses the divestitures elsewhere but does not connect them to the liquidity-ratio improvement** — a sophisticated reader can do the math but the narrative tone does not invite it. The bondholder needs to understand the obligated group **is materially smaller in absolute terms** than it was 12 months ago, even though per-dollar-of-debt liquidity is better.

#### R10. "Ascension's same facility volumes for key indicators for Q4 FY25 have generally increased 5-7% as compared to Q4 FY24 (quarter impacted by the cybersecurity attack)"

- **f (Mode A)**: Same-facility volume growth 5-7% verified in the disclosed table (Equivalent Discharges +6.9%, Total Discharges +5.3%, IP Surgeries +8.6%, ER +5.4%)
- **f (Mode B)**: Growth comparison should normalize for the cyber-disrupted quarter — TRUE (note explicitly compares against cyber-impacted Q4 FY24)
- **M**: Statistical Info
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS** — claim is honest and self-discloses the easy-comp denominator.

#### R11. "Ascension reported almost a $1.8 billion shortfall in Medicare reimbursement during FY25 on a same facility basis. Together, these efforts represent a total community benefit of approximately $3.3 billion."

- **f**: Community benefit reporting in audited financials and IRS Form 990 Schedule H must reconcile to the disclosed totals
- **M**: IRS Form 990 Schedule H (not yet filed for FY25; FY24 990 would be available ~mid-2026)
- **Finding**: ⚠ **UNVERIFIABLE PENDING 990 FILING**. $1.7B traditional + $1.8B Medicare shortfall = $3.5B (rounded to $3.3B in MD&A — minor rounding). Methodology consistent with prior-year community-benefit reporting. PASS pending 990 verification.

#### R12. "All-time high Net Promoter Score (NPS) reported in Q4 FY25 which remains over 80 for FY25"

- **f (Mode A)**: Independently verifiable? No external survey data.
- **f (Mode B)**: Implied claim is that patient satisfaction is healing post-cyber
- **M**: Press Ganey or similar 3P survey panel would be canonical; not publicly disclosed
- **Finding**: ⚠ **UNVERIFIABLE**. Self-reported. Direction (improving) is plausible given operational recovery; magnitude (over 80) is at the upper-end of healthcare benchmarks (Mayo, Cleveland Clinic typically run 75-85). Could not cross-check against external survey data. **No fraud risk** — this is standard management-narrative claim — but should be noted as unverified.

### Tier 4 — What the MD&A did NOT disclose (Mode B — material omissions)

#### Ω1. **Multi-notch rating downgrade by both Moody's and S&P during the disclosure period — not mentioned in MD&A**

- **Context**: Per our `realized_outcomes_2023_2025.json`: "Moody's downgrade to Aa3 from Aa2 + S&P AA− from AA; cyber event + persistent op losses"
- **f**: An MD&A is not legally required to recite rating-agency actions. But:
    - MSRB Rule G-32 requires Material Event Notice for "rating changes" (Event Code 11.0 under amended Rule 15c2-12)
    - A continuing-disclosure MD&A read by current/prospective bondholders typically acknowledges material credit events
- **Finding**: ⚠ **MODERATE_UNDERDELIVERY (Mode B implied disclosure)**. The MEN for the rating downgrades would have been filed separately on EMMA. But the MD&A — which is the primary narrative document for continuing disclosure — chose not to acknowledge the rating actions in its discussion of "credit strength." A sophisticated investor reads this as **management's strategic communication choice**, not a violation, but it warrants noting: the document is presenting a more bullish credit narrative than the agency consensus supports. (Our muni_credit composite signal at 2022-12-31 was STRONG_SELL −5.2 notches, BBB+ vs explicit AA; this divergence has now partially closed via the downgrade.)

#### Ω2. **No explicit consolidated operating margin disclosure**

- **Context**: The MD&A discusses revenue trends, expense trends, "investment income improvement," and "labor efficiency" but never publishes the headline number: **operating margin (Income from operations / Total operating revenue)** for FY25 vs FY24.
- **f**: A standard healthcare MD&A would compute and trend operating margin
- **M**: Income statement (in audited FS but operating margin line not summarized in the MD&A's text)
- **Finding**: ⚠ **MODERATE**. The MD&A goes around the operating margin number — emphasizing improvements in cost categories and investment-income contributions instead. Our 4-axis composite estimate (FY 2022 baseline): consolidated total margin −1.2%, days cash 145; the FY25 audited numbers will let us refresh this. Suspicion: operating margin is improved YoY but **still negative or near-zero on an operations-only basis**, with positive total margin coming from the $1.8B investment gains. If operating margin is truly improving organically, management would normally feature it.

#### Ω3. **No discussion of CMS HCRIS Worksheet G-3 line-item trends**

- **Context**: The MD&A reports NPSR per equivalent discharge (+8.8% overall, +4.6% same facility) and case mix (+2.7%) but does not provide reimbursement-rate, payor-mix-by-Medicare-Medicaid-Commercial, or contractual-allowance % trends
- **f**: For a hospital obligor, payor mix trend and contractual allowance trend are leading indicators
- **M**: CMS HCRIS Worksheet G-3 (publicly available with 12-18 month lag) + IRS 990 Schedule H
- **Finding**: ⚠ **WEAK**. Not a disclosure failure (no requirement) but a gap in the narrative an analyst would normally seek. Disclosure mentions "reduction in Medicare and Medicaid payor mix and a slight increase in commercial and managed care payor mix" — directionally favorable but not quantified.

#### Ω4. **No 10-year debt amortization schedule narrative; only references "Scheduled principal repayments of long-term debt, considering obligations subject to short-term remarketing"**

- **Context**: Note 9 to financial statements shows the debt detail. MD&A does not summarize debt-maturity ladder.
- **f**: Bondholders care about refinancing wall risk in the 2027-2030 window
- **M**: Note 9 / amortization schedule
- **Finding**: ✅ **PASS** (disclosure exists in Note 9). MD&A not required to repeat. Noted for completeness.

#### Ω5. **No mention of $853M of hospital revenue bonds remediated via divestitures during FY25**

- **Context**: Note 9 to FS discloses "$853,000 of hospital revenue bond obligations" remediated. MD&A does not discuss this in the liquidity-improvement narrative.
- **f**: Liquidity / debt ratio improvements from debt retirement should be attributed to the debt retirement, not framed as organic balance-sheet strengthening
- **M**: Note 9 — Debt
- **Finding**: ⚠ **MODERATE (Mode B)**. Connects to Ω1 / R9. The cash-to-debt ratio improvement (220.6% → 255.5%) is in part driven by the $853M debt reduction, which is in turn driven by divestiture proceeds. The MD&A celebrates the ratio improvement without crediting the underlying mechanism.

## Methodology note — what R/f/M adds on top of the composite score

The muni_credit **composite score** is a *systematic* signal: it takes structured inputs (CMS HCRIS, CAFR overlays, EMMA codes, BLS data) and computes a numeric divergence. It runs across hundreds of obligors and tells you *which* names to look at.

The **R/f/M divergence analysis** is the *deep dive* for a single obligor: it reads the management narrative, extracts specific claims, applies f-rules, and cross-checks against canonical M-authorities. It surfaces both **Mode A** divergences (claim X says 228, audited source says 229 — minor PASS) and **Mode B** divergences (the document didn't mention the rating downgrade — material omission).

Together they form the two-layer pipeline:
1. Composite identifies the ~25 names worth diligence-ing (out of 200+ obligors)
2. R/f/M on each delivers the qualitative case to the PM

This Ascension run is the first demo of layer 2 on a hospital muni issuer. Methodology validated on the buyside_dd side (Parametric 20260526 run).
