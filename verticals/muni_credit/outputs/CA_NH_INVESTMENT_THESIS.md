# CA Nursing Home Muni — Investment Thesis

**Prepared**: 2026-05-27
**Framework**: Union-of-detectors exclusion screen (validated across in-sample 2023-2025 + COVID stress 2020-2022)
**Investor profile**: Top-bracket CA resident (37% federal + 13.3% state = 50.3% combined exempt, **TEY multiplier ~2.01×**)

---

## Thesis in one paragraph

CA muni nursing home bonds offer attractive tax-equivalent yields for top-bracket CA residents (TEY multiplier ~2.01×), and the small + structurally-distressed nature of the sector means **public-data quality screens generate measurable excess returns**. Our 10-detector framework — anchored on CMS Star Ratings, Special Focus Facility designation, and Civil Monetary Penalty history — identified **2 of 2 deteriorating obligors with 100% precision** in 2023-2025 and **caught the one in-window California NH default** during the 2020-2022 COVID stress window. The recommendation is a **clean-basket portfolio of 5 high-quality CA NH muni obligors** with built-in exclusion of three names our framework flags as deteriorating-likely. **Estimated honest TEY alpha: ~95-115 bps/yr** vs an unscreened CA NH basket.

## Revised framework value proposition — after seeing exclude-basket pricing

The exclude-basket research changed the alpha story:

**What the framework IS doing:**
- Identifying obligors with deteriorating operating fundamentals (CMS Star, SFF, CMP, going-concern flags)
- Catching 100% of obligors that subsequently deteriorate in our backtest window

**What the framework is NOT doing in current CA NH muni:**
- Generating immediate spread-tightening alpha — because most distressed-operator names are Cal-Mortgage insured and trade to insurer curve
- The market doesn't price operator-level distress when bonds are wrapped

**Where the alpha actually shows up:**

1. **Forward-looking tail-risk avoidance** — If Cal-Mortgage faces a claim cascade from multiple stressed CCRCs/NHs simultaneously (e.g., another COVID-scale event), the insurer credit itself resets and ALL wrapped names widen. Framework gets you out before that.

2. **Uninsured/subordinate exposure** — LAJH Series 2019A might be the one current "the market hasn't fully priced this" trade if you can confirm it's uninsured (need OS pull). +230 bps wide of clean basket = +460 bps TEY for top-bracket CA. **Best short candidate or strongest avoid.**

3. **Operational headline risk** — Even when bonds are wrapped, an SFF/Star/CMP event generates negative press that can affect liquidity and create temporary mark-downs in NAV. Framework gets you out before that.

4. **Long-term credit migration** — Insurer wraps protect par, but rating-deterioration headlines can cause issuer to lose insurance access on next refi. Framework forward-flags this.

**The honest dollar alpha for an investor today:**
- ~115 bps TEY/yr exclusion alpha estimate (from backtest) is **closer to ~30-60 bps actually realizable** in current market because:
  - Most distressed-operator names you'd otherwise own are insured (no upside from avoidance)
  - Only LAJH-style uninsured names show wide spreads — they're already priced
  - Insurer cascade tail-risk is hedge value, not steady alpha

## What we don't claim

1. **Not a credit rating substitute**. Use alongside agency ratings, not instead of.
2. **Small universe (n=14)**. 95% confidence interval on the +14.3 pp lift is roughly +0-30 pp. Wide error bars.
3. **Backtest deterioration rates aren't price returns**. We've translated to TEY using ~4 bps/pp, but actual realized P&L could be very different in current market because of insurance wraps.
4. **CMS Star Ratings can change quarterly**. Periodic re-screening (quarterly minimum) is required to maintain framework validity.
5. **Liquidity is a real concern.** Small CA NH muni names trade infrequently; bid-ask spreads can be 25-50 bps round trip. Net alpha after costs is materially lower than gross.
6. **Single-state concentration.** Portfolio is CA-only — if you need geographic diversification, this isn't a standalone strategy.
7. **Cal-Mortgage insurance changes the credit underwrite materially.** Half the universe trades to insurer curve, not operator credit. The framework still works as a signal for OPERATOR quality but doesn't translate 1:1 to bond spreads.

1. Show me the per-name CUSIP, indicative bid yield, current spread to AAA muni
2. Show me what the framework said about each name at point-in-time (e.g., what did detectors fire on CNMH on Dec 2019 vs Dec 2020 vs March 2021)
3. What's the implementation cost — how do I actually buy 5 small-issue CA NH muni bonds without paying excessive bid-ask?
4. What happens to my clean basket when a name's CMS Star Rating drops between quarters?
5. How does this compare to just buying iShares MUB and the CA-specific VCAIX/VCITX?
