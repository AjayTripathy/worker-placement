## Where the +1.8 pp after-tax TEY edge comes from (honest decomposition)
1. **Fee**: +0.06 pp — trivially, no fund fee.
2. **Duration / term premium**: a meaningful slice. We carry ~11.9y duration vs VTEC's **6.7y** (Vanguard
   fact sheet, 3/31/26) — ~5.2 extra years of the curve, ~78% more rate sensitivity. Part of the yield
   pickup is simply being paid for more duration, not free alpha.
3. **Deep-discount after-tax structuring**: several holdings (Turlock 3s@82, Natomas 3s@77, Baldwin Park
   3s@77, Anaheim 3s@79) are bought below par; the pull-to-par accretes as ordinary income (taxed, already
   netted in the 8.15%) on top of the tax-exempt coupon. VTEC holds par/premium bonds — no such pickup.
4. **Illiquidity premium**: we harvest the spread on thin, individually-traded names a $2.6B fund can't
   touch at size. Real, but it's compensation for giving up daily liquidity.
5. **AI-insulation + screening**: not a yield source per se, but it changes WHAT risk the yield is paid on.

## The durable, non-yield differentiators (the real product)
- **AI-crash insulation.** VTEC is market-cap weighted on the S&P CA AMT-Free index — which is led by
  **State of California GO**, the single name most exposed to the AI/tech-crash → capital-gains →
  state-GF revenue channel (the dot-com precedent: −71% cap-gains revenue, State GO −4–5 notches, while
  local school GO was untouched). Our book deliberately holds the **local property-tax-secured** names
  that survived that episode and screens the State-GF channel OUT. VTEC buys the exposure we avoid.
- **Uniform top-tier security.** Every holding is an unlimited ad-valorem GO with the SB-222/§53515
  statutory first lien — the class with no CA default history (downgrades, not defaults, even Paradise).
  VTEC mixes in COPs, lease-revenue, and revenue bonds that don't carry the unlimited-tax pledge.
- **Defined maturities (HTM control).** Our ladder matures 2034–2046, each rung returning par — so the
  long duration is a mark-to-market figure, not a hold-to-maturity risk. VTEC never matures; its NAV is
  permanently exposed to rates. For a true buy-and-hold investor this is a structural point in our favor.
- **Zero fee**, forever.

## The honest costs (what we give up)
- **Concentration**: 24 names vs 3,738. Diversification is in QUALITY (all strongest-class GO, zone-
  capped to 17% worst single-event), not quantity — but 24 names is genuinely concentrated idiosyncratic
  risk vs a 3,738-bond fund.
- **Rate risk**: ~11.9y duration → a +100 bps move marks the book ~−12% vs VTEC ~−6.7%. Only painful if you
  might SELL; irrelevant if you hold each rung to par.
- **Liquidity**: HTM ladder, no daily exit at tight spreads. VTEC is instantly tradable.
- **Effort / operational**: self-managed sourcing, RFQs, monitoring vs. one-click VTEC.

## Duration-matched recompare (term premium stripped out)
Holding duration constant at VTEC's 6.7y isolates the edge that is NOT just being paid for the curve.

| | duration | after-tax TEY | names | fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long book (max after-tax TEY) | 11.9y | 8.15% | 24 | 0 |
| **Duration-matched book (~7y)** | **7.3y** | **6.75%** | **10** | 0 |
| VTEC | 6.7y | 6.33% | 3,616 | 0.06% |

- Long-book edge vs VTEC: **+1.82 pp** — but at +5.2y extra duration.
- **Duration-NEUTRAL edge: +0.42 pp.** So **~1.40 of the 1.82 pp was term premium / duration**, not free alpha.
- And the matched book is *forced*: the screened universe has **no paper under ~6.7y duration** (nothing
  matures before 2034), so a 6.7y match collapses to ~10 names bunched at 2034–2035 — barely a ladder, more
  concentrated, reaching into short discount CCDs. Even the +0.42 pp is partly concentration compensation.

**Implication:** the screened book's yield advantage *requires* the long-duration tilt — it harvests term
premium + long-end after-tax discount accretion via hold-to-maturity. At VTEC's duration the yield case
nearly vanishes (~+0.4 pp for a far less diversified, less liquid book). So this is **not a same-duration
VTEC-beater** — it's a long-duration, AI-insulated, HTM income vehicle. At matched duration you'd just hold
VTEC unless you specifically want the AI-insulation screen, the zero fee, and the uniform unlimited-GO
security — which remain the only durable, non-duration edges.

## Verdict
Not strictly "better" — a **different instrument**. For a CA top-bracket, genuinely buy-and-hold investor
who wants maximum after-tax income, AI-insulation, and defined maturities and can tolerate concentration +
illiquidity, the screened book delivers **~180 bps more after-tax TEY** and avoids the State-GF AI exposure
VTEC carries. VTEC wins decisively on **liquidity, diversification, duration flexibility, and zero effort** —
it's the right core holding; the screened book is the higher-yield, thesis-expressing satellite (or the whole
sleeve for someone who values the after-tax yield and HTM control over liquidity).
