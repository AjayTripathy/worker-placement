                                             Organization

Mass General Brigham Incorporated (the Company) is the parent organization and sole corporate
member of numerous organizations whose financial conditions and operations are described in
Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Performance and the consolidated financial
statements that follow. The terms Mass General Brigham, system, we, our, or us as used herein, unless
otherwise stated or indicated by context, refer collectively to the Company and its affiliated
organizations.


Mass General Brigham operates academic medical centers, community acute care hospitals, inpatient
and outpatient mental health services facilities, urgent care centers, facilities that provide rehabilitation
medicine and long-term care services, physician organizations, home health services, nursing homes
and a graduate level program for health professions. Our mission is to provide world class health care
services to the local communities in which we operate as well as to patients across the United States
and the world. In addition, we are a nonuniversity-based non-profit private medical research enterprise
and a principal teaching affiliate of the medical and dental schools of Harvard University. Our licensed,
not-for-profit managed care organization (Mass General Brigham Health Plan, Inc.) and licensed, for-
profit insurance company (Mass General Brigham Health Insurance Company) (collectively referred to
as Mass General Brigham Health Plan or the Health Plan) provide health insurance products and
administrative services to the Massachusetts Medicaid program (MassHealth), Medicare Advantage
program, ConnectorCare (a state subsidized program for adults who meet income and immigration
guidelines) and commercial populations.




                                        Strategic Initiatives

As an academic health care system, Mass General Brigham has a four-part mission: to provide
exceptional patient care, to drive innovation through groundbreaking research and discovery; to
educate the next generation of health care professionals; and to improve the health of the
communities we serve.


                                                                                                      Page 3
To advance this mission, the organization undertook a wide range of initiatives in 2025, including:

   •   Improving quality for every patient: Mass General Brigham's unified patient care strategy, For
       Every Patient, continues to drive better outcomes, particularly in inpatient mortality. Using
       national benchmark data from Vizient, the gold standard for hospital outcome reporting,
       Massachusetts General Hospital (MGH) and Brigham and Women's Hospital (BWH) are now
       among the top tier of academic medical centers for both overall quality and inpatient mortality.
       Additional progress has been made in reducing average length of stay, which helps the system
       improve throughput and patient access amidst and unrelenting capacity crisis. This progress is
       driven by MGB's transformation to become a High Reliability Organization, where clinicians are
       empowered to make changes that enable improvements in outcomes. Validating this progress,
       five Mass General Brigham hospitals were once again named among the top hospitals in the
       nation by U.S. News & World Report.

   •   Patient-facing institutes shaping the future of medicine: Mass General Brigham has launched
       three disease-focused institutes in Cancer, Heart and Vascular, and Neuroscience, which are
       uniting clinical teams, empowering innovation, and enhancing patient access. Mass General
       Brigham Cancer Institute is accelerating progress by improving patient outcomes, building
       world-class spaces, and embracing a single identity, with a single front door for care. The Heart
       and Vascular Institute is uniting teams and improving care. For example, teams recently
       collapsed four call centers into one and created faster pathways to life-saving aortic care. The
       system also launched a process to build a Neuroscience Institute, which is driving a strategy to
       unite neurology and neurosurgery care across the system.

   •   Building world-class, patient-centered spaces: Mass General Brigham announced major
       investments in next-generation inpatient care at its academic medical centers and community
       hospitals. A 100,000 sq. ft. campus expansion at Brigham and Women's Faulkner Hospital added
       78 beds and additional MRI and endoscopy capabilities. A $100 million gift from the New
       Balance Foundation will support the construction of the New Balance Foundation Tower at the
       Phillip and Susan Ragon Building on the MGH campus. The tower will anchor world-class heart
       and vascular care and train future medical leaders. Mass General Brigham also announced the
       beginning of a process that will transform its BWH campus by replacing the aging Braunwald


                                                                                                   Page 4
       building with a new complex care inpatient building. With these current and future upgrades,
       MGB will accelerate work to ensure care is delivered in the right setting, harnessing expertise at
       its academic medical centers, community hospitals, outpatient sites and Home Hospital.

   •   Groundbreaking research and discovery: This year, Mass General Brigham announced the
       formation of the Mass General Brigham Research Institute (MGBRI), a unified structure that will
       replace the BWH and MGH research institutes and support researchers across the system.
       MGBRI will strengthen clinical research, streamline operations, and amplify the impact of our
       world-class research community. This year, Mass General Brigham researchers launched
       FaceAge, an AI technology that estimates biological age from facial images to help guide cancer
       treatment decisions and improve survival predictions. These efforts reflect our commitment to
       using technology to enhance care, protect vulnerable populations, and personalize treatment.

   •   Building healthier communities: Mass General Brigham strengthened its commitment to health
       equity through targeted investments and innovation. The inaugural Kraft Prize for Excellence
       and Innovation in Community Health honored ThriveLink, Mae, and Sober Sidekick for
       breakthrough solutions in safety-net access, maternal health, and addiction recovery.
       Simultaneously, a $9 million investment through Community Health Impact Funds expanded
       food access across Greater Boston, supporting infrastructure and local partnerships. Together,
       these efforts reflect a systemwide strategy to address social determinants of health and
       improve outcomes.




            Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Results

Fiscal Year 2025 Financial Results

Mass General Brigham reported income from operations of $59 million for fiscal year 2025, which
ended on September 30, 2025. In fiscal year 2024, the system reported income from operations of $46
million. The 2025 results include a nonrecurring charge of $53 million related to a strategic
reorganization of management and administrative positions to improve efficiency and reduce the rate
of expense growth. These actions, which are expected to yield annualized savings in excess of $240
million, will support Mass General Brigham’s ability to continue funding its mission. The system also

                                                                                                   Page 5
recognized $54 million and $118 million in revenue that pertains to prior year health care provider
activity in 2025 and 2024, respectively. Excluding the non-recurring charge and prior year activity, Mass
General Brigham generated income from operations of $58 million (0.3% operating margin) in 2025 and
a loss from operations of $72 million in 2024 (-0.4% operating margin).


The system recognized total operating revenue of $22.8 billion in 2025, an increase of $2.3 billion (11%)
over 2024. Patient care revenue grew to $14.5 billion, an increase of $1.0 billion (8%, inclusive of $98
million in 2024 from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services to partially remedy underpayments
for 340B-acquired pharmaceuticals in 2018-2022). Ongoing efforts to coordinate systemwide capacity
management resulted in an average acute care inpatient length of stay of 5.9 days, a 1.5% decline from
2024, and contributed to growth in discharges (3%). Robust outpatient activity also contributed to
revenue growth.


Premium revenue increased $367 million (17%) to $2.5 billion. The Health Plan’s fully insured
membership increased by 14,734 (6%), as growth in fully insured commercial (12,667 or 14%) and
Medicare Advantage (5,050 or 180%) members more than offset a reduction in MassHealth members
(-2,983 or -2%). As of September 30, 2025, the Health Plan had 405,028 total members (including
151,882 self-insured members), with approximately 26% in commercial fully-insured plans, 35% in
managed Medicaid, 37% in self-insured plans (including 118,541 Mass General Brigham employees and
family members) and 2% in Medicare plans.


Research and nonresearch sundry revenue increased $140 million (5%) to $3.0 billion. Other revenue
increased $710 million (35%) to $2.7 billion, inclusive of government transfers to mitigate losses
incurred during the COVID-19 pandemic of $54 million and $20 million in FY25 and FY24, respectively.


Operating expenses totaled $22.7 billion, an increase of $2.2 billion (11%) over 2024. Wages and
benefits increased $858 million (8%), reflecting patient utilization trends, inflationary pressure on
wages, higher health benefit utilization and the $53 million restructuring charge. Supplies and other
expenses increased $985 million (18%) to $6.5 billion, reflecting increased costs and use of
pharmaceutical ($540 million or 29%) and other clinical ($181 million or 16%) supplies.

                                                                                                        Page 6
Health Plan medical claims increased $314 million (21%) to $1.8 billion and the medical loss ratio (the
percentage of insurance premiums that were used to pay medical claims) increased to 96.1% from
93.3% in 2024. The increase in the medical loss ratio is attributable to higher care acuity and increased
pharmaceutical costs, including GLP-1s, that outpaced revenue especially in government programs. The
administrative expense ratio (the percentage of insurance premiums that were used to pay general and
administrative expenses) was 6.6% in 2025 and 6.7% in 2024.


In 2025, the system absorbed $1.0 billion in Medicaid and Health Safety Net shortfalls due to certain
government reimbursements that do not cover the full cost of providing care to low-income and
uninsured patients, an increase of $141 million (16%) over 2024. Mass General Brigham also invested
approximately $700 million to support its community health ($210 million), research ($190 million) and
education ($300 million) programs. Collectively, these mission-related initiatives comprised
approximately 7% of Mass General Brigham’s total operating revenue.


Management continues to focus on initiatives that can drive sustained performance improvement,
including patient care throughput and labor management efficiencies, and monitoring emerging federal
actions and developing action plans to mitigate potential financial impacts.


Depreciation and interest expense totaled $957 million and $954 million in 2025 and 2024,
respectively. In 2025 and 2024, operating cash flow (operating income before interest expense,
depreciation and amortization, exclusive of the nonrecurring charge and prior year activity), totaled
$1.0 billion (4.5% cash flow margin) and $881 million (4.3% cash flow margin), and capital expenditures
totaled $1.2 billion and $1.5 billion, respectively.


The system reported an overall gain of $2.4 billion in 2025, including a nonoperating gain of $2.3 billion.
Nonoperating activity includes gains and losses on investments and interest rate swaps, which can vary
significantly year-to-year due to volatility in the financial markets, and philanthropic activity. In 2024
the system reported an overall gain of $2.0 billion, including a nonoperating gain of $2.0 billion.

Fourth Quarter Fiscal Year 2025 Financial Results

For the three months ended September 30, 2025, the system reported income from operations of $103
million. This includes the recognition of $54 million in grants from the federal government to mitigate
losses incurred during the COVID-19 pandemic that were deferred pending audit. Excluding the prior
year revenue, the system generated operating income of $49 million (0.8% operating margin). For the
three months ended September 30, 2024, the system reported a loss from operations of $113 million
(-2.2% operating margin).


Total operating revenue increased $699 million (13%, excluding $54 million prior year revenue) to $5.9
billion. This reflects increases in net patient service revenue ($433 million, 13%), premium revenue
($130 million, 23%), research and academic revenue ($24 million, 3%) and other revenue ($113 million
or 19%, excluding $54 million prior year revenue).


Total operating expenses increased $537 million (10%) to $5.9 billion, reflecting increases in wages and
benefits ($202 million, 8%), supplies and other expenses ($237 million or 16%, of which $139 million
pertains to pharmaceuticals) and medical claims ($83 million, 20%).


For the three months ended September 30, 2025 and 2024, depreciation and interest expense totaled
$250 million. Operating cash flow (operating income before interest expense, depreciation and
amortization and excluding prior year revenue), totaled $300 million (5.1% cash flow margin) and $137
million (2.6% cash flow margin), and capital expenditures totaled $273 million and $409 million,
respectively.


The system reported an overall gain of $1.1 billion for the three months ended September 30, 2025,
including a nonoperating gain of $959 million. In the comparable prior year period the system reported
an overall gain of $384 million, including a nonoperating gain of $496 million.

 9 COVID-19 Pandemic and Government Funding
   On March 11, 2020, the World Health Organization designated COVID-19 as a global pandemic resulting in an extraordinary
   impact to Mass General Brigham. During certain periods since 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic has continued to impact
   patient activity and operating expenses. In response to COVID-19, various types of economic support was enacted by
   federal, state and local authorities to mitigate the financial impact, with the most significant impacts to Mass General
   Brigham in 2025 and 2024 summarized below.

    Provider Relief Funds (PRF)
    We received PRF in 2020 and 2021 that was used to prevent, prepare for and respond to COVID-19. As of
    September 30, 2025 and September 30, 2024, we have deferred $32,487 and $86,487, respectively, of PRF received.

    Federal Emergency Management Agency Funding (FEMA)
    We submitted applications to FEMA for the reimbursement of COVID-19 expenses and recognized grant
    revenue totaling $0 and $19,699 for the twelve months ended September 30, 2025 and 2024, respectively, as other
    operating revenue in the consolidated statements of operations. As of September 30, 2025 and September 30 2024,
    we have deferred $53,367 and $54,792, respectively, of FEMA payments received.
