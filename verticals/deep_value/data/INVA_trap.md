# INVA (Innoviva, Inc.) — Deep-Value Trap-Filter

**Date:** 2026-06-26 | **Live price:** $23.57 (NASDAQ, contract 218462822; YTD +17.9%; 52w 16.52–25.14, trading ~96% of range — already re-rated)
**Verdict: TRAP** (with a partial-conglomerate-discount nuance — see caveat). Not ownable as a clean deep-value royalty annuity at this price.

Primary sources: FY2025 10-K (filed 2026-02-25, acc 0001193125-26-071776), Q1-2026 10-Q (filed 2026-05-06, acc 0001193125-26-209110), EDGAR XBRL companyfacts (CIK 1080014), live IBKR snapshot.

## 1. Why the screen lied — the cheapness is manufactured by non-operating mark-to-market

The screener saw EV/EBIT ~8.7x, earn-yield ~12%, ROIC 13%, EBIT -4% YoY. Every one of those is distorted:

**(a) 60% of "earnings" is non-cash Armata mark-to-market, capitalized at an operating multiple.**
FY2025 GAAP net income $271.2M ($4.02 basic EPS). Of that:
- Changes in FV of equity-method investments (Armata, etc.): **+$141.4M**
- Changes in FV of equity/long-term investments: **+$20.2M**
- **Total non-cash MTM gains: +$161.6M = 60% of net income.** Net income ex-MTM ~$109.6M.

This is *exactly* the one-time-gain trap. FY2024 the same line was **−$123.4M** (a loss), which is why 2024 net income collapsed to $23.4M / $0.37 EPS. The "earnings" swing 10x year-to-year purely on Armata's stock price. Q1-2026 already booked +$191.2M of MTM gains. An earnings-yield struck on this number is meaningless; a forward multiple on it is a coin flip on one micro-cap biotech (Armata) the screener never saw.

**(b) The operating profit IS the royalty annuity, and it is in decline.**
FY2025 income from operations $163.7M decomposes to:
- **Royalty (GSK respiratory), net of capitalized-fee amort: $236.5M** — near-zero direct cost; this is essentially the *entire* operating profit.
- **Opco (Innoviva Specialty Therapeutics): ≈ −$73M EBIT drain.** Net product sales $172.1M − product COGS $77.4M − intangible amort $26.3M = $68.5M product gross profit; + $2.7M license; − $143.9M opex (SG&A $113.3M + R&D $30.6M) = **−$72.7M**. (2024 opco was −$74.9M.)

So the structure is: a melting royalty ice-cube ($236M) subsidizes a ~$73M/yr specialty-pharma cash drain, and the *residual* operating profit is the thing trading at the multiple. The screen's "EBIT" is a declining annuity minus a growing-but-still-lossmaking opco — not a stable operating business.

## 2. The royalty-decline schedule — the annuity is melting AND faces a mandated IRA cut

**Royalty terms (GSK LABA Collaboration):**
- RELVAR/BREO ELLIPTA: **15% on first $3.0B annual global net sales, 5% above $3.0B.**
- ANORO ELLIPTA: tiered **6.5%–10%**.

**Trajectory (gross GSK royalty):**
- FY2024 $255.6M → FY2025 $250.3M = **−2.1% YoY** (slow melt so far).
- **Q4-2025 $58.4M vs Q4-2024 $66.0M = −11.5% YoY — the decline is accelerating.**
- Q1-2026 royalty (net of amort) $55.2M vs $57.8M PY = −4.5%.

**The hard catalyst — IRA Medicare drug-price negotiation (mandated, dated):**
- **RELVAR/BREO ELLIPTA was SELECTED for negotiation; its negotiated Maximum Fair Price (MFP) takes effect January 1, 2027.**
- **ANORO ELLIPTA selected in the next cycle; MFP expected to take effect 2028.**
These are not "if generics arrive someday" risks — they are *legislated price cuts* to the exact royalty base, ~6 months out for the larger drug. A 15% royalty on a Medicare-negotiated price is mechanically lower. BREO is the bulk of the GSK stream; Medicare is a large slice of US respiratory scripts. Generic FF/VI (FDA draft guidance issued) is the longer-tail cliff on top.

**Net:** the royalty is a declining annuity stepping down hard in 2027–2028. Capitalizing it at ~8–9x as if stable is the core error.

## 3. Capital allocation — royalty cash redeployed into speculative biotech, mostly NOT returned

Management has used the royalty cash to build a $614.0M "strategic assets" portfolio (as of 12/31/25): **$397.9M Armata** (anti-phage/biologics, controlled stake, consolidated/equity-method), $136.4M other strategic equity + convertible debt (InCarda, Syndeio/Gate Neuro, ImaginAb, Lyndra, Nanolive, Beacon Biosignals $17.5M Series B in Oct-2025), $79.7M in ISP Fund LP (an internal hedge-fund vehicle).

This is a melting royalty being recycled into negative-/unproven-NPV venture bets, plus the opco's −$73M/yr drain. Capital *return* is minimal and very recent: a $125M buyback authorized only Nov-3-2025, of which just **$16.0M (797k shares) executed** by year-end. The headline "earnings" that look like value are the *paper* appreciation of those bets (Armata), not distributable cash. The bull case implicitly asks you to underwrite Innoviva's biotech stock-picking — a different, worse business than a royalty annuity.

## 4. The FCF crossover

Royalty operating contribution ~$236M today, declining mid-single-digits and stepping down at the 2027 IRA cut. Opco drains ~$73M/yr EBIT (improving as product sales grow 77% YoY — ZEVTERA/GIAPREZA/XACDURO/XERAVA — but still loss-making; could reach breakeven in a couple years if the ramp holds). Add ~$5.6M/yr interest on the 2028 notes and ~$56M cash taxes. The consolidated entity is FCF-positive today and the crossover to FCF-negative is *not* imminent on the base royalty — but it compresses materially with the 2027/2028 IRA steps stacked on the natural decline and continued opco/venture spend. The risk is not running out of cash (they have $603M); it is the *operating* earning power eroding while management spends the annuity on equity stakes.

- Preferred: **0** outstanding.
- **Shares: 73.81M cover-count basic; 84.76M fully diluted (the 11M gap is the 2028 convert + RSUs).** Use the diluted count — the converts are in the money / dilutive in the EPS table (diluted EPS $3.30 vs basic $4.02).

**Net cash ≈ $603M − $261M = ~$342M, or ~$4.0/sh on diluted shares.** True — but it is being actively spent on biotech stakes, not returned. The plus-side: the equity portfolio ($614M) plus net cash ($342M) ≈ $956M ≈ ~$11.3/sh of "non-royalty" value vs $23.57 price, leaving ~$12/sh (~$1.0B core EV) for the declining royalty + lossmaking opco — i.e., the market is paying ~6.4x the current (peaking, pre-IRA) operating income for an annuity about to be cut. That is not cheap once you mark the annuity to its forward.

## VERDICT: **TRAP**

The "cheap-and-quality" signal is an artifact of (1) capitalizing a 60%-non-cash, Armata-driven earnings number at an operating multiple, and (2) striking that multiple on a royalty annuity that is declining (~−2% and accelerating to −11.5% in the latest quarter) and faces a *legislated* IRA Medicare price cut effective Jan-1-2027 (BREO) / 2028 (ANORO). ROIC of "13%" is flattered by the same MTM gains in the numerator. This is a melting royalty ice-cube being redeployed into speculative biotech, with management compensation/empire incentives, not a return-of-capital story.

**Caveat (why not a pure short / why "WAIT" is defensible for a specialist):** there is a real sum-of-parts: ~$342M net cash + $614M marked portfolio ≈ $11/sh of hard-ish value, a $125M buyback just begun, and a product opco growing 77% that *could* offset royalty decline if XACDURO/ZEVTERA ramp. A value buyer who (a) marks the royalty to its post-IRA forward, (b) haircuts the Armata stake for liquidity/concentration, and (c) demands the buyback accelerate could find a price — but **not at $23.57, ~96% of the 52-week range, after a +18% YTD re-rate.** For a sector diversifier in a small-cap-value book, this adds idiosyncratic single-biotech (Armata) and IRA-policy risk, not the stable healthcare annuity the screen implied. **Pass; revisit only on a dislocation below ~$17–18 (net cash + discounted portfolio floor) with the 2027 IRA impact already in the print.**

### FV bear / base / bull
Treating royalty as a declining annuity (DCF, ~10% disc, −8%/yr royalty post-2027 IRA + terminal generic erosion), opco at conservative breakeven-to-small-profit, portfolio haircut 20–30% for concentration/liquidity, net cash at face:
- **Bear ~$14:** IRA cut deeper than feared + Armata re-rates down (it drove the whole 2025 gain) + opco stays lossmaking. Portfolio haircut 40%.
- **Base ~$19–20:** royalty −6–8%/yr through the IRA steps, opco to breakeven by ~2027, portfolio at ~75% of mark, net cash credited, buyback completes $125M. ~15% below spot.
- **Bull ~$28–30:** opco ramp offsets royalty decline (product sales keep +70%), Armata appreciates further, full $125M+ buyback, take-private at a control premium (the 2028-note fundamental-change put is the only friction). Requires you to be right on the biotech bets.

Spot $23.57 sits above base FV — paying for the bull's biotech optionality.

### 3 kill-triggers (would flip to BUY/ownable)
1. **Price dislocation < ~$17–18** that puts you at/below the net-cash + discounted-portfolio floor, with the Jan-2027 IRA cut already in the trailing print (de-risks the central catalyst).
2. **Management pivots to return-of-capital**: $125M buyback *completed* + a second larger authorization or a special dividend funded by royalty cash, AND a public commitment to stop new speculative biotech investments. That would re-rate the annuity as a wind-down.
3. **Opco reaches sustained operating breakeven** (product GP > opex for 2+ quarters) so the royalty no longer subsidizes a $73M drain — converts the SOTP from "melting annuity funding a drain" to "annuity + standalone-viable pharma."

### Single most likely way THIS thesis breaks (i.e., the bull is wrong)
**The Armata mark reverses and takes the headline earnings with it.** 60% of FY2025 net income and the entire YoY EPS swing came from non-cash appreciation of one controlled micro-cap biotech stake (Armata, $397.9M). The screen's earn-yield/ROIC/"EBIT -4%" all lean on that mark. When Armata's stock gives back gains (it is volatile and Innoviva is the marginal price-setter via its own funding rounds), GAAP earnings collapse the way they did in 2024 (to $0.37 EPS) — and simultaneously the royalty steps down at the 2027 IRA cut. The two de-ratings are correlated to the downside: a risk-off tape hits both the Armata mark and the multiple on a now-visibly-declining annuity. The "value" was never operating value; it was a biotech stock-picking mark dressed as an earnings yield.
