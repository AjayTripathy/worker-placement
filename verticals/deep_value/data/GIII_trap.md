## 2. The PVH cliff is REAL, LARGE, and quantified by management

This is not an over-discounted fear — it is a disclosed, scheduled, and already-in-progress
earnings event. From the FY26 10-K (verified, primary):

- **CK + TH were 34% of net sales in FY25, 28% in FY26**, declining by schedule.
- Licenses expire on a **staggered schedule**: began **Dec 31, 2024**, continue through **Dec 31, 2027.**
- Management's own roll-off table of CK/TH sales contribution (% of total FY26 sales):
  - Dec 31, 2025 expiry: **$435.6M (15%)**
  - Dec 31, 2026 expiry: **$372.2M (13%)**
  - Dec 31, 2027 expiry: **$19.3M (1%)**
- FY27 guidance **"incorporates the loss of approximately $470M of sales from CK/TH."** Revenue
  guided down to $2.71B from $2.96B. The cliff is happening in the numbers NOW, not someday.
- **Active two-way litigation with PVH** (filed June 2025): G-III sued PVH over denial of a
  women's-suits extension; CK/TH counter-sued G-III for breach. Loss range UNVERIFIABLE
  (company "unable to estimate"). This removes any optionality of a friendly extension.

**Finding: REFUTES the bull's "market over-discounts the cliff."** The cliff is fully disclosed,
on a fixed calendar, ~$470M of FY27 revenue gone, with litigation foreclosing extension. By the
honesty-alpha rule this is **disclosed bad news = not the catch by itself** — but it directly
falsifies the "over-discounted" leg of the bargain thesis.

## 3. What's replacing it — real, but margin-dilutive and unproven at scale

Owned brands (DKNY, Donna Karan, Karl Lagerfeld, Vilebrequin) are the stated profit engine, plus
new licenses launched FY25-27: **Nautica, Halston, Champion (FY25), Converse, BCBG (FY26),
French Connection (FY27)**, and the pending **Marc Jacobs** acquisition.

Reality check on the offset:
- Karl Lagerfeld + Donna Karan grew +$80.8M in FY26 — real, but **CK/TH fell -$285.8M** the same
  year. The replacement is running well behind the roll-off.
- FY27 non-GAAP EPS is guided **DOWN 16%** ($2.61 → $2.20) even WITH the new brands ramping. The
  transition does **not** offset the cliff on the 1-2 year horizon — management's own numbers say so.
- **Marc Jacobs**: ~$500M investment (cash on hand + revolver), a 50/50 JV with WHP Global buying
  from LVMH; G-III operates under license from the JV IPCo. This **consumes the entire ~$390M net
  cash cushion and then some** (drawing the revolver) — the net-cash support for "below book / EV
  cheap" largely disappears post-close. Guidance explicitly **excludes** any MJ impact, so accretion
  is unproven and integration/JV-economics are an open risk. Closing subject to antitrust.

## 4. Verdict — TRAP / FAIR, not a REAL bargain

| Trap-filter test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Is the "E" durable or peak/rolling-off? | **Peak + rolling off.** FY25 EBIT $293M → FY26 $108M; CK/TH (28% of sales) leaving on a fixed calendar; FY27 EPS guided down. |
| Does the cheapness survive recompute on latest filing? | **No.** "5.7x EV/EBIT" → 9.7x GAAP / ~15x clean P/E on current earnings. The bargain was a stale-peak artifact. |
| Is the discount justified (structural) or temporary (beatable)? | **Justified/structural.** A ~$470M revenue hole, replaced by lower-margin owned/new-license sales that don't fill it within 1-2 yrs. |
| Honesty-alpha read (takeaway vs data) | **Clean disclosure — no lying.** Company quantifies the cliff explicitly. This is not a polished-story catch; it's a genuinely impaired transition that is honestly disclosed → screen the bargain OUT on fundamentals, not on dishonesty. |
| Re-rate catalyst for a rotation? | Weak. The 18% FCF yield + below-book + net cash give downside support, but the EARNINGS trajectory is down through FY27; a small-cap rotation re-rates beatable temporary cheapness — this is neither beatable nor temporary. |

**Bottom line:** GIII is **cheap-looking on stale peak data and on trailing FCF, but FAIR-to-rich
on the durable forward earnings the cliff leaves behind.** The PVH roll-off is real, scheduled, and
litigated; the owned-brand + Marc Jacobs transition is plausible long-term but margin-dilutive,
self-funded out of the very net cash that anchors the "below book" case, and per management's own
guidance does NOT offset the cliff over the next two years. This is the deep-value **value-trap**
pattern, not an over-discounted bargain. **EXCLUDE from the quality-value rotation basket.**

- Not an honesty/lying catch (disclosure is clean) — exclude on FUNDAMENTALS.
- Watch-item, not a buy: a real entry would need (a) evidence the owned-brand/new-license book is
  re-accelerating margin faster than the FY27 guide implies, (b) Marc Jacobs closing accretive and
  JV economics disclosed, (c) PVH litigation resolved, AND (d) a price that puts clean forward P/E
  near ~8-10x. At ~15x clean forward P/E on declining EPS, no margin of safety.

**Indicative fair value (if forced):** clean FY27 non-GAAP ~$2.20 EPS × 9-11x (shrinking-revenue
apparel licensor/owner) = **~$20-24/share equity value before MJ optionality**; add modest net-cash
/ FCF-support premium → **~$24-28 fair-ish zone.** Live $34 sits ABOVE that. Not a buy here.

UNVERIFIABLE: PVH litigation outcome/loss range (company can't estimate); Marc Jacobs accretion
(excluded from guidance, not yet closed); whether owned-brand margins re-accelerate beyond FY27 guide.
