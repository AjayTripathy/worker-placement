## 2. The annuity — slow-maturing, NOT a rollover; but the bull's "compounder" is too strong
- **Refutes the trap thesis ("FCF = liquidation of a melting base"):** even on the bear path (hardware −10%/yr), the 89%-GM Platform stays **roughly flat for 2-3 years** because ARPU (+3-5% via the new Cricut Access *Premium* / AI-credit tiers) offsets slow sub erosion and installed-base subs renew with inertia. **The rollover, if it comes, is a Year-4+ event.** The deep-value floor (net cash + 4.4% + buyback) holds across both scenarios.
- **But downgrades the trap-filter's "+6% compounder" to "~3-4%/yr, decelerating":** net adds are *halving* (+177k → +104k YoY), the **first sequential paid-sub decline is on the tape** (3,091k → 3,078k), penetration of the engaged base is tapped at ~92%, and **management itself guides subs flat-to-down near-term and concedes sub growth is gated by hardware "it doesn't yet control."**
- **The leading indicator to watch:** 90-day-engaged users are eroding ~3%/yr — paid subs are downstream of engagement, propped up for now by penetration + ARPU. When penetration can't rise further (it's at 92%), the sub count converges to ~92% × a shrinking engaged base. That convergence is the rollover.
- **The moat is shallower than "software lock-in":** Design Space is **free** (no subscription needed to run the machine — you can upload your own SVGs); the 2021 user backlash (100k-signature petition forced a same-week reversal of an upload cap) proves the base will revolt against pricing-power extraction — a real cap on the ARPU lever. The moat is brand + ecosystem + convenience-content, not a SaaS switching wall.
- **No churn/retention disclosure** anywhere = the central UNVERIFIABLE for an annuity-durability thesis. International "growth" is ~half FX (reported +16.6% Q1 ≈ +5.7% constant-currency).

## 3. Valuation & governance — governance is the decisive risk, and it's worse than "73%"
- Live $4.57 → mcap $959M, net cash $256M, EV $704M, **EV/EBIT 7.9x TTM, FCF yield 14% headline / ~9% normalized.**
- **Control: Class B carries 5 votes/share → Class B is 93.4% of the vote; the public Class A float controls just 6.6%. Petrus & affiliates hold ~74% of the total vote.** Explicit Nasdaq "controlled company" (entitled to skip majority-independent board / independent comp + nom committees).
- **Take-private is a capped-downside lowball risk, NOT an upside premium.** A controlled, net-cash, ~$137M-FCF small-cap is a textbook cheap-take-private target, and with 93% of the vote Petrus **doesn't need a market premium** to squeeze out the 6.6% minority. There can't be a bidding war. **Do not underwrite a buyout premium in the bull case — size it as a risk.** Mitigants: no TRA, no related-party operating leakage, dividends flow pro-rata to both classes (Petrus is paid the same per share — aligned on cash return, not value extraction).
