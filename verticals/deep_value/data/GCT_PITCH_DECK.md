## Slide 3 — What the business actually is (and what it is not)

**Punchline: GCT is a cross-border logistics-anchored distributor of big, bulky furniture — not the asset-light software marketplace the bulls sometimes pitch. We underwrite it as the former, which is why the price is interesting at all.**

GigaCloud connects mostly-Asian furniture manufacturers to Western (US and EU) resellers across three linked businesses:

- **A B2B marketplace** where third-party sellers list large-bulky goods and GCT earns commission plus fulfillment fees (1,299 active third-party sellers in 2025).
- **A first-party (1P) distributor** that buys inventory on its own balance sheet and resells it — the bulk of revenue and ~29.8% product gross margin.
- **GigaCloud Logistics**, an owned cross-border 3PL / fulfillment network (a multi-million-square-foot warehouse footprint financed through leases, not capex) that physically holds the goods. This is the genuine moat: it makes the platform hard to disintermediate and creates real buyer stickiness.

Total merchandise volume (GMV) ~$2.06B: marketplace GMV $1,576.8M (third-party $851.2M + first-party $725.6M) plus off-platform / Noble House product ~$486.8M. **Note the distinction that matters for the rest of the deck:** GMV is gross merchandise flow; revenue is $1,289.9M. On the third-party slice only commission and freight hit GCT's revenue — the goods themselves never touch its income statement. Hold that thought for Slide 6.

*What could make this wrong: the 1P book is working-capital-heavy and cyclical — it is arbitraging a China-to-West cost gap that can compress. We are paying a distributor multiple, not a marketplace multiple, on purpose.*

## Slide 5 — The quality check: why this is not a fraud and not a trap

**Punchline: every box that usually fails on a cheap China-linked small-cap passes here on primary evidence. The discount is for the business model, not the books.**

- **Auditor:** KPMG Huazhen LLP (PCAOB ID 1186), auditor since 2020, with a 404(b) attestation and effective internal controls. This is a Big-Four member firm, PCAOB-inspectable — not the obscure-auditor archetype that precedes blow-ups. The single Critical Audit Matter is benign (IT systems over revenue), not fraud-flavored.
- **No VIE.** The 10-K states directly that there is no variable-interest-entity structure — GCT consolidates direct subsidiaries. That removes the single biggest structural red flag on China-linked issuers.
- **Net cash is real and available.** Of the cash balance, only a low-single-digit-percent share sits in mainland China; the large majority is onshore (US) or in jurisdictions without exchange controls. The buyback is funded out of US cash, which is direct evidence the cash is spendable. We have stress-tested net cash by haircutting *all* PRC cash and a full repatriation-tax overhang — EV/EBIT moves only from ~5.1x to ~5.3x. The thesis does not hinge on the trapped-cash question.
- **Earnings convert to cash.** Operating cash flow has exceeded net income for three consecutive years — the signature of clean accruals. (One nuance we monitor: FY25 operating cash flow benefited from a payables stretch; we watch supplier terms.)
- **No customer concentration; one supplier >10% of purchases** (disclosed, monitored). **It is buying back stock** — ~5–6% of the cap in FY2025 at ~8.6x earnings and a ~15% FCF yield, which compounds per-share value even with no re-rate.

*What could make this wrong: an auditor change away from KPMG, any restatement, or a rise in mainland-China cash as a share of the total would each re-open the quality question. None is present today; all are on the kill-trigger list.*

## Slide 6 — THE TARIFF BEAR, SIZED (the centerpiece)

**Punchline: the bear's load-bearing number is the China-origin share of GMV — which the company does not disclose. We triangulated it. And the punchline the bears miss is that origin share is not the margin denominator: the biggest China bucket sits OFF GigaCloud's income statement.**

### Step 1 — We had to build the number the company won't give us
GCT discloses the tariff *risk* but not its *magnitude* — it never publishes a China-origin percentage of GMV. We read this as competitively-sensitive honest non-disclosure (a supplier map is a trade secret), not a red flag. But it means the bear case cannot be sized from the filings alone — so we built it from primary trade data.

**Method:** decompose GMV into its three buckets, assign each an independent China-origin probability, and cross-check the result against US customs Bill-of-Lading records and Census trade statistics.

| Bucket | GMV | Est. China-origin | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Third-party marketplace | $851.2M | ~85% | Asian manufacturer base, heaviest China tilt |
| First-party (1P) inventory | $725.6M | ~73% | GCT's own sourcing, some diversification |
| Off-platform / Noble House | $486.8M | ~55% | Vietnam/India-tilted (Noble House supply base) |

Blended, China-origin lands at roughly **62% / 74% / 82% (low/base/high) of GMV ≈ $1.5B at base.** Customs Bill-of-Lading records on "Giga Cloud Logistics" shipments read approximately 80/20 China/Vietnam — directional corroboration of the high-China read (with the usual caveat that BOL data undercounts and consolidates).

### Step 2 — The insight the bears get wrong: origin share ≠ margin exposure
A tariff only dents *GigaCloud's own* profit on goods GigaCloud owns. The largest China-exposed bucket — the **$851M third-party marketplace** — sits **off** GCT's P&L. On those goods the importer of record is the third-party seller or the US buyer; GCT books only commission and freight. The tariff incidence falls on someone else's balance sheet. **GCT's own-inventory China landed cost is ~$558M** (the 1P book plus Noble House) — that, not the $1.5B headline, is the figure tariffs actually attack.

### Step 3 — Size the worst case, then look at what actually happened
- **Worst case (mechanical):** apply the incremental China furniture duty to the ~$558M of own-inventory landed cost and assume GCT recovers **only 60%** through price. Un-recovered EBIT hit ≈ **$67M, about 42% of $161.2M pretax.** Painful — but the company stays net-cash, FCF-positive, and solvent. It bends; it does not break.
- **Demonstrated case (actual):** in Q3-2025, product margin **expanded to 29.9%** despite an estimated ~2.5-point one-quarter tariff headwind — GCT offset it with targeted price increases. At the **full pass-through it actually demonstrated**, the un-recovered EBIT hit is **near $0.**
- **Regime context that helps the math:** the headline 145% figure the price still seems to fear no longer reflects the durable rule set — the IEEPA "reciprocal" tariffs were struck down in early 2026, leaving a China furniture stack closer to ~17–42% and Vietnam ~10–17%. The denominator the bear is using is stale and high.

**Conclusion: this is not a margin value-trap. The tariff is real on volume and demand (Slide 8), but on EBIT it is bracketed between ~$67M worst-case and ~$0 demonstrated — and the company has shown it lives in the favorable half of that range.**

*What could make this wrong: our China-origin estimate is a triangulation, not a disclosure — if true China-origin is materially above our high case AND pass-through fails, the EBIT hit exceeds $67M. We size the position accordingly and treat product gross margin as the real-time referee (kill trigger: <22% for two quarters).*

## Slide 8 — Risks and what would break it (no soft-pedaling)

**Punchline: the real residual bear is demand elasticity and governance — not the margin story. We give both honest weight.**

1. **Demand elasticity is the genuine open risk.** Tariffs raise end-prices; higher prices mean Western resellers buy fewer units. This is the channel the sized-EBIT analysis does *not* fully neutralize, and it is already visible: **spend per active buyer fell from $144k to $130k in 2025.** If volumes contract faster than pass-through can defend, revenue and operating leverage erode together. This, not gross margin, is what we watch hardest.
2. **Founder voting control (~70.7%, held by Larry Wu on ~19.5% of the economics).** Minority holders cannot force the re-rate and cannot block a controller-led take-private at an opportunistic, lowball price. A Founder Undertaking blocks a take-private below the IPO price only until ~Aug-2027, then lapses. This is a real, permanent governance discount — part of why the stock is cheap, and part of why it may stay cheaper than fair value longer than we'd like.
3. **The 1P model is working-capital-heavy and cyclical.** It is, at bottom, a China-to-West supply-chain arbitrage with a ~29.8% product gross margin. That arbitrage can erode slowly as supply chains diversify and as competitors replicate the logistics network.
4. **It is a distributor, not the asset-light marketplace some bulls pitch.** True marketplace commission is a small slice of revenue; most gross profit comes from owned inventory and logistics. The discount is *partly deserved* — we are not pretending otherwise. The bet is that it is over-deserved.

**Kill / re-underwrite triggers (any one forces a re-underwrite):**
- Product gross margin below 22% for two consecutive quarters.
- Auditor change away from KPMG.
- Organic (ex-acquisition) revenue turns negative.
- A take-private proposal emerges (especially as the Founder Undertaking nears its ~Aug-2027 lapse).
