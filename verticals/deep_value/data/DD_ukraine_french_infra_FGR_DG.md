### Sum-of-the-parts — the key analytical call
**Eiffage:** the crown jewel is **APRR/AREA motorways** — Concessions €3.978bn revenue (+2.3%), €1.712bn operating profit, **43% operating margin, ~70% EBITDA margin**. APRR alone ~€2.7–2.8bn EBITDA. Reasonable APRR enterprise values ~€18–22bn; net of APRR's ~€6–7bn project debt, the equity value of the motorway stake is on the order of the **entire Eiffage market cap (~€12.4bn) by itself** → the bull SOTP gets the €21.3bn-revenue contracting arm (4.6% margin, roughly net-cash) for almost nothing.

**The catch (why it's not a free lunch):** APRR's concession **expires 30 November 2035 — ~10 years.** At handback the asset reverts to the French state for zero consideration. APRR is not a perpetual annuity you capitalize at 11–12x EBITDA; it is a **depleting cash stream that DCFs to ~zero terminal value.** Mark APRR honestly and **Eiffage moves from "deeply cheap" to "fairly valued, maybe modestly cheap." The discount is earned.**

**Vinci:** Concessions €12bn revenue / €8bn EBITDA, split between **VINCI Autoroutes** (€6.7bn rev, 71% EBITDA margin) and **VINCI Airports** (€4.8bn rev, 63% margin). Crucially, **Vinci Airports is largely long-dated/perpetual** (Gatwick freehold, ANA Portugal to ~2093, Mexico, Cambodia) — it does **not** face the French motorway cliff. Plus €63bn of contracting/energy revenue, record €34.2bn construction order book. Vinci's 7x EV/EBITDA is fair-to-slightly-cheap for a diversified global infra compounder.

## 2. CYCLE / DURABILITY — concession life is the whole ballgame
**Concession expiry (VERIFIED):** APRR/AREA (Eiffage) **30 Nov 2035**; Cofiroute (Vinci) 30 Jun 2034; ASF (Vinci) 30 Apr 2036; Escota (Vinci) 29 Feb 2032. ~90% of France's 9,300 km toll network expires 2031–2036.

Both concession books are healthy (Eiffage Concessions +2.3% revenue, 43% margin; Vinci Autoroutes +2.3%/71%; Vinci Airports +6.0% revenue, traffic fully recovered). Contracting margins improving (Eiffage 4.6%, Vinci >4% EBIT, record books). **No cyclical deterioration — the durability risk is the expiry cliff, not the cycle.**

- **Eiffage is the more concentrated bet** — a single asset (APRR) drives ~65% of group operating profit and rolls off on one date (2035). Post-2035 absent renewal, Eiffage becomes a ~4.6%-margin contractor and loses ~€1.7bn of operating profit.
- **Vinci is structurally protected** — French motorways are only part of concessions; airports (the growth engine) are long-dated.

The post-2035 model is politically undecided (French "fin des concessions" planning). Incumbents may bid to *operate* roads post-expiry, but as fee-for-service operators returns are a fraction of today's owner economics. **Do not assume APRR's 70% margins survive 2035 in any form.**

## 3. MODE B — disconfirming pass
**B1 — French infrastructure tax is real, recurring, already in the numbers (VERIFIED, not a tail risk).** The "tax on long-distance transport infrastructure operators" (TIILD, ~€600m/yr, almost entirely hitting motorway concessionaires) is live and being paid: **Eiffage €127m hit in 2025** (the entire reason reported net profit fell −1.6%); **Vinci ~€260m/yr** + the broader €449m exceptional contribution cutting net income from ~€5.4bn to €4.9bn. Both, plus Sanef, have **appealed to the Conseil d'État** arguing the tax violates the tax-neutrality clauses in their concession contracts. **The single biggest standalone risk, structural not one-off.** Treat as a permanent ~5–10% tax on concession profit unless/until the legal challenge succeeds (uncertain, slow).

**B2 — Debt sits at the concession level.** Eiffage's €8.5bn group net debt is dominated by APRR's own (largely non-recourse) project debt; the holding/contracting business is roughly net-cash. Bad news for the SOTP bull: valuing APRR you must net its ~€6–7bn debt, which shrinks the "free contracting arm." Vinci's €19.1bn / 1.4x EBITDA is comfortably IG, covered by €7bn FCF.

**B3 — "Stable infrastructure compounder" vs the data.** For **Vinci this framing is TRUE** — diversified, long-dated airports, record FCF, growing dividend. For **Eiffage it is PARTIALLY misleading** — compounding is real today but concentrated in a single asset with a 2035 terminal date. That's the one takeaway-vs-data divergence and the reason to grade Eiffage WATCH not STARTER.

**B4 — Honesty / disclosure check: CLEAN, both names.** Both explicitly broke out the tax headwind (Vinci showed net income would be €5.4bn ex-tax; Eiffage showed +8.9% at constant tax) — honest disclosure of bad news, not masking. No going-concern, no aggressive non-GAAP, no concession-debt obfuscation.
