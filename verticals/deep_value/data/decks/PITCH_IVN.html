1 · The asset, and what broke

Kamoa-Kakula (DRC) is one of the highest-grade major copper complexes on earth, with bottom-quartile cash costs (C1 $2.58/lb — below the company's own guide) and its own smelter ramping. Ivanhoe owns 39.6%, co-equal with Zijin Mining (39.6%), with the DRC state carrying 20%.

The accident and the reset

Why the reset matters: the current 290–330kt guide needs only ~73–86kt per quarter for the rest of the year — Q1 already ran at 71.4kt. The bar is 2–20% above demonstrated performance, not the heroic 45–60% step-up the old guidance implied. The recovery ask is modest; the market's memory of the accident is not.

Also in the company, largely free at this price: Western Forelands (100%-owned discovery district, 9.4Mt contained copper and doubling every 18 months, resource update due imminently), Kipushi (record zinc quarters), Platreef (platinum-group Phase 1 starting mid-2026).

2 · What's already in the price (the honest slide)

At C$10.70, the market is not working off stale information. Everything public is in the tape:

  • The guidance cut is fully consensus. It was a front-page mining-press headline on March 31; brokers re-based the same week (BMO cut its target from $23 to $16 citing the new output). Nobody credible still models the old numbers. Our own diligence initially claimed the market was anchored on stale guidance — the red team proved that wrong, and we dropped the claim.
  • A copper haircut. Major-desk 2026–27 copper forecasts sit at ~$4.85–6.25/lb (spot ~$6). The stock trades below broker targets built on those decks — the market is pricing copper skepticism the sell side isn't.
  • Recurrence fear. One year after a cascade failure, no amount of engineering language fully retires the "what if it happens again" discount — nor should it.
  • The DRC + co-control discount. Jurisdiction risk, a 20% state carry, royalty load, and the fact that Ivanhoe shareholders can never be bought out (Zijin + DRC control 59.6%) cap the multiple permanently. We underwrite this as a structural 15–25% discount, not a gap that closes.
Net: C$10.70 sits below where re-based analyst targets (~C$16) and our own probability-weighted value (~C$13.6) point. The market is pricing something worse than the reset guide — a residual fear premium for copper reversion, tremor recurrence, and Congo. That premium is real compensation. It is not mispricing born of ignorance.

3 · Why we may have edge — honestly, thin, and named

The red team killed the information-edge claim (the guidance reset is three months public). What survives is smaller and different in kind:

a. The over-discount to an already-reset bar (~0–3pp, the residual)

The market has re-based to 290–330kt and then discounted below it. If the mine simply does what the reset guide says — a bar barely above demonstrated output — and no new tremor prints, the fear premium bleeds out slowly. This is drift, not a catalyst pop.

b. The free options nobody is paying for

At a price that roughly covers Kamoa-at-reset-guidance plus net debt, Western Forelands (a growing tier-one discovery district, MRE due within weeks), Kipushi zinc, and Platreef come approximately free. Optionality with dated news flow.

c. The structural fit (why this book specifically)

  • ~60% volatility with zero dividend — the ideal tax-asymmetry instrument for a CA-top-bracket year: losses harvest at ~50¢ subsidy by Dec-15, gains defer. The volatility itself carries ~5%/yr of after-tax option value to us that a taxable dividend payer can't match.
  • Quarterly production prints give dated, checkable resolution points — this book's preferred shape.
  • The smelter quirk: DRC's concentrate-export restrictions squeeze peers who ship concentrate; Ivanhoe smelts to 99% copper anode on site and is largely exempt from the choke point the headlines price against "DRC miners" as a class.
Summary of the edge claim: we claim no informational advantage. We claim the risk premium on offer is somewhat too wide versus a reset bar, that three real assets ride along free, and that the volatility is worth more to this specific taxable book than to the average holder. That is the whole claim.
