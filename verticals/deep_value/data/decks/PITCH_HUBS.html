Slide 2 · The setup

A −53% de-rate against rising numbers

  • The stock is off ~53% from its highs. At ~$185 it trades at ~2.1x forward (FY26) sales and ~14x non-GAAP earnings — multiples for a business that has stopped growing.
  • What the market is pricing. Back the scenarios out of the price and the market pays for roughly a 70% chance of the terminal-bear outcome — AI collapsing seat-based CRM for small and mid-sized business. Our read of the current data supports closer to 25%.
  • That ~45-point gap between the market's odds and ours is the trade. It's not an accounting question — disclosure is clean — it's a narrative-versus-numbers divergence: sentiment has run far ahead of anything in the financials.
What could make this wrong: the bear is a forward risk. Current data can look pristine right up to the quarter seat-compression actually starts. That is why this is a 7, not a 9.

Slide 3 · The thesis in plain words

A compounder priced as if it's about to stop

  • HubSpot is a profitable, net-cash, share-count-shrinking software compounder still growing revenue 23% with customers up 16% — priced as if that's about to end.
  • The fear (AI could cut the number of paid seats a business needs) is legitimate as a possibility. But every metric that would show it becoming real today is moving the right way: revenue per customer recovering (+6% reported / ~+2% constant-currency), retention rising, billings outrunning revenue.
  • So the trade is simple: buy a high-quality business at a distressed multiple, ahead of a print more likely to confirm strength than break it — kept small enough that the one un-disprovable risk can't hurt the book.

Slide 5 · The bear we tried to make

What survived the attack — and what we couldn't close

We ran an independent pass whose only job was to break the thesis.

  • The bear: AI lets small businesses run sales and marketing with far fewer paid seats. HubSpot's seat-based model is the most exposed in software. Revenue per customer rolls over, retention cracks, the multiple was right to collapse.
  • What survived contact with the data: on every current datapoint the bear predicts, the number moves the opposite way — ARPC recovering (softer in constant currency), retention rising, billings leading revenue. The mechanism the bear needs is empirically absent this quarter.
  • What we could NOT disprove — and won't pretend to: this is a forward tail. Seat compression, if it comes, shows up later; today's strength doesn't rule out a break in 2027–28.
This is precisely why conviction is capped at 7, not 9. A valid, un-disprovable forward risk sits under a set of pristine current numbers. We size to the tail, not the target.

Slide 9 · Kill triggers

What makes us exit — no debate

  • Revenue per customer flat or negative year-on-year (seat compression finally appears).
  • Net revenue retention below 100%, or the +1–2pt expansion guide walked back.
  • Billings grow slower than revenue for two quarters running.
  • Breeze / AI credit-consumption declines, or Starter-tier churn accelerates.
  • Full-year revenue guide cut (breaks the eight-quarter beat-and-raise streak).
  • Non-GAAP operating margin contracts quarter-on-quarter.
  • Share count reverses, buyback suspended, or a debt-funded acquisition.
  • Re-rate above ~5x sales without estimate support → exit; the cheapness that is the whole thesis is exhausted.
