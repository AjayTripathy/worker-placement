Shift4 Payments (FOUR)

A de-rated, levered compounder priced like a utility

Verdict: Starter now, add on confirmation  ·  Conviction 6/10  ·  Live $49.20  ·  52-wk $34.75–$108.45  ·  fwd EV/EBITDA ~7.9x

The market re-rated a mid-teens organic grower down to a payments-utility multiple on a leverage fear that turns out to be guidance, not a covenant — while the founder was buying stock in the open market and the company quietly removed a $1.04B governance overhang. The catch, stated up front: on genuinely conservative assumptions, fair value sits below today's price. The upside is earned on delivery, not handed over.

Prepared for a self-managed account. Not investment advice. All figures traced to SEC filings, insider Form 4s, and live broker prices.

Slide 2 · The setup

Why it's cheap

  • Shift4 fell from ~$108 to ~$49 in a year — roughly −55% off the high — and now trades at ~7.9x forward EV/EBITDA, in line with slow-growth legacy processors, not a company still guiding to mid-teens gross-revenue-less-network-fees (GRLNF) growth.
  • Three things drove the de-rate: (1) a balance-sheet leverage scare, (2) the Global Blue acquisition, which bolts luxury-tourism cyclicality onto a levered balance sheet, and (3) a broad sell-off in payments multiples.
The task was to separate the fears that are real and permanent from the ones that are misread. Two of the three loudest bear points do not survive the filings; the third — Global Blue cyclicality — is real, and we carry it as the primary risk.

Slide 3 · The thesis, plainly

A still-growing acquirer at a no-growth multiple

  • You are buying growth at a no-growth price — the gap closes if the company simply delivers the organic growth and de-leveraging it already guides to.
  • The single most-cited reason to avoid the stock — dangerously high, constraining leverage — rests on a number management chose to put in its guidance, not a lender-imposed limit. The real lender protection is far looser and nowhere near binding.
  • Management's own money is on the table: the founder and former CEO (current CEO: Taylor Lauber) bought roughly $15.7M of stock in the open market at $44–48 — the prices on offer today.
This is a "the discount is bigger than the problem" idea, not a "hidden fraud" idea. The disclosure is clean; the honest negatives are disclosed by the company itself, and we carry them openly.

Slide 5 · The bear we tried to make

What survived the attack

  • "Leverage forces a dilutive raise or covenant breach." Did not survive. Binding test is ≤3.10x springing secured, only if the revolver is >40% drawn; the company sits well inside it with a de-lever track record.
  • "The Up-C / tax-receivable structure siphons value from public holders." Did not survive. Collapsed, and ~$120M of the liability waived (~$247M remains) [corrected 2026-07-01] — verifiable on the balance sheet.
  • "Insiders are cashing out at the top." Did not survive. The founder was a net buyer at today's prices.
  • "Global Blue is a cyclical, tourism-levered bolt-on." This one survives. A genuine, unhedged exposure to cross-border luxury-tourism spend, on a levered balance sheet. It is why the position is a starter and why a tourism downturn is an explicit kill trigger.
The conservative-case caveat that also survives: on genuinely bearish inputs (~7.25x exit multiple, low EBITDA delivery, no further de-lever), fair value is roughly $43 — below today's $49.20. That is the honest ceiling on conviction here.

Slide 7 · Entry & catalysts

Start before the print, add after it confirms

  • First calibration event: the Q2 2026 print, pre-open July 30, 2026. The first item on our calendar that resolves the thesis — so we start before it and add after it confirms, never building full size into an unproven quarter.
  • What we watch: GRLNF vs the ~$615M guide, adjusted EBITDA vs the ~$278M guide, organic growth ex-legacy above ~7%, continued de-leveraging, and the first hard read on the Global Blue tourism trend.
  • Integration status is company-reported and not independently verifiable until this print — we treat management's integration commentary as a claim to confirm on July 30, not established fact.

We froze a ~62% probability that the print resolves favorably, built from guidance-credibility history and the de-lever track record.

Slide 9 · Kill triggers

What makes us exit or refuse to add

  • Net leverage runs above ~3.9x for two-plus quarters, or the revolver goes past 40% drawn and trips the ≤3.10x springing secured test.
  • Q2 GRLNF comes in materially below ~$615M, or organic growth ex-legacy drops below ~7%.
  • Global Blue tax-free-shopping volumes decline — the luxury-tourism-recession signal.
  • Integration slips, or acquisition add-backs balloon (a sign the "adjusted" numbers are hiding real cost).
  • FY26 adjusted free cash flow tracks below ~$300M.
  • Isaacman and insiders flip to net selling — the conviction signal reverses.
