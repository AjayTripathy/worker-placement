## Slide 3 — How we pick (1/3): what we're actually hunting

We sort every idea into one of four buckets — and only two are buys:

1. **Mispriced visible risk** — a risk everyone can *see* but the market over-charges for. *(Buy.)*
2. **Unpriced fact** — something true the market hasn't recognized yet. *(Buy.)*
3. **Fairly-priced risk** — a real discount that is *correctly* sized. *(Own only if you want the exposure; don't expect an edge.)*
4. **Trap** — cheap-looking, but the cheapness understates a real, often permanent, problem. *(Avoid.)*

> The discipline is refusing to confuse #1 and #2 (real edges) with #3 (fair) and #4 (traps). Most "cheap" stocks are #3 or #4.

*Speaker note: Halyk is a #1; KB is a #1 that has mostly converted to #3; the four passes are one each of #1-already-gone, #3, #3, and #4.*

## Slide 4 — How we pick (2/3): we try to kill our own ideas

Every idea gets **two independent passes**:

- **Pass A — verify the bull case.** Take each claim the thesis rests on and check it against primary filings, not broker summaries.
- **Pass B — attack it.** An independent, skeptical pass that assumes the idea is *wrong* and hunts for the disconfirming fact. It has to be actively refuted before we'll size the position.

Only ideas that **survive being attacked** get capital. This is why the deck shows the tails, not just the upside — if we can't argue ourselves out of a thesis, that's the signal to act.

*Speaker note: this is the differentiator — a promoter shows you the bull case; we show you the bear case we couldn't make stick.*

## Slide 5 — How we pick (3/3): the bank toolkit

Three tools do most of the work on bank ideas — in plain terms:

- **A cross-sectional fair-value model.** Predict a bank's price-to-book from its returns, growth, and country risk, using a pooled set of ~30 developed- and emerging-market banks. Then flag the ones trading *below* what their own fundamentals justify.
- **A "would you rather own the bond?" floor.** A risky bank equity has to offer more excess return than you'd demand to hold the *same country's government bond*. If it doesn't clear that floor, we buy the bond instead of the stock.
- **An anti-lowball discipline.** We have a documented tendency to under-value good businesses when we de-risk them (cutting the growth rate *and* the multiple at once). We correct for it deliberately, and sanity-check every fair value against the most bearish published analyst.

*Speaker note: keep this concrete — these three are exactly what separated Halyk (clears all three) from PKO (fails the first — no discount to capture).*

## Slide 7 — Halyk Bank: the thesis

**A genuinely elite bank at a frontier-market price, where the fear is receding.**

- **What it is:** Kazakhstan's dominant bank. **25% return on equity** last quarter (**34%** in 2024), **~1.1x book**, **investment-grade sovereign** (S&P BBB− / Moody's Baa1 / Fitch BBB, all stable) — and its government bonds trade *tighter* (~85 bps over US Treasuries) than Georgia's.
- **The setup:** it's priced as a "Russia-tarred frontier bank," but the two channels that create that discount are *structurally improving*:
  - It **cut its own Russia retail-payment rails** (dropped the Mir card network Feb-2024, halted the Zolotaya Korona transfer system Aug-2024) — de-risking the sanctions channel itself.
  - The **tenge is appreciating** (+7.6% year-on-year), not devaluing — the other headline fear isn't playing out.

> You're being paid a frontier risk premium on a bank whose two biggest risks are shrinking.

*Speaker note: the "priced for a risk that's receding" is the core asymmetry. Next slide is the decisive proof.*

## Slide 8 — Halyk: the decisive finding

**The single biggest bear case — refuted by the bank's own audited numbers.**

The standard worry about a family-controlled frontier bank is that the controlling owner *loots it* through insider loans. We went to the audited related-party note to test it:

- **Related-party loans are ~0.1% of the entire loan book** (~0.4% of equity).
- The controlling family's entities are net **depositors** at the bank (~KZT 263bn placed *in*), not borrowers.

> The mechanism through which frontier-bank value usually gets destroyed is **empirically absent** here. That's not "unproven" — it's disproven, in the audit.

**The honest offsets (we're not hiding them):**
- The 34% return was a **rate-cycle peak**; the central bank is now cutting, and the durable mid-cycle return is likely **~20–24%** — still excellent, but this caps the upside.
- Controlling holder ALMEX (~62%) is **selling down** — it placed 7.6% in Nov-2025 at $23; a manageable overhang, and the stock is ~30% above that price.

*Speaker note: B8 is the "we tried to kill it and couldn't" slide — the looting bear is the one that would break the thesis, and it fails.*

## Slide 10 — Halyk: risks, triggers, and tradeability

**We size to the tail, not the target.** The dangerous risks are binary and hard to hedge:

- **Secondary-sanctions / correspondent-bank action** — a low-probability but *binary* event that would flip this from an edge to a trap overnight.
- **Tenge devaluation** — a *gap* risk (2015 and 2022 both saw sudden 25%+ moves), tied to oil.
- **Durable-return compression** below ~18% as the rate cycle turns.
- **Single-country, oil-linked concentration** and thin trading in the listed shares.

**Kill triggers (we exit if):** a sanctions/correspondent action touches Halyk · the sovereign loses investment grade · durable return falls below ~18% · related-party loans balloon above ~2–3% of the book · a tenge devaluation event · a family-stake expropriation.

**Tradeability — the one open item:** the only tradeable line is a seasoned Reg-S depositary receipt on the London international board (liquid, ~$1.7M/day, carried by our broker, not sanctioned). It is *very likely* tradeable in the account, but Reg-S lines can be blocked at order entry for US persons — **we confirm this by staging the order; a rejection is an automatic pass.**

*Speaker note: the tradeability caveat is the Ferrexpo lesson applied — never assume you can exit.*

## Slide 11 — Halyk: sizing and structure

**Our best idea — sized as a starter, not a slug.**

- **Target 2.5%** of the book — the *largest* single position among the survivors, honoring that it's the genuine edge.
- **Why not bigger?** Size scales with edge × confidence × *how bounded the downside is*. Halyk is strong on edge, only 6/10 on confidence, and has a **binary, uninsurable tail** (sanctions) plus **gap-risk FX**. A 6/10 idea with an overnight −40% tail can't be allowed to drive the book's drawdown — at 2.5% a −40% shock costs ~1% of the book; at 5% it costs ~2%.
- **Structure:** start ~1% now, **earn up to full size by averaging into $28 / $25** — the edge is mechanically *larger* at a lower price — and only after the order-entry check clears. Full conviction below $24; don't chase above $32.

*Speaker note: this is the answer to "if it's an edge why not size up" — because the tail is binary, you put the size in where the edge widens, not at the top.*

## Slide 12 — KB Financial: the quality-carry thesis

**The cleanest large Korean bank, with a legislated tailwind.**

- **Best-in-class capital and payout:** highest capital ratio of the big four (13.8%), a **52.4% total-shareholder-return ratio** in 2025, ~11% return on equity.
- **A real, legislated catalyst:** Korea's amended company law now **mandates cancellation of buyback shares** (effective March 2026) and expands directors' duty to *all* shareholders — the machinery of the long-awaited "Korea discount" unwind.
- **The payout is the point:** ~3% dividend plus buy-back-and-cancel gives a **~8% annual cash-return floor**, backed by the group's excess capital.

*Speaker note: KB is the "own it for the carry" idea — set up the honest downgrade on the next slide.*

## Slide 13 — KB: the honest downgrade, valuation, and sizing

**We verified every bull number — and then downgraded it anyway.**

- **What the deep dive found:** the cheap re-rate is **~90% spent.** KB already trades at ~1.0x book (not the ~0.85x the screen implied); the market has already closed most of the discount. And the new cancellation law mainly re-rates the *laggard* banks — KB already does this voluntarily, so it's less of a *marginal* beneficiary than the headline suggests.
- **So we reclassified it:** from a re-rating trade to a durable **~8% cash-return carry** position, and cut the size.

**Valuation:** live ~$100.81; fair-value bear/base/bull **$84 / $112 / $142**; expected ~**+17% total return** (price + cash). Base value is set deliberately *below* the average analyst target, per our anti-lowball sanity-check against the bearish end.

**Sizing:** **1.5%**, add in the low-$90s. **Currency left unhedged** — the won is ~15–20% below fair value, so the exchange rate is a tailwind option, not a drag.

*Speaker note: the credibility move here is "we checked, it's real, and we still cut it" — the opposite of talking our own book.*

## Slide 14 — Portfolio construction & sizing framework

**Two rules govern how these enter the book:**

1. **Size = edge × confidence × bounded-downside.** High-confidence, bounded-downside edges get sized up. A real-but-modest edge with a binary tail (Halyk) is *capped* even though it's our best idea. Carry positions (KB) are sized for the income, not for conviction.
2. **Cap the correlated sleeve, not just the name.** Halyk is the third bank in a **frontier political-premium sleeve** (Georgia: TBC + BGEO; Kazakhstan: Halyk) that shares tails — EM political, sanctions, commodity/FX. We hard-cap the **combined sleeve at ~5–6%** so three ~2% "starters" don't quietly compound into one 6% correlated bet.

| | Target | Enters as |
|---|---|---|
| Halyk | 2.5% | ~1% now, average into $28/$25 |
| KB | 1.5% | half now, add low-$90s |
| Frontier sleeve cap | ~5–6% | TBC + BGEO + Halyk combined |

*Speaker note: the sleeve cap is the real risk control — it's why Halyk being "the edge" still doesn't mean 5%.*
