Slide 3 · How we pick (1/3)

What we're actually hunting

Every idea sorts into four buckets — only two are buys:

  • 1 · Mispriced visible risk — a risk everyone sees but the market over-charges for. Buy.
  • 2 · Unpriced fact — something true the market hasn't recognized yet. Buy.
  • 3 · Fairly-priced risk — a real discount, correctly sized. Own only if you want the exposure.
  • 4 · Trap — cheap-looking, but the cheapness understates a real, often permanent, problem. Avoid.
The discipline is refusing to confuse #1 and #2 (edges) with #3 (fair) and #4 (traps). Most "cheap" stocks are #3 or #4.

Slide 4 · How we pick (2/3)

We try to kill our own ideas

Every idea gets two independent passes:

  • Pass A — verify the bull case. Every claim the thesis rests on, checked against primary filings, not broker summaries.
  • Pass B — attack it. An independent, skeptical pass that assumes the idea is wrong and hunts the disconfirming fact. It must be actively refuted before we size the position.
Only ideas that survive being attacked get capital. That's why this deck shows the tails, not just the upside — a promoter shows you the bull case; we show you the bear case we couldn't make stick.

Slide 5 · How we pick (3/3)

The bank toolkit

  • A cross-sectional fair-value model. Predict a bank's price-to-book from its returns, growth, and country risk across ~30 developed- and emerging-market banks — then flag the ones trading below what their fundamentals justify.
  • A "would you rather own the bond?" floor. A risky bank equity must offer more excess return than you'd demand to hold the same country's government bond. If it doesn't clear that floor, we buy the bond.
  • An anti-lowball discipline. We correct for our own tendency to under-value good businesses when de-risking them, and sanity-check every fair value against the most bearish published analyst.

These three are exactly what separated Halyk (clears all three) from PKO (fails the first — no discount to capture).

Slide 7 · Halyk Bank

The thesis

An elite bank at a frontier price, where the fear is receding.

  • What it is: Kazakhstan's dominant bank. 25% return on equity last quarter (34% in 2024), ~1.1x book, investment-grade sovereign (BBB−/Baa1/BBB, all stable) — its government bonds trade tighter (~85 bps) than Georgia's.
  • The setup: priced as a "Russia-tarred frontier bank," but the two channels creating that discount are improving:
    • It cut its own Russia retail-payment rails (dropped Mir Feb-2024, halted Zolotaya Korona Aug-2024).
    • The tenge is appreciating (+7.6% year-on-year), not devaluing.
You're paid a frontier risk premium on a bank whose two biggest risks are shrinking.

Slide 8 · Halyk

The decisive finding

The biggest bear case — refuted by the bank's own audited numbers.

The standard worry about a family-controlled frontier bank is that the owner loots it via insider loans. We went to the audited related-party note:

  • Related-party loans are ~0.1% of the loan book (~0.4% of equity).
  • The family's entities are net depositors (~KZT 263bn placed in), not borrowers.
The mechanism through which frontier-bank value usually gets destroyed is empirically absent — not "unproven," but disproven, in the audit.

The honest offsets:

  • The 34% return was a rate-cycle peak; durable mid-cycle is likely ~20–24% — still excellent, but it caps the upside.
  • Controlling holder ALMEX (~62%) is selling down (placed 7.6% in Nov-2025 at $23) — a manageable overhang.

Slide 10 · Halyk

Risks, triggers & tradeability

We size to the tail, not the target. The dangerous risks are binary and hard to hedge:

  • Secondary-sanctions / correspondent-bank action — low-probability but binary; would flip edge → trap overnight.
  • Tenge devaluation — a gap risk (2015 and 2022 saw sudden 25%+ moves), tied to oil.
  • Durable-return compression below ~18%; single-country, oil-linked concentration; thin trading.

Kill triggers: a sanctions/correspondent action · sovereign loses investment grade · durable return <18% · related-party loans >2–3% of book · a tenge devaluation event · a family-stake expropriation.

Tradeability — the open item: the only line is a seasoned Reg-S depositary receipt on the London international board (liquid, ~$1.7M/day, broker-carried, not sanctioned). Very likely tradeable, but Reg-S lines can be blocked at order entry for US persons — we confirm by staging the order; a rejection is an automatic pass.

Slide 11 · Halyk

Sizing and structure

Our best idea — sized as a starter, not a slug.

  • Target 2.5% — the largest single position among the survivors, honoring that it's the genuine edge.
  • Why not bigger? Size scales with edge × confidence × how bounded the downside is. Halyk is strong on edge, only 6/10 on confidence, with a binary, uninsurable tail (sanctions) plus gap-risk FX. At 2.5% a −40% shock costs ~1% of the book; at 5% it costs ~2%.
  • Structure: start ~1% now, earn up to full size by averaging into $28 / $25 — the edge is mechanically larger at a lower price — and only after the order-entry check clears. Full conviction <$24; don't chase >$32.

This is the answer to "if it's an edge, why not size up?" — because the tail is binary, you put the size in where the edge widens, not at the top.

Slide 12 · KB Financial

The quality-carry thesis

The cleanest large Korean bank, with a legislated tailwind.

  • Best-in-class capital and payout: highest capital ratio of the big four (13.8%), a 52.4% total-shareholder-return ratio in 2025, ~11% return on equity.
  • A real, legislated catalyst: Korea's amended company law now mandates cancellation of buyback shares (effective March 2026) and expands directors' duty to all shareholders.
  • The payout is the point: ~3% dividend plus buy-back-and-cancel gives a ~8% annual cash-return floor, backed by excess capital.

Slide 13 · KB

The honest downgrade, valuation & sizing

We verified every bull number — and downgraded it anyway.

  • The deep dive found the cheap re-rate is ~90% spent: KB already trades ~1.0x book (not the ~0.85x the screen implied). The new cancellation law mainly re-rates laggard banks — KB already does this voluntarily.
  • Reclassified from a re-rating trade to a durable ~8% cash-return carry, and cut the size.

Valuation: live ~$100.81; bear/base/bull $84 / $112 / $142; expected ~+17% total return. Base is set deliberately below the average analyst target (anti-lowball sanity-check).

Sizing: 1.5%, add in the low-$90s. Currency unhedged — the won is ~15–20% below fair value, so FX is a tailwind option.

The credibility move: "we checked, it's real, and we still cut it" — the opposite of talking our own book.

Slide 14 · Portfolio construction

Sizing framework

  • Size = edge × confidence × bounded-downside. High-confidence, bounded-downside edges get sized up. A real-but-modest edge with a binary tail (Halyk) is capped even though it's our best idea. Carry positions (KB) are sized for income, not conviction.
  • Cap the correlated sleeve, not just the name. Halyk is the third bank in a frontier political-premium sleeve (Georgia: TBC + BGEO; Kazakhstan: Halyk) that shares tails — EM political, sanctions, commodity/FX.
