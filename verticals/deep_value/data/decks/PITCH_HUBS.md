# HubSpot (HUBS) — Investment Review

**Prepared for a self-managed private account · 2026-07-01 · Not investment advice.**

Verdict: **BUY** — fear-driven dislocation in a business whose numbers are still accelerating.
Conviction: **7 / 10.** Spot **~$185**. Probability-weighted fair value **~$281** (**+52%**).

One line: *The market is pricing a growth collapse that HubSpot's own current filings contradict — so we buy the quality before the Aug-5 print, sized to the one risk we can't yet disprove.*

---

## Slide 2 — The setup: a −53% de-rate against rising numbers

- HUBS has de-rated roughly **−53%** from its highs. At **~$185** it trades at **~2.1x** forward (FY26) enterprise-value-to-sales and **~14x** non-GAAP earnings — multiples normally reserved for a business that has stopped growing.
- **What the market is pricing.** Back the scenario probabilities out of the price and the market is paying for roughly a **70% chance of the terminal-bear outcome** — i.e. that AI collapses seat-based CRM for small and mid-sized businesses and growth breaks. Our read of the current data supports closer to a **25%** chance.
- That ~45-point gap between the market's implied odds and ours is the entire trade. It is not an accounting question — disclosure is clean — it is a **narrative-versus-numbers divergence**: sentiment has moved far ahead of anything visible in the financials.

> What could make this wrong: the bear is a *forward* risk (see Slide 5). Current data can look pristine right up to the quarter the seat-compression actually starts. That is why this is a 7, not a 9.

---

## Slide 3 — The thesis in plain words

- HubSpot is a **profitable, net-cash, share-count-shrinking** software compounder that is still **growing revenue 23%** with **customer counts up 16%** — and it is priced as if that is about to stop.
- The fear driving the discount is legitimate as a *possibility* (AI could compress the number of paid seats businesses need). But every metric that would show that fear *becoming real today* is instead moving the **right** way: revenue per customer is re-accelerating, net revenue retention is rising, and billings are outrunning revenue.
- So the trade is simple: **buy a high-quality business at a distressed multiple, ahead of a print that is more likely to confirm the strength than break it** — and keep the position small enough that the one un-disprovable risk can't hurt the book.

## Slide 5 — The bear case we tried to make, and what survived

We ran an independent pass whose only job was to **break the thesis**. Here is the strongest bear we could build, and what happened to it.

- **The bear:** AI agents let small businesses run sales and marketing with far fewer paid seats. HubSpot's seat-based CRM is the most exposed model in software. Revenue per customer rolls over, retention cracks, and the multiple was right to collapse.
- **What survived contact with the data:** on **every current datapoint the bear predicts, the number is moving the opposite way** — ARPC is recovering (+6% reported, ~+2% constant-currency — not compressing), retention is *rising* (not cracking), billings *lead* revenue (not lag). The mechanism the bear needs is empirically absent this quarter.
- **What we could NOT disprove — and won't pretend to:** this is a **forward tail.** Seat compression, if it comes, shows up *later* — the current strength doesn't rule out a break in 2027-28. We could not manufacture a *present-tense* version of the bear, but we also could not close the *future* one.

> This is precisely why conviction is capped at **7, not 9.** A valid, un-disprovable forward risk sits underneath a set of pristine current numbers. We size to the tail, not the target.

## Slide 7 — Entry structure and the print

**Decision: enter BEFORE the Aug-5 print.**

- **Catalyst:** Q2 FY26 reports **2026-08-05, after market close.** Options imply a **~11–14% move** (implied volatility ~80%).
- **Why enter before a binary event.** Normally we wait. Here the **bad-news asymmetry is largely spent**: the multiple already prices the bear, so a miss gaps into our add zone (~$160, above bear FV $145) while a beat re-rates a stock the whole market is offside on. The eight-quarter beat streak and every leading indicator argue the print confirms rather than breaks.
- **Structure:**
  - **Core now** in the **$181–190** band (this is the pre-print entry).
  - **Add $155–170** on any print or macro weakness — the edge is mechanically larger at a lower price.
- **The honest catch on timing — corrected 2026-07-01:** Aug-5 is the **second consecutive Breeze consumption disclosure**, not a cold reveal — Q1 already printed the baseline (**credits +67% QoQ; Customer Agent 9,000+ customers at 70% resolution; Prospecting 14,000 customers; Data Agent +122% QoQ**). The bar is the *sequential trajectory*, a much lower hurdle. Grade the print on sequential consumption + retention direction; a missing standalone Breeze revenue figure is **not** a failure (peers rarely print one in year one: Intercom Fin reached $100M+ ARR and Salesforce's Agentforce ~$800M ARR — still ~2% of its revenue). Evidence sweep refined the joint odds to ~0.63 vs the 0.70 originally penciled — the difference is conjunction math (beat AND raise AND constructive-AI), not AI weakness.

## Slide 9 — Kill triggers

We exit or stand down — no debate — if any of these print. Stated plainly:

- **Revenue per customer goes flat or negative year-on-year** (seat compression finally appears).
- **Net revenue retention drops below 100%,** *or* the +1–2pt expansion guide is walked back.
- **Billings grow slower than revenue for two quarters running.**
- **Breeze / AI credit-consumption declines,** *or* Starter-tier churn accelerates.
- **The full-year revenue guide is cut** (breaks the eight-quarter beat-and-raise streak).
- **Non-GAAP operating margin contracts quarter-on-quarter.**
- **Share count reverses, the buyback is suspended, or the balance sheet funds an acquisition with debt.**
- **The stock re-rates above ~5x sales without estimate support** → exit; the cheapness that is the whole thesis would be exhausted.
