Burberry Group (BRBY.L)

An early, real turnaround where the edge lives in the up-tail

Verdict: 1.0% starter now, gated add on July 17  ·  Conviction 5/10  ·  Live ~1054 GBp  ·  52-wk 985–1377 GBp

The margin recovery is genuine and quality-led — gross margin rose +530bps to 67.9% on only +2% comparable sales, which the "it's just cost-cuts" bear cannot explain — and comps accelerated every quarter through FY26. But base-case fair value is roughly at today's price, so the return you're paid for lives in the up-tail, not the middle. That is why it's a small, gated starter, not a slug.

New in v2: this thesis has now been through five independent reviews. Each one made it smaller and sharper — the size was cut, the edge claim was narrowed to what one verified number supports, and a brand-attention check was added.

Slide 3 · The setup

Why it's cheap

  • Burberry sits near a de-rated 52-week low (985–1022 support), down from ~1377 GBp, on a genuinely weak FY24–early-FY25, a suspended dividend, and a soft luxury tape.
  • The consensus bear read was that any margin recovery was "cost-cuts only" — a business shrinking to profitability, not a brand regaining pricing power.
  • We tested that read against the company's own reported numbers. It does not hold: the margin gain came with rising full-price sell-through and accelerating comps — the signature of a quality recovery, not a cost story. That single fact is the crux of the whole thesis.

Slide 4 · The thesis, plainly

Real recovery, and the right kind

  • The recovery is real and it's the right kind. Gross margin up +530bps to 67.9% on just +2% comps means the profit came from selling more at full price, not cutting to the bone. The ~£80m of opex cuts are additive, not the source.
  • The trajectory improves quarter by quarter. FY26 comps went −1 → +2 → +3 → +5 — a clean sequential acceleration, not a one-off bounce.
  • The balance sheet is run honestly. Net debt fell to £852m (1.6x) and the dividend remains suspended — discipline over a cosmetic payout, which we read as a positive tell.
  • Disclosure is clean. No gap between what management markets and what the numbers show — a straight fundamental-normalization call, not a company overstating its progress.

The bear cases we tried to make — and what stuck:

  • "Margin is just cost-cutting on a shrinking brand." Did not survive — full-price mix recovery; cost cuts are additive.
  • "The comps are a dead-cat bounce." Did not survive — four straight quarters of acceleration is a trend.
  • "The suspended dividend signals distress." Weakened — net debt is falling; we read it as deliberate.
  • "The core region still isn't working." This survives — EMEIA, the largest region, is flat-to−2%.

Slide 6 · The corrected edge

What we do — and don't — claim

The skeptic pass forced us to throw out the easier story. This is what's left — deliberately narrow.

  • What we are NOT claiming. We are not claiming the market is behaviorally mispricing Burberry by lumping it in with a collapsing sector. That story doesn't survive its own data: LVMH is flat-to-up (+1%) while Kering/Gucci collapse (−14%) — the market plainly discriminates within luxury. Burberry's price probably reflects Burberry's own facts (weak EMEIA, easy comps). We removed that claim.
  • What we ARE claiming — narrowly. A differing probability on the up-tail. We put the odds of the full-normalization path at ~72% versus the ~62% implied by our scenario tree at today's price. That ~10-point gap is the entire edge.
  • What it rests on — one verified number. We weight the +530bps full-price gross-margin recovery more than the market does, because it's a mix-quality fact that promotion-driven comps physically cannot produce. That is the whole grounding — one clean, primary-sourced datum. No behavioral mechanism is claimed.
  • What could make this wrong (the falsifier). If July 17 comps hold but the stock does not re-rate, then the market was weighting EMEIA/China correctly and the margin fact was already in the price — the disagreement resolves against us; the thesis was priced, not broken. That is the most likely way to be wrong without anything "failing."
Base fair value is essentially at today's price. All of the return lives in the up-tail. A modest, multiple-sensitive edge grounded in one datum earns a small gated starter — not conviction size.

Slide 7 · Brand-attention check

A demand signal — with honest error bars

The two consumer-demand signals we track disagree in a specifically bullish way — and the tool is noisy.

  • Search interest is at an 8-quarter high. Google search interest for Burberry is +35% year-on-year in exactly the quarter July 17 reports — the strongest reading in the name's eight-quarter history (prior best +19%).
  • News/curiosity attention is down. Wikipedia and news pageviews are −22% year-on-year over the same window.
  • Why the divergence matters. Search rising while news falls means commercial interest is climbing without a news event — the pattern that looks like real shopping demand. Contrast Gucci, where both spiked on the Demna designer drama while sales printed −14% — attention, not demand.
  • Honest instrument quality. Across the full peer set, the correlation with printed sales is modest once outliers are removed (~0.41–0.46) — the eye-catching 0.90 headline was driven almost entirely by one name (Miu Miu). Directional, never precise. Within Burberry the signal has tracked the direction of comps in 8 of the last 8 quarters (a tighter ~0.67), which is what gives it any weight here.
Verdict: mild corroboration. It supports placing the small starter before July 17 rather than waiting. It does not change the size.
The "is it priced in?" test — run, and honestly NULL. Across ~50 luxury print events, high attention did NOT predict the stock's reaction to the print (correlations ~0 to −0.18; the two instruments even disagree on direction) — and printed sales levels themselves don't predict reactions either (−0.11): luxury prints trade on surprise vs expectations, not headline levels. Attention carries no print-trading edge; it is used here only as timing corroboration at unchanged size.
Burberry-specific warning: the last three positive-comp prints all saw negative 4-day reactions (+2%→−5%, +3%→−12.6%, +5%→−6.9%) — good news has recently been sold on this name. The July-17 add-gate is about the fundamental confirmation (comps ≥+3–4%, EMEIA ≥−3%), not an instant pop. Mitigant: unlike before those prints, the stock now sits near its 52-week low.

  • Start now with the 1.0% starter: 940 shares at a 1060 GBp limit, inside the 1000–1080 GBp band (support 985–1022, the 13-week low).

  • Add to 3–5% only if July 17 confirms — specifically group comps at roughly +3–4% or better and EMEIA no worse than −3%. If either fails, we do not add.

Slide 10 · Sizing & scale path

Small now, real size only on validation

  • Starter: 1.0% now (rebuttal-trimmed from 1.5%). Add to 3–5% only on a confirming July 17. Hard cap 5%.
  • Why capped: the edge is modest and multiple-sensitive (base fair value ≈ price), the largest region is still soft, and a broad luxury sell-off is an exogenous tail we cannot hedge. Size scales with edge × confidence × how bounded the downside is — here the edge is thin and the sector tail is open.
  • Why staged: a small position rides the easy-base July print; real size goes in only after the recovery is validated, into the November catalyst. We pay for confirmation rather than pre-committing.

What could make the sizing wrong: if July 17 confirms and the stock re-rates immediately, the gated structure means we add higher and capture less of the move. We accept that cost in exchange for not sizing up on an un-validated up-tail.

Slide 11 · Kill triggers

Where we exit or refuse to add

  • The July 17 group comp comes in below ~+2%, or negative.
  • EMEIA falls below −5%, or Greater China swings back to negative — the easy-base tailwind exposed as base-effect, not recovery.
  • Gross margin rolls over or the brand returns to markdown selling — the quality thesis breaks.
  • The November H1 result fails to expand operating margin year-on-year, or FY27 margin-expansion guidance is walked back.
  • A fifth strategy pivot in a decade, or the CEO (Schulman) exits — the execution continuity we're underwriting is gone.
  • A broad luxury sector leg-down (Gucci/Kering contagion, a China demand break).
