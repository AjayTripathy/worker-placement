Slide 2 · The setup

A sales multiple cut to a third, on a real debate

  • monday.com trades at 1.66x forward (FY26) sales. Its own historical band ran near a 4.6x median — so the multiple has compressed to roughly a third of where it lived, even as revenue keeps growing.
  • What the market is pricing. Back the scenarios out of the price and the market pays for roughly a 58% chance of the bear / terminal-impairment case — AI eventually hollowing out horizontal work-management — and assigns essentially zero value to the bull.
  • Our data supports a materially lower bear probability. That ~28-point gap between the market's odds and ours is the trade. This is not an honesty catch — disclosure is clean throughout; it's a probability disagreement on a crowded, emotional debate.
What could make this wrong: one cohort is actually cracking (Slide 5). The bear isn't imaginary — it has one real foothold, and that is our number-one watch item.

Slide 3 · The thesis in plain words

Priced as if impaired — while still growing and buying back stock

  • monday.com is profitable, growing revenue ~24%, generating free cash, buying back stock, and sitting on net cash worth a third of its market value. It is priced as if the business is impaired.
  • The deceleration bears point to is real but gradual — a twelve-quarter glide of ~2 points per quarter, not a cliff — and management has been honest about it, even guiding retention down rather than hiding it.
  • So the trade is: buy a cash-rich, still-growing business at a liquidation-adjacent multiple, accept a slow re-rate because the debate is crowded, and size in patiently — starting small now and adding into the post-print weakness this stock reliably hands you.

Slide 5 · The bear we tried to make

Here part of the bear stuck — and we lead with it

We ran an independent pass built to break the thesis. Unlike HubSpot, one finding survived.

  • The bear: AI compresses seats and pricing across horizontal work-management; the biggest customers defect first; net-new expansion stalls; the multiple was right to collapse toward the cash.
  • What survived — the real crack: the >$100k-ARR cohort cracked in Q1. Net-adds fell to +88 — the low of the series — and its retention inverted below the smaller >$50k cohort. Large-customer expansion is where an AI-seat bear shows up first, and it is exactly where the softness appeared. A genuine, disconfirming finding — the single most important thing to watch.
  • What did NOT break: the self-serve / small-customer base is still healthy, free cash flow is real, the balance sheet is a floor, and the overall glide has no cliff. The bear has one foothold, not the whole house.
This is why we enter small and patient, not big and now. The thesis is intact but has a live fault line; the >$100k cohort is on a two-quarter clock (Slide 9).

Slide 7 · Entry & the print

Enter AFTER — because this stock sells off on good news

  • Catalyst: Q2 FY26 prints ~Aug-10 to Aug-17 (our estimate — not company-confirmed). Frozen read: a headline beat (~$362–368M revenue), AI-consumption detail inconclusive, retention ~109–110%.
  • Why bias entry to after the print. This stock has a documented habit of selling off on good news: recent reactions to beats were −30% / −12% / −21%. Buying before means paying up for a catalyst that historically fades — so we let the recurring gap-down do the accumulating.
  • Structure:
    • Starter now — justified purely by the net-cash floor and a Street low above spot; you are not paying for the catalyst.
    • Build into post-print weakness (~Aug-10+), targeting the $58–84 accumulation band, sweet spot below $72.
Contrast with HubSpot deliberately: HUBS we enter before its print because the bad news is already priced; MNDY we enter after because good news here tends to be sold. Same discipline, opposite tactic — driven by each stock's own reaction pattern.

Slide 9 · Kill triggers

What makes us exit — no debate

  • Overall net-dollar retention below 108%, or the >$100k cohort's retention below 112%.
  • >$100k net-adds decline for a second straight quarter (Q1's +88 was already the series low).
  • Revenue prints below guidance (breaks the five-for-five beat pattern).
  • AI as a share of net-new ARR stalls off ~3% over two-to-three quarters.
  • Net-adds in the >10-user base turn negative (self-serve / search-driven erosion reaching the core).
  • Free-cash-flow-margin guide cut below 18%, or cash-torching M&A — the net-cash floor is load-bearing.
