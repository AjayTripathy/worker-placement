**Shift4 Payments — a de-rated, levered compounder priced like a utility**

## Slide 2 — The setup

- Shift4 fell from ~$108 to ~$49 over the past year — roughly **−55% off the high** — and now trades at **~7.9x forward EV/EBITDA**, in line with slow-growth legacy processors rather than a company still guiding to mid-teens gross-revenue-less-network-fees (GRLNF) growth.
- Three things drove the de-rate: (1) a **balance-sheet leverage scare**, (2) the **Global Blue acquisition**, which bolts luxury-tourism cyclicality onto a levered balance sheet, and (3) a broader sell-off in payments multiples.
- Our job was to separate the fears that are real and permanent from the ones that are misread. Two of the three loudest bear points do not survive contact with the filings; the third (Global Blue cyclicality) is real and we carry it as the primary risk.

---

## Slide 3 — The thesis, plainly

- You are buying a **still-growing acquirer at a no-growth multiple** — the gap closes if the company simply delivers the organic growth and de-leveraging it already guides to.
- The single most-cited reason to avoid the stock — that leverage is dangerously high and constraining — is **based on a number the company chose to put in its guidance, not a lender-imposed limit**. The actual lender protection is far looser and is not close to binding.
- Management's own money is on the table: the founder and former CEO (current CEO: Taylor Lauber) bought roughly **$15.7M of stock in the open market at $44–48** — the same prices on offer today.
- This is a "the discount is bigger than the problem" idea, not a "hidden fraud" idea. The disclosure is clean; the honest negatives are disclosed by the company itself, and we carry them openly below.

## Slide 4 — What we verified against the primary filings

We checked every load-bearing claim against SEC filings and insider Form 4s, not broker notes.

| Claim under test | What the primary source shows | Read |
|---|---|---|
| "Leverage cap is a hard covenant" | The **~3.7x is management guidance, not a covenant.** The only lender covenant is a *springing* secured-leverage test at **≤3.10x that only activates if the revolver is >40% drawn.** Net leverage has averaged **~3.1x since 2021** across three prior de-lever cycles. | **Defused** |
| Founder conviction | Founder and former CEO Jared Isaacman made **~$15.7M of open-market purchases at $44–48**, confirmed on **SEC Form 4s** — real buys, not option exercises. | **Verified** |
| Governance overhang | The dual-class "Up-C" structure was **collapsed**: Class B/C shares now **zero**, a single **79.33M Class A** line remains, and the related tax-receivable liability was **reduced — ~$120M (the Rook portion) waived; ~$247M remains on the balance sheet** *(corrected 2026-07-01; the original "$542M terminated" claim was wrong)*. | **Verified on the balance sheet** |
| Cap structure above the common | **10.42M shares of Series A mandatory-convertible preferred** (~**$1.04B liquidation preference**, 6%, converts ~May 2028) sit **senior to common** and are already in our enterprise value. Net debt ~**$4.09B**. | **Verified — and priced in** |

*The honest negatives, also from the company's own disclosure:* adjusted-FCF conversion is only **~4%** (self-disclosed), stock-based comp runs ~**7% of adjusted EBITDA**, and Global Blue adds **luxury-tourism cyclicality on a levered balance sheet**.

## Slide 5 — The bear case we tried to make, and what survived

We built the most aggressive bear we honestly could and tested it against the filings:

- **"Leverage forces a dilutive raise or a covenant breach."** *Did not survive.* The binding lender test is ≤3.10x springing secured, only when the revolver is >40% drawn; the company sits well inside it with a track record of de-levering.
- **"The Up-C / tax-receivable structure siphons value from public holders."** *Did not survive.* The structure was collapsed and ~$120M of the liability waived (~$247M remains) [corrected 2026-07-01] — verifiable on the balance sheet.
- **"Insiders are cashing out at the top."** *Did not survive.* The founder was a **net buyer** in the open market at today's prices.
- **"Global Blue is a cyclical, tourism-levered bolt-on."** *This one survives.* It is a genuine, unhedged exposure to cross-border luxury-tourism spend, sitting on a levered balance sheet. We do not paper over it — it is why the position is a starter and why a tourism downturn is an explicit kill trigger.
- **The conservative-case caveat that also survives:** on a genuinely bearish set of inputs (a ~7.25x exit multiple, low EBITDA delivery, no further de-leveraging), fair value is roughly **$43 — below today's $49.20.** That is the honest ceiling on conviction here.

## Slide 6 — Valuation

Three scenarios, each with its own fair value and our own probability. Live price **$49.20**.

| Scenario | Fair value | Prob. | vs. live |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** — multiple compression, tourism/leverage bite, no re-rate | **$27** | 30% | −45% |
| **Base** — organic growth delivered, gradual de-lever, partial re-rate | **$67** | 45% | +36% |
| **Bull** — mid-teens organic holds, tourism tailwind, full re-rate | **$113** | 25% | +130% |

- **Probability-weighted fair value ≈ $66.5 → about +35% versus the live price.**
- The **market is implying roughly a 23% chance** of the favorable path; our blend is higher, which is where the raw edge comes from.
- **But apply the conservative guard** — a deliberately pessimistic exit multiple and no de-lever — and fair value lands near **$43, below the current price.** That is deliberate: it is why this is a **6, not an 8**. The upside is contingent on delivery you can watch quarter by quarter, not a fat pitch you buy and forget. Net of that guard, the effective edge is a more sober **~+15–20%**, not the headline +35%.

## Slide 7 — Entry structure & catalysts

- **First calibration event: the Q2 2026 print, pre-open on July 30, 2026.** This is the first item on our calendar that resolves the thesis, so we **start before it and add after it confirms** — we do not build full size into an unproven quarter.
- **What we're watching in the print:** GRLNF against the **~$615M guide**, adjusted EBITDA against the **~$278M guide**, organic growth **ex-legacy above ~7%**, evidence of continued **de-leveraging**, and the first hard read on the **Global Blue tourism trend**.
- **Integration status is company-reported and not independently verifiable until this print** — we treat management's integration commentary as a claim to be confirmed on July 30, not as established fact.
- We froze a **~62% probability** that the print resolves favorably, built from guidance-credibility history and the de-lever track record.

## Slide 8 — Sizing & scale path

- **Starter now: 2–3%** at $43–49, sized to the tail, not the target.
- **Add to 3–5% only after July 30 confirms** organic growth, continued de-leveraging, *and* a stable tourism trend at Global Blue.
- **Hard cap 5%.** The reason it is capped even though the raw edge is large: the downside has a **cyclical, unhedged tail (Global Blue)** layered on **~$4.1B net debt**, and the conservative fair value is below today's price. Size goes in where the edge widens and after the first data point, not at the top of an unproven quarter.
- Sizing logic: **edge × confidence × how bounded the downside is.** Strong on edge, only 6/10 on confidence, with a genuinely open cyclical tail — so it earns a starter that scales on confirmation, not a slug up front.

## Slide 9 — Kill triggers (plain language)

We exit or refuse to add if:

- Net leverage runs **above ~3.9x for two-plus quarters**, or the **revolver goes past 40% drawn** and trips the ≤3.10x springing secured test.
- Q2 GRLNF comes in **materially below ~$615M**, or organic growth ex-legacy drops **below ~7%**.
- **Global Blue tax-free-shopping volumes decline** — the luxury-tourism-recession signal.
- **Integration slips**, or acquisition add-backs start ballooning (a sign the "adjusted" numbers are hiding real cost).
- **FY26 adjusted free cash flow tracks below ~$300M.**
- **Isaacman and insiders flip to net selling** — the conviction signal reverses.
