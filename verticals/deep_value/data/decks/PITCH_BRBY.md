## Slide 3 — The setup

- Burberry sits near a de-rated **52-week low** (985–1022 support), down from ~1377 GBp, on a genuinely weak FY24–early-FY25, a suspended dividend, and a soft luxury tape.
- The consensus bear read was that any margin recovery was **"cost-cuts only"** — a business shrinking to profitability, not a brand regaining pricing power.
- We tested that read against the company's own reported numbers. It does not hold: the margin gain came with **rising full-price sell-through and accelerating comps**, which is the signature of a *quality* recovery, not a cost story. That single fact is the crux of the whole thesis.

---

## Slide 4 — The thesis, plainly

- **The recovery is real and it's the right kind.** Gross margin up **+530bps to 67.9%** on just **+2% comps** means the profit came from **selling more at full price**, not from cutting to the bone. The ~£80m of opex cuts are *additive* on top of that, not the source.
- **The trajectory is improving quarter by quarter.** FY26 comps went **−1 → +2 → +3 → +5** across the four quarters — a clean sequential acceleration, not a one-off bounce.
- **The balance sheet is being run honestly.** Net debt fell to **£852m (1.6x)** and the **dividend remains suspended** — management is choosing discipline over a cosmetic payout, which we read as a positive tell.
- **Disclosure is clean.** There is no gap between what management markets and what the numbers show — this is a straight fundamental-normalization call, not a case of catching a company overstating its progress.

| "Margin recovery is cost-cuts only" | Gross margin **+530bps to 67.9% on +2% comps** — the gain came from **full-price sell-through**. Promotion-driven comps cannot produce that. The **£80m opex program is additive**. | **Bear refuted** |
| Comps are improving | FY26 quarterly comps **−1 → +2 → +3 → +5** — sequential acceleration, verified in the results releases. | **Verified** |

| Next catalyst date | **Q1 FY27 trading update on July 17, 2026** — verified **twice**, against both the FY26 RNS and the company IR calendar. | **Verified ×2** |

**The bear cases we tried to make — and what stuck:**

- *"Margin is just cost-cutting on a shrinking brand."* **Did not survive** — full-price mix recovery, cost cuts are additive.
- *"The comps are a dead-cat bounce."* **Did not survive** — four straight quarters of acceleration is a trend.
- *"The suspended dividend signals distress."* **Weakened** — net debt is falling; we read the suspension as deliberate.
- *"The core region still isn't working."* **This survives — EMEIA, the largest region, is flat-to−2%.** The recovery is carried by other regions.
- *"The whole sector is rolling over."* **This survives** — peer comps are soft (Kering/Gucci −14%), and a broad luxury leg-down would drag Burberry regardless.
- *"Part of the margin gain is a one-off reset."* **Partly stuck** — some of the +530bps laps last year's inventory-clearance markdowns, so it is *mildly* over-attributed to pure pricing power.
- *"The hard part is still ahead."* **This survives — the hard comparison periods arrive in H2 FY27, after both of our near-term catalysts.**

## Slide 6 — The edge, corrected: what we do and don't claim (new)

The skeptic pass forced us to throw out our first, easier story. This is what we are left with — and it is deliberately narrow.

- **What we are NOT claiming.** We are *not* claiming the market is behaviorally mispricing Burberry by lumping it in with a collapsing sector. That story does not survive its own data: **LVMH is flat-to-up (+1%) while Kering/Gucci collapse (−14%)** — the market plainly *discriminates within luxury*. So Burberry's price probably does reflect Burberry's own facts (weak EMEIA, easy comps), not lazy sector guilt. We removed that claim.
- **What we ARE claiming — narrowly.** A **differing probability on the up-tail**. We think the odds of the full-normalization path are **~72%**, versus the **~62%** implied by our scenario tree at today's price. That ~10-point gap is the entire edge.
- **What it rests on — one verified number.** We weight the **+530bps full-price gross-margin recovery** more heavily than the market does, because it is a *mix-quality* fact that promotion-driven comps physically cannot produce. That is the whole grounding — one clean, primary-sourced datum. No behavioral mechanism is claimed.
- **What could make this wrong (the falsifier).** If July 17 comps hold **but the stock does not re-rate**, then the market was weighting EMEIA/China correctly all along and the margin fact was already in the price. In that case the disagreement simply **resolves against us — the thesis was priced, not broken.** We are honest that this is the most likely way to be wrong without anything "failing."

> Bottom line: base fair value is essentially *at* today's price. All of the return lives in the up-tail. A modest, multiple-sensitive edge grounded in one datum earns a small gated starter — not conviction size.

## Slide 7 — A brand-attention cross-check (new)

Alongside the filings, we track live consumer-demand signals. For Burberry, the two we watch **disagree in a specifically bullish way — and we are honest that the tool is noisy.**

- **Search interest is at an 8-quarter high.** Google search interest for Burberry is **+35% year-on-year in exactly the quarter July 17 reports** — the strongest reading in the name's eight-quarter history (prior best was +19%).
- **News/curiosity attention is *down*.** Wikipedia and news pageviews are **−22% year-on-year** over the same window.
- **Why the divergence matters.** Search interest rising *while* news attention falls means **commercial interest is climbing without a news event driving it** — the pattern that looks like real shopping demand, not a headline. Contrast **Gucci**, where *both* search and news spiked together on the Demna designer drama while actual sales printed **−14%** — attention, not demand.
- **Be honest about instrument quality.** Across the full peer set, the correlation between this signal and printed sales is **modest once outliers are stripped out (~0.41–0.46)** — the eye-catching 0.90 headline was driven almost entirely by one name (Miu Miu). Treat it as directional, never precise. *Within Burberry specifically*, the signal has tracked the direction of comps in **8 of the last 8 quarters** (a tighter ~0.67 relationship), which is what gives it any weight here at all.
- **Verdict: mild corroboration.** It supports placing the small starter *before* July 17 rather than waiting. **It does not change the size.**
- **The "is it priced in?" test — run, and honestly NULL.** We tested whether high attention predicted the stock's *reaction* to the subsequent print across ~50 luxury print events (4-day excess return vs each name's home index). Result: no relationship (correlations ~0 to −0.18, and the two instruments disagree on direction). Even the printed sales numbers themselves don't predict reactions (−0.11) — luxury prints trade on *surprise versus expectations and guidance*, not headline levels. So attention data carries **no print-trading edge**; we use it only as timing corroboration, at unchanged size — which is exactly how it's sized here.
- **A Burberry-specific warning from the same test:** the last three positive-comp prints all saw *negative* 4-day reactions (+2% comp → −5%, +3% → −12.6%, +5% → −6.9%). Good news has recently been sold on this name. The July-17 add-gate is therefore about the *fundamental* confirmation (comps ≥ +3–4%, EMEIA ≥ −3%) — do not expect, or require, an instant pop. One mitigant: unlike before those three prints, the stock now sits near its 52-week low.

- **This is the honest reason it's a 5, not higher:** most of the expected return lives in the **up-tail (the ~2035 GBp bull)**, and the middle of the distribution barely clears the current price. All fair values are model outputs and are multiple-sensitive.

## Slide 10 — Sizing & scale path

- **Starter: 1.0% now** (rebuttal-trimmed from 1.5%). **Add to 3–5% only on a confirming July 17.** **Hard cap 5%.**
- **Why capped:** the edge is modest and multiple-sensitive (base fair value ≈ price), the largest region is still soft, and a broad luxury sell-off is an exogenous tail we cannot hedge. Size scales with edge × confidence × how bounded the downside is — and here the edge is thin and the sector tail is open.
- **Why staged this way:** a small position rides the easy-base July print; real size goes in only *after* the recovery is validated in the numbers, into the November catalyst. We deliberately pay for confirmation rather than pre-committing.

> What could make the sizing wrong: if July 17 confirms *and* the stock re-rates immediately, the gated structure means we add higher and capture less of the move. We accept that cost in exchange for not sizing up on an un-validated up-tail.

## Slide 11 — Kill triggers (plain language)

We exit or refuse to add if:

- The **July 17 group comp comes in below ~+2%, or negative.**
- **EMEIA falls below −5%, or Greater China swings back to negative** — the easy-base tailwind exposed as base-effect, not recovery.
- **Gross margin rolls over** or the brand returns to markdown selling — the quality thesis breaks.
- The **November H1 result fails to expand operating margin year-on-year**, or FY27 margin-expansion guidance is walked back.
- A **fifth strategy pivot in a decade, or the CEO (Schulman) exits** — the execution continuity we're underwriting is gone.
- A **broad luxury sector leg-down** (Gucci/Kering contagion, a China demand break).
