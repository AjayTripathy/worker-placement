# SandRidge Energy (NYSE: SD) — R/f(M) Trap-Filter

**Date:** 2026-06-26 · **Live price:** $13.51 (IBKR, contract 249453965) · **Shares (dil.):** ~36.99M · **Mkt cap:** ~$500M
**Cash:** $102.7M (Q1-26) · **Debt:** $0 · **ARO:** $73.7M ($65.6M noncurrent + $8.1M current) · **EV ≈ $471M** (cap + ARO − cash)
**Verdict: WAIT — lean ownable but NOT at this print. The screen's "trap" diagnosis is half-right and half-wrong.**

---

## Bottom line first (Mode B — disconfirm the bull)

The screen flagged SD as cheap-and-quality on a **trailing-peak commodity print** — that is the canonical
E&P value-trap setup, and the prompt's instinct ("+100% EBIT YoY = cyclical-peak warning") is the right
reflex. But the primary data **partially refutes the trap thesis**: SD's trailing numbers are NOT struck on
a 2022-style gas spike. The 2022 spike already washed out (FY22 rev $254M → FY24 trough $125M → FY25 $156M).
The "+100% EBIT YoY" is **operating leverage on a recovery off the 2024 trough plus a genuine production ramp**,
not a fresh price blow-off. Realized gas in Q1-26 was $3.13/Mcf — essentially *at* the EIA mid-cycle deck
(~$3.40), not 3-4x above it. So the trailing earn-yield is closer to mid-cycle than the screen's worst-case.

What IS a trap-flavored problem: (1) the **17% trailing earn-yield is on EBIT, but the real owner cash flow is
lower** once you fund the now-much-larger Cherokee drilling capex ($77M/yr run-rate vs ~$35M before); (2) the
**"return of capital story" has already de-rated by ~85%** — the $3.50 special (FY25 payout era, ~35% yield)
is gone, replaced by a $0.52/yr regular + $0.20 one-time ≈ $0.72/yr ≈ **5.3% forward yield**; (3) **oil at
$71 realized is above the WTI strip ($60-65)** and oil is now 21% of volumes (up from 17%) — that's the part
of the print that IS above mid-cycle and will fade. Net: this is a real, honestly-disclosed, NOL-shielded,
debt-free, modestly-growing Mid-Continent E&P — **not a liar, not a liquidation** — but the marketed
"17% earn-yield + net-cash + return-of-capital" takeaway overstates the through-cycle, post-capex reality by
roughly half. Hence WAIT, not REAL-VALUE, not TRAP.

## Claim-by-claim verification (Mode A)

### Claim 1 — "+100% EBIT YoY = growth" / prompt's counter "= cyclical-peak spike"
**Method:** EDGAR XBRL `OperatingIncomeLoss`, `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax`, full 2022-2026 series.
**Authority:** data.sec.gov companyconcept (audited 10-K/10-Q XBRL).
**Finding — BOTH framings REFUTED; it's recovery-off-trough + real volume growth.**

| Period | Revenue | Op. Income (EBIT) | Net Income |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 (gas spike) | $254.3M | n/a (peak) | — |
| FY2023 | $148.6M | $64.2M | $60.9M |
| FY2024 (trough) | $125.3M | $33.2M | $63.0M* |
| FY2025 | $156.4M | $61.0M | $70.2M* |
| Q1-2025 | $42.6M | $12.2M | $13.0M |
| **Q1-2026** | **$49.8M** | **$17.9M** | **$18.7M** |

- The "+100% YoY EBIT" is **FY25 $61.0M vs FY24 $33.2M (+84%)** — off the *2024 trough*, not a fresh peak.
  Q1-26 EBIT $17.9M vs Q1-25 $12.2M = **+46%**, of which a chunk is volume (18.6 vs 17.9 MBoe/d, +3.9%) and
  lower LOE ($5.35/Boe vs $6.61). This is operating leverage on a recovery, **partly durable**.
- *Net income is flattered by deferred-tax-asset / valuation-allowance releases (NOL), so don't use NI for
  earn-yield — use EBIT.* FY24 NI > EBIT and FY25 NI > EBIT because of $72.8M (FY24) and incremental (FY25)
  valuation-allowance releases. The screen's 17% "earn-yield" likely used NI and is therefore **inflated by
  non-cash tax benefit**.

### Claim 2 — "Net-cash, clean balance sheet"
**Method:** XBRL cash + ARO tags; 10-K contractual obligations.
**Authority:** Q1-2026 10-Q balance sheet (sd-20260331).
**Finding — TRUE on debt, but the screen IGNORES a $73.7M ARO the prompt correctly flagged.**
- Cash $102.7M, **zero funded debt** — genuinely net-cash on a financial-debt basis.
- BUT asset-retirement obligation = **$73.7M** ($65.6M noncurrent + $8.1M current) and **growing every quarter**
  ($54.6M end-2023 → $73.7M Q1-26, +35% in 9 quarters via accretion + new Cherokee wells). On an EV basis this
  is a real claim senior to equity. EV ≈ $500M cap − $103M cash + $74M ARO = **~$471M**, not the "~$400M
  net-of-cash" a naive screen would carry. Still leaves EV/PV-10 ≈ 1.07x (see Claim 5).

### Claim 3 — mid-cycle normalization (the core question)
**Method:** Q1-26 realized prices vs current Henry Hub / WTI strip; re-strike EBIT on a mid-cycle deck.
**Authority:** 10-Q realized-price table + CME/EIA strip (cal-27 HH ~$3.5-4.0 front, falling to ~$3.0 by spring-27; EIA mid-cycle $3.34-3.46; WTI strip $60-65).
**Finding — trailing print is ~10-20% above a clean mid-cycle deck, NOT 2-3x. Trap risk is MODERATE, not severe.**

Q1-26 as-reported realized: **Gas $3.13/Mcf, Oil $71.11/Bbl, NGL $18.64/Bbl**; blended $29.78/Boe.
- Gas $3.13 is **at/below** EIA mid-cycle ($3.34-3.46) — *not* a spike. No haircut; arguably a small uplift.
- Oil $71.11 is **~$8-11 above** the $60-63 WTI strip → haircut. Oil is 21% of Boe.
- NGL $18.64 is depressed already.

Mid-cycle re-strike (hold gas flat at strip ~$3.40, cut oil to $63, hold NGL): blended realization drops
~6-8% → ~$27.5/Boe. On ~$30/Boe of Q1 revenue that's roughly a $1.5-2.0M/qtr revenue hit, ~$1.3-1.7M EBIT/qtr.
Mid-cycle EBIT ≈ **$16/qtr → ~$64M/yr**.

**Through-cycle earn-yield:**
- Trailing screen number: 17% (on flattered NI / peak-ish blend).
- **Mid-cycle EBIT $64M / EV $471M ≈ 13.6% EBIT yield** (EV/EBIT ≈ 7.4x, vs the screen's 5.9x).
- **More honest owner-yield: mid-cycle EBIT $64M − maintenance/growth capex.** Capex is now $77M/yr run-rate
  (Q1 $19.3M D&C), of which maybe $35-45M is maintenance to hold 18.5 MBoe/d flat. Free-cash to owners
  ≈ EBIT $64M + DD&A add-back $48M − capex $77M ≈ **$35M**, ≈ **7% FCF yield on a $500M cap** at mid-cycle —
  before the oil-price fade. **That is the real number, and it's roughly HALF the marketed 17%.**

### Claim 4 — depleting / liquidating asset? reserve base & reinvestment
**Method:** 10-K reserve report (Cawley Gillespie, 97.9% of reserves), proved-developed mix, reserve replacement.
**Authority:** FY2025 10-K (sd-20251231) reserves section.
**Finding — NOT liquidating; reserves GREW and PUDs are being converted. This part of the trap thesis is REFUTED.**
- Proved reserves **69.1 MMBoe (2025) UP from 63.1 (2024)** — +9.5% YoY; R/P = **10.2 yrs**, weighted economic
  reserve life **35 yrs**. Proved developed 60.3 MMBoe (87%), PUD only 8.8 MMBoe (13%) — **low PUD reliance =
  high-quality reserve mix**, not a PUD-stuffed paper book.
- 2025 reserve changes: +4.7 MMBoe PUD→PDP converted (Cherokee), +4.5 MMBoe commercial improvements, +0.8
  extensions; −6.8 production, −3.1 price revisions. **They more than replaced production.** This is a
  reinvestment-and-grow posture (capex up 2.4x), *not* the harvest-and-die liquidation the trap thesis assumed.
- Standardized Measure = PV-10 = **$439.6M** (the two are equal *because NOLs zero out the future-tax term* —
  confirms the NOL shield is real and material). PV-10 up from $362.7M (2024).

### Claim 5 — hedge book / forward cash-flow protection
**Method:** Q1-26 10-Q derivative footnote.
**Authority:** sd-20260331 Note (open derivative contracts as of 3/31/26).
**Finding — LIGHTLY hedged; ~70-80% spot-exposed past 2026. This is the genuine downside vector.**
- Gas: swaps **16,430 MMBtu/d @ $4.17** + collars 4,500 @ $3.35/$5.35, **Apr-Dec 2026 only**. Gas production
  ≈ 55,400 Mcf/d (4,988 MMcf/qtr) → ~**38% of 2026 gas hedged at $4.17** (above strip — good), but **nothing
  meaningful in 2027+**.
- Oil: swaps **799 Bbl/d @ $74.37 (2026)**, 200 Bbl/d @ $65 (2027). Oil production ≈ 3,920 Bbl/d → ~**20%
  hedged 2026, ~5% 2027**.
- **Verdict:** 2026 cash flow is partially insulated and at *above-strip* prices (helpful). **2027+ is almost
  fully exposed to a strip that has gas dropping toward $3.00 and oil at $60-63.** The mid-cycle math above is
  therefore the right base case, and the hedges only delay — not prevent — the oil-realization fade.

### Claim 6 — capital-return durability
**Method:** dividend 8-Ks + 10-K cash-flow statement.
**Authority:** PRNewswire/8-K 2026-05-05; FY25 10-K ("$15.9M / $0.46 per share in 2025").
**Finding — the return-of-capital narrative has ALREADY collapsed ~85% from the spike era; current yield is ordinary.**
- Spike-era payout: FY25 *cash paid* included a **$3.50 special + $1.22 regular = $4.72/share** (that special was
  declared off 2024 surplus). At $13.51 that was a ~35% yield — the number that built SD's "return-of-capital" reputation.
- **Reality now:** 2025 *declared* dividends were only **$0.46/share total ($15.9M)**. Q1-26: regular raised 8%
  to **$0.13/qtr ($0.52/yr) + a $0.20 one-time** = **~$0.72/yr forward ≈ 5.3% yield.** Healthy, covered, but
  **ordinary** — and now competes with $77M/yr of growth capex for the same free cash. The "special dividend
  machine" is structurally over unless gas spikes again.

## (c) Fair value (if ownable) — bear / base / bull
Anchors: PV-10 $439.6M ($11.9/sh), net cash $103M ($2.78/sh), ARO −$74M (−$2.00/sh).
- **Bear $9-10:** gas to $2.75 / WTI $55 strip holds into 2027; oil realization fades to ~$60; FCF ~$15-20M;
  market re-rates to 0.9x PV-10 on a shrinking SM. ARO drag bites. (≈ −30%)
- **Base $13-14:** mid-cycle deck (HH $3.40 / WTI $62); ~$35M owner FCF; ~5% yield + slow Cherokee growth;
  trades ~1.0-1.05x PV-10 + cash. **≈ current price — i.e., fairly valued, no margin of safety.**
- **Bull $18-20:** gas back to $4.50+ on LNG pull / cold winter; specials resume; Cherokee IRRs lift PV-10 to
  $550M+; re-rate to 1.2x. (≈ +40%) — *this requires a commodity move, i.e., it's a gas call, not a value re-rate.*

**Margin of safety at $13.51: roughly zero.** Base case ≈ spot. That is the WAIT.

## (d) Three kill-triggers (any one → DECLINE / exit)
1. **Henry Hub cal-2027 strip breaks below $3.00 and stays** — with <10% of 2027 gas hedged, mid-cycle EBIT
   re-strikes down 20%+ and owner FCF goes to ~$15M (3% yield) → the value case is gone.
2. **Capex outruns cash and the regular dividend gets trimmed/suspended** — watch quarterly D&C vs operating
   cash flow; if D&C > OCF for two quarters while drawing the $103M cash, the "return-of-capital + net-cash"
   thesis is internally contradicted.
3. **ARO acceleration or a reserve negative-revision** — if the $74M ARO jumps on an Oklahoma plugging mandate,
   or Cawley-Gillespie cuts proved reserves on price (watch the next 10-K SM/PV-10), EV/PV-10 inverts above
   1.2x and the asset coverage disappears.

## (e) The single most likely way THIS thesis breaks
**It's not a fraud and not a classic peak-print trap — it's a "you're buying a sub-$4 gas call dressed as a value
stock at fair value with no margin of safety, right as the return-of-capital flywheel has already stopped."**
The most probable break: **the 2027 strip sags toward $3.00, the lightly-hedged book fully reprices, owner FCF
compresses to ~7%→~3%, the special-dividend optionality (the only thing that ever made SD a *standout* yield)
stays switched off, and the stock dead-money-drifts to $10-11.** You don't lose 60% like a typical IPO/E&P trap
(the reserves, NOL shield, zero debt and PV-10 floor protect the downside) — **you lose the opportunity cost of
holding a fairly-priced commodity proxy in a value sleeve, with the upside gated on a gas-price move you have no
edge on.** For a consumer-disc-heavy book seeking a *diversifier*, that's a real (if unspectacular) hedge — but
buy it on a gas-driven dislocation to $10-11, not at $13.51.
