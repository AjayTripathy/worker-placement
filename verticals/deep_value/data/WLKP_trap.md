## 1. Is the 2.1x EV/EBIT / 48% earnings yield REAL? — NO. It is a consolidation artifact.

The decisive structural fact (10-K, verified): **WLKP's sole revenue-generating asset is a 22.8%
limited-partner interest in Westlake Chemicals OpCo LLC.** It CONSOLIDATES OpCo because it controls
the GP, so the income statement shows 100% of OpCo's revenue and EBIT — but **77.2% of the economics
belong to the parent, Westlake (WLK), booked as noncontrolling interest (NCI).**

| Item (FY2025, XBRL-verified) | Value |
|---|---|
| Consolidated Operating Income (EBIT) | **$319.6M** (100% of OpCo) |
| Consolidated Net Income | $48.7M |
| — Net income to noncontrolling interest (WLK's 77.2%) | **$249.9M (84%)** |
| — Net income to WLKP unitholders | **$48.7M** |
| Units outstanding (common + GP) | 35.25M |
| Equity market cap @ $22.66 | $798.7M |
| Total debt (all intercompany, owed to WLK subs) | $399.7M ($377.1M MLP Revolver + $22.6M OpCo Revolver) |
| Cash | $44.3M |
| Noncontrolling interest (carrying) | $536.3M |

**Why the screen prints 2.1x / 48%:** the screener divided the **full consolidated EBIT ($319.6M
= 100% of OpCo)** by an enterprise value built off a market cap that only represents the **22.8%
economic claim**. Numerator = whole pie; denominator = one slice. That mechanically deflates EV/EBIT
and inflates earnings yield and ROIC by ~4x. This is the canonical MLP/consolidated-NCI value-trap
signature.

**Recomputed, economically correct:**
- Full consolidated EV (equity + net debt + **NCI**, which EV *must* include) / consol EBIT = **5.3x**.
- True look-through: WLKP's 22.8% share of OpCo EBIT ($72.9M) vs WLKP-level EV ($1,131M) = **~15.5x**.
- Earnings yield to actual WLKP unitholders = $48.7M / $798.7M = **6.1%** (NOT 48%). P/E ≈ **16.4x**.
- "ROIC 30%" = OpCo's return measured against WLKP's tiny capital slice — same look-through artifact.

The name is not cheap. On any honest measure it trades at a market-to-rich industrials multiple.

## 2. Captive entity / transfer-pricing — YES, by design.

OpCo's EBITDA is essentially one number set by the parent: the **Ethylene Sales Agreement** — WLK
buys **95% of OpCo's planned ethylene production on a cost-plus basis at a fixed margin of $0.10/lb**,
with a **Buyer Deficiency Fee** if WLK under-takes. So WLKP "earnings" are not market-determined; they
are a transfer price the parent sets and the parent (as 77.2% OpCo owner + 40.1% LP holder + GP +
IDR holder) controls on both sides of the table. The 10-K explicitly lists "renewal or renegotiation
of, or determinations made pursuant to, our contractual arrangements with Westlake" as a risk factor.

**Contract reset is live.** Initial ESA term = **12 years from the 2014 IPO → 2026**. We are inside
the reset window now. The $0.10/lb fixed margin is a negotiated transfer price, not a market rate;
at reset the controlling parent has every incentive (and the votes) to set terms that favor WLK
equity over LP units. This is the single largest unpriced risk.

## 3. Distribution mechanics — a fixed-income-like instrument, no growth re-rate.

- Distribution $0.4714/qtr = **$1.8856/yr → 8.32% yield** at $22.66.
- **Payout = 136% of GAAP EPS** ($1.89 paid vs $1.38 GAAP NI/unit) — covered only via the DCF
  depreciation add-back, i.e. return-of-capital-flavored, not growth-funded.
- No organic re-rate engine: capacity is fixed (~3.7B lb/yr across 3 plants + a 200-mile pipeline),
  margin is contractually fixed at $0.10/lb, and growth requires dropdowns/issuance the parent
  controls. The unit behaves like a leveraged perpetual on a parent-set coupon. There is no catalyst
  that closes a "value gap" because there is no value gap — the screen manufactured one.

## 4. Take-under / roll-up risk — STRUCTURAL and elevated.

WLK holds 40.1% of LP units, the GP, **and the IDRs (never eliminated)**, and 66-2/3% of units is
required to remove the GP — which at 40.1% WLK can block. MLP simplification history (peer GPs rolling
up LPs at lowball exchange ratios) is directly applicable: a controlled LP whose only asset is a
minority OpCo stake, trading at an 8%+ yield, is a textbook take-under candidate. The minority holder
has no leverage on price.

## (a) Verdict: **TRAP**
The cheapness is a consolidation/look-through artifact, not real value.

## (b) True EV/EBIT and why it differs from 2.1x
- **5.3x** (full consolidated EV incl. NCI / consol EBIT) — the minimum-honest figure.
- **~15.5x** (look-through: WLKP's 22.8% of OpCo EBIT vs WLKP-level EV).
- Difference vs 2.1x: the screen put 100%-of-OpCo EBIT over a 22.8%-economic market cap and omitted
  NCI from EV. Correcting either error alone roughly doubles the multiple; correcting both ~7-8x's it.

## (c) Fair value — "not ownable" as a value name; if held, it is a yield instrument
Not a re-rate vehicle. As a yield proxy only: at 8.0–9.0% required yield on $1.89 distribution →
**bear ~$21 (9.0%, reset haircut risk) / base ~$23.5 (8.0%) / bull ~$26 (7.25%, benign reset)**.
This is a ±10% rate/spread band, not a deep-value double. No deep-value FV case exists.

## (d) Three kill-triggers
1. **ESA reset on unfavorable terms** — any 8-K/10-Q disclosing a renegotiated Ethylene Sales
   Agreement margin below $0.10/lb or a shortened term → distribution coverage and FV both impair.
2. **Take-under announcement** — WLK proposes to acquire LP units; historically priced at slim/no
   premium for controlled MLPs. Watch 8-K / SC 13E-3.
3. **Distribution cut or coverage break** — payout already >100% of GAAP EPS; a downcycle in ethane
   spreads + an annual turnaround year compresses DCF below the distribution.

## (e) Single most likely way THIS thesis breaks (i.e., where I could be wrong)
The bull doesn't need the screen's math to be right — it needs the **8.3% yield to simply persist and
the parent to leave the ESA alone**. If WLK keeps the $0.10/lb margin and never rolls up the LP, WLKP
delivers a durable, well-covered (on a DCF basis) 8%+ income stream and modestly outperforms cash —
a perfectly fine *income* holding. So the way "TRAP" is too harsh: if the book's slot for WLKP is
**income/diversifier, not value**, the position is defensible. The thesis as a *deep-value re-rate*
is dead; the thesis as a *parent-dependent high-yield coupon* survives — at ~16x earnings, not 2x.
The break is therefore reclassification, not blow-up: you were sold a value name and you actually own
a controlled-MLP bond-proxy.
