### (1) Recomputed valuation — the "4.6x EV/EBIT, 21% FCF" claim is REFUTED / misleading

| Metric | Screen claim | f(M) recompute (primary) | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| EV/EBIT | 4.6x | **11.7x** | REFUTED |
| FCF yield | 21% | 20.6% simple — but on an LTO model where it isn't comparable | MISLEADING |
| Net debt | (implied ~0) | **$1.47B** | the missing $1.47B is the whole error |
| P/E (FY25) | — | 15.8x | FAIR |

- **EV is wrong in the screen.** Gross debt = $450M 6.375% notes (2029) + $872.8M term loan (2032) + $264.0M ABL = **$1.587B**; cash $120.5M → **net debt $1.466B**. EV = $1.16B mcap + $1.47B net debt = **$2.62B**.
- FY2025 EBIT (operating profit) = $223.3M → **EV/EBIT = 11.7x**, not 4.6x. The 4.6x is almost certainly a vendor artifact: LTO data feeds add back rental-merchandise depreciation (~$1.4B/yr, which runs through COGS not D&A) to manufacture a huge "EBITDA," then strike EV against it. On honest EBIT this is a low-double-digit multiple — i.e., roughly FAIR for a no-growth, leveraged, regulated subprime lender.
- **The 21% FCF yield is real arithmetically** (FY25 OCF $305.6M − capex $66.9M = $238.7M simple FCF; /$1.16B mcap = 20.6%) **but is not the bargain it looks like.** Reported D&A is only $52M because the real capital intensity of the lease-to-own model (depreciation of on-rent merchandise, ~$1.4B/yr) sits in cost of rentals. FCF-yield-on-equity on a 5.3x-net-debt/EBITDA balance sheet is a levered-equity yield, not a cheap-asset yield; ~$90.5M went to dividends and $278.9M to the Brigit acquisition in FY25.
- **Leverage:** net debt / unadjusted EBITDA 5.3x; / company-style adjusted EBITDA ~3.1x. **EBIT/interest coverage = 1.98x** — thin. $112.98M of annual interest is a hard claim ahead of equity.

### (2) Segment mix, credit trend, regulatory — the operating reality

Four segments: **Acima** (virtual LTO at third-party retail POS — the "grower"), **Rent-A-Center** (legacy stores — secular decline), **Brigit** (cash-advance/earned-wage-access app, acquired Jan-2025), **Mexico**.

- **Acima growth engine just inflected NEGATIVE.** Acima GMV was **+8.6% for FY2025** but **−5.9% YoY in Q1-2026**. Q1 revenue grew only on GMV booked in prior quarters. The structural-POS-financing growth thesis is the bull case — and the leading indicator (GMV) is now contracting. This is the single most important tell: a "secular grower" whose volume turned down.
- **Rent-A-Center in managed decline** as expected: revenue −$7.4M, operating profit −$4.1M in Q1, fewer early-purchase exercises and lower franchise revenue. Honestly disclosed, not the catch.
- **Total revenue +3.7% YoY in Q1 is almost entirely Brigit lapping** ($35.8M of the $43.4M increase is Brigit going from 2 to 3 months of consolidation — a one-time accounting comp, not organic growth). Strip Brigit's comp artifact and the core LTO business is roughly flat-to-down.
- **LTO credit quality is actually CONTROLLED — this is NOT a charge-off cliff at Acima/RAC.** Acima lease charge-offs 8.8% of revenue (vs 8.9% PY); RAC 4.7% (vs 4.6%). Stable. Trap hypothesis (a) — "peak-cycle earnings that crater on rising LTO charge-offs" — is **not supported** by current loss data. Credit it where due: this is honest, well-managed loss performance.
- **The credit cliff is in Brigit, not LTO.** Brigit net advance losses **$13.9M Q1-2026 vs $5.0M Q1-2025 (+178%)**. Total merchandise+advance losses $94.2M vs $87.5M (+7.7% on +3.7% revenue) — losses growing ~2x revenue, driven by the new fintech segment. Upbound paid up to **$460M** ($278.7M cash + 2.7M shares + $75M deferred; **$196.9M goodwill**) for Brigit — a serial-pivot into the highest-loss, most-regulated adjacency right as its loss rate spikes.
- **Regulatory exposure is structural and rising, not trough.** CFPB/FTC, Dodd-Frank, state rent-to-own statutes, plus — newly — earned-wage-access / small-dollar / cash-advance rulemaking risk that the Brigit deal added. Disclosed as a risk factor; the point is the company *increased* its regulatory surface area with the pivot.

### (3) Trap-hypothesis scorecard
- (a) Consumer-credit cycle / LTO charge-off cliff — **NOT the trap.** Acima/RAC LCOs stable ~8.8% / 4.7%. Honestly managed.
- (b) Regulatory — **elevated and rising**, amplified by the Brigit earned-wage-access pivot. Real tail risk, not imminent cliff.
- (c) Secular mix — **NEGATIVE and decisive.** RAC declining (expected) AND Acima GMV turned −5.9%. The "structural grower" stalled; the only top-line growth is a one-time acquisition comp.
- (d) Leverage — **the hidden problem.** $1.47B net debt the screen omitted, 5.3x gross leverage, 2.0x interest coverage. This is why EV/EBIT is 11.7x not 4.6x.

## DECISION
**TRAP** — not because the cash flow is fake (it is real) but because the marketed cheapness diverges from the data:
the 4.6x EV/EBIT is a depreciation-addback/missing-net-debt artifact (true ~11.7x), the 21% "FCF yield" is a levered-equity yield on a 5.3x-levered subprime lender, the "Acima structural grower" just printed −5.9% GMV, and the FY25 growth is a Brigit consolidation comp masking a flat-to-down core with a tripling fintech loss rate. **Exclude from the quality small-cap-value basket.**

This is a takeaway-vs-data divergence catch, not a re-detection of disclosed bad news: RAC's decline and the LTO loss rates are honestly disclosed and fine. The catch is (i) the valuation artifact and (ii) the GMV inflection + Brigit-loss spike hidden under a headline "+3.7% revenue / 44% EPS growth."

## If one insists on a fair-value frame (not a buy)
At 11.7x EV/EBIT on no organic growth, a leveraged regulated subprime lender deserves ~8–11x EV/EBIT → EV $1.8–2.5B → equity $0.3–1.0B → **~$6–17/share fair range**, i.e. current ~$20 is FAIR-to-RICH, not cheap. A genuine re-rate requires Acima GMV to re-accelerate (watch the next 1–2 prints) AND Brigit advance-loss normalization — neither is in evidence yet.
