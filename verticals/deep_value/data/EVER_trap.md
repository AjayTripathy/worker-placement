**Reading the tape:**
- The **+132% annual EBIT print IS a trough-recovery artifact** — FY2023 op income was roughly **−$52M** (carriers slashed customer-acquisition spend during the 2022-23 auto-underwriting-loss cycle); FY2025 was **~+$50M**. Off a near-zero/negative base, any percent is enormous. So the *growth rate* framing is cyclically inflated — that part of the trap hypothesis is correct.
- **But the cyclical-PEAK / rolling-over hypothesis is REFUTED by the most recent quarter.** Q1-26 posted the **highest operating income in company history ($23.4M)** and the **highest operating margin in the series (12.3%)**. Margin is still making new highs, not fading.
- The −2.3% QoQ revenue dip Q4-25→Q1-26 is **seasonal**, not a rollover: Q4 is the heavy auto-shopping/open-enrollment quarter. Controlling for seasonality via Q1-over-Q1: op margin went **1.9% (Q1-24) → 4.8% (Q1-25) → 12.3% (Q1-26)** — a clean structural step-up, with operating leverage on a roughly flat opex base.
- YoY revenue growth has decelerated (Q3-25 +20% → Q1-26 +15%) but that is a **tough-comp effect** (Q1-25 itself was +83% YoY), not demand collapse. Management guides FY2026 revenue up YoY on continued carrier-spend increases.

**Mechanism encoded:** masking channel = trailing-growth-rate framing struck off a cycle-trough denominator (the classic deep-value base-effect distortion); signal channel = the *latest-quarter margin/op-income level* (not the YoY %), which shows the rebound has converted into a structurally higher margin plateau rather than peaking. Latency: the rebound is ~8 quarters old and STILL setting margin highs — late-cycle but not post-peak.

## 3. Mix and concentration (the real catch)

**Vertical mix (Q1-26):** Automotive **$172.4M (90.3%)**, Home & renters **$18.5M (9.7%)**, Other ~$0. The company **exited the direct-to-consumer agency** (sold P&C commission rights, May 2025) — it is now a cleaner pure lead-gen/referral marketplace, but **~90% levered to a single end-market (auto insurance)**. Home/renters is the only diversifier and it is small. There is **no health vertical** in the current numbers (the "expanding into health" bull point is not in this filing's revenue).

**Customer concentration — VERIFIED and WORSENED:**
> "For the three months ended March 31, 2026, **one customer represented 40% of total revenue.** For the three months ended March 31, 2025, two customers represented 43% and 13%..."

A year ago the top exposure was spread across two carriers; now a **single carrier is 40% of revenue.** AR concentration corroborates (3 customers = 30%/17%/17% of receivables). This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk: one carrier pulling or cutting its acquisition budget (a normal event when its loss ratio turns) would hit ~40% of the top line directly. The screen's "durable" framing does not price this.

## 4. Entry / fair value (REAL → sizing the buy)

- At ~$670M EV against ~$74M TTM EBIT (9.1x) / ~$97M FCF (EV/FCF ~6.9x, FCF yield ~11.4%), with net cash and a record-margin latest quarter, EVER is **not a value trap on the cyclical axis** — the cheapness is real and the earnings are at/above trend, not below a sustainable level.
- **The cap on the thesis is concentration, not the cycle.** A reasonable fair multiple for a ~15% grower, asset-light, net-cash marketplace is high-teens EV/EBIT — but the 40%-single-customer risk and ~90% auto exposure warrant a discount. A defensible FV range is **EV/EBIT ~12-14x on ~$90-95M normalized EBIT → EV ~$1.1-1.3B → ~$35-41/share** (vs $23 today), i.e. roughly **50-75% upside** if (a) the top carrier relationship holds and (b) home/renters keeps growing. 
- **Downside trigger to watch:** the next 10-Q's customer-concentration footnote and any carrier-budget commentary. If the 40% customer's share rises further or YoY revenue growth goes negative (true demand rollover, distinct from the Q1 seasonal dip), the cyclical-peak hypothesis would re-open.
- **Sizing:** REAL but **size as a concentrated-customer name** — half-position vs a diversified comparable. The honesty-alpha point: the marketed "durable structural grower" takeaway diverges from the data on *concentration* (40% single customer) and on the *growth-rate framing* (trough base-effect), even though the cyclical-trap fear itself is disproven.
