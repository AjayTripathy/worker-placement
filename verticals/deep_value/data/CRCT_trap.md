# Cricut, Inc. (Nasdaq: CRCT) — R/f(M) Deep-Value Trap-Filter

**Date:** 2026-06-26 | **Live price (IBKR):** $4.57 (bid 3.91 / ask 4.68 — wide; thin small-cap) | **Div yield (IBKR):** 4.52%
**Primary source:** Q1 FY2026 10-Q filed 2026-05-06 (period ended 2026-03-31), acc. 0001828962-26-000039; FY2025 10-K; XBRL companyconcept.

## VERDICT: REAL (lean toward REAL/FAIR) — NOT a liquidation trap

The trap thesis ("whole base melting, FCF is liquidation") is **REFUTED by the company's own data.** The high-margin subscription annuity (Platform) is **growing** — paid subscribers, ARPU, and Platform revenue all up YoY — while only the hardware (Products) leg declines. This is a two-speed business where the cash-generative, sticky leg is expanding. It is closer to a slowly-repricing recurring-revenue compounder than a melting ice cube. The cheapness is real and the bull's "market over-discounts hardware cyclicality" framing is **supported**, not contradicted, by the filing.

The honest caveat: top-line is flat-to-down (~-1%/yr) and engagement (90-day) is gently eroding, so this is a *low-growth* annuity, not a *grower*. Grade: REAL on the trap axis, FAIR-to-cheap on the price axis.

## (2) The decisive trajectories — is the annuity growing or melting?

This is where the trap thesis lives or dies. The 10-Q (vs prior-year 10-Qs) gives the clean read:

| Metric | Q1'24 | Q1'25 | Q1'26 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid Subscribers (000s)** | 2,797 | 2,974 | **3,078** | **+3.5% YoY, up every year** |
| **Platform ARPU (TTM)** | $52.26 | $53.10 | **$55.65** | **+4.8% YoY, rising** |
| **Platform revenue (Q1)** | — | $79.99M | **$84.77M** | **+6.0% YoY** |
| Platform gross margin (Q1'26) | — | — | **89%** (rev 84.8 / COGS 9.4) | high-margin annuity |

Paid-subscriber path back to 2023: Q3'23 2,699 → Q1'24 2,797 → Q3'24 2,838 → Q1'25 2,974 → **Q1'26 3,078.** Monotonic growth. ARPU rising in parallel. **The annuity is compounding ~+6%/yr in revenue, NOT rolling over with the hardware.** This directly refutes trap leg (b) and (c).

### Hardware (Products) — DECLINING, as the trap thesis says (leg a)
| Metric | Q1'25 | Q1'26 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Products revenue (Q1) | $82.65M | **$74.70M** | **-9.6% YoY** |
| Products gross margin (Q1'26) | — | **23%** (rev 74.7 / COGS 57.4) | low-margin |

Hardware is genuinely shrinking ~10%/yr — the post-Covid fad fade is real. **But** Products is the *low-margin* leg (23% GM vs Platform 89%). The decline drags revenue but barely dents profit. Total revenue: FY23 $765M → FY24 $712.5M → FY25 $708.8M → Q1'26 $159.5M vs $162.6M ($-1.9% YoY). Total revenue is *gently* declining, mix-shifting toward the high-margin annuity.

### The funnel question — is the engagement base supporting the annuity?
| Metric | Q1'24 | Q1'25 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active Users (000s) | 5,952 | 5,926 | 5,969 (flat) |
| 90-Day Engaged Users (000s) | 3,527 | 3,372 | 3,345 (-0.8% YoY) |
| Paid Subscribers (000s) | 2,797 | 2,974 | 3,078 (+3.5%) |

This is the one genuine yellow flag: **engaged users (3,345k) are slowly eroding while paid subs (3,078k) grow** — paid penetration of the engaged base is climbing (now ~92% of 90-day-engaged are paid subs). That conversion runway is nearly exhausted; further sub growth must come from re-growing the user base, which needs hardware to stop shrinking. So the annuity is growing *today* but its growth is **maturing**, not accelerating. Not melting; plateauing on the horizon.

## (3) Verdict logic — TRAP vs REAL vs FAIR

**Trap thesis tested leg-by-leg:**
- (a) Hardware declining → **TRUE** (Products -9.6%). But it's the low-margin leg; immaterial to FCF.
- (b) Paid-sub base rolling over with hardware → **FALSE.** Subs +3.5%, ARPU +4.8%, Platform rev +6%.
- (c) FCF/ROIC = harvesting a melting base (liquidation) → **FALSE.** The cash is thrown off by a *growing* 89%-margin recurring-revenue stream, not by drawing down an impaired asset. Inventory stable ($102.7M→$106.0M, normal seasonal), AR declining ($92.0M→$67.7M, healthy collection) — **no channel-stuffing or harvest tell.**
- (d) Inventory/channel → **CLEAN.** No build-up masking demand collapse.

**This is the inverse of a deep-value trap.** A trap is a stale-data bargain or a liquidating cash-rich shell. Cricut's cash generation is backed by an *expanding* annuity disclosed transparently in its own KPI table — the marketed takeaway (sticky growing subscription) does **not** diverge from the data. On the honesty axis this is CLEAN: management is not hiding hardware decline, it reports it in plain segment detail, and the bull case (annuity > hardware drag) is the literal arithmetic of the filing.

**Why FAIR rather than screaming-cheap:** total revenue is flat-to-down, the conversion runway is maturing, and growth depends on stabilizing a still-declining hardware funnel. You are buying a ~7.5–8x EV/EBIT, 14% FCF-yield, net-cash, ~all-FCF-returning recurring-revenue business that is **low/no-growth, not a grower.** That is cheap for the quality and the capital-return, but the multiple is partly *deserved* by the absence of growth — not purely a market misjudgment.

## (4) Entry / fair value (REAL → size it)

- **Earnings power:** TTM EBIT $89.5M, FY25 $96M. Tax ~19–20%. Owner FCF ~$130–140M, with ~$80M+ of that durable (ex-WC-timing).
- **What you're paying:** EV ~$716M = 8.0x EV/EBIT, ~5x EV/FCF, 14.4% FCF yield on mcap.
- **FV frame (no-growth annuity + declining hardware):** A flat ~$80–90M EBIT stream from an 89%-GM annuity (offset by a melting low-margin hardware tail) reasonably supports **10–11x EV/EBIT** → EV ~$900–985M + $256M net cash = **equity ~$1.15–1.24B → ~$5.45–5.85/share**, roughly **+19% to +28%** upside, before any capital-return compounding. Add the 4.5% cash yield + buyback shrink and the IRR is attractive even with zero re-rate.
- **Bull optionality (not in base case):** if hardware sales stabilize (new machine cycle, installed-base replacement) the funnel re-opens and subs re-accelerate — re-rate to mid-teens EV/EBIT, $7+. The market is pricing terminal decline; the data prices maturity. That gap is the edge.
- **Entry:** scale in $4.20–4.60. Note the IBKR quote is wide (3.91/4.68) — use limits, this is thin. Net-cash + 4.5% yield + ~all-FCF buyback gives a hard valuation floor and pays you to wait.

## UNVERIFIABLE / caveats flagged
- **FY2025 capex/dividends-paid exact totals:** XBRL PPE-only and dividends-paid tags returned empty on pull (capex is reported bundled with capitalized software); TTM FCF ~$140M is built from Q1 10-Q capex ($9.1M/$4.9M incl. cap software) + FY25 OCF $200.2M — directionally firm, FCF point-estimate ±$10M.
- **FCF conversion 1.9x is partly working-capital release** (AR collection, inventory timing) — do not extrapolate 1.9x as a permanent conversion ratio; normalized conversion is closer to ~1.0–1.2x of net income.
- **Governance:** Class B (155M, 73% of shares) is the founder/Petrus controlling block — dual-class, you are a minority. Capital-return policy is at controller discretion (the special dividend was their call). Not a trap tell, but a governance discount is warranted.
- **Demand cyclicality risk is real:** the thesis rests on hardware decline *decelerating* and the engaged base stabilizing. If 90-day-engaged keeps eroding and paid-sub penetration is tapped out, the annuity plateaus then declines with a 1–2yr lag — that's the genuine bear path, and it's a *slow* melt, not the fast liquidation the trap thesis posited.
