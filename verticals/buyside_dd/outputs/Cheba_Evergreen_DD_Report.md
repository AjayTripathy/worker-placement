# Project Evergreen — Cheba Hut / Elevated Inc. Co-Investment
## Due-Diligence Report

**Prepared:** June 25, 2026 · **Asset:** Elevated Inc. (David Timmons) — 24-unit Cheba Hut franchise group, Colorado & Nevada · **Sponsor/SPV:** "B Wild Investments" (PCM Hospitality) · **Document reviewed:** *Project Evergreen "B Wild"* CIM (63 pp, Summer 2025), cross-checked against the Cheba Hut 2025 Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD), county records, and live primary sources.

---

## Recommendation: PROCEED TO TERMS — do not commit capital yet

The operating asset is real, well-run, and **better than the original teaser implied** — every load-bearing operating claim we could test against a primary source held up. **But the investment itself cannot be underwritten from what we've been given:** the CIM contains no purchase price, no leverage, no fees/promote, and no return math, and the headline "22.9% EBITDA" is a store-level figure that is not what the equity actually earns. This is a *conditional* go: the gate is now entirely the transaction terms (the 14-item request in the appendix), not the quality of the business.

**One-line verdict:** A genuine, premium-performing franchise operator wrapped in a marketing document that discloses the restaurant honestly but omits the deal — underwrite the terms before sizing.

## 2. What did not clear (investment case — unverifiable from the CIM)

The document is a pure operating story. A full-text scan confirms it contains **none** of: purchase price, sources & uses, debt/leverage, management fee, carry/promote, hold period, or any IRR/MOIC. The marketed economics therefore cannot be taken at face value for three specific reasons:

**(a) "22.9% EBITDA" is store-level, not buyer cash flow.** The headline margin (p49) is a four-wall + royalty number — *before* above-store corporate overhead, owner/executive compensation, maintenance capital, and any acquisition debt. Loading those brings it down materially:

| Bridge (PF trailing-twelve, $M) | |
|---|---:|
| Store-level EBITDA (as marketed) | 14.5 |
| − above-store G&A (~6% of sales, est.) | (4.1) |
| − owner/exec replacement comp (est.) | (1.0) |
| − franchise-fee true-up (see below) | (1.6) |
| **= "true" EBITDA** | **~7.9** |
| − maintenance capex (~3% of sales, est.) | (2.0) |
| **= unlevered free cash flow** | **~5.8** |

True EBITDA is roughly **54% of the marketed store-level figure**; unlevered free cash flow is **~40%**. *(The deductions are our estimates — the CIM discloses none of them; each should be replaced with an audited actual.)*

**(b) The rumored "4.7×" entry is really ~8.7× on true earnings.** A 4.7× multiple struck on the store-level base implies an enterprise value of ~$68M — which is **~8.7× true EBITDA**, an honest ~8.5% unlevered cash yield, not the ~21% the headline multiple suggests. *Confirming which EBITDA base the price is struck on is the single most important open question — it swings implied value by roughly $28M.*

**(c) The marketed franchise fees look light versus the FDD.** The store-level P&L carries a single "royalty" line at ~4.7% of sales. The Cheba Hut FDD (Item 6) sets recurring franchisor fees at **5% royalty + a 2% Brand Fund = 7% of Net Sales** (the Brand Fund can be raised to 4.2% on notice). The ~0.3-point royalty gap may simply reflect a Net-vs-gross-sales base difference, but the **2% Brand Fund (~$1.4M)** is a separate mandatory fee that the deck's single line does not name — confirm whether it is captured in store operating expense or omitted. If omitted, store-level margin is overstated by ~2 points.

## 3. Return analysis (what the deal math actually implies)

Because price and leverage are undisclosed, we modeled returns as a function of the two undisclosed levers — EBITDA growth and exit multiple — at a representative 4.7× entry on store-level EBITDA, 50% leverage at 9%, six-year hold. *(Illustrative; replace with terms when provided.)*

| EBITDA outcome at exit | MOIC @ flat 4.7× | @ 5.0× | @ 5.5× |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Flat (no new units) | 1.5× | 1.6× | 1.8× |
| +29% (the deck's own 2025E) | 2.1× | 2.2× | 2.5× |
| +60% (~14 new units) | 2.7× | 2.9× | 3.2× |
| **Double (the deck thesis, ~23 new units)** | 3.4× | 3.7× | **4.1×** |

- **The marketed "4–6×" lives only in the top-right corner** — it requires EBITDA to *double*, which means **~23 new units (nearly tripling the 24-unit footprint)** in unproven Kansas / Kansas City markets, **plus** multiple expansion **plus** the assumed leverage. Each is a separate bet the document does not support with data.
- **The honest base case** (the deck's own +29% growth, flat exit) is **~2.1× / ~13% IRR**; the **floor** (no new units) is **~1.5× / ~7%** — a poor risk-adjusted return for an illiquid, single-operator, single-region position.
- **Growth is capital-bought, not organic.** Same-store sales have decelerated sharply (14.3% → 5.0% → 1.5% year-to-date), so the entire growth case rests on the new-build program — a multi-year capex obligation, not the existing book.

## 4. Key risks and watch items

1. **No transaction terms (the central gap).** Price, the EBITDA base the multiple is struck on, leverage, and fees/promote are all absent. Everything in §3 is conditional on them.
2. **Store-level EBITDA marketed as if it were buyer cash flow** (§2a) — the equity earns materially less than 22.9%.
3. **Growth is a capital obligation, not organic** — ~23 new units needed to "double," while same-store growth has flatlined to 1.5%.
4. **Case-study cherry-pick.** The three featured new-unit returns are the three newest units; one headline 267% cash-on-cash is driven by an atypically low ~$323K build (likely a heavy tenant-improvement allowance), not a repeatable economic.
5. **Two sister units excluded from the deal.** The franchisor lists two additional Timmons-family Cheba Huts not in the 24-unit roster — Fort Collins-Harmony and Las Vegas-Centennial. Scope/non-compete question: are they retained by the seller? Harmony shares the Fort Collins market with two acquired units.
6. **Related-party real estate — pricing.** Both "owned" parcels are confirmed franchisee-controlled, but: Cap Hill is assessed at $1.23M (improvements carried at $1,000) against a 2022 purchase of $1.80M, and UNC is flat to its 2020 basis. The price the seller proposes for these must be reconciled to the public records.
7. **Co-principal economics.** Whether Kyle Archer holds equity (vs. title) is unconfirmed — i.e., whether the buyer is acquiring 100% of Timmons or a Timmons + Archer cap table.
8. **Concentration.** One operator, two states, one regional cannabis-branded niche brand, ~⅓ of a sub-80-unit system.

**Honesty grade — MIXED (but not a liar's deck).** The deck is candid on the operating data (it shows the same-store deceleration, the full store-by-store P&L, the FDD-consistent stratification, and the related-party real estate). The divergence is takeaway-vs-data: a store-level number labeled "EBITDA," a decelerating sales slide titled "consistently outperforming," and a cherry-picked outlier return. The operating story is real; the buyer economics are simply absent.

## 5. What we need to close — questions for the sponsor

**Tier 1 — Transaction terms (gating):**

- What is the purchase price, and is the ~4.7× struck on store-level EBITDA (~$14.5M) or on a true/adjusted EBITDA net of corporate overhead and owner compensation?
- What are the sources & uses at close — equity, senior debt, any seller rollover/financing, transaction fees, and any earnout or holdback?
- What are the debt terms — facility size, rate, amortization, covenants — and is the buildout program financed separately?
- What is the target hold period and underwritten exit multiple, and what supports an exit at or above the entry multiple for a sub-80-unit, single-region concept?

**Tier 2 — Quality of earnings:**

- Can you provide a reviewed/audited P&L bridging store-level EBITDA to *true* EBITDA (loading above-store G&A and normalized owner/exec comp), and then to levered free cash flow (maintenance capex + debt service)? What does the equity actually earn versus the marketed 22.9%?
- The FDD (Item 6) sets recurring fees at 5% royalty + a 2% Brand Fund (= 7% of Net Sales). The deck's P&L shows a single 4.7% "royalty" line — does that figure include the 2% Brand Fund, and what royalty/Brand-Fund rates does the buyer inherit at close?
- Can you provide the un-pro-forma trailing-actual EBITDA, with the pro-forma adjustments itemized?

**Tier 3 — Structure, fees, alignment:**

*Confirmed by the sponsor: ~90–100% of the SPV is PCM principal capital, invested pari-passu with co-investors, and funded via a single capital call (no multi-year follow-on calls) — to be reflected in the LPA.*

- What is the fee and promote structure at the co-invest level (management fee, carry, and any deal-level acquisition/monitoring/disposition fee), confirmed in the LPA rather than verbally?

**Tier 4 — Asset & scope:**

- What is the disposition of the two excluded sister units (Fort Collins-Harmony and Las Vegas-Centennial) — carved out, retained by Timmons, or a different franchisee — and what are the non-compete / territory terms if retained?
- Does Kyle Archer hold equity (versus an officer title) — i.e., is the buyer acquiring 100% of Timmons or a Timmons + Archer cap table?
- What is the asking price for each of the two franchisee-owned parcels — Cap Hill (638 E Colfax) and UNC (1645 8th Ave)? We need both figures to fold the real estate into our underwriting. If you would rather we lease the parcels, what rent and terms apply?
- What is the new-build schedule and per-unit cost (deck $775K vs. FDD range $631K–$2.086M), what AUV is underwritten for the new Kansas / Kansas City markets, and is the DTC unit's 267% cash-on-cash representative or an atypical low-build (tenant-improvement / second-generation) one-off?

---

## Bottom line

Operating diligence clears and the asset is genuinely attractive — a premium operator, 24 healthy units (4.6–4.9★, all open), real and owned real estate, and a clean principal. **Do not commit capital until the transaction terms are disclosed and an audited bridge from store-level EBITDA to buyer cash flow is in hand.** On the deck's own data, the honest base case is ~2.1× / ~13% over a long hold, with the marketed 4–6× available only if a near-tripling of the footprint executes flawlessly. The decision is a *terms* decision now, not a *business* decision.
