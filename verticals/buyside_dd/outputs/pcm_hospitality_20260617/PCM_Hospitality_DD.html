Prepared: 2026‑06‑17 · Basis: the June 2026 deck + a follow‑up "key terms" email + primary filings as of this date · Method: two‑mode (first‑principles disconfirmation + primary‑source verification: SEC Form ADV, FDD/franchise trade press, Prudential/Butterfly/KBP records).
Verdict — conditional; not investable until the questions in §7 resolve, and not the deal the deck implies. This is not a fraud: PCM Encore is a real $1.9B RIA with a clean record, Michael Paulus genuinely founded Assurance IQ ($2.35B Prudential exit), and Barry Dubin really co‑built KBP Brands (the #4 US restaurant franchisee). The catch — same pattern as a typical promoter deck — is facts mostly true, takeaways tilted in the sponsor's favor: your own wealth manager is soliciting you into its principals' illiquid, affiliated franchise deals (a conflict its ADV doesn't properly disclose); the headline 4–6x return rests on dressed‑up operational assumptions that an honest base case pulls toward ~2x net over a long, illiquid hold; and the actual asset ("Sublime Huts," 23 stores) cannot be verified from any public source.
What the "key terms" email improves. A follow‑up from PCM states no management fee and no carried interest on this deal, $100k minimum, long‑term hold. If confirmed in the binding documents, this removes the single largest economics critique (co‑invest MOICs quoted gross of fees overstate LP returns 300–600 bps; here net ≈ gross), and — if it reflects a true pari‑passu ride alongside PCM's own capital — is the most‑aligned structure an LP can get. It does not fix the unverifiable asset, the dressed‑up return math, the long illiquid single‑operator hold, or the ADV disclosure gap. The "long‑term hold" guidance also quietly walks back the deck's own "4x by year 5."

2 · First‑principles findings (ranked)

HIGH  Your RIA is selling you its own principals' deal, under‑disclosed in its ADV

PCM Encore (the RIA) is offering its own advisory clients co‑investments sponsored by affiliated PCM Growth. The Form ADV Part 2A — which the deck points you to "for conflicts of interest" — discloses PCM Hospitality only as a "Michael Paulus may allocate time" footnote, with zero mentions of "co‑invest," "Qdoba," "Cheba," "carried interest," or "private fund," and Section 7.B private‑fund reporting = "No Information Filed" (reports advising zero private funds while marketing pooled co‑invest vehicles). The no‑fee term softens the "monetizing you twice" sharpness, but the disclosure/compliance gap remains.

HIGH  The headline 4–6x rests on dressed‑up assumptions; honest base case ≈ 2x net

AUV overstated ~20%: deck ~$2.8M vs. Cheba Hut's own 2024 figures $2.27M mature / $1.97M all‑store ($2.8M ≈ the brand's 2030 aspiration). "Conservative 5x exit" is misleading: entry is 4.7x, so 5x is ~flat — and for a 23‑unit franchisee of a sub‑80‑unit niche concept, 5x is top‑of‑range, not conservative. "Double EBITDA over the hold" = ~15%/yr CAGR, achievable only via capex‑heavy new builds, not organic — amid restaurant labor/food‑cost margin pressure. If new‑build ramp disappoints (common), at a flat 4.7x exit with ~2.5x leverage the equity compresses toward ~2x (~15% IRR), and the leverage makes the downside non‑linear. (IRR math: 4x/5yr = 32%; 6x/10yr = only 19.6% — the "6x by year 10" reads better than it compounds.)

HIGH  The actual asset can't be verified

The deal is "buy these 23 specific Cheba Hut stores," yet "Sublime Huts" appears in no public source. The publicly‑identified largest Cheba Hut franchisee is Elevated Inc. (~22 units). Either Sublime is a private entity not yet in press, or it's a deal‑entity name for that platform — but you cannot currently verify the asset you'd be buying, its unit count, or its standing.

MODERATE  Logo‑wall inflation + credential framing

"OpenAI" shown as a PCM Growth seed/control investment — false (a personal/passive stake at most; OpenAI never took control capital from a family office this size). Bradford Lin's KKR / J.P. Morgan logos are a KKR associate (2022–25) + one JPM summer internship presented as marquee credentials. On the operator: Dubin is real, but Mike Kulp (unnamed in the deck) is KBP's CEO, and the "$500M equity value / 100 acquisitions" is a KBP‑era, unrealized, never‑exited figure imported into a deal where Dubin's independent post‑KBP record is <2 years old with zero realized exits.

MODERATE  The Qdoba thesis sentence is false; the "pipeline" is a capital obligation

"Limited competition beyond Chipotle" is wrong (Taco Bell ~7,200 units, Moe's, Del Taco, Rubio's…) — Qdoba is the perennial #2 because the category is contested. The "55‑location pipeline offered by Qdoba Corporate" is a ~$45–50M buildout obligation (50 units × ~$900K–1M) with ramp/site risk, not a gift. Qdoba unit economics: AUV ~$1.6–1.7M, ~16% store EBITDA post‑fee, bottom‑quartile AUV ~$926K (wide left tail).

7 · Questions to PCM (send before any commitment)

Alignment, fees & structure
  1. Pari passu: Are Encore co‑investors investing on the exact same terms as PCM's own capital — same security/class, per‑unit price, and liquidation priority — with no preferential allocation, side letters, or seniority for PCM or its principals? Confirm in writing.
  2. Fees/carry — in the binding docs (not email): confirm no management fee and no carry at the PCM co‑invest level. Is there any operator‑level promote (B Wild / 7 Star Eats) or PCM Growth deal‑level fee (acquisition, monitoring, financing, disposition) ahead of or alongside LP returns?
  3. "90%+ PCM's own capital": confirm the actual $ and % commitment from PCM/principals vs. the client co‑invest slug for these specific deals.
  4. Disclosure: will these co‑invest vehicles be reported as private funds on an amended Form ADV, and where is the affiliated co‑investment conflict disclosed? (Part 2A currently covers PCM Tax and PCM Kinect but not PCM Hospitality.)
Liquidity, hold & capital calls
  1. "Long‑term hold": target hold period, and is there any liquidity — redemptions, transfer/tag‑along, or secondary? Expected timeline to first distribution?
  2. Capital calls: is the $100k fully funded at close, or are there follow‑on calls (e.g., the Qdoba buildout)? Committed‑vs‑called structure?
  3. Governance: what information and consent rights (LPAC, major decisions, affiliate transactions) do co‑investors have?
The Cheba Hut asset
  1. "Sublime Huts" identity: confirm the legal entity, the exact 23‑store list/locations, and provide the Cheba Hut FDD Item 20 franchisee roster confirming Sublime Huts and its unit count. (Public sources identify Elevated Inc. at ~22 units as the largest — clarify the relationship.)
  2. Store‑level financials: audited/reviewed trailing P&Ls for the 23 units — actual AUV and store‑level EBITDA margin (reconcile the deck's ~$2.8M vs. the brand's ~$2.0–2.3M).
  3. Entry basis: what EBITDA is the 4.7x struck on (LTM actual vs. adjusted/pro‑forma), and the purchase price and debt at close (the ~2.5x leverage)?
Return assumptions
  1. EBITDA "double": split projected growth between new builds and same‑store, with the build schedule, per‑unit capex, ramp assumptions — and the case if new‑build ramp underperforms.
  2. Exit multiple: what supports calling 5x "conservative" given a 4.7x entry? Show the modeled equity outcome at a flat 4.7x exit.
Qdoba
  1. Development agreement: total committed units (deck 55 vs. press 50), build schedule and non‑performance penalties, total buildout capex and financing, and the store‑level economics underwritten vs. the FDD (~$1.6–1.7M AUV, ~16% post‑fee EBITDA, bottom‑quartile ~$926K).
