## MODE B — FIRST-PRINCIPLES (LEAD): is HIMS ever a buy?

**Synthesis verdict: the bear case is not a forecast — it is already in the Q1 2026 10-Q.** This is the rare case where the marketed "consumer-health platform compounding to durable 20%+ growth" takeaway **diverges from the company's own filed data**, which shows a US business that *shrank* year-over-year while margins fell 8 points. That divergence — takeaway vs data — is the honesty-alpha catch here. Grade is PASS, and the reasons are disconfirming evidence, not promoter framing.

**(a) The GLP-1 compounding cliff is here, not hypothetical.**
- Hims began compounded GLP-1 in May 2024, rode the FDA semaglutide shortage. **Feb 2026: the FDA issued a statement that it intends to restrict GLP-1 APIs used in mass-marketed compounded drugs — and Hims was DIRECTLY NAMED in that statement** (per the 10-Q's own risk section). That is the regulator pointing at this specific company.
- **March 2026: Hims announced the "2026 US WL Announcement"** — a forced strategic shift of US weight-loss to *branded* GLP-1, with compounded "on a limited scale." Booked **$33.5M non-recurring restructuring** ($28.5M of it inside cost of revenue). Translation: the high-margin compounded engine is being dismantled by regulation, and the replacement (branded GLP-1 resale) is structurally lower-margin and supply-constrained.
- Management's own words: customers "who previously accessed compounded alternatives may be unwilling or unable to transition to branded therapies, which could adversely affect demand, conversion rates, and revenue" + "increased churn." The company is telling you the cliff exists.

**(b) The Novo blow-up is a channel/management-quality tell — and now litigation.**
- Hims/Novo announced a branded-Wegovy partnership in 2025; **Novo publicly terminated it (June 2025) and accused Hims of "illegal mass compounding" and deceptive marketing.** The 10-Q confirms the fallout: **Novo sued Hims Feb 9 2026** (D. Del.) for infringing Novo's '343 semaglutide patent, seeking enhanced (willful) damages and a **permanent injunction** barring semaglutide products until the patent expires Dec 5 2031. Novo voluntarily dismissed *without prejudice* March 9 2026 — **reserving the right to refile.** Refile risk is live, not closed.
- Worse for management quality: there is now a **securities class action AND derivative suits** alleging Hims misrepresented "the business relationship between the Company and Novo Nordisk." A partnership the company marketed as validation became a lawsuit alleging the marketing itself was misleading. That is the exact takeaway-vs-data masking pattern this desk exists to exclude.
- Governance overlay: founder/CEO Andrew Dudum holds **Class V super-voting stock at 175 votes/share** (8.4M Class V ≈ 1.47B votes vs 222.5M Class A) — he controls the vote outright. In a name facing securities-fraud claims over promotional conduct, concentrated founder control with no shareholder check is a negative, not a positive.

**(c) Retention/CAC in a promotional model — ARPU is already rolling.**
- Subscribers +9% YoY (2.366M → 2.584M) **but Monthly Revenue per Average Subscriber FELL −6% ($85 → $80).** Subs growth is now bought partly via M&A (ZAVA/intl, CSBio, Livewell), not pure organic pull, while the *unit economics per sub deteriorate.* That is the signature of a promotional acquisition model whose mix is shifting away from its highest-value cohort (compounded WL).

**(d) Is 3x sales pricing perfection on a decelerating, regulatorily-exposed top line? Yes.**
- **Revenue growth collapsed from +111% (FY24→FY25 was +59%; Q1'25 was +111% YoY) to +3.8% YoY in Q1 2026.**
- **The deceleration is worse than the headline:** US revenue, the core, **DECLINED −8.4% YoY** ($578.7M → $529.9M). 100% of the +$22M total "growth" came from Rest-of-World (+$70.9M, i.e. +969%) — almost entirely the **ZAVA (Europe) acquisition closed July 2025**, not organic Eucalyptus/intl pull-through. Strip M&A and the franchise is shrinking domestically.
- Profitability inflection *reversed*: **Q1 2026 net loss −$92.1M** (vs +$49.5M Q1'25); **adj-EBITDA halved to $44.3M / 7% margin** (from $91.1M / 16%); **gross margin 65% vs 73%.** A 26x EV/adj-EBITDA multiple on a business whose EBITDA is contracting and whose GM just fell 8 points is paying for a recovery that has not been demonstrated.

**(e) Positioning .71 = crowded long — who is the marginal buyer?** This is a heavily-owned, heavily-shorted, retail-and-momentum name (note the proliferation of 2x/3x HIMS leverage ETPs and YieldMax single-stock products in the contract list — a microstructure tell of speculative crowding). With the compounding engine in regulatory wind-down, the marginal incremental buyer thesis ("durable platform compounding") is the one the data just refuted. Crowded long + refuted thesis + super-voting founder + live securities litigation = asymmetric downside on any guide-down.

### When IS it a buy? Only on a reset that resolves the divergence, not on price alone:
1. **Price reset to ~$18–22** (≈2.0–2.3x EV/sales, ≈14–17x a stabilized adj-EBITDA), AND
2. **A clean post-compounding growth proof:** ≥2 quarters where *US* (not RoW-M&A) revenue re-accelerates on *branded* GLP-1 with gross margin stabilizing ≥68%, AND
3. **Novo litigation overhang resolved or time-barred** (no refile by the patent-injunction route), AND
4. churn from the compounded→branded transition shown to be absorbed (ARPU stops falling).
Until then it is a falling knife whose own filings flag the knife.
