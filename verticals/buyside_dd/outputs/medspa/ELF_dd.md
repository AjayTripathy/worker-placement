## MODE B — FIRST-PRINCIPLES (LEAD)

I derived the decisive question with no promoter framing: **is the +25% a skinification/share-gain
machine available at a reset price, or acquisition-fueled optics masking a decelerating, tariff-hit,
GAAP-unprofitable mass name still priced for growth?** Verdict: **mostly the former, but the bull
narrative materially over-states the "cheap" and under-states the GAAP-economics absorption.**

### The honesty-alpha catch (takeaway-vs-data divergence)
The marketed takeaway — *"7th consecutive year of net sales & share growth, +25%, Adj EBITDA up 13%"* —
is **technically true and the company discloses the offsets clearly**, so this is NOT a liar's tell.
But the takeaway-vs-data gap an institutional reader must internalize:
- **GAAP operating income fell 53% (FY25 $158.0M → FY26 $73.6M); GAAP net income fell 77% ($112.1M → $26.3M); GAAP EPS $0.44.** Revenue grew 25%, gross margin held (~71%), yet GAAP profit collapsed.
- The wedge is real and disclosed: a **$57.6M fair-value charge on the Rhode earnout** (an honesty
  marker — it ballooned from an initial $7.1M *because Rhode is outperforming*), heavy acquired-intangible
  amortization (~$79M D&A), Rhode acquisition/seller costs ($47.1M), and rising tariff cost in COGS.
- The Q4 standalone was a **GAAP net LOSS of $49.4M ($0.82 loss/share)** even as net sales rose 35%.

Grade: **CLEAN on disclosure integrity** — every offset is itemized in the reconciliation; this is a
genuinely accretive deal absorbing one-time/non-cash costs, not a polished story diverging from its
own data. The catch is that **a reader anchoring on "Adj EBITDA +13%" misses that the business is
running near GAAP breakeven while it integrates two acquisitions and eats tariffs.** Use adj metrics,
but size the position knowing the GAAP cushion is thin.

### Steelman the bear — point by point
- **(a) De-rate justified / tariff structural?** PARTLY. China was ~100% of sourcing in 2019, now
  "a little less than 75%." The 25% Section 301 China tariff (since May 2019) is structural and
  stays. The 2025 "reciprocal" stack that pushed the weighted-average rate toward ~60% (from ~25%)
  was the acute pain — **but on Feb 20, 2026 the US Supreme Court INVALIDATED the IEEPA tariffs**
  (per the 10-K, verbatim). The administration re-imposed under alternative authority, so uncertainty
  persists and *refund timing/amount is "highly uncertain,"* but the worst-case legal basis is gone.
  e.l.f. also **raised prices globally Aug 1, 2025** to offset, and Q4 gross margin actually rose
  ~140bps YoY to 73% (pricing > tariff that quarter). Net: tariff is a real margin headwind but is
  **legally contested + partly mitigated, not cleanly "structural."** This is the single most
  important Mode-B fact and it tilts AGAINST the structural-bear.
- **(b) Organic deceleration — what's organic?** REAL CONCERN, partly confirmed. FY27 guide is
  **+12-14%** vs FY26 +25% vs FY24 +77%. The decel is unambiguous. Rhode ($390M FY26 net sales, +80%)
  and Naturium (~$250M retail, 2x pre-deal) are doing heavy lifting; back them out and the legacy
  e.l.f. Cosmetics core is growing far slower (US color-cosmetics *category* grew only ~4% last
  quarter, skincare ~8%; e.l.f. beats ~2x but off a decelerating base). **The +25% IS substantially
  acquisition-and-share-gain fueled.** Bull mitigant: share gains are durable (7 straight years) and
  the category mix-shift to skincare is structural, not a fad.
- **(c) Rhode = expensive celebrity bet?** NO on price, YES on key-person risk. $897.5M for a brand
  doing $390M net sales / ~$500M annualized retail = **~2.3x net sales / ~1.8x retail for an
  80%-growth, Sephora-#1 brand** — that is *cheap-to-fair*, not expensive, and the earnout
  revaluation proves it's beating plan. The real risk is **Hailey Bieber key-person/celebrity-fade**:
  Rhode's equity is the founder's relevance. The 10-K flags this explicitly. This is the position's
  largest single idiosyncratic risk.
- **(d) Mass-beauty faddishness + retailer concentration (Target/Walmart).** VALID, unquantified
  here but structurally true; Gen-Z loyalty is fickle and 2 retailers anchor US distribution. The
  international/Sephora/prestige-adjacent push (Rhode, Naturium) is precisely the de-concentration
  hedge.
- **(e) ~3x sales not cheap for a decel'ing tariff-hit name.** At $67 it's **2.77x FY26 / 2.45x FY27
  EV/sales and ~20x adj-P/E** — that is NOT priced-for-perfection (perfection was the $150 high =
  ~7x sales), but it's also not distressed. For a +12-14% grower with ~20% adj-EBITDA margin and thin
  GAAP cushion, ~20x adj-P/E is **roughly fair**. The deep-value entry is $48-55 (where it traded
  this spring), not $67.

### Synthesis
**Skinification thesis: REAL and primary-source-confirmed** (skincare 9%→23% of consumption in 3
years; Naturium fastest-growing top-50 skincare brand at ~$250M retail; Rhode +80% to $390M). The
de-rate from $150 was *justified* (it corrected a ~7x-sales perfection multiple into a tariff +
decel reality) — so the de-rate is **an opportunity only at a price**. At $67 you are paying fair
value for a real franchise; the asymmetric entry is on tariff-headline or single-quarter-organic-miss
weakness into the $48-58 zone. **Not a value trap. Not a bargain at $67. A buy-on-weakness.**
