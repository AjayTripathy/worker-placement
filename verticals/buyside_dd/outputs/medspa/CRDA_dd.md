## Mode B — first-principles synthesis (LEAD)

**Verdict: a CYCLICAL specialty-chemical major with one genuine structural-growth jewel (Beauty Actives) embedded in it — fairly priced as a post-bubble chemical, NOT mispriced as a skincare pure-play. The deck's "purest beauty-actives shovel" framing is the marketed takeaway that diverges from Croda's own segment data.** This is a **REVIEW/CONSISTENT** name (honest disclosure, but the pitch's label overstates the structural-growth purity), not an ELEVATE catch — Croda discloses the cyclicality plainly. The honesty tell is in the *thesis framing*, not in Croda's filings.

The decisive disconfirming facts, derived without promoter framing:

1. **Beauty Actives is a slice of a slice — it is NOT the company.** Group sales £1,699.4M. Consumer Care = £972.7M (57% of group). Beauty Actives is **one of four** Consumer Care sub-units (Beauty Actives / Beauty Care / Home Care / Fragrances & Flavours) and Croda **does not even disclose it in absolute £m**. Triangulating from "~10% of an $8bn beauty-ingredients market" (Croda's own words), Beauty Actives is on the order of **£250–350M of sales — roughly 15–20% of the group.** So the thesis instrument is ~1/6 of what you're buying. You are buying **Croda the diversified chemical company** to get exposure to the beauty-actives jewel. That is the opposite of "purest exposure."

2. **The other ~80% is exactly the cyclical/specialty-chem bear case, and it's live right now.** Industrial Specialties (£194.5M) *shrank* −4.6% and earns a **4.6% margin** (down from 7.6%). Life Sciences (£532.2M) is whipsawed by destocking and the COVID-lipid unwind. Group adjusted operating margin is **17.4%** — versus **24.8% at the 2021 peak.** That 740bp of margin is the bubble that left.

3. **The 2021 multiple is gone for good, and correctly so.** 2021 was a COVID-lipid super-spike: sales ~£1.9bn, adj operating profit £469M (24.8% margin), driven by ~$120M (2022) of vaccine-lipid sales that fell to ~$60M (2023) and have now been written down — Croda **placed its Lamar (USA) lipids plant on standby and took a £44.6M impairment in FY2025.** The peak ~2.3x sales / ~50x P/E reflected earnings that no longer exist. Pricing CRDA off the 2021 anchor is the trap.

4. **Is it cheap or a value trap on normalised earnings? — Neither; it's roughly fair.** At 3,022p: trailing adj P/E **~20.7x** (146.2p adj EPS), forward **~19x** (consensus ~159p), **EV/Sales ~2.8x**, **P/Sales ~2.5x**, net debt £523.8M at a comfortable **1.3x EBITDA**, FCF £161.6M (9.5% of sales), dividend 111p = **3.5% yield, covered 1.3x by adj EPS.** That is *not* a value-trap multiple and it is *not* a bargain — it is a quality-compounder paying ~20x for ~mid-single-digit organic growth with margin-recovery optionality. The de-rate from the bubble has happened; what's left is a fair price for a recovering specialty major. (Caveat: statutory EPS is only 44.4p vs 146.2p adjusted — the gap is the Lamar impairment + amortisation; watch that adjusted-to-statutory bridge, but FY2025 it is explained and disclosed.)

**Net:** clean, honestly-disclosed business; the *only* lie-risk is in the **sleeve's marketing label** ("purest cosmetic-actives shovel"), which the segment math refutes. If the sleeve owns CRDA, own it as **a fairly-priced cyclical-quality chemical with a beauty-actives kicker and a Life-Sciences-trough call option** — not as a structural-skincare pure-play. The genuinely "pure" beauty-actives names don't exist at this quality/liquidity; Croda is the *best diversified proxy*, which is a real but different thesis.

5. **"Consumer Care recovering while Life Sciences stabilises."**
   Verified. 6. **Margins / growth / balance sheet / valuation / dividend.** All VERIFIED above from FY2025 RNS: group margin 17.4% (target **>20% by FY2028**), adj EPS 146.2p, dividend 111p (3.5% yield, 1.3x cover), net debt £523.8M / 1.3x, FCF £161.6M.
