## MODE B — first-principles disconfirmation (LEAD)

**The decisive question, with no promoter framing:** *Is INSP a coding-driven dislocation in a structurally-growing franchise (buyable), or a structurally-impaired growth story where coding is just the trigger that exposed GLP-1 + competition + saturation (avoid)?*

### Steelman the bear (the part the "short is wrong" screen wants to dismiss)
- **(a) GLP-1 is real for the mild-moderate segment.** SURMOUNT-OSA (tirzepatide, NEJM) is not soft: AHI −55% to −62.8%; **43–51.5% of moderate-severe obese patients met disease *resolution*.** That is a genuine bite into the *lower* end of Inspire's funnel. Inspire's own CFO conceded on the Q1 call: *"in the short term, our revenue is being adversely impacted by their increasing prevalence and adoption."* This is **VERIFIED**, management-confirmed, not hypothetical.
- **(b) Competition is now real.** Nyxoah's **Genio** got FDA approval **Aug 8, 2025** (AHI 15–65, leadless/bilateral, battery-free, MRI-compatible) on the DREAM trial (n=115, safety "similar to Inspire"). LivaNova/aura6000 and ResMed/Zoll loom. INSP's first credible US implant competitor exists.
- **(c) Deceleration + multiple compression is independent of GLP-1.** SEC-verified revenue: FY21 $233M → FY22 $408M (+75%) → FY23 $625M (+53%) → FY24 $803M (+28%) → **FY25 $912M (+14%)** → **FY26 guide $825–875M = −4% to −10%.** Q1'26 was +1.6%. The S-curve was already bending *before* the coding shock; coding accelerated it into the **first revenue decline in company history.**
- **(d) Is "DISCOVERING / short is wrong" just hope?** Partly. The screen reads attention ~0 / positioning .32 and infers a contrarian long. But the de-rate is *informed*, not retail-absence: BofA→Underperform $39, Piper→Neutral ($85→$55), 24-analyst consensus PT cut 24% in 3 months. This is not an un-discovered name; it's a *known, repriced* one. Treating low retail attention as un-priced edge here would be a mistake.

### The bull, honestly stated
- **The coding gap is mechanical and time-boxed, not demand destruction.** Management: the CPT code used for ~10,000 Inspire V cases in 2025 *"is no longer available."* The fix is a single-lead Category-I CPT code; realistic effective date **Jan 1, 2028** (Sept-2026 CPT panel is the next swing). In the interim, Medicare facility payment is bridged via a new C-code = Inspire IV's 64582 economics; commercial still bills 64568; physicians are pushed to unlisted 64999 (manual review → pipeline drag). **The demand exists — management: *"we still don't have the capacity to treat the patient demand that we have"*** — but the *billing plumbing* for the newest device is temporarily broken. That is a classic **dislocation mechanism**: a money-good franchise marked down on a reimbursement-administration headline that does not reach the underlying clinical demand.
- **GLP-1 as a funnel tailwind is a genuine mechanism, not spin.** SURMOUNT-OSA *resolves* OSA mostly in the lower-severity band; the **moderate-severe, CPAP-intolerant, surgical-candidate pool that is Inspire's core is far less addressed by weight loss alone.** Two real funnel-expansion channels: (i) very-high-BMI patients currently *ineligible* for Inspire become eligible after GLP-1 weight loss; (ii) GLP-1 prescribing *pulls undiagnosed OSA patients into the medical system* → more sleep studies → more diagnosis. CEO: *"long-term benefit to our market from GLP-1s as prospective patients lose weight and become eligible."* And GLP-1 durability is conditional on staying on the drug — discontinuation reverses AHI.
- **Inspire V + margin.** GM 86.5% (+180bps YoY) *on a higher Inspire V mix* — the simplified single-incision, accelerometer-sensing (no separate pressure-sensing lead) device is margin-accretive even through the disruption.
- **Clean balance sheet + buyback.** $400M net cash, no debt/preferred, **$200M repurchase authorization (Aug 2025); shares down 29.67M→28.81M.** Company is buying its own dislocation.

### Mode B verdict
**This is a real dislocation wrapped around a real deceleration — a "two-clock" situation.** The short is *right about 2026* (revenue declines, coding drag is genuine, GLP-1 nibbles the bottom of the funnel) and *probably wrong about the terminal franchise* (core moderate-severe surgical pool persists, GLP-1 net-expands diagnosis, balance sheet is fortress, V is margin-accretive, a 2028 code likely re-rates it). The screen's "GLP-1 short is wrong" is the **right answer to the wrong question** — the load-bearing bear is *coding*, not GLP-1, and that bear is time-boxed to ~18 months.

**Honesty grade: CLEAN.** Management is not masking. They explicitly disclosed the lost CPT code, the −4% to −10% guide cut, the $120–150M coding+GLP-1 impact, the territory consolidation (284), and conceded GLP-1 short-term pain. There is **no takeaway-vs-data divergence** — the marketed story ("transition year, return to growth 2027") is, if anything, *more optimistic than BofA's ~0% 2027*, but it is honestly bounded and the negatives are on the page. This is honestly-disclosed bad news, not a polished lie. Per the honesty-alpha framework, a genuinely-impaired-but-honest name is CLEAN; the alpha here (if any) is *fundamental dislocation*, not *liar-exclusion*.
