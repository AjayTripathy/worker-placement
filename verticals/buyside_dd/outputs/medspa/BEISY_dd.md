## MODE B — FIRST PRINCIPLES (LEAD) — steelman the bear, hunt disconfirming evidence

The promoter frame ("structural derm tailwind at a non-glamour multiple") buries the fact that the
*whole company is shrinking right now* and the multiple already compressed. The decisive question is
not "is Derma good" (it is) — it's **"does the sleepy/declining core cap the blended outcome, and is
the cut-in-half price now compensation for that?"**

**(a) Is Derma decelerating, and how much is US base-effect?** YES, decelerating.
- FY2025 Derma organic **+11.7%** (5th straight double-digit year — VERIFIED) → Q1-2026 **+8.2%**.
- The deceleration is partly base effect (North America was the launch whitespace; NA Q1-26 only
  +7.0% vs >2x Brazil and +87% China). The non-US engines (Brazil 2x, China +87%) are still torrid,
  so Derma is **decelerating from exceptional to merely excellent**, not breaking. Not the bear's
  strongest card.

**(b) Does the mass core cap the blend? — THIS IS THE BEAR'S REAL CARD, and it's worse than "sleepy."**
- **Group Q1-2026 organic was −4.6% (nominal −7.7%).** NIVEA −7.0%, La Prairie −14.9%. The
  +8.2% Derma was a *bright spot inside a contracting quarter*, not a tailwind lifting the group.
- FY2025 group organic was only **+2.4%**; Consumer **+2.5%**; **NIVEA +0.9%** with nominal NIVEA
  sales *falling* €5,601M → €5,529M.
- Derma is **€1,492M = 15.1% of group / 18.2% of Consumer.** Even doubling Derma's growth premium,
  a 15% slice cannot drag a 56%-of-sales NIVEA that is *losing share 20–120 bps/month in W. Europe*
  (Feb-25→Jan-26) into double digits. **The core caps the blend. Verdict: the bear is right that the
  blended grower is low-single-digit, not low-double-digit.**

**(c) La Prairie / China.** Confirmed drag: −4.5% FY2025, **−14.9% Q1-2026** on US retail + China
travel-retail disruption. It's only €478M (4.8% of group) so it can't sink the thesis, but it's a
live negative-momentum line, not a stabilizer.

**(d) Family-controlled capital-return discount — REAL but partly disconfirmed.**
- **maxingvest AG (Michael & Wolfgang Herz) owns 52.01%**; Beiersdorf holds 9.99% treasury; free
  float ~38%. Classic perma-discount setup (low float, control block, conservatism).
- BUT the disconfirming evidence: they ran a **€500M buyback in 2025** and there were **insider
  buys on the 2026 drop**. So the "no capital return" leg is *weaker than the bear assumes* — net
  cash is finally being deployed. The discount is real; the "they never return cash" version is not.

**(e) Is ~1.7x sales / 17.6x EPS actually cheap for a low-single-digit blended grower?**
- This is the crux, and the thesis got the *number* wrong (it's ~1.7x, not 3.5x) but that helps the
  bull. ~17.6x trailing for a 14% EBIT-margin business with **~€4B+ net cash** (so ~14–15x ex-cash)
  and a structurally-growing 15% derm slice is **fair-to-modestly-cheap, not a trap**. Versus L'Oréal
  (~30x+) and Estée Lauder's wreck, this is the value end of beauty.
- The honest read: it's cheap *because the core is broken right now*, not in spite of a sleepy core.
  You are paid to wait, but the re-rate needs NIVEA to **stop bleeding share**, which has no proven
  date.

### MODE B SYNTHESIS
**A genuinely good derm division trapped inside a controlled conglomerate whose mass core is actively
losing share — but the market already knows, and has cut the stock ~45%.** This is **honesty-clean**:
management did NOT polish the takeaway. They *named* the NIVEA share loss (20–120 bps/month), cut 2026
guidance to flat-to-slightly-positive (vs ~4% consensus) themselves, and disclosed La Prairie −14.9%.
There is **no takeaway-vs-data divergence** — the bad news is on the tin. Per the honesty-alpha
framework, that makes it CLEAN, not a catch. The edge here is *not* lie-exclusion (there's no lie); it's
a **dislocation/mix-shift call**: pay ~14–15x ex-cash for a 14%-margin compounder where the
fastest-growing, highest-quality 15% is structurally taking share and the de-rating overshot.
