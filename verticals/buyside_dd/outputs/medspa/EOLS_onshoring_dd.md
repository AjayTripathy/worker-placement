# Evolus (NASDAQ: EOLS) — Onshoring / Tariff-RELIEF Path, Primary-Source Diligence

**Date:** 2026-06-25 · **Live price:** $7.14 (IBKR last) · **CIK:** 1570562
**Companion note:** `EOLS_tariff_dd.md` (quantified the ~$4–9M/yr durable drag; treated escape routes as parentheticals)

**Question under test:** Is onshoring / domestic-investment relief a CREDIBLE path that WAIVES the 15% tariff on Korean-made Jeuveau, or is Evolus structurally stuck eating it?

**VERDICT: The relief lever is STRUCTURALLY OUT OF REACH for Evolus. The drag is DURABLE.** The tariff is the Section 232 PHARMA tariff (which *does* have a domestic-investment relief mechanism), but (a) the onshoring rate for Korea-origin product is 20% — WORSE than the 15% cap Korea already enjoys — so onshoring is economically pointless unless EOLS reaches the 0% MFN-pricing track, which is unavailable to a cash-pay aesthetic; (b) the application window CLOSED June 12, 2026 with no evidence Evolus or Daewoong applied; (c) Evolus doesn't own the manufacturing, can't fund US biologics capacity, and a BLA manufacturing-site change is multi-year; (d) Daewoong is building ALL new capacity in Korea (Hwaseong), none in the US. **EOLS is actually WORSE-positioned than the majors** who can buy 0% exemptions with US capex + MFN-pricing deals. Onshoring/relief does NOT change the prior conclusion: a durable ~$4–9M/yr (~1–3 GM-pt) headwind stays.

---

## Q1 — WHICH tariff, and does it have a domestic-investment relief mechanism?

**It is the Section 232 PHARMACEUTICALS tariff — NOT the broad reciprocal/country tariff.** Confirmed by Evolus's own 10-Q (filed 2026-05-04, period 3/31/26):

> "on April 2, 2026, the U.S. government issued a proclamation under **Section 232** of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 authorizing the imposition of significant tariffs on certain imports of **patented pharmaceuticals** and related ingredients, including tariffs of up to 100% (**currently 15% with respect to South Korea**), subject to various conditions, exceptions and potential reductions... may apply to products such as Jeuveau®, although the scope, timing and applicability... remain uncertain... including **product classification, country of origin, and potential agreements or exemptions**."

| Element | Finding | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Regime | Section 232 pharma proclamation (Proclamation 11020), Apr 2, 2026 | White House proclamation; EOLS 10-Q |
| Default rate | 100% ad valorem on patented pharma + APIs | Proclamation clause 3 |
| Korea/EU/Japan/Switzerland rate | **15%** ("unless a lower rate applies under clause 3") | Proclamation; US-Korea framework caps Korea Sec-232 pharma at ≤15% |
| Effective | Sept 29, 2026 (general); July 31, 2026 (Annex companies) | Proclamation |
| Coverage | Patented articles in FDA **Orange OR Purple Book** + APIs/key starting materials. **Generics & biosimilars EXEMPT** | Proclamation; multiple law-firm alerts |
| **Relief mechanism** | **YES — a company-specific onshoring-agreement pathway exists** | Fed. Reg. 2026-09489 (May 13, 2026) |

**The relief mechanism, exactly:**
- **Onshoring agreement → 20% rate** (through Apr 2, 2030, then steps to 100%). Requires a Commerce-approved plan: new US capex committed Jan 20 2025–Jan 20 2029, share of US-made production by 2029, milestones, senior-officer certification, periodic audited progress reports.
- **0% rate** (through Jan 20, 2029) only if the company ALSO signs an **MFN drug-pricing agreement with HHS.**
- **Application deadline: June 12, 2026** (electronic submission to pharma232@bis.doc.gov). **This window has CLOSED** as of this DD (June 25, 2026).

**The decisive structural trap — onshoring is the WRONG lever for Korea-origin product:**
The Korea cap is **15%**. The onshoring-agreement rate is **20%**. So an approved onshoring plan would *raise* Evolus's rate from 15% → 20% unless it reaches the **0% MFN-pricing track** — and an MFN HHS-pricing agreement is a mechanism for branded therapeutic drugs reimbursed by government payers, **not for a cash-pay aesthetic neurotoxin.** Jeuveau has no government-payer pricing to concede. **The 0% door is effectively shut; the 20% door is worse than status quo.** This is why "buy an exemption like Lilly/J&J/AstraZeneca did" does not translate to EOLS: the majors' exemptions are 0% MFN-pricing deals backed by tens of billions in US capex (Lilly $27B, J&J $55B). EOLS has neither the capex capacity nor a pricing lever to trade.

**Does onshoring FILL-FINISH (vs Korean drug substance) avoid the tariff?** No clean escape. The tariff covers the **patented finished article AND its API/key starting materials** in the Purple Book. US fill-finish of Korean drug substance would (i) still import the dutiable drug-substance/API, and (ii) require a multi-year BLA manufacturing-change supplement (below). It does not "waive" the tariff; at best it shifts the dutiable base to the (cheaper) substance — a partial, slow, capital-intensive mitigation, not elimination.

## Q2 — Has Evolus or Daewoong announced an onshoring / US fill-finish / alternate-source / domestic-investment plan?

**NO concrete plan. The "longer-term solution" is undefined.**

- **Evolus management (Q1 2026 call, verified via transcript):** *"we believe there is a pathway to mitigate or eliminate the impact of this tariff,"* evaluating "multiple options"; near-term = "secure significant U.S. inventory, supported by the product's three-year shelf life... as we bridge to **longer-term solutions**"; **"update expected by year-end."** No named solution — no US site, no second source, no capex figure, no onshoring application disclosed. The "longer-term solution" was **NOT specified** on the call or in any filing.
- **10-Q (written, primary):** the words "onshore," "fill-finish," "second source," "domestic manufacturing," "Section 232 onshoring agreement" appear **ZERO times.** Only qualitative language: "proactively managing inventory flows into the United States to protect against such tariffs, there can be no assurance that we will be able to do so." Tariff disclosed solely as a risk that "may have a material adverse impact."
- **8-Ks (2025-09 → 2026-06):** the only post-Q1 8-K (June 12, 2026) is annual-meeting voting results — **nothing on onshoring or tariff relief.**
- **Daewoong disclosures:** ALL new toxin capacity is in **Korea** — the 3rd Nabota plant (~$74.6M / ₩100B) and a broader ~$300M expansion are at **Hwaseong (Hyangnam), Gyeonggi Province**, taking capacity to ~18M vials/yr. **No US plant, no US fill-finish, no domestic-investment announcement** found in Daewoong's disclosures or Korean trade press (KBR, KED, FiercePharma).

**Finding: there is NO concrete onshoring/relief plan — for either company.** "Mitigate or eliminate" + "longer-term solutions" + "update by year-end" is stated *intent without substantiation.* The only demonstrated lever is the inventory pre-buy (a DEFERRAL, quantified in the companion note), not elimination.

## Q3 — Feasibility for THIS company (structural reality)

Every structural gate points the same way: relief is out of reach for Evolus on any timeline that matters.

| Gate | Reality | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership of manufacturing** | Evolus does NOT make the toxin. Daewoong makes it in Korea under a long-term supply/license deal; Evolus is a distributor/marketer. | Evolus cannot unilaterally onshore; it can only ask Daewoong to. |
| **Biologic BLA site change** | Moving a botulinum-toxin biologic to a new site requires an FDA **BLA manufacturing-change supplement with comparability** (analytical + potentially clinical bridging). Industry norm = **multi-year (~3–5 yr)**. | Even if funded today, no relief before ~2029–2030 — past the 20%→100% step and most of the deferral window. |
| **Capital** | EOLS: ~$50M cash (3/31/26), just at breakeven, ~$120M total dry powder mostly earmarked for growth/working capital. US biologics drug-substance capacity costs **hundreds of millions** and years. | Evolus **cannot fund** US biologics capacity. Confirmed structurally infeasible. |
| **Onshoring application** | Deadline was **June 12, 2026**; required senior-officer-certified new-US-capex commitments. No evidence EOLS or Daewoong applied; neither has US capex to certify. | The formal relief door is **already closed** for this cycle, and EOLS had nothing to put through it. |
| **Daewoong's incentive** | Daewoong supplies its OWN global markets (5 continents) + its US partner. US capacity would serve only the Evolus US volume (~$300M retail → a far smaller toxin-substance value). Daewoong is **expanding in Korea**, signaling it will serve global demand (incl. US) from Korea and **absorb/pass the tariff, not relocate.** | Daewoong has **weak incentive** to build US capacity for the Evolus slice alone. The party that COULD onshore won't; the party that WANTS relief CAN'T. |

**Even the favorable path (Daewoong builds US capacity) would not get Evolus a better rate than the 15% it already pays** — because for Korea-origin the relief tiers are 20% (onshoring) or 0% (MFN-pricing, unavailable). The only way US production *eliminates* the tariff is if the finished article becomes **US-origin** (made-in-USA, no dutiable import) — which requires Daewoong to build and FDA-qualify a US drug-substance + fill-finish line, a multi-year, multi-hundred-million-dollar commitment neither company has announced or can fund for this volume.

## Q4 — VERDICT

**The tariff is a DURABLE ~$4–9M/yr (~1–3 GM-pt) drag. Onshoring/relief is NOT a credible near-term waiver for Evolus.**

**Probability the tariff is eliminated via onshoring/domestic-investment relief on a thesis-relevant horizon (next ~2–3 yr): LOW, ~10–15%.** The realistic relief paths are NOT onshoring:
- **Most likely relief = product-classification / biologic carveout (NOT onshoring).** The proclamation reserves a 0% specialty-product carveout (orphan, plasma-derived, cell/gene, ADC, **CBRN medical countermeasures**). Botulinum toxin IS a CBRN countermeasure in its *antitoxin/therapeutic* framing — but Jeuveau is FDA-approved **aesthetic-only**, so the countermeasure carveout almost certainly does NOT reach it. Evolus's own 10-Q flags "product classification" uncertainty — this is the residual ambiguity, but it cuts toward "covered," not "exempt." **UNVERIFIABLE which way Commerce/CBP classifies cosmetic botulinum toxin; the weight of evidence is COVERED.**
- **Second path = the 15% Korea cap gets renegotiated lower** in future US-Korea trade talks (out of Evolus's control; CNBC reported Trump *threatened to raise* Korea pharma to 25% in Jan 2026 over enactment delays — risk is to the UPSIDE of the rate, not down).
- **Onshoring proper is the LEAST viable path** (20% > 15%; can't fund; multi-year; window closed; Daewoong building in Korea).

**Is EOLS worse off than the majors? YES.** Lilly/J&J/AstraZeneca/Novartis/Roche convert tens of billions of US capex + MFN-pricing concessions into **0%**. EOLS has no capex capacity and no pricing lever, so it is stuck at the **15% cap** with no door to a lower rate — structurally disadvantaged exactly where the big-pharma exemption machine works. The "buy an exemption" lever is a big-pharma instrument; a small-cap single-sourcing a Korean biologic it doesn't manufacture cannot pull it.

**Does this change the EOLS BUY?** **No change to the prior call — still BUY (small/spec), with the margin slope trimmed.** Onshoring optionality should be valued at ~zero; do NOT underwrite tariff elimination. The durable ~$4–9M/yr drag (roughly one year's nascent profit, ~1–3 GM pts, deferrable ~4–6 quarters by the pre-buy) stands as the steady-state assumption. The thesis survives on revenue growth + self-funding, not on relief.

**Honesty read (takeaway-vs-data divergence):** Management's *"pathway to mitigate or eliminate"* + *"longer-term solutions"* markets an optionality that the structure does NOT support — no plan, can't fund, doesn't own the plant, wrong relief tier for Korea-origin, supplier building in Korea, application window closed. This is **soft promotional framing**, not a contradicted datum (they correctly disclose the risk as potentially "material adverse" and that mitigation is uncertain). Grade: **REVIEW-flag, not ELEVATE** — the takeaway leans more hopeful than the data warrants, but the company hasn't asserted a false fact. Discount the "eliminate" language to "defer," and watch the year-end update for whether "longer-term solution" ever acquires a noun.

## What to MONITOR for relief (in priority order)

1. **A Section 232 product-classification ruling / exemption that excludes cosmetic botulinum toxin** — the single highest-probability *genuine* off-ramp (would moot the tariff entirely). Watch CBP rulings, BIS/Commerce specialty-carveout lists, and any EOLS 8-K announcing favorable classification.
2. **The promised Evolus YEAR-END (2026) tariff update** — does "longer-term solution" become a named plan with a number? Absence of specifics = confirmation the lever is out of reach.
3. **A Daewoong US-capacity / US fill-finish announcement** (Korean trade press + Daewoong IR) — would be the ONLY credible structural onshoring signal; none to date, and all current capex is Korea.
4. **Future US-Korea trade terms** moving the 15% Section 232 pharma cap (risk is two-sided; Jan-2026 reporting flagged upside-to-25% threat).
5. **Q2 (June) / Q3 (Sept) 2026 10-Q inventory line** — a real pre-buy must break above ~$33M / >4 months cover (deferral evidence, not elimination).
6. **An Evolus or Daewoong onshoring-agreement approval** (Commerce notifications / 8-K) — low-probability given the closed June-12 window and unfavorable 20% Korea tier; would still only matter if paired with US-origin production.
