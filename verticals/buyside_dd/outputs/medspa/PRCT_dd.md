## MODE B — First-principles disconfirmation (LEAD)

**The decisive question, with no promoter framing:** In a razor-razorblade model, the whole thesis
is that each placed system generates a growing, high-margin handpiece annuity. So: *is utilization
per system rising or falling?* Derive it from the company's own numbers:

- US install base **+40% YoY** (765 systems, Q1'26).
- US procedures **+30% YoY** (~12,200).
- **Procedures grew SLOWER than the install base.** Utilization per system is DECLINING, not
  compounding. Management itself flagged "low single-digit utilization growth" at Q4 and a "slower
  HYDROS placement ramp coupled with elongated launch timeline." **This is the bear's smoking gun and
  it is sourced to the company, not a short report.** (B1, B-utilization)

This inverts the marketed razor-blade story: PRCT has been selling capital boxes faster than it can
fill them with procedures — capital revenue front-loads the print while the annuity dilutes per unit.

**(a) Durable S-curve or capital-equipment hype curve?** The 2025 BPH-procedure data is damning for
the TAM-expansion version of the bull: total BPH surgical volume was ESSENTIALLY FLAT (284.7k '23 →
282.3k '24 → 288.9k '25). Aquablation is at ~45k procedures / 15.6% share — it is taking share from
TURP, NOT growing the pie. That makes this a **share-shift story inside a flat market**, which has a
mathematical ceiling and gets harder as the easy early-adopter hospital accounts saturate. The
utilization stall is the first visible evidence of that saturation dynamic. (B2)

**(b) Capital-equipment lumpiness / hospital-capex sensitivity.** Confirmed and live. Q4'25 was the
"strongest capital quarter" (65 systems) immediately followed by a soft Q1'26 (49 systems, 2 of which
were replacements) — textbook lumpiness. System ASP ~$485k means each placement is a hospital capital
committee decision, exposed to any hospital-capex pullback. (B3)

**(c) How much growth is priced?** At EV ~$1.03B on FY26 rev guide $390-410M, that's **~2.5x EV/
sales** for a 20-30% grower still losing ~$30M EBITDA. That is NOT priced-for-perfection — peers at
this growth/quality trade 4-8x. The market has ALREADY de-rated for the stall (Piper $50→$28, Wells
$51→$34, BofA→Underperform, Oppenheimer→Perform). The disconfirming risk here is to the BEAR: a lot of
bad news is in the tape. (B4)

**(d) Competitive / reimbursement.** Aquablation's differentiated niche (large prostates 80-150mL,
ejaculatory preservation) is real and now guideline-backed (see Mode A). UroLift (Teleflex, ~38k
procedures, office-based, declining) and Rezum (BSX, ~16k) compete in SMALLER glands / office settings
— different venue, less direct overlap. The durable competitive risk is not them; it's HoLEP/laser
enucleation (the academic gold standard for large glands) and the structural ceiling of a flat market.
(B5)

**(e) "Under-followed"?** REFUTED as a thesis pillar. PRCT is covered by Piper, Wells, BofA, Oppenheimer,
TD Cowen, Leerink et al. and trades ~$32M ADV. It is not neglected — it is **well-covered and recently
DOWNGRADED.** "Under-followed" is the wrong frame; "out-of-favor and freshly cut" is the right one.

**Mode B synthesis:** The bear case is the utilization stall in a flat market — and it is REAL and
company-disclosed. The bull case is that the stall is realignment-driven (self-inflicted, transient)
not saturation-driven (structural), and the price already pays you for the bear. **This is a
genuine cyclical-vs-structural coin-flip on one metric, bought at a de-rated price — a WATCH, not a
conviction long, and not a short either (too much bad news priced, guideline catalysts pending).**
