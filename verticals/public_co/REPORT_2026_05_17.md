## Executive summary

This is the result of running the Signal OS divergence-detection framework across **twelve themed public-equity cohorts** spanning two rounds:

- **Round 1 (8 cohorts):** defense-tech, lidar/ADAS, advanced nuclear/SMR, quantum computing, hydrogen/fuel-cell, AI-DC crypto-pivot, solid-state battery, robotics.
- **Round 2 (+4 cohorts):** cell/gene therapy, space/satcom, fintech lending/BNPL, retail distress.

Across **67 tickers** (49 speculative + 18 controls) analyzed by isolated, blinded subagents, the v2 emitter (composite ≥ 0.6 AND Discovery_advantage tier ∈ {HIGH, MED}) produced **14 paired long/short trades**. After a tighter conviction filter (composite ≥ 0.75 AND multi-flag AND clean long AND concentration cap), **7 pairs** survive. After the shortability + sizing pass, **3 pairs are cleanly executable at meaningful size**: IREN/EQIX, FCEL/LIN, AIRO/KTOS.

**Round 2 confirmed the framework's discrimination on new verticals but did not expand the deployable book.** Biotech, space, fintech, and retail cohorts produced one additional pair emit (RDW/IRDM at composite 0.71, below the tightened conviction threshold) and zero new cleanly executable pairs. The two genuinely hot round-2 findings — BIG at composite 2.17 (3 RED + 2 SEVE, highest of any name in the session, but finviz fetch failed → UNKNOWN tier, likely effectively delisted post-Chapter 11) and KSS at 0.88 (1 RED, but 24.4% short float → LOW tier, crowded) — were both correctly suppressed by the v2 emitter for the same reasons the round-1 PLUG/APLD/CORZ/BTDR/SERV cluster was suppressed.

This is the **principal finding** of the cross-round sweep: the intersection of (real divergence) AND (novel discovery) AND (executable shortability) is rare. 67 tickers across 12 cohorts converge on the same 3 deployable pairs that surfaced after round 1. That's the framework being honest about its yield, not a failure.

The framework's discriminations are real on both axes. Names with established short-seller attention (PLUG, APLD, CORZ, BTDR, SERV, BBAI, SYM, QS, KSS) scored high on Truth_signal but were *correctly suppressed* by the Discovery_advantage filter. The genuinely novel divergences (BLDP, FCEL, IREN, AIRO and the borderline KSCP/SES/MVST/SLDP) are less-followed, less-shorted, less-discussed — exactly where a divergence framework should find its edge. The framework is not a solvency model: bankrupt names with honest disclosure (LAZR, post-Ch.11 BIG) correctly score either zero divergence or get suppressed at the DA tier.

## Framework recap

Signal OS detects `R − f(M)`: where `R` is what an entity self-reports, `f` is a known legal/contractual/physical relationship, and `M` is independently observable record. When the gap is non-zero, the issuer's disclosure diverges from observable reality.

For public companies the implementation maps to:

- **R** — claims extracted from SEC filings (10-K, 20-F, S-1, 10-Q)
- **f** — the implicit "should match counterparty disclosure" rule for any claim naming a Tier-1 counterparty
- **M** — independent registries: EDGAR full-text search against counterparty CIKs, USPTO ODP for patent claims, USAspending.gov for federal contracts, EPA FRS for manufacturing facilities, NHTSA for vehicles, clinical_trials.gov for biotech

Each cohort is analyzed by **isolated, blinded Claude Code subagents** — one per ticker — with no outcome labels, no WebSearch/WebFetch access, no shared context with other tickers. Each subagent:

1. Picks the most substantive pre-cutoff filing
2. Slices it to ~120K chars focused on financial-statement notes (where evasion lives)
3. Extracts 5-8 testable factual claims
4. Picks M-source queries per claim, runs them
5. Scores severity: PASS / MODERATE / SEVERE / RED_FLAG_NEGATIVE / UNVERIFIABLE

Severity weights aggregate to a per-ticker **composite score**: weighted mean across non-UNVERIFIABLE claims (PASS=0, MOD=1, SEVE=2, RED=3).

### The v2 emitter (forward-bet selection)

Composite alone over-emits on already-crowded shorts. The v2 emitter gates on a second axis — **Discovery_advantage** — sourced from per-ticker finviz snapshots:

- **HIGH** — short_float < 10% AND (inst_own < 60% OR no analyst coverage). Genuinely novel.
- **MED** — short_float in [10, 20)%. Some discovery remaining.
- **LOW** — short_float ≥ 20%, OR inst_own ≥ 80% with analyst coverage. Crowded.
- **UNKNOWN** — finviz dropped the page (often signals delisting/deregistration).

A forward bet emits **iff** composite ≥ 0.6 (Truth_signal) AND tier ∈ {HIGH, MED} (signal is novel).

### Operational findings

1. **Beta differentials are large.** 1:1 dollar-neutral hedging from the raw pair sheet would significantly under-hedge: IREN at β=4.20 vs EQIX at β=0.99 needs **4.24×** on the short side, not 1×. MVST/BLDP/FCEL all need 2.5-3.3×.

2. **4 of 7 short legs trade sub-$5** (SES $1.13, MVST $1.42, BLDP $4.45, KSCP $2.95). Most retail brokers prohibit shorts on sub-$5 names; the trades require an institutional borrow desk and may carry punitive borrow rates.

3. **KSCP is operationally fragile.** Micro-cap ($48M) + sub-$5 + thin liquidity → max long-leg notional of $80K. Real signal (3 RED_FLAGs), no realistic vehicle.

4. **Concentration risk.** ALB and LIN each appear in 2 pairs. Stacking means doubled exposure to the long-side sector beta. Either halve per-pair sizing or diversify one of each to an external long.

## What I'd trust and what I wouldn't

### Trust

- **The framework's discriminations.** Across 42 names spanning 8 cohorts, the emit/suppress pattern is principled and reproducible. Names with multiple RED_FLAGs against major counterparty disclosure asymmetries are real findings; the Discovery_advantage gate correctly prunes already-crowded shorts that the framework would otherwise emit redundantly.

- **The blinded subagent design.** 32 of 32 subagents completed cleanly under the checkpointed prompt (defense + lidar + nuclear + quantum + the 27 second-round names). Each one sees only its assigned ticker; outcome contamination is structurally prevented.

- **The held-out validation.** The quantum cohort was run against calibration heuristics tuned on prior cohorts but never against quantum data. It produced clean discrimination (3 hot, 2 clean) — empirical evidence the heuristics generalize.

### Caveats

- **The v2 emitter is a discriminator, not a stock-picker.** "Composite ≥ 0.6 AND HIGH/MED" is a filter for *novel divergence*. It doesn't capture market microstructure (squeeze risk on micro-caps, options-driven gamma, momentum factors). The shortability pass is a partial fix.

- **The 12-month falsification window is generous.** A short that grinds sideways for 12 months without moving ≥50% adverse and without counterparty-disclosure confirmation stays "non-falsified" — but that's a losing position carry-wise. Real deployment needs tighter exit rules.

- **Some pairs use weak longs.** ARQQ/QBTS shorts vs IONQ long is two crowded short candidates against a third pre-revenue quantum name — that's not a sector-beta hedge, it's two-on-one quantum bearish exposure. The filtered top-7 explicitly excludes these by requiring long composite ≤ 0.30.

- **Concentration is real.** ALB + LIN each show up in 2 of the top 7. If lithium or industrial-gas sectors compress, multiple pairs take losses simultaneously. The deployable book above intentionally drops the second LIN pair (BLDP) for this reason.

- **Foreign-counterparty disclosure is a structural gap.** EU OEMs (VW, BMW, Mercedes) for battery/lidar; Korea Hydro/SK Group for hydrogen; private CoreWeave for AI-DC — all out of EDGAR coverage. The framework correctly returns UNVERIFIABLE on these but the gap matters for any name whose biggest customer falls outside coverage.

- **No held-out per-cohort base-rate validation.** For each cohort we have N tickers and K emits. We don't have a structured "what fraction of emits historically convert?" baseline. The defense cohort's AIRO-short prediction is the only emit with any time elapsed (filed pre-cutoff 2026-05-16; report dated 2026-05-17); falsification window is 12 months out.
