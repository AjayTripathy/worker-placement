# Signal OS — B1 Backtest Report

_Cutoff: 2024-05-17  ·  Forward window: 2024-05-17 → 2025-05-17_
_Initially generated 2026-05-17; updated after materiality fix to deterministic_scorer._

## TL;DR — final read

Three cohorts (fintech, hydrogen, solid-state battery) re-run through the
full Phase 1 → 2 → 3 pipeline at the May 17, 2024 cutoff. 17 tickers, 14
parallel LLM planner subagents — all completed without stalls.

**Headline: after fixing the Heuristic-7 materiality bug in the framework,
the May 2024 deterministic scorer emits ZERO signals across all 3 cohorts.**
Every non-control ticker scores composite 0.00. The framework would have
correctly declined to trade — and would have correctly declined to even
*rank* names within each cohort. There is no synthetic short to build.

This is qualitatively different from today (May 2026) where the same
3 cohorts produce composites of 0.67–1.57 with 4 emitted pairs across
them. Either:
- (a) The 2023 10-Ks really had less divergence than 2024-25 10-Ks
- (b) The M-source catalog at May-2024 was thinner (FDIC, sec_filings,
      FINRA, fdic_bankfind all added post-2024) — without them, most
      claims score UNVERIFIABLE
- (c) Both

The honest interpretation: B1 strongly suggests the framework's current
findings depend heavily on the recently-built M-source catalog. The
framework had no signal at May 2024 even on the same names that today
score 1.0+ composites.

## What changed between first and final read

The initial B1 run found 3 "synthetic pair" trades by relaxing the
0.6 emit threshold. Returns: PLUG/LIN +82%, SES/ALB -22.6%, UPST/COF -47.6%.
Average +4.0%, hit rate 33%, beat by SPY's +12.4%.

But every one of those 3 trades was driven by a Heuristic 7 firing
against a mega-cap counterparty:

| Pair | H7 trigger | Counterparty disclosure threshold |
|---|---|---|
| UPST/COF | UPST hosts on AWS; AMZN 10-K 0 hits | Amazon (~$600B rev) doesn't name individual AWS customers |
| PLUG/LIN | PLUG names Walmart as 23% customer; WMT 10-K 0 hits | Walmart doesn't disclose every vendor representing <1% of WMT revenue |
| SES/ALB | SES JDA with GM; GM 10-K 0 hits for "SES AI" | GM doesn't routinely name JDA partners in 10-K |

All three are the **same false-positive pattern**: small-cap focal company
× mega-cap counterparty × routine vendor/customer/JDA claim. The
counterparty silence is the expected baseline, not a disclosure gap.

We had already documented this exact bug earlier and built rescore_legacy.py
to patch it post-hoc. **But we never fixed the framework code itself, so
the bug reproduced when we re-ran the framework in B1.** This was the
right architectural fix to make at the time and we missed it.

After fixing `_score_edgar_fts` in `deterministic_scorer.py` to soft-fail
Heuristic 7 against a list of mega-cap counterparties (Amazon, Microsoft,
Apple, Walmart, GM, Ford, etc.), the picture changes:

| Ticker | Composite BEFORE fix | Composite AFTER fix |
|---|---:|---:|
| UPST | 0.25 | **0.00** |
| PLUG | 0.25 | **0.00** |
| SES | 0.33 | **0.00** |
| All others | 0.00 | 0.00 |

The PLUG/LIN +82% win was **right for the wrong reason** — the framework
flagged PLUG because of a bogus Walmart H7 firing, not because of the
real distress signals that materialized later (the FY2024 going-concern,
the SOFR+9.75% convertibles, etc., which weren't in the FY2023 10-K).

## What B1 actually tells us — final version

1. **The framework at May 2024 has essentially no signal across these
   3 cohorts after fixing the materiality bug.** Maximum legitimate
   composite is 0.00 for every non-control ticker.

2. **The framework's emit gate (composite ≥ 0.6) is irrelevant** because
   no name reaches even 0.1. The "conservatism gate worked" framing
   was overstated — there was nothing for the gate to filter.

3. **The framework's current findings (today, May 2026) hinge on
   connectors built since 2024.** FDIC Call Reports adjudicates fintech
   bank-partner credit. SEC submissions API adjudicates ABS issuance
   cadence. FINRA BrokerCheck adjudicates broker-dealer registration.
   None of these were in the catalog at May 2024. Without them, most
   claims score UNVERIFIABLE.

4. **The PLUG +82% lookback was a coincidence.** PLUG did fall over the
   year, but the framework's "short PLUG" signal at May 2024 was a
   bogus H7 firing. The right framework decision at May 2024 was: "I
   don't know whether PLUG is a short" — and that's now what it says.

5. **The framework's hindsight discipline held in Phase 1.** Plans
   correctly avoided post-cutoff events (no "Molecular Universe pivot",
   no "Walmart warrant forfeit", etc.). The contamination, when it
   appeared, was structural (H7 over-firing) — not LLM hindsight leakage.

## What B1 still doesn't tell us

1. **Whether the framework's CURRENT findings (May 2026) would have
   predictive power going forward.** B1 only tests if the framework
   at a past cutoff had signal — and shows it didn't. But today's
   catalog is materially richer.

2. **Whether the catalog expansion is genuinely surfacing real
   divergences, or just adding more ways to score MODERATE on weakly-
   evidenced patterns.** Need to control-test the current framework on
   "boring" cohorts — if today's framework also finds composites
   0.5+ on regional banks or utilities, it has the same false-
   positive problem.

3. **Generalization across cutoff dates.** May 2024 might be unusually
   barren. Need to run May 2023, May 2022, possibly May 2025.

4. **The 9 untested cohorts.** Defense, lidar, nuclear, quantum, dcpivot,
   robotics, cellgene, space, retail. Some (quantum, lidar) had distress
   names with established track records at May 2024 — the framework
   might have had more signal there.
