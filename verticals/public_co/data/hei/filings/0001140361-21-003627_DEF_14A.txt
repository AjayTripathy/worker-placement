Only holders of record of HEICO Common Stock, $0.01 par value per share (“Common Stock”), and Class A Common Stock, $0.01 par value per share (“Class A Common Stock”), as of the close of business on January 22, 2021 (the “Record Date”) will be entitled to vote at the Annual Meeting. Voting Requirements The presence, in person or by proxy, of a majority of the shares of all classes of HEICO’s common stock entitled to vote shall constitute a quorum at the Annual Meeting. If a quorum is present, approval of Proposals 1, 2 and 3 require the affirmative vote of a majority of the shares of all classes of HEICO’s common stock represented at the meeting in person or by proxy and entitled to vote on the subject matter. A proxy submitted by a shareholder may indicate that all or a portion of the shares represented by such proxy are not being voted by such shareholder with respect to a particular matter (“non-voted shares”). This could occur, for example, when a broker is not permitted to vote shares held in “street name” on certain matters in the absence of instructions from the beneficial owner of the shares. Under the New York Stock Exchange (“NYSE”) rules, a broker does not have the discretion to vote on Proposals 1 and 2. Non-voted shares with respect to a particular matter will be counted for purposes of determining the presence of a quorum but with respect to Proposals 1 and 2 will have no effect on the outcome of such proposals. Under the NYSE rules, a broker has the discretion to vote on Proposal 3. Shares voted to abstain as to a particular matter will be counted for purposes of determining the presence of a quorum and will count as a vote against such matter. Under the terms of the HEICO Savings and Investment Plan (the “401(k) Plan”), all shares allocated to the accounts of participating employees will be voted or not voted by the trustee of the 401(k) Plan as directed by written instructions from the participating employees, and allocated shares for which no instructions are received and all unallocated shares will be voted by the trustee of the 401(k) Plan in the same proportion as the shares for which instructions are received. Voting instruction cards are being mailed to all participants in the 401(k) Plan. If a participant also owns shares outside the 401(k) Plan, the participant must return both the proxy card and the voting instruction card as indicated on those cards in order to cause all of their shares to be voted in accordance with their instructions. To be assured that the trustee will receive voting instruction cards on a timely basis, voting instruction cards for shares in the 401(k) Plan must be duly signed and received no later than March 15, 2021. The total number of shares in the 401(k) Plan as of the Record Date represents approximately 2.9% of the voting power of all classes of common stock outstanding as of the Record Date and entitled to vote at the Annual Meeting. ​ | ​ TABLE OF CONTENTS February, 5, 2021 – For the first time ever, we are foregoing our “glossy ” printed annual report and we are sending only an expanded proxy statement to you. Over the last few years, we’ve learned that people prefer to learn about HEICO from our website and other means—and we believe we can provide you with the information you need in this format, so we decided that we could reduce our carbon footprint and save money by eliminating the annual report this year. Late last month, we sent you a letter accompanying our most recent dividend payment. We feel that letter remains an appropriate description of events at HEICO over the past year, so we are mostly repeating it below. We believe that HEICO’s historical success is very much due to what we call “HEICO Culture and Strength.” While this HEICO Culture and Strength is ultimately distilled down to financial characteristics, it exists because of, and rests upon, the following pillars: ▪ | HEICO’s remarkable and cooperative culture among Team Members which emphasizes quality, advancement, honesty and fair dealing; ▪ | HEICO’s placement of our customers and Team Members first; ▪ | HEICO’s unceasing investment in new products and services; ▪ | HEICO’s emphasis of cash flow generation; ▪ | HEICO’s healthy balance sheet; ▪ | HEICO’s acquisition program where we acquire excellent businesses for fair prices. By adhering to these principals, our Company achieved compound annual growth of 19%, 18% and 15% in operating income, net income and net revenues, respectively, since 1990. Although we took a step back with the rest of the world and our industry in fiscal 2020, HEICO performed admirably as a result of its diversified operations, Team Member dedication, expense controls and strong market positions. Investors seem to recognize HEICO’s strength. A recent research report ranked HEICO as the ninth best performing stock of the past 30 years, out performing well-known companies like Apple, Netflix and Microsoft. During fiscal 2020, this enabled HEICO to generate $409.1 million in cash flow from operating activities, to complete six acquisitions, including four during the COVID-19 pandemic, and to continue investing significantly in new product and service development. As previously reported, we started the year with a record first quarter which continued well into our second quarter. Unfortunately, as the pandemic unfolded, the commercial aviation industry declined precipitously in a matter of weeks as worldwide air travel virtually ceased. This made for a very challenging balance of our second quarter and an even harder third quarter. Fortunately, our fiscal fourth quarter witnessed a substantial improvement from our fiscal third quarter earnings, which helps anchor our optimism for improvement at some point in 2021. Because HEICO maintained very low debt levels, while also maintaining high cash levels, we were never concerned about surviving the pandemic. Having been through prior downturns, we understood clearly how to both sustain our business and flourish during the recovery. We felt it crucial that we not sacrifice HEICO’s long-term success due to what we believed would be a short-term setback. We were, and remain, convinced that HEICO would thrive once the health emergency passed. 2 | ​ | ​ | 2021 PROXY STATEMENT

We first turned our attention to health and safety by coordinating critical safety practices and health monitoring in our facilities, including ensuring adequate supplies of sanitizing items and protective gear, while having those who could work remotely do so. We also recognized that work levels would be dramatically lower for some time for our commercial aviation-focused businesses and that they needed to lower their cost burden, but we were determined to do so in the most humane and respectful way possible. Though we minimized outright layoffs, we regretted that the cost reductions meant job losses for some, while it meant reduced work hours and temporary pay reductions for others, which included 20% pay reductions for the entire corporate staff and the Board of Directors. The corporate-level pay cuts were removed later in the fiscal year as conditions began improving, but remained in place for the three of us, other members of the Company’s most-senior management and the Board of Directors until February 1, 2021 to demonstrate continuing cost-containment leadership. Fortunately, and as a testament to our Company’s strength, even though fiscal 2020 saw more than its share of tough challenges brought on by the Pandemic, HEICO also experienced some very positive occurrences, which include: ▪ | HEICO declared its 84th and 85th consecutive semiannual cash dividends since 1979 ▪ | HEICO increased its revolving credit facility to $1.5 billion and extended its term ▪ | HEICO completed six acquisitions, four of which happened during the Pandemic ▪ | HEICO subsidiaries provided critical components on major space missions, including NASA’s Mars 2020/ Perseverance Launch ▪ | HEICO subsidiaries continued to win important contracts, like a contract with the Belgian Air Force As we write this letter, we are optimistic about the fiscal year ahead and beyond. While no one, including us, knows exactly how the recovery will evolve, we are confident that commercial aviation will recover as more people are vaccinated and that our other businesses should prosper overall. We continue to work on numerous acquisitions, particularly of businesses being sold by their entrepreneur-founder-manager who is looking for an excellent home for their business where culture and integrity are important to them. Further, our practice of investing heavily in new product development is yielding its typical results and we are gratified we didn’t sacrifice those efforts during 2020’s most difficult months. As always, we thank you, all of our wonderful Team Members, our Board of Directors and our customers for your support and confidence. During this most difficult of times, that support and confidence help demonstrate why this Company is so unique. Sincerely, Laurans A. Mendelson Chairman and Chief Executive Officer | Eric A. Board Risk Oversight While the Company’s management team takes primary responsibility for risk management, the Board plays a large role in the oversight, evaluation and strategy for handling the material risks facing the Company. The risk environment in which HEICO currently operates includes a variety of risks, both financial and operational, some of which may manifest themselves in unforeseen ways, which may affect our ability to anticipate, fully comprehend, mitigate or respond to them. At regular intervals, HEICO’s management team presents the Board with reports on the status of critical risks that are currently affecting or have the potential to impact the business. These reports are designed to provide the Board with timely identification of the nature of any risks, so they may respond appropriately. The Board addresses risk management at both the full Board and committee levels. The full Board oversees risks that may impact HEICO and its subsidiaries as a whole, with particular emphasis on operational and strategic risk; while each committee oversees specific areas of risk within its purview. The full Board is responsible for oversight of HEICO’s cybersecurity risks. To this end, the full Board receives regular reports from management on system vulnerabilities and security measures in effect to deter or mitigate breaches or hacking activities. The Finance/Audit Committee is responsible for oversight of HEICO’s financial risks, including the adequacy of internal controls, compliance, financial reporting, and tax positions. To this end, the Finance/Audit Committee meets regularly with the Company’s internal and external auditors to ensure visibility into pending risks and the mitigation of the financial and non-financial impact of these risks. The Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee is responsible for the oversight of the Company’s directorship policies and practices, succession planning and the evaluation and recommendation of qualified Board candidates. Other Board committees also consider areas of risk within their particular subject matter, for example, the Compensation Committee considers the areas of risk related to the compensation policy and programs of the Company.
