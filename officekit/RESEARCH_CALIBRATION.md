# Research calibration

Open **Shared research → Research calibration**, or `/research/scorecard` in an
office. This is the same page and persistence path locally and when hosted.

Every sealed general court's explicit binary forecasts enter the derived index
automatically. The adjudicating agent produced those probabilities; the red and
blue advocates are not credited for them. Imported research retains its original
submitter metadata, including anonymous, claimed and verified attribution. A
reimport does not create another scored observation. Ordinary prose, verdicts,
conviction and expected-return assumptions are not probability forecasts.

Additional predictions require a subject, stable event key, exact resolution
criteria, deadline, probability, declared base rate, submitter, agent, model and
protocol. Strategy is optional. Capture time is generated by the application.
The same submitter and agent cannot replace an event's probability by registering
it again. These records are private office data and are not automatically shared.

Resolve a prediction with a public HTTPS source and a true/false result. The
source is the resolver's cited support, not automatic independent verification.
Corrections append to the outcome ledger, retaining the prior entry and its
digest. The UI rejects stale office/research revisions. Pending and overdue
forecasts remain visible; absence of a resolution never means false.

Brier is the mean of `(probability - outcome)^2`, where true is 1 and false is 0.
Lower is better. The scorecard groups by submitter, forecasting agent and their
combination. Agent identity includes model/protocol configuration; a model name
or intelligence tier is not evidence of empirical skill.

Raw scores are available immediately. Skill relative to the declared base rate
requires 20 resolved forecasts and a nonzero reference score. Pooled Brier is
descriptive smoothing against other unique resolved forecasts, with up to 20
observations of weight; forecasts already in the group are excluded from its
pool. It is not a confidence interval. Correlation, selection and differences in
question difficulty limit comparisons. Nothing here gates research or allocation.

Durable files are sealed general records, `research/predictions.jsonl` and
`research/outcomes.jsonl`. The SQLite index can be rebuilt. Office snapshots and
exports preserve these files. Public exchange records use opt-in pseudonyms;
private submitter names must not be copied into public attribution.

Agents may register via `python -m officekit_research.corpus predict --office
OFFICE prediction.json`, resolve via `resolve`, and read `scoreboard --by agent`,
`--by submitter` or `--by submitter_agent`. Legacy desk freezes accept explicit
submitter/agent/model/protocol metadata; absent historical attribution stays
unattributed. Legacy MIXED outcomes are excluded from binary actor Brier.
