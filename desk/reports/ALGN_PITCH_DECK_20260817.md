# PITCH — ALGN — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-17 — WATCH** — fair-to-rich at ~$172; no position today, one price gate at $143.

---

## What we believe

Align Technology (Invisalign clear aligners, iTero scanners) is a genuinely good business that is currently priced like a good business. That is the whole finding.

The cash it actually generates for an owner — cash from operations, minus what it spends on plant and equipment, minus the stock it hands to employees — is about **$454 million over the last twelve months**. Against an enterprise value of roughly $11.2 billion (market cap ~$12.3B less ~$1.1B of cash and no borrowed money at all), that is **24.6 times owner earnings** for a company growing revenue about 4%.

That is not a bargain. It is also not a bubble. It is a fair price, so we wait for a worse one.

Three things we believe underneath that:

1. **The operating business is not deteriorating.** Headline Q2 operating income fell 5.5% year-over-year, but that is a large legal charge. Strip legal costs out and first-half operating income rose 22.5%, and gross margin widened from 69.70% to 71.27% while the cost of goods sold fell in absolute dollars. Whatever else is wrong here, the factory economics improved.
2. **The "activist forces a big buyback" story is not real yet.** Elliott has not filed a 13D. The only 13D on file for this company is from 2009. The July 29 press release commits to a *search* for three independent directors, not to three appointments. And the "increase" to $400–500M of buybacks in 2026 is actually flat-to-down 14% against what the company actually spent in 2025 ($465.9M) — while first-half 2026 spend was $98.1M, a third of the pace needed to hit even that.
3. **Where the money is going is a mild negative.** In the first half the company put $189.3M into other people's securities (convertible notes $69.8M, equity investments $100.5M, small acquisitions $19.0M) versus $98.1M into its own stock. The equity-investment line went from $10M last year to $100M this year — a 10x jump. That is not idle-cash management.

## What the market believes

Seventeen analysts cover it, average price target $208.6. The stock is 14.6% below its 52-week high of $200.44 and 40% above its 52-week low of $122.00. So the market's view is roughly: a temporarily depressed high-quality compounder, activist pressure is a positive catalyst, and the multiple compresses as growth reaccelerates.

The market is not obviously wrong. At 24.6x owner earnings with no debt, a 71% gross margin, and improving underlying operating leverage, $172 is a defensible price. Our disagreement is narrow and about *price*, not about *quality*: we think the activist leg is being paid for before it exists, and we think the forward multiple is worse than the trailing one.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the actual cash-flow statement and the market read the press release.** The July 29 8-K markets a bigger buyback; the 10-Q filed one week later shows repurchase spend down 67% year-over-year. Both are true; only one is in the headlines. ([10-Q cash-flow statement, filed 2026-08-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1097149/000109714926000060/R7.htm))
- **We checked whether Elliott actually filed anything.** They did not. "One of Align's largest investors" is self-description, not a disclosed position. An undated, unsized stake is not something you can underwrite. ([EDGAR SC 13D index for CIK 1097149](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001097149&type=SC+13D))
- **We found the capex inflection nobody is discussing.** First-half capital spending was $66.45M versus $46.77M a year ago — up 42% — against full-year 2025 of $102.4M. If 2026 lands at $145–150M, forward owner earnings drop about $25M and the multiple goes to ~26x on the same price.
- **We found why the quality screens rank it mediocre, and it's an accounting artifact.** The company carries a $1,479.9M deferred tax asset, 23% of total assets and 35% of book equity. That inflates the equity base and mechanically pushes reported return-on-equity down to ~10.2%. Measured against equity excluding that asset (and goodwill/intangibles), return on equity is ~19–20%. The screen was ranking a high-return business as average because of a tax balance. Worth noting the flip side, which cuts against us: that same asset is running off and adding ~$63M/year back to reported cash flow — about 14% of owner earnings is a tax subsidy, on a roughly 22-year tail.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- 24.6x owner earnings for a debt-free, 71%-gross-margin medical device franchise is not a screaming misprice in either direction. The market may simply be right.
- The improving gross margin and the 22.5% ex-legal operating income growth are visible to anyone who reads the release. We did not find those; we confirmed them.
- The buyback shortfall is disclosed in a public filing — there is no information asymmetry, only an attention asymmetry, and attention asymmetries close on the next print.
- The stock is already 14.6% off its high. Some of the disappointment we are describing has been paid for.
- The activist situation could turn out to be exactly what the bulls think. Elliott not filing a 13D means the stake is under 5% or held through derivatives — it does *not* mean there is no engagement. The company publicly announced an operating review and a director search; that is a real event, just not a datable one.

## What remains unverified

- **The Elliott stake, entirely.** Size unknown, date unknown, terms unknown. It may be below 5%, it may be held through derivatives. We struck the leg rather than guess.
- **The Q3 earnings date.** 2026-10-28 comes from a data vendor. No company announcement names it.
- **Whether 2026 capex lands at $145–150M.** We are extrapolating from one half. If it does, the forward multiple is ~26x, and with the tax-asset runoff normalized out, ~30x — which would mean today's price is genuinely rich, not merely fair.
- **Whether second-half buybacks get funded.** The company would need to spend $302–402M in six months, three to four times its first-half pace, to hit its own stated target.
- **The legal charge.** $69.3M was accrued in the first half, not paid. We do not know when it converts to cash, or whether the UK appeal produces more.
- **Product-safety and recall history.** The FDA device database check could not be run this session. No part of this verdict rests on product quality, but the gap is real and unclosed.
- **Which price feed is authoritative.** Four sources gave $171.16 / $172.095 / $172.435 / $173.305. Immaterial at a $143 gate; unresolved nonetheless.
- **Plant utilization.** All four manufacturing sites are outside the US (Juarez, Ziyang, Wroclaw, Petah Tikva), which puts them beyond both the US workplace-safety data and any satellite check we ran. Nobody disputes the plants are running; we simply did not independently measure it.
- **The durability of the deferred-tax subsidy.** We model it as a ~22-year runoff, which makes it a feature rather than a cliff. That is an assumption about tax jurisdiction stability, not a verified fact.
