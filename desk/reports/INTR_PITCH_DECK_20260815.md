# PITCH — INTR — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY (FLAT).** Inter & Co's credit deterioration is real and still arriving, the −50% drawdown is company-specific rather than a Brazil-wide selloff, and at ~1.16x book the stock is fairly priced for a 16% ROE bank — not distressed. Reopen only on the 3Q26 flip-test or at ~1.0x book with credit stabilizing.

## What we believe

Inter & Co (NYSE: INTR), the Brazilian digital bank, has fallen from a 52-week high of $10.36 to $5.19 — down 49.9%, sitting 3% above its 52-week low — and the fall is deserved, not a dislocation. Every credit metric in the company's own vetted 2Q26 filings is deteriorating year-over-year:

- Early-stage delinquency (loans 15–90 days late, total book): 4.1% → 4.8% (+70bps YoY)
- Late-stage delinquency (90+ days): 4.6% → 5.3% (+70bps YoY)
- Cost of risk (annualized provisioning rate): 5.0% → 5.9%
- Provision coverage of bad loans: 143% → 134% (peaked at 146% in 3Q25 — a steady three-quarter glide down, not a one-quarter collapse)

The single largest driver is the new private-sector payroll loan product ("Crédito do Trabalhador"), which grew from R$0.7bn to R$2.8bn in a year and accounts for 53 of the 70 basis points of the yearly rise in 90+ delinquency — meaning the rest of the book deteriorated only ~17bps, which is genuinely contained. But that new book is young, still seasoning, and management's spoken (not printed) guidance implies cost of risk keeps rising toward ~6% for the year. This is damage still arriving, not damage fully in the rearview. We classify it as not worth deploying capital into now, and not close enough to a decision point to convene a full position debate (court-worthiness 5/10).

## What the market believes

The market has already re-rated INTR as having a company-specific credit problem. Actual Brazilian bank peers are down far less from their highs — Itaú (ITUB) is ~23% off its high, Nubank (NU) ~20% off — so INTR's ~50% drawdown carries roughly 27–30 percentage points of excess, idiosyncratic decline. At $5.19, the stock trades at ~1.16x book value (equity ≈ R$10.3bn implied by R$421m quarterly profit at 16.3% ROE; market cap $2.29bn × 5.21 BRL/USD ≈ R$11.9bn). With Brazilian cost of equity around 15.5–17% (priced off the falling forward rate curve — the central bank is four cuts into an easing cycle from 14%), the justified multiple for a 16% ROE bank is roughly 0.9–1.15x book. In plain terms: the market has priced this correctly. It is not pricing a death spiral, and it is not offering a bargain. Ten analysts cover the name, so "overlooked" is not an available argument.

## Why we might have edge

Modest, and mostly forward-looking rather than present:

1. **We have a pinned, vetted-numbers flip-test the market narrative doesn't articulate.** Because both benches reconciled every series to the actual SEC-filed exhibits (not transcripts or aggregators), we know exactly which numbers on the 3Q26 print would mean the credit cycle is turning: total 15–90 delinquency ≤4.8% AND cost of risk ≤6.0% AND coverage ≥134% AND ROE ≥16% flips us toward "damage absent"; 15–90 above 5.0% with coverage below 130% confirms the bear.
2. **The payroll attribution is a real analytical wedge.** The filed earnings release states the new payroll product accounts for 53bps of the 70bps yearly rise in 90+ delinquency. If Brazil's central bank monthly credit data shows that payroll vintage curve flattening, the core book (~17bps/yr deterioration) is fine and the stock at ~1.1x book becomes interesting before the print — a leading indicator most price-watchers won't be tracking.
3. **We know what we don't know.** The falling coverage ratio may be partly mechanical: the loan book is mixing toward collateralized and insured products (payroll, FGTS-backed, home equity), which requires less provisioning under accounting rules. If a product-level decomposition shows required coverage stable, the scariest-looking metric loses its teeth.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, it largely is — that's the verdict. The de-rating to ~1.16x book against a justified band of 0.9–1.15x means the market has already done the arithmetic we did. Ten analysts cover the stock. The credit deterioration was disclosed by the company itself in its own earnings materials — there is no hidden fact here, only a disagreement about trajectory, and our own numbers say the trajectory is still worsening. Both benches converged on the same conclusion from opposite directions: the bear case is correct but already reflected, and the bull case ("it's just sector beta") is refuted by the peer tape. Buying here means betting the credit cycle turns before the market sees it — a bet we currently have no evidence for.
