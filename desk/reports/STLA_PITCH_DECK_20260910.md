## What we believe

Stellantis is a €150–160bn-revenue carmaker trading at $5.285 on the NYSE line, 56.7% below its 52-week high and 4.7% off its 52-week low. The original thesis was that the market was pricing a cash-burn story that the cash statements no longer supported. That thesis, as written, is **wrong** — and we are saying so before we say anything else.

What survives is narrower and honest:

1. The burn is real but it is **restructuring-funded**, not obviously structural. Headline H1'26 industrial free cash flow was **−€0.9bn** (Q1 −€1.9bn, Q2 +€1.0bn), but roughly €0.9bn of the ~€2bn of H2'25 charge payments the company itself ring-fenced in its guide landed in H1. Ex-charge, H1 is about **breakeven** versus H1'25's −€3.0bn — a ~€2.1bn underlying swing. The company guides FY26 IFCF "improved Y-o-Y" and says positive IFCF arrives in **2027**.
2. Against that, there is a real mechanism working the other way — the **inventory-build trap**: US dealer stock rose 43% year-over-year and 22% versus year-end 2025, to roughly 92 days of supply in June, while North American revenue rose 32%. Shipping cars to dealers books revenue and spreads factory fixed costs over more units (which is where the +120bps of margin came from). Clearing those lots later costs either cash (incentives) or margin (production cuts). Management called the build intentional.
3. So the two things we would want to be true — margin holds *and* cash turns — are **one lever, not two**. Nobody, including our own benches, has yet measured which way that lever is moving in September/October 2026. Until we measure it, the correct position is flat.

Our own 12-month-to-December scenario arithmetic (below) values the stock at **$5.38 against a $5.285 tape: +1.7%**. That is not a thesis. That is noise, and it is the single most important number in this deck.

## What the market believes

- The street is **long the recovery**: 27 analysts, mean target **€7.87** (range €4.00–€12.50), which is roughly $8.66 at a 1.10 EURUSD — about 60% above the current NYSE line. A separate 10-analyst S&P Global set sits at $9.19.
- Consensus FY26 EPS has reportedly been **raised to €1.21**, which implies roughly €3.5bn of net profit against H1 actuals of **€670m** (Q1 €377m + Q2 €293m, ≈€0.23/share). That requires roughly 4x the first half in the second half while days supply normalizes and residual tariff and charge cash still flows. We could not corroborate the €1.21 from a second source and we are flagging it as uncorroborated, not as fact.
- But the tape disagrees with the targets: the stock printed **−7.7% to $4.884** on the July 30 Q2 release (AOI €773m vs €914m expected), then **recovered ~8% to $5.285** with the inventory wedge fully public. So the market is not naive about the wedge — it has seen it and marked the stock back up anyway.
- Net read: sell-side is bullish and crowded; the price action is ambivalent. The bear case is *partly* in the price. That is precisely why "it's down 57%, it's cheap" is not an argument we will make.

## Why we might have edge

1. **Nobody is computing the right cash number.** The issuer ring-fenced the charge cash inside its own guidance table. The bull quotes Q2's +€1.0bn; the bear quotes the cumulative −€0.9bn and calls it structural. The gradeable instrument is **9M ex-charge IFCF**, and neither of our benches computed it before the courts closed. That number is buildable from the Oct 28 release on the day it prints, ahead of anyone re-basing a model.
2. **The pincer has a single direct read-out and it is dated.** North American *shipments* versus North American *retail sales* in the mid-October Q3 shipments release is the only place where "did the wedge close?" is directly observable. Most of the street will read the October 28 P&L instead.
3. **Days supply has no baseline in anyone's note.** 92 days is quoted as an extreme. It may be a normalization — Cox reported VW at 143 days in January 2026 with several Stellantis brands above four months entering the year. Whoever gets the September/October industry-average days supply **and per-unit incentive dollars** knows whether the lots are clearing on demand or on discount. That distinction is the whole trade.
4. **A same-day credit test exists that nobody ran.** There is a live 424B5 (Stellantis Finance US notes off the August 3 F-3ASR). Its final coupon against the captive's 5.400%'29 / 5.800%'31 curve is a clean, immediate read on senior credit — far cleaner than inferring distress from an 8.250% subordinated sterling perpetual.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The stock already fell and then recovered with the wedge public.** −7.7% on the print, +8% since. If the market has priced the pincer and is waiting for the 2027 cash turn, our "edge" is a slower version of the consensus view.
- **27 analysts and a target 60% above spot** means our upside case is the crowded case. Crowded longs do not pay for being right slowly; they get liquidated in the middle.
- **The 2027 positive-IFCF guide is in the company's own headline table** and was covered on the July 30 call. Reading a guidance table is not proprietary. Our bench correctly downgraded the "novel" tag on this to consensus.
- **Ex-charge breakeven is a bull argument that has already been made.** If the sell-side model already normalizes out the €2bn of charge cash — and a model producing €1.21 of FY26 EPS almost certainly does — then our "correct instrument" is their default instrument.
- **We are 4.7% off a 52-week low.** Cheapness near a low is often the market being right early, not wrong.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The lots clear on incentive dollars, and we call it a normalization.** Days supply comes down from ~92 toward 75 by the October 1 print. We read that as gate 1 satisfied, take the one-third starter, and only later learn that per-unit incentives went up $600–900 to do it. Margin degrades into Q4, the cash "improvement" turns out to be a working-capital release bought with price, and the stock grinds to the $4s. *Monitorable tripwire: yes* — Cox / Motor Intelligence per-unit incentive **dollars**, not units, alongside the October 1 sales release. This is the failure we are best defended against, which is exactly why it is not the one that will hurt us.

**Failure narrative 2 — The absorption reversal, arriving through the P&L rather than the cash statement.** Management defends cash, cuts production, and the +120bps of margin that came from building 43% more dealer stock unwinds. Revenue falls faster than cost, Enlarged Europe (already at **−0.6% AOI margin**, with CO2/EV regulatory cost running into 2027) deepens, and FY26 EPS lands near our **€0.55–0.70** rather than the reported €1.21 consensus. The stock re-rates to the 2027 story from a lower base. *Monitorable tripwire: partial* — mid-October shipments give us the wedge direction, but the absorption hit shows up two quarters later in segment margin, and by then we own it.

**Failure narrative 3 — the one with NO monitorable tripwire.** A new strategic reset lands off-calendar: a kitchen-sink quarter, a large impairment, an equity-linked capital action, a brand or plant rationalization, or a tariff/CO2 regime change by executive or Commission action that re-bases the entire North American or European P&L overnight. As a foreign private issuer, Stellantis **furnishes a 6-K after the fact** — there is no 8-K obligation, no pre-announcement duty, and therefore no sensor we can arm that fires *before* the fact. Our own filing-watch sensor is structurally a post-mortem instrument here, and we should stop pretending otherwise.
**Unknown-unknown class named: management-discretion and sovereign-policy discontinuity** — decisions made inside a boardroom or a ministry, disclosed only on completion, where the entire information asymmetry is one-directional and no public dataset leads it. Secondary class inside the same bucket: **captive-finance / counterparty credit**, where the transmission runs through funding cost rather than through anything in the operating disclosures we monitor.

**The reflexive / structural leg (our own size, flow, and doctrine).**
- *Size vs ADV*: our maximum gated starter is one-third of a full position. Even at a full 3%-of-book sizing, that is a low-single-digit-million-dollar order against an estimated NYSE-line ADV of roughly 12–18m shares (~$65–95m notional) — call it well under 1% of ADV. **We are not the marginal buyer here and we cannot be.** That cuts both ways: no market impact to worry about, and also no ability to defend the position or exit into a gap. Our ADV figure is an estimate and sits in the unverified queue. Note also that the NYSE line is the *secondary* listing — the Milan ordinary line carries the price discovery, so our sensors watch a derivative venue.
- *Predicted flow*: dividend suspended; up to €5bn of hybrids pre-announced by the Board; a live USD senior note deal off the August shelf; nine same-day Form 144s on September 4 under a non-issuer filer CIK whose documents **neither bench pulled**. None of these is large enough to move a €160bn-revenue issuer, but the 144 cluster is an unresolved directional signal we chose not to resolve, and hybrid coupons (~€300m+/yr) rank ahead of the common regardless of what net profit shows.
- *Doctrine interaction — the dangerous one*: this case generated **two** mechanisms that both apply and point opposite ways. `wholesale_absorption_pincer` says the margin and cash guides are one lever and cannot both hold. `restructuring_funded_burn_masking` says both benches misread the cash metric and the ex-charge series is roughly breakeven. **Both can be true simultaneously**: cash improves ex-charge *while* the absorption trap degrades margin. In that world our gates 1 and 4 can both fire green and the stock still falls, because we will have confirmed the cash leg and bought into the margin leg. That is the specific structural way this framework kills us, and no single sensor in the armed list catches it — it requires reading the two together, which is a judgment, not a trigger.
- *Crowding*: 27 analysts on the same recovery. Our entry gates are all pre-print and public; anyone running the same October 1 / October 13 reads gets the same signal at the same minute. There is no queue advantage.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___

___ = *that we bought blue's ex-charge normalization and got red's absorption reversal at the same time — days supply cleared on incentive dollars and production cuts rather than on demand, so the cash leg printed green exactly as the margin leg broke, and the two doctrines that were supposed to argue with each other both turned out to be right.*
