# PITCH — CWH — PROPOSED VERDICT

**REJECT — no position, watch-only with defined re-court triggers (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION).** Both benches independently reached no-entry; the blue bench softened the red bench's near-terminal framing to "watch with triggers" but did not overturn it.

## What we believe

Camping World is not a buy at $6.34, and it is not yet a confirmed zero either. The stock is down roughly 65% from its 52-week high of $18.34, and the bull case — that this is a convex recovery bet on a cheap equity stub over a refinanceable balance sheet — fails on inspection of its own arithmetic:

- The headline "net debt ~$1.31B" is computed against the smallest available debt basis. Adding total operating leases ($874.7M), finance leases (~$133M), and the $1,324.2M floor-plan borrowing that finances the dealer inventory, the fixed-obligation stack is roughly **$3.6 billion**.
- The deleveraging shown so far ($222M of net-debt reduction; $333M of year-to-date operating cash flow against only ~$70M of six-month net income) is **mostly a one-time release of working capital from shrinking inventory** (new units down 16–18%), not earnings power. Real cash, yes — but not repeatable.
- Free cash flow at the twice-cut guidance midpoint (~$250M EBITDA) works out to only ~$50–75M a year after interest, lease charges, and capex. That does not get the balance sheet to a comfortable place before the debt maturities that begin springing in early 2028.
- The bull case's "normalized" $400M EBITDA equals the **2017 peak** ($394.2M). It is not fantasy — the store base is ~40% larger now — but it is a peak-cycle assumption made 18 months after a 15% industry-wide guidance cut. Meanwhile the modern trough (2019: $166.0M) sits *below* the thesis's own $180M bear case.

The honest concession cutting the other way: the floor-plan lending syndicate — the parties closest to the actual insolvency channel — recently **raised** capacity from $1.85B to $2.15B, leaving ~$825M of nominal headroom. Lender behavior currently points away from stress. That is why this is a watch, not a write-off.

## What the market believes

The market has already repriced this hard: −65% from the high, and the $5.485 low is recent (price is only ~16% above it). At $6.34 the enterprise value is about **7.9× the already-twice-cut EBITDA guide** — the market is pricing a damaged, levered, cyclical retailer, but *not* pricing distress or a restructuring. Two recent Schedule 13G filings (passive holders, including BofA) suggest institutional money treats this as an ordinary beaten-down position, not a workout. In short: the market believes roughly what our benches concluded — damaged but probably solvent — which is exactly why there is no entry here.

## Why we might have edge

- **We rebuilt the debt stack from the filing extract rather than accepting the headline number.** The ~$3.6B fixed-obligation figure, versus the marketed $1.31B, changes the leverage picture from "2.5–3× after recovery" to something much less refinance-friendly.
- **We identified the deleveraging as inventory liquidation, not earnings** — a distinction the recovery narrative glosses over.
- **We found a second springing-maturity linkage** neither the bull brief nor the red bench initially had: the revolver matures 2028-03-03 *or 91 days before the FreedomRoads floor-plan facility matures, whichever comes first*. The maturity wall is more interconnected than advertised.
- **We anchored the "normalization" debate in primary company releases**: 2017 peak $394.2M, 2019 trough $166.0M ([FY2018 results](https://investor.campingworld.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2019/Camping-World-Holdings-Inc-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2018-Results/default.aspx), [FY2019 results](https://investor.campingworld.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2020/Camping-World-Holdings-Inc-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2019-Results/default.aspx)).
- The court also produced a reusable insight: for floor-plan-financed retailers, **the floor-plan facility, not headline net debt, is the actual liquidity channel** — a screen we are adding to the toolkit.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands this section carry real weight, because it is why the verdict is REJECT rather than SHORT:

- A 65% drawdown means the market has done most of the repricing already. There is no evidence of concealment — the guide cuts, unit declines, and margin compression are all disclosed and reflected.
- 7.9× cut-guide EBITDA is not distress pricing, but neither is it expensive for a franchise with the largest RV dealer network if the cycle turns.
- The floor-plan capacity *increase* is a genuine bull datum on the exact channel our bear analysis says matters most. If the lenders who see the inventory weekly are extending, the near-term insolvency scenario is weaker than the debt-stack arithmetic alone implies.
- Nothing forces action: the next catalyst (Q3 earnings, ~2026-10-27, unconfirmed) is ~2.5 months out, and our own falling-knife discipline says a recent low plus no catalyst equals wait.
