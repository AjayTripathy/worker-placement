## What the company does

Greif makes the boring containers industry runs on: steel and plastic drums, jerrycans, and
intermediate bulk containers — the packaging layer for chemicals, lubricants, food ingredients
and agriculture, sold globally to customers who reorder for decades. A smaller sheet-fed
containerboard segment remains after the company sold its main containerboard business to
Packaging Corp of America. That divestiture is the pivot of the story: proceeds cut net
leverage from **3.1x to 1.1x**, converting a leveraged packaging conglomerate into a
lightly-levered industrial cash machine.

## Why Class A and not Class B

The charter allocates cash 1:1.5 — the B share carries 1.5x the A's dividend and earnings
claim. At A $82.94 / B $105.56 the ratio (1.28) sits *inside* that fair band, so neither class
is mispriced against the other (that pair trade was courted separately today and killed). The
reason to own A specifically:

- **The buyback prefers A.** Historical repurchases ran **5:1 A-over-B** (1,813,600 A vs
  371,449 B), and the buyback is restarting. The A holder owns the retained-cash strip.
- **A is the liquid class**; ~45% of B is held by the founding family and rarely trades.
- **Honest caveat, pre-registered as the kill-variable:** the 5:1 skew is management practice,
  *not* a charter right. The FY26 10-K discloses the per-class split — if it comes in below
  3:1, the specific reason to own A over B is gone (B carries the bigger cash claim), and the
  ruling says so in advance rather than discovering it later.

## Honest risk list

1. **Packaging cycle** — 8.1x is fair only if post-divestiture EBITDA holds; industrial
   destocking hits drum volumes directly, and organic volume is already flat (the growth is
   cost-out, which ends).
2. **Family control** — 45% of B (and its votes) is never yours; the discount can persist
   indefinitely. The yield is the compensation, not a catalyst.
3. **The A-preference is discretionary** — if the restarted buyback stops favoring A, own-the-A
   weakens; the January 10-K grades this by rule.
4. **Sheet-fed drag** — 23% of EBITDA is losing money at the adjusted line; the $60/ton
   recognition is expected, not guaranteed.
5. **Verification gap at ruling** — EDGAR was down; decisive numbers came from the company's
   own release cross-checked against the call transcript, with the primary re-pull queued and
   gating any adds.
