## What the company does

Argan is, mostly, **Gemma Power Systems**: an engineering-and-construction firm that builds
power plants — overwhelmingly natural-gas plants — under fixed contracts for utilities and
independent power producers. When a developer wins approval for a 1,350-megawatt gas plant in
Texas, Gemma is the kind of firm that gets paid ~$1B over three years to actually build it.
A small second segment does industrial construction (tanks, plant work); a third does telecom
field services. Power is ~78% of revenue and nearly all of the story.

**Why it's interesting right now:** the AI datacenter buildout has produced the first genuine
wave of new US gas-plant construction in a decade — datacenters need firm power, and firm power
means gas turbines. Argan is one of a handful of proven EPC contractors for that work. This is
the picks-and-shovels layer of AI power capex.

## The bear case, and what the court did to it

**Claim 1 — "The order cycle peaked; backlog is rolling over."** This was the loudest claim
and it is **dead**. The headline backlog dipped $2.9B → $2.8B, but both numbers are rounded to
the nearest $100M — the dip is inside the rounding. The decisive evidence points the other way:
the company's own disclosure of backlog converting within 12 months **accelerated** (from ~32%
of $1.9B to ~42% of $2.8B — a +45% jump in monthly run-rate). Backlog is getting
*shorter-dated and larger*, the opposite of stale. One caveat our adjudication added: the blue
bench's original "+93%" compared a 12-month window to a 9-month window; +45% is the honest
number, and the position is sized to it.

**Claim 2 — "Insiders are dumping."** True and unexplained-away: 31 insider sales, zero buys
in six months; the chairman sold ~$37M at $726–760. Context that cuts the other way: the
company itself repurchased stock at a $458 average and holds $85M of live buyback
authorization — today's price is **7.6% below management's own buy price**. We treat the
insider cluster as a size argument, not a kill.

**Claim 3 — "It's expensive."** Correct, and it survived: at ~$424 the enterprise trades at
roughly **37–41x operating earnings** (stripping interest income from E and netting only true
excess cash from EV). That multiple is the entire remaining bear case, and it is why the
position is a quarter-percent starter and not a full slot. Wall Street's $12.09 FY27 estimate
embeds a margin *decline* that has no disclosed driver — Q1 printed 21% gross margin with
clean revenue recognition — so the street number is likely low, but "cheaper than consensus
thinks" still isn't "cheap."

**What we added at adjudication (the benches couldn't see the tape):** the options market
prices roughly a **±10–15% move** through Wednesday's earnings, and the chain is too thin to
hedge with (open interest: 4 calls, 21 puts). On 0.25% of the book, that's ±2–4 basis points
of NAV — survivable by design.

## Honest risk list

1. **Valuation** — 37–41x operating earnings prices years of continued award growth. A
   multiple-only derate to 25x is ~−35% with no thesis error at all.
2. **AI capex dependence** — the order book's growth engine is datacenter power. If the AI
   buildout breaks (house probability 0.45 by end-2027), awards stall with a lag.
3. **Insider distribution** — 31 sells / 0 buys is what a top looks like from the inside,
   even when the buyback argues otherwise. 10b5-1 status unverified (coverage gap).
4. **Fixed-price EPC risk** — one bad project can erase a year of margin; the industry's
   history (including Gemma's own Alto pre-2020) is littered with write-downs.
5. **Event risk Wednesday** — ±10–15% implied, unhedgeable chain, and we're long into it on
   purpose at quarter size.
