# PITCH — JBGS — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: DO NOT BUY. Both benches recommend rejecting this idea (conviction 8/10 each); no position, and — unusually — no standing buy-the-dip price level, because the problem is an open-ended legal liability, not a valuation dispute. Re-open only on the specific events listed below.**

## What we believe

JBG SMITH is a Washington-DC office and apartment landlord that has lost roughly half its market value over the past year and now trades around $11.50, within about 7–10% of its 52-week low. The original pitch argued this was an overreaction. After adversarial review, we believe the opposite: the decline is a reasonable response to a genuine, still-unresolved threat.

On July 31, 2026, a DC trial court entered a judgment of approximately **$356.1 million** (a tripled damages award) against the defendants in the Wardman Tower condominium construction-defect case — and the company's own quarterly report says those defendants **include JBG SMITH itself**. The company's disclosed worst case is the full $356.1 million **plus attorneys' fees plus interest that accrues during the appeal** — call it $400 million or more against roughly $840–850 million of total equity value (counting both public shares and the operating-partnership units held by insiders). That is a tail equal to nearly half the company.

Around that tail sits a business with no cushion: same-store income is shrinking, office occupancy is about 75%, the largest tenant is the federal government (~24% of rent) during a period of federal downsizing, debt is about 12.4x annual cash earnings, and the dividend is not fully covered once you account for the recurring costs of re-leasing office space. The company recorded **zero liability** for the judgment, calling a loss "not probable" — on a judgment a court has already entered. We read that as posture, not information. To collect nothing here you likely need to win the appeal outright, which takes 18–30 months.

## What the market believes

The market has cut the stock roughly in half over twelve months, with two distinct drops around the judgment news on top of a longer fundamental grind. The roughly $187 million of market value lost across the judgment-related gaps equals about 45–50% of the disclosed worst-case loss — i.e., the market is pricing the judgment as a serious but survivable event, discounted for the chance the appeal succeeds or reduces it. The market also still receives a $0.175 quarterly dividend (declared July 30, one day before the judgment was entered) and has watched the company shrink its share count from 114 million (2023) to 58.6 million (August 2026) through buybacks, which has supported the price.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: less than the original pitch claimed. What survives:

- **We read the primary documents; much commentary hasn't.** The quarterly filing's own words — defendant status, the "$0 to $356.1 million **plus** fees **plus** interest" range, the zero accrual, the warning that posting appeal collateral "may impact our liquidity" — are more alarming than the headline coverage.
- **We identified the live near-term catalyst others may be ignoring:** the next dividend declaration (expected around October 30). The dividend exceeds true cash coverage, and cash ($74.8 million) plus a covenant-restricted credit line must now also fund appeal collateral. A cut is a real possibility and would be a fresh down-leg.
- **We noticed what is missing:** the litigation disclosure contains **no mention of insurance recovery** for a construction-defect judgment. For a claim of this type, silence about insurance is itself a signal.

Note the asymmetry: our "edge," such as it is, points **against** owning the stock, not toward it.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry requires saying this plainly: a ~50% decline may already embed everything above. The market cap loss attributable to the judgment is roughly half the worst-case tail — not obviously too little for a company that might win on appeal, might have the tripling reversed (the tripled portion is ~$237 million of the $356 million), and might share the bill with a construction-company co-defendant. The company publicly maintains a loss is improbable. If the appeal succeeds, today's price is cheap. Our answer is not "the market is wrong about the size" — it is that the outcome is a coin-flip on an appellate court we cannot handicap, sitting on a levered, deteriorating business, and we are not paid to hold that. That is a reason to abstain, not a reason to short.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry at any price.** The courts explicitly declined to set a buy level: the risk is an unbounded legal tail plus deteriorating operations, so cheapness alone cannot clear it. Per our discipline, a business-risk finding gets a size answer (here: zero), not a price answer.

**Reopen conditions (any one triggers a fresh court, not an automatic buy):**

1. **Appeal-bond ruling favorable on liquidity** — the court sets appeal collateral materially below the $356.1M judgment without requiring full cash collateral. Watch 8-K filings; no date scheduled.
2. **Insurance disclosure appears** — any filing language describing insurance recovery against the judgment. Currently absent entirely.
3. **Dividend cut that clears the overhang without the stock re-rating** — i.e., the company frees ~$41M/year of cash and the price does not already reward it. Next declaration expected ~2026-10-30 (date inferred from the July 30 pattern, not confirmed).
4. **Appellate briefing showing the tripled damages (~$237M of the $356M) are severable and reviewed fresh on appeal** — this alone would shrink the realistic tail below ~$120M plus fees and change the arithmetic.

If none of these occur, the position stays at zero through the appeal (18–30 months).
