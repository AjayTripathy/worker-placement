# PITCH — OWL — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — weakest of the private-credit cohort (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

*(Blue Owl Capital Inc., CIK 1823945, NYSE: OWL — tape $11.84, −35.0% from the 52-week high, +52.4% off the 52-week low. Book today: NO POSITION, no working orders. Both benches landed on ACCEPT-WITH-CUTS / PARTIALLY-OVERTURNED at 7/10 conviction each.)*

---

## What we believe

Blue Owl collects management fees on $190.6B of fee-paying assets. Those fees are contractual, mostly on perpetual or long-dated vehicles, and they arrive whether or not markets cooperate. The stock is down 35% from its high. The measurable damage the two benches could actually prove — a fee-rate slip of 2.1 basis points a quarter, worth about **$40M a year or 2.5 cents per share** — is roughly 3% of the share price. A 35% de-rate against 3 cents of proven annual damage is the whole idea.

We think the market has re-priced this from "compounding fee machine" to "shrinking private-credit sponsor" on a story about non-traded fund redemptions, and that the redemption channel is mechanically too small to do that job. The benches sized it: the biggest redeeming vehicle (OCIC) has roughly **$19.1B of NAV**, its quarterly redemption cap is **5% = $0.957B**, which is **0.50% of total fee-paying assets per quarter**, against **$7.8B of gross fundraising in the same quarter**. The pipe is not wide enough to drain the tank unless fundraising halves again.

At $11.84 we pay about **13.3x** the street's 2026 distributable earnings ($0.89) and about **18.0x** once we subtract the ~$365M of stock compensation that sits outside the adjusted numbers (**$0.23/share**). The dividend is **$0.92 a year, a 7.8% yield**.

We are proposing a **starter only** — roughly a third of a full position — because the payout is above 100% of earnings and the two nearest catalysts are unverified.

## What the market believes

The market believes the wealth-channel redemption cycle is early, not late; that private-credit marks have not yet caught up to reality; and that a 7.8% yield paid out of 104% of earnings is a yield that gets cut. Sell-side price targets run **$10 to $17** (BofA cut $17 to $16, still Buy) — a spread that tells you the analysts agree on the earnings and disagree entirely on what to pay for them. The tape sits at the bottom of that range.

The market also believes — correctly — that fee-paying assets growing while fees fall is a bad sign. Assets rose 1.17% quarter over quarter while management fees fell 0.34%, and the CFO said credit fee-paying assets "saw a slight decline."

## Why we might have edge

1. **The disputed variable is the multiple, not the earnings.** The red bench's strongest attack was "we agree with the street's $1.02 fee-related earnings / $0.89 distributable earnings, therefore we have no edge." The blue bench overturned that using red's own evidence: the street's estimate is $0.89 and the street's price targets are $10–$17 against a tape of $11.84. Agreeing on the numerator is not the same as agreeing on the price. Nobody in the pack has priced the multiple; that is where we are taking the other side.
2. **The redemption bound is arithmetic, not narrative,** and it was rebuilt independently from a *different* SEC registrant's filings — OCIC's own tender documents — after one bench wrongly declared the calculation impossible. Two separate roads landed on the same $0.96B/quarter cap.
3. **The scariest damage number in the bear case is a rate-cycle artifact.** Red pointed at Part I incentive fees falling 0.7% year over year as proof the franchise is decaying. Those fees are earned on floating-rate credit income; they track short-term interest rates, not investor redemptions. Blue caught the substitution. If the market is de-rating the whole franchise off a line item that moves with SOFR, that is a mispricing we can hold.
4. **One real dilution scare is fake.** The share count in the audited data jumps 498M → 656M, which looks like heavy dilution. It is the Up-C structure converting private units into public Class A shares — the public count rises, total ownership units do not. We have been burned by this exact artifact before and got it right this time.

## Why it might be priced in

- **There is no company guidance.** The "$1.02 guide" both the original pitch and the bulls lean on is not a company number — management said it expects to *beat* a named third-party consensus ($1.02 fee-related earnings / $0.89 distributable earnings). The audited earnings release contains **no forward per-share figure at all** ([Q2'26 deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001823945/000182394526000036/blueowlearningsdeck63026.htm) carries only realized $392.2M / $0.25 and $351.2M / $0.22). So our forward number *is* the street's forward number. We concede this fully. Our edge cannot be the estimate.
- **The dispersion may already be harvested.** The stock is +52.4% off its low. If that bounce happened after a spring redemption shock, we are buying the recovery, not the dislocation. Blue correctly flagged that we never verified *when* the low was set — we ran no price history — so this discount is being applied on a guess.
- **Stock comp outside adjusted earnings is a decade-old, universally published criticism of every alternative manager.** Nobody is unaware of it. Our 18x ex-comp number is not news.
- **Fee-rate walks are in every sell-side model.** The 2.1bp compression we treat as small is a line item in everyone's spreadsheet.
- **The payout is public.** A dividend at 104% of earnings alongside fresh 6.750% ten-year debt is visible to anyone who reads the filings. If the yield gets cut, nobody will be able to say they weren't warned — including us.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The dividend gets cut and the holder base changes hands all at once.** The declared dividend is $0.23/quarter = $0.92/year against $0.88 of run-rate distributable earnings (104.5%) and $0.89 consensus (103.4%). Meanwhile the company issued **$750M of 6.750% notes due 2036** on 2026-08-18 ([8-K 0001193125-26-355671](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001823945&type=8-K)), interest payable each February 18 and August 18 starting 2027-02-18 — $50.6M of gross coupon, about 3 cents a share, though proceeds repaid the revolver so the *net* increment is smaller (red corrected this against its own case; we adopt the correction). A yield cut on a 7.8%-yield name doesn't re-price 10%, it re-prices 25–35%, because the buyers who own it for the income are mandated out and sell into each other. **Tripwire: exists** — the quarterly declaration, normally alongside the print.

**Failure narrative 2 — Fee-rate compression is a trend, not a quarter.** Realized rate went 1.4094% → 1.3884%. If that repeats four times, we lose 10 cents of earnings power, not 2.5 — and at 13x, that is $1.30 of share price, plus the multiple compression that comes with a shrinking franchise. Blue softened this finding (the rate was computed with period-*end* assets against in-period fees, which biases the rate low when assets are rising) but "partly a construction artifact" is not "zero." **Tripwire: exists** — realized rate at each print; we need ≥1.400%.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** The money that pays our fees comes through wirehouse and RIA home-office platforms. A single platform deciding to de-authorize or cap Blue Owl's non-traded products is a private commercial decision: it appears in no 8-K, no tender document, and no monthly NAV release. We would see it two or three quarters later, as a fundraising number that missed. The same blindness covers the loan-level quality inside the private vehicles — those are unobservable, manager-marked private credit assets in funds we do not own and cannot look through. Red's 10%-markdown scenario (10% × ~$100B of credit assets ≈ $130M of fees ≈ **8 cents**, 2.7x the entire redemption bound and capped by nothing) was thrown out of the verdict on a technicality — our own rule says verdicts rest only on confirmed or refuted findings, and red graded its own markdown "plausible." **Unknown-unknown class, named: off-filing counterparty and distribution-channel decisions, plus opacity of manager-determined (Level 3) marks inside vehicles we neither hold nor can monitor.** There is no sensor for this. The only defense is size.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** Our own footprint is not the problem: average daily volume is not in the pack, but on an estimated 7–11M shares/day (~$85–130M) a 2.25%-of-book position is a small fraction of one day's trade, and we are not the marginal price-setter. The problem is *other people's* predicted flow. This is an income-mandate holder base at a 7.8% yield, and the flow is one-directional at exactly the wrong moment: on a cut, forced sellers arrive together and there is no natural bid until the yield-agnostic value buyer shows up 25% lower. That is why the bad branch in our table is a retest of the low, not a 10% drawdown.

Three of our own rules interact badly here, and we should say so. (a) "Verdicts only on confirmed or refuted evidence" correctly deleted the 8-cent markdown from the *verdict* — but if we let it delete the risk from the *sizing* too, we are under-hedged against the fattest tail in the book. We size for it anyway. (b) "Crowding and timing are a reason to size down, never to reject" means we cannot reject on the +52% bounce — but we also never verified the price path, so we are applying a discount to a story we haven't checked. (c) The venue rule ("audited filing beats the spoken word") strips us of the "$1.02 guide" language, which means our forward number has no company commitment behind it at all. All three push the same way: smaller.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be a dividend cut that pushes the income-mandate holders out the door simultaneously, in a name where the true earnings power was already 26% below the headline once stock compensation was counted, and where we had no way to see the fundraising channel closing until the quarter after it closed.

Breakeven volatility comparison: our table dispersion is 22.5% annualized. Roughly 60% of that dispersion resolves at the two October dates, which for a 2026-12-18 expiry (T ≈ 0.28 years) works out to about **$1.60 of expected move = 13.5% of spot = ~25.5% annualized**. So a December call is only cheap against us if chain-level implied volatility prints below ~25.5% — while range-based realized vol suggests 45–50%.

**Instrument recommendation, as the output of that arithmetic: STOCK. No options.** Two reasons, both from the numbers above. (a) Implied vol below 25.5% is very unlikely when realized has been running near 45%, so calls will almost certainly screen expensive against our table. (b) The reverse trade — selling premium into rich implied vol — is *worse*, because our own table (22.5%) is narrower than the tape (~45%): selling puts priced off a table that is too narrow is how you get run over. The correct move is to widen the table with real price bars first. When the chain is pulled, use **chain-level midpoint implied vol and live quotes on the actual strikes, never the aggregate underlying vol feed** — those two diverge badly, and we have paid for that lesson before (71 vs 101).

**Bimodality note.** The table is fork-dated on two clustered events (~Oct 1/2 and Oct 29). That produces two humps, not a bell curve — a single strike will misprice it either way. If we ever do express this with options, it should be stock plus a small out-of-the-money call, not one strike trying to straddle both forks.

**Tax overlay.** 2026 is a harvest year, so the loss branch must land inside 2026. Both catalysts are in October, so a **2026-12-18 expiry captures both forks and keeps the loss branch in the intended tax year.** Any January-2027-or-later expiry defers the loss into next year and is **excluded**, unless the edge dominates numerically — meaning our table value is at least 1.5x the chain ask. Nothing in the current arithmetic comes close to clearing that bar, which reinforces the stock-only recommendation. The common stock starter is unaffected by the overlay; if it is underwater in December, the stop levels above already put the realization inside 2026.
