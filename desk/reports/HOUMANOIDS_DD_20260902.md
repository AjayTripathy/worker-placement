schools, property managers, construction sites, and retail — the pitch is American software,

## The §889 analysis (the decisive finding)

Federal rules (§889, 2 CFR 200.216) bar covered Chinese telecom/video equipment for
federally-funded buyers — which describes **both** of Houmanoids' anchor verticals: sheriffs
(federal grants) and schools (E-Rate). The product as deployed today is a PRC-origin quadruped
carrying a §1260H-listed sensor, and the company concedes it cannot certify compliance — which
its own anchor LOI requires *before deployment*. The "compliance moat" is therefore a bet that
they onshore assembly and secure FCC approval **before the ban bites their pipeline**. That is
a regulatory-arbitrage venture bet with an unexecuted mitigation — a legitimate thing to bet
on, but the opposite of a moat that exists today, and the memo's headline framing inverts it.

## The unit-economics base rate

Their own memo cites the anchor against itself: **Knightscope — a decade of public
security-RaaS actuals at comparable price points — has spent $1.38–$1.71 to deliver every
$1.00 of service revenue, every year FY21–25**, and has never reached unit profitability.
Houmanoids' counter-thesis is deployment density (60 dogs in one parish → ~31% cost ratio).
Density is a spreadsheet scenario, labeled "planning" in their own model. The 75-unit cash
model through 2028 rests entirely on it.

## The honesty-alpha finding (genuinely in their favor)

This is **not** a masking case — it is the opposite. The room voluntarily discloses: the PRC
robot base, the related-party first customer and founder loans, the failed pilot, and hedges
its weakest claims in plain language ("founders' understanding," "nothing is executed,"
"indicative sizing"). On the desk's honesty framework this is a high-integrity disclosure
posture, and it speaks well of the founders as future counterparties. It does not change the
verdict: an honestly-disclosed hard bet at a rich price is still a pass. Candor prices the
relationship, not the SAFE.

## Verdict and reopen conditions

**PASS at $60M post on an uncapped SAFE.** The desk has no edge underwriting a pre-revenue
regulatory-arbitrage bet at a founder-asserted venture price — and the honest documents
themselves say the moat is unbuilt.

**It becomes a conversation when all three exist:**
1. A **capped** instrument at a defensible number (the uncapped-MFN structure on self-asserted
   terms is the wrong risk for outside money at this stage);
2. The moat **executed, not described**: signed FTZ assembly agreement + the FCC approval
   instrument in hand;
3. **One arm's-length paying deployment surviving 90 days** without hardware failure.

## Provenance: how Amoriss was traced to the founder

The related-party identification was a **cross-document join within the company's own data
room** — three separate documents each disclosed one leg, and the connection is the desk's:

1. **The team roster/bio** lists Amoriss among CEO Taylor Hou's founded ventures (alongside
   APM Help/Syndi and Fyxed).
2. **The KPI/sales snapshot** names Amoriss as the "first paying customer" commencing
   September 1, 2026 — with no related-party label *on that page*.
3. **The balance sheet** shows the founder-entity finance pattern: a $450k converting loan
   from Syndi Co (Hou-affiliated, $10M cap) and $98k from Hou Ventures.

Joining (1) and (2) converts the traction headline into a related-party transaction; (3)
establishes that founder entities already act as the company's lenders, making the
customer-lender-channel concentration a single-person diligence surface. Credit where due:
the facts are all *theirs* — the room disclosed each piece — but the pieces sit on different
pages, and the join is the finding. This is the same method as the LZ Census-tier catch and
the GEF charter read: nothing hidden, everything separated.

**Independent verification status: OWED.** State-registry confirmation of Amoriss's officers
(expected Texas or Louisiana, matching Hou's other ventures) could not be completed —
