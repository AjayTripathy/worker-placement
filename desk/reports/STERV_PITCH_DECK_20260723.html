Two lenses. On total group forest — €10.80 gross, €9.16 after deferred tax — the standing forests alone match or exceed the share price: the €9.3bn-revenue operating business is implicitly free-to-nearly-free. But the dated 2027 catalyst crystallizes only the Swedish estate (€7.10–7.23/sh), which is less than the share price. The honest frame is a sum-of-the-parts where each part is individually cheap and a dated event splits the company into two cleaner securities — not "the catalyst hands you the operating company for nothing."

03

The forest mark is a price × quantity claim — each half verified separately

Price — verified by a transaction. May 2025: Stora sold 12.4% of the Swedish estate for €900M, "in line with accounting fair value," to private institutional buyers. Real money paid the book mark, in cash, for the exact piece the 2027 event monetizes.

Quantity and condition — the half nobody checks. The marks assume healthy, sustainably-harvested standing forest. That is a physical quantity, observable from satellite imagery. Slide 06.

What's inside the €10.80 — and what the spin actually separates: Swedish estate ~1.2M ha = €7.10–7.23/sh (the spin perimeter) · a 41% stake in Finland's Tornator plus a retained Swedish associate = €2.18/sh (stays with the parent, equity-accounted — illiquid stakes, not cash) · China/Brazil/Uruguay plantations = €1.42/sh (stay with the parent). The May-2025 transaction validates the price of the Swedish piece specifically. Deferred tax on the forests (~€1.64/sh) nets the €10.80 to €9.16 after-tax.
04

The clock: a real forcing event — read precisely

  • What is published: a statutory partial cross-border demerger separating the Swedish forest business into a new listed company — named (Bergslagets Skogar), dual-listed Stockholm + Helsinki, shares distributed to holders pro-rata, both share classes identical. Announcement H2-2026, completion H1-2027, a capital-markets day set for November 3, 2026. Structures of this type are designed to be tax-neutral in Finland and Sweden — an inference from the statutory form; the company itself doesn't use the phrase, and US tax treatment is unaddressed (see slide 08).
  • What the event does: crystallizes the Swedish estate as a separately-priced security and hands it to shareholders. What it does not do by itself: force the market to pay total-group forest value for everything — the Tornator stake and the plantations stay inside the parent and re-rate only if the market chooses to.
  • The votes: the two anchor holders (the Finnish state's vehicle, ~27.3% of votes; the Wallenberg sphere, ~27.4%) have publicly supported the demerger and stated they intend to hold both companies. These are supportive statements, not binding undertakings, and the required general-meeting supermajority is two-thirds — so ~55% supportive is highly encouraging, not yet decisive.

Why only half-size — the honest slide, now honestly calibrated both ways

  • The operating trough is real but not desperate: ~22× last year's earnings excluding forest-revaluation noise (likely leaner in 2026 as packaging prices sag); free cash flow thin but positive (+€122M after investment last year), with the big Oulu mill conversion now past its earnings-breakeven point. (An earlier draft said "~55× and breakeven cash flow" — bent pessimistic; corrected.)
  • You are barely paid to wait, and here is the exact mechanism: ~1.8% net dividend as actually held — our broker withholds Finland's 35% statutory rate at source on Finnish dividends (it is not a registered Finnish intermediary, so the 15% US-treaty rate does not apply automatically). The treaty rate (~2.4% net) is recoverable only via a manual Finnish reclaim that would recover roughly €15–30 a year at this position size — not worth filing. Of the 35% withheld, only the treaty 15 points are creditable against US tax; the rest leaks.
  • The split costs the operating company its captive fiber: an 18-year supply agreement converts ~9% of its Nordic wood from owned to purchased-at-market. The parent left behind is structurally a slightly worse business than today's integrated one.
  • Dead-money window: 12–18 months where the asset floor protects value but nothing forces the price.
  • What remains open on the structure — and this one is real for a US holder: the published shape is holder-friendly (pro-rata, classes identical), and in-country tax-neutrality is the standard design of this statutory form. But the company says nothing about US treatment, and a foreign demerger is not automatically tax-free for US holders — absent a qualifying US reorganization analysis, receiving the forest-company shares could be a taxable dividend at their fair value. Resolved at the prospectus, any US tax opinion, and the post-completion basis filing. Until then, the "value unlock" for a US taxable holder carries a possible tax haircut we won't pretend away.

The conservation lens

A growing, verified carbon sink — valued honestly, not hopefully

The measured fact first: the estate cuts only ~70% of what it grows (audited: 4.1M m³ harvested vs 5.9M m³ growth) — standing volume rises ~1.8M m³/yr, a net sink on the order of one-to-two million tonnes of CO₂ per year under certified management. Our satellite pass screened the harvest claim — no evidence of elevated cutting — and re-checks it quarterly. A conservation claim under continuous independent observation, not a sustainability-report sentence.

What the separation reliably does — and what it doesn't

  • Reliably: converts one discounted conglomerate into two pure-play securities the market prices on their own merits, and hands the forest to holders pro-rata (tax-neutral in the Nordics by design; US treatment resolves at the prospectus). A conservation-minded investor gets the clean vehicle in 2027 without paying for it today.
  • Not reliably: a re-rating to appraised value. The honest comparables cut against that hope — Sweden's own listed forest owners trade at ~0.70× (SCA) and ~0.85× (Holmen) of their forest values, and US timber REITs at 24–35% below appraised NAV. The discount is cyclical (REITs have touched parity in lumber booms), so listing can compress it episodically — but the buyer who demonstrably pays full value is private (the 2025 sale). Underwrite the event as "crystallized value + cleaner securities," not "new flows re-rate the stock."
  • The optionality rides free at today's price: carbon-sink value as EU climate accounting matures, nascent biodiversity credits, wind-lease rents (established income for large Swedish forest owners), certified-timber premiums, EU deforestation-traceability compliance edge.
The honest tension — this is not a nature reserve: commercial rotation forestry is genuinely contested by conservation groups even at sustained yield; tighter EU harvest restrictions would raise the land's conservation value while cutting its timber cash flow; and carbon-credit additionality rules block selling credits for restraint already practiced. The claim we stand behind: a well-stewarded, independently screened, net-growing forest with real conservation optionality — bought at a price that pays nothing for it.

The orthogonality case

The coupling that exists is ordinary market beta, not tech — every forest name correlates less with software than with the broad market, because no driver of a Nordic forest equity (sawlog and pulp prices, European construction, packaging demand, EU forest regulation, the krona, biological growth) shares a cash-flow channel with AI capex or mega-cap tech earnings.

The live test, July 2026: on the month's worst software day (software index −3.0%), the three forest names rose +2.7% to +4.5% — textbook rotation — and month-to-date through the AI-capex derate they beat the Nasdaq-100 by roughly 10–13 percentage points. In the 2022 growth bear, their correlation to tech fell toward zero. That is the regime this position is built for.

Where the orthogonality honestly breaks: (1) a systemic liquidity panic — in 2020-style crashes correlation snapped to ~0.6, because in a true everything-selloff nothing is uncorrelated; (2) a synchronized rate shock, which re-rates all long-duration assets — forests and growth stocks — together; (3) a global recession, via shared cyclical demand. And one sector scar worth naming: this industry already absorbed a full technology disruption once — digitization killed graphic paper — though that vector has largely played out and the paper exposure sits in the operating company, not the forest. Also honest: Stora carries the most market beta of its peer group (1.07) — this position is orthogonal to the tech factor, not to a broad crash. It is the sleeve's catalyst leg, not its minimum-beta ballast.
