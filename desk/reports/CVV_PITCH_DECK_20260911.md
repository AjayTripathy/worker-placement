## What we believe

CVD Equipment is no longer an equipment company. On September 3, 2026 the board committed to stop taking new system orders, cut the workforce roughly in half, and book a $0.8–1.0m severance charge; the CEO left the same day (primary: 8-K filed 2026-09-10, accession 0001493152-26-042189). What's left is a debt-free Nasdaq-listed cash box with a Long Island building, a $3.9m draining backlog, and a spares/quartz/services remnant doing about $2m a quarter at 17% gross margin.

We believe three things, in order of confidence:

1. **The "melting ice cube" story is factually wrong.** Cash $23.5m, no long-term debt, total liabilities about $2.5m — cash is roughly 9x everything the company owes. Book equity $36.0m = $5.17/share against a $4.74 tape. There is no funding cliff and no lender to run from.
2. **But the cheapness is a mirage once you charge for the wind-down.** Take out two years of listed-company carry (~$5m), the restructuring cash ($0.8–1.0m), and a 50% haircut on ~$4.6m of receivables and inventory (−$2.3m), and realizable value is roughly $27.8m ≈ **$4.00/share** — *below* the $4.74 tape. You are paying about a 19% premium to conservative liquidation value, not an 8% discount to it.
3. **Nothing is scheduled.** The board "continues to evaluate" cost reduction, asset monetization and "potential alternatives involving its real estate assets." Evaluate is not a signed contract. There is no dissolution plan, no tender, no buyback, no special dividend, and no filed appraisal of the building.

Net belief: **right kind of situation, wrong price, no clock.** We want this name at $3.40 or below, or with a signed document in hand. Not at $4.74 on hope.

## What the market believes

The market believes almost nothing, because almost nobody is looking. There is no published sell-side consensus for the coming quarter; the only visible "street" number is a vendor-generated forward P/E of 11.4 on a company that just announced it is exiting its business — an artifact, not an opinion. Ten-day average volume is roughly 90–109k shares, about **$0.45m a day** of notional.

What the tape says: the stock went from about $7.14 (August 12) to about $6.42 (September 5) to $4.74 — roughly a 26% one-day gap on the restructuring news. It sits 47.9% below the 52-week high of $9.10 and still **60.7% above** the 52-week low of $2.95. So the market read the news as bad, marked the stock to roughly book-minus-a-bit, and moved on. That is a reasonable, lazy, approximately-correct mark. It is not a mistake we can harvest.

## Why we might have edge

- **Nobody is measuring it.** No coverage means the name is unmeasured, which is the *precondition* for edge, not proof of edge. If a filed appraisal, a signed sale contract, or a post-cut expense run-rate shows up, we would be among very few people who had already priced what it means.
- **We have done the frictions arithmetic that both the tape and the original brief skipped.** Both anchored on book value ($5.17) or net current assets ($3.69 + property). Charging for carry, severance tails, and asset-recovery haircuts moves fair value about $1.17/share lower. That is the one genuinely non-consensus number in this file.
- **Two unpriced options exist.** (a) The building: the CFO called the $10.3m carrying value "a conservative number" on a call, referencing an old sale-leaseback valuation — but he also said he "can't speak to having multiple valuations," and no appraisal appears in any filing. (b) Tax attributes: $12.6m of consolidated net income on the divestiture gain sits against only $688k of taxes payable, which implies real sheltering attributes. A debt-free Nasdaq cash box with attributes is reverse-merger currency. Neither is in the price, and neither is verified.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: it mostly is. At $4.74 the stock is between our friction-adjusted realizable value ($4.00) and book ($5.17). That is exactly where a rational market puts a cash box whose board *might* monetize a building. The entire remaining discount to book is $0.43/share (~$3.0m) against corporate carry we estimate at $2–4m a year ($0.29–0.58/share) — so the discount self-consumes in roughly **three to twelve months** even if nothing else goes wrong. There is no forced seller, no covenant, no activist on file, and the one catalyst that matters is a governance decision with no deadline attached to it. A prize table that never clears +50% without the building fetching **3.1x** its carrying value is not an edge; it is a fairly-priced stub.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The Slow Bleed (highest probability).** No sale contract, no dissolution plan. The backlog drains over two to three quarters, revenue falls to spares and quartz, and the retained shell — audit, D&O, exchange fees, an acting CEO plus CFO, and the departed CEO's base salary through October 2, 2026 *plus a further nine months* — burns $0.29–0.58/share a year. Every quarter of waiting subtracts from the asset stack we are buying. **Tripwire: monitorable** — quarterly opex and cash on each 10-Q; fires if opex exceeds $0.7m/quarter or cash falls below $21m.

**Failure narrative 2 — The Building Is Worth Less Than Book.** Everything in both prize tables rests on $10.276m of net PP&E. There is no appraisal in any filed venue. Central Islip is a single-tenant, purpose-built, furnace-heavy industrial property; the buyer pool for that is thin, and a 0.7x-of-book outcome takes realizable value to about $3.55/share. **Tripwire: partially monitorable** — a filed appraisal, LOI, or PSA would resolve it, but only *after* the fact. There is no leading indicator.

**Failure narrative 3 — THE ONE WITH NO TRIPWIRE: the Zombie Shell.** The board neither liquidates nor sells; it keeps the listing alive and looks for a reverse merger, an acquisition, or a "new strategic direction," spending cash on bankers and diligence for two to four years. Holders get no distribution, and the asset stack we underwrote quietly becomes deal currency for someone else's business at terms we would never have chosen. **There is no monitorable tripwire for this.** No filing is required to *not* do something. "Continues to evaluate" is legally satisfied by doing nothing forever, and we cannot observe intent. **Unknown-unknown class: PRINCIPAL-INTENT OPACITY** — the controlling decision lives in an unobservable private preference (board and insider intent about the shell's future use), with no disclosure obligation, no periodic report, and no external sensor that can be armed against it. Adjacent unknown-unknown classes we also cannot sensor: **latent-obligation surprise** (warranty and installed-base support after the engineers who built the tools are gone — accrual not isolated in any extract we have; neither bench ran a tool on it) and **governance self-dealing** (comp resets, retention grants, or a related-party transaction that transfers value before any distribution).

**The reflexive / structural leg.** This is where the position is most likely to hurt us mechanically rather than analytically:

- **Our own size versus liquidity.** $0.45m/day of notional, 90–109k shares. A 1% book position is multiple days of volume. Our *entry* moves the price we are trying to buy, and our *exit* is worse: in the bad branches (Narratives 2 and 3) the natural holders are also leaving, and a microcap cash box with no catalyst has no bid. Anything above ~10% of daily volume per day means we are the marginal price.
- **Predicted flow works against the reopen band.** The exact conditions that would take the stock to $3.40 — no monetization, burn confirmed, delisting chatter — are the conditions that thin the book further. We should expect to get filled on our reopen band *only* during the worst liquidity of the cycle, i.e. an adverse-selection fill.
- **Doctrine interaction, and a double-count we must not commit.** Two detectors point at this name at once: `public_shell_carry_burn` (the discount is eaten by listed-company carry faster than the board can realize it) and `friction_adjusted_nlv_gap` (book equity overstates realizable value by 20–30%). **They overlap.** The friction-adjusted NLV already subtracts about two years of carry; charging the annual burn *again* on top of the $4.00 NLV double-penalizes the same dollars and would make us too bearish, just as anchoring on book made the original brief too bullish. Both benches wrote the two arguments side by side without netting them. Our $4.00 anchor is the carry-inclusive number; further burn only applies *beyond* two years.
- **Anchoring risk in our own process.** Every number in this file descends from one unaudited-for-fair-value figure: $10.276m of net PP&E. Anchor-and-adjust on a single load-bearing book number is precisely how both benches produced tables that agreed on direction and disagreed by 29% on level.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid a premium to realizable value for a board that never signs anything — the building never gets monetized, the listed-shell carry quietly eats the remaining book discount while we wait, and we discover our $10.276m property anchor was a book entry rather than a bid.
