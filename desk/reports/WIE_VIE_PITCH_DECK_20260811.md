## What we believe

Wienerberger (the Austrian brick, pipe, and roofing maker, listed in Vienna) is a business we might want to own cheaply — but not today, and not at this price without new information. Three weeks ago the company cut its full-year 2026 operating-profit target to "approximately €700 million" (from an implied ~€800 million), blaming a €100 million hit from weak residential construction, about €40 million of which already landed in the first half. Its own words: the recovery "did not materialize," demand will "remain subdued over the coming quarters," and "we have not seen any material government support programs or stimulus measures" ([company trading update, primary source](https://www.wienerberger.com/content/dam/corp/corporate-website/downloads/press-releases/2021-2030/2026/20260721_PA_wienerberger_Trading-Update_Q2.pdf)).

Underneath the headline, second-quarter operating profit was €230 million versus €253 million a year ago — and more than €16 million of this year's number came from newly acquired businesses. Strip that out and the existing business shrank roughly 15% year-over-year.

Then, two days before the results date, the CEO of 17 years, Heimo Scheuch, resigned with immediate effect for health reasons; CFO-turned-deputy-chairman Gerhard Hanke stepped in as interim CEO. The transformation story we would be underwriting was Scheuch's.

Our belief: the deciding number — how much debt the company carries against the newly lowered profit target — publishes tomorrow at noon, and we cannot reconstruct it in advance. Buying today would be guessing, with the skew against us.

## What the market believes

The stock trades at €21.22, only 6.6% above its 52-week low of €19.90, after analyst downgrades that followed the guidance cut. Nine analysts cover the name — this is not an overlooked company; the bad news is known and largely priced. At the current price, the whole company (equity plus roughly €2.04 billion of estimated net debt) is valued at about 6.2 times the new €700 million profit target — a level that says the market already treats this as a cyclically depressed, moderately leveraged building-products company waiting for a housing recovery that keeps not arriving. The market is also, reasonably, nervous that an interim CEO's first results presentation could bring a second guidance cut.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: for tomorrow, we don't, and that is the verdict. But the court identified real reasons the post-print re-review is live rather than a formality:

- **The dividend is already paid.** The ~€104 million dividend (€0.95/share) went out on May 18, so it is already inside the June 30 debt figure. Our own original brief got this wrong (see the table below) — it claimed the second half had to generate cash *while also* paying the dividend. In fact the second half is the seasonally strong cash-collection half with no dividend outflow, which makes the debt-reduction target more attainable than either side first thought.
- **The second-half profit bridge has a named mechanism, not just hope.** The company explicitly commits that cost offsets "including targeted price increases, will be fully reflected in earnings during the second half of 2026," and acquired businesses add over €16 million per quarter. Second-half profit needs to be ~€373 million versus €371 million in the same period last year — roughly flat, with identified drivers.
- **The interim CEO is an insider who co-signed the current guidance.** Hanke was CFO from 2021–25 and helped set the €700 million target 22 days ago. A fresh outsider slashing targets to reset the bar is the classic risk; an insider cutting his own three-week-old number is less likely.

## Why it might be priced in

- The guidance cut, the downgrades, and the CEO departure are all public; the stock already sits near its low. There is no information asymmetry in our favor.
- The "cheap" multiple is measured against a profit target the company itself just lowered and could lower again — the denominator is not safe.
- The decisive unknowns (June 30 net debt, loan-covenant terms, how the Italcer acquisition's minority-stake obligations are treated) resolve in less than 24 hours. Any position taken today is a coin flip on numbers we cannot see, against sellers who may know more.
