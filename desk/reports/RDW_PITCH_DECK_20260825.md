# PITCH — RDW — VERDICT

**FLAT — no position, no order, no entry band. PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION.** Both benches independently landed on "no trade" (each at 8/10 conviction); the bull case that opened this file rested on an arithmetic error that the company's own segment table refutes, and the one real variant view we found points *down*, where we are barred from acting.

---

## What we believe

Redwire is a space-hardware company that bought a drone company (Edge Autonomy, closed 2025-06-13) and is now two businesses stapled together. Our belief, stated plainly:

1. **The original space business is not growing, and it already shrank hard.** Space revenue fell from $255.3M in FY24 to $209.8M in FY25 — down 17.8%. The first half of 2026 was $107.9M against $108.8M a year earlier: flat. So 2026 is stabilization at a collapsed base, not a recovery.
2. **The record backlog is a drone story, not a space story.** At 6/30/26, Space backlog went $299.8M → $321.9M on $107.9M of revenue (booking 1.20x of what it billed). Defense Tech went $111.4M → $220.2M on $106.2M (2.02x). Total backlog of $542.1M is genuinely a record and genuinely growing — but the growth is in the acquired drone unit.
3. **Neither segment makes money.** For FY25, Space earned $4.9M of adjusted EBITDA on $209.8M of revenue — a 2.3% margin. Defense Tech lost $10.9M. And "adjusted" is doing heavy lifting: Q2'26 GAAP net loss was $41.0M against adjusted EBITDA of negative $3.2M, a $37.7M gap in a single quarter. FY25: $226.6M GAAP loss vs $50.3M adjusted loss.
4. **The price already assumes the step-up.** At $11.68 with 249,992,609 shares out and $509.7M of net cash, enterprise value is roughly $2.41B — about 5.1x consensus 2026 revenue for a business with roughly zero segment-level profit. And it is 140% above its 52-week low of $4.88 (though 56% below the $26.63 high). This is a giveback of a triple, not a broken security.
5. **Our own revenue number is slightly below the street's, with a reason.** Street is $468.8M. Adding run-rate to backlog conversion we get $446–465M, call it $455M — about 3% light. The mechanism: to hit the street, Space has to step above a ~$54M/quarter plateau it has not exceeded in six quarters.

None of that is a long. It is also not a short we are permitted to take (see below). So: nothing.

## What the market believes

The market believes the 2026 guide of $450–500M is achievable and roughly correctly set. Consensus sits at $468.8M — essentially the midpoint of management's own range, which implies second-half revenue of about $255M and a Q3 of roughly $120–125M against $103.4M last year.

Under the hood, the market is buying three things: a record $542M backlog, a genuine defense/drone demand cycle, and a balance sheet that went from $52.3M of cash (9/30/25) to $557.7M (6/30/26). It is looking past GAAP losses on the view that percentage-of-completion space programs mature and margins normalize.

The market is not obviously wrong about any of that. The stock is a battleground name with heavy retail participation, so price is also partly a sentiment instrument rather than a discounted-cash-flow instrument.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: our edge in this file was **disconfirming**, not finding a trade. Three specific things we know that are not published anywhere we can find:

- **We derived Q3'25 space-segment revenue, which the company never disclosed.** The Q3'25 press release omits the segment split. But FY25 Space ($209,817k) minus first-half ($108,815k) minus Q4'25 ($54,454k) = **$46,548k**. That single number reframes the upcoming quarter: the comparison Redwire faces in Q3'26 is *easy*, not a cliff. The original file (and, initially, one of our own benches) had this backwards.
- **We reconstructed the company's revealed selling price for its own stock.** The equity note in the 10-Q discloses, per program, both shares sold and the weighted-average price achieved: $9.38, then $14.59 (the May 2026 $350M program, sold out in full — 23,986,658 shares), then $16.05 (June 2026 $500M program, of which only $149.6M was used, leaving **$350.4M live**). Then shares outstanding went 249,221,102 (6/30) → 249,992,609 (8/3) — up 0.31% in five weeks. The company stopped selling when the stock broke $16. That converts a vague "dilution overhang" into a dated price level and an on/off state.
- **We caught an acquisition artifact in our own reasoning.** A "seasonality" claim computed across the Edge close is not seasonality. See the table below — this was our error, not someone else's.

The general edge claim: segment-level and equity-note-level arithmetic on primary filings, done consistently, produces numbers the sell-side aggregate does not carry. That is real but modest. It did not produce a position here.

## Why it might be priced in

Strongly, and we should say so:

- **Almost everything we "found" is in the company's own press release table.** Our second bench downgraded two findings that the first bench labeled novel — the flat space line and the earnings-adjustment risk — to consensus, because management itself described the FY25 losses as driven by "substantial negative impact from EAC adjustments… largely related to programs in the development stage," and trade press covered it in February.
- **Eight analysts cover it and sit at the guide midpoint.** Our variant is 3%. A 3% revenue difference on a name that moves 10% in a day is not a thesis.
- **The dilution is disclosed, dated, and on the tape.** Shares outstanding rose 25.3% in a single quarter (198,918,728 → 249,221,102). Nobody is unaware.
- **The balance sheet removes the distress trade.** $557.7M cash against first-half operating cash burn of $31.6M (improved from $132.7M) means roughly $48M/year of burn and no financing need. The ATM is opportunistic, not survival. So there is no forced-seller angle and no rescue-financing angle.

Net: we have no differentiated *number* large enough to trade, and the qualitative story is fully in the open.

## What could go wrong

**This section is the pre-mortem. Since we hold nothing, "losing money" means two distinct things: (a) missing a move we chose to skip, and (b) losing on the re-entry if our trigger fires. Both are covered.**

**Failure narrative 1 — We are watching the wrong segment (monitorable).**
Our re-entry trigger is keyed to *space* revenue. But the backlog growth, and plausibly the entire equity story, is *drones*: Defense Tech booked 2.02x its billings while Space booked 1.20x. European and US drone procurement accelerates, Edge converts $220M of backlog at improving margin, and the stock doubles while our chosen sensor (space revenue vs $46.5M) prints a boring in-line number and never fires. We stand aside through the whole move and then have to buy it 60% higher.
*Tripwire:* Defense Tech revenue and segment backlog in the Q3'26 10-Q, plus DT segment adjusted EBITDA crossing from negative $10.9M toward breakeven. Fully monitorable — we simply have not armed it.

**Failure narrative 2 — We re-enter on a revenue step-up that is an accounting true-up (partly monitorable, with a lag).**
Space prints $58M+ in Q3'26, our trigger fires, we buy — and the step-up turns out to be favorable cumulative catch-up on percentage-of-completion contracts rather than new throughput. The next quarter takes a negative true-up in the other direction. This company has a demonstrated $37.7M-per-quarter gap between adjusted and GAAP results, and management has already attributed a prior quarter's damage to exactly this mechanism.
*Tripwire:* the adjusted-EBITDA-to-GAAP-net-loss gap, and the EAC disclosure in the 10-K. Monitorable but **backward-looking** — we would learn we were wrong one to two quarters after buying.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
Redwire's revenue increasingly comes from government and allied-defense programs, some of which are classified, foreign, or subject to export control. A program cancellation, a lost recompete, a stop-work order, an ITAR or export-licensing action, or the loss of a single large foreign ministry-of-defense customer can be entirely non-public until it surfaces as a backlog number months later — or is never separately disclosed at all, because it is netted inside a segment total. There is no filing, no press release, no dataset, and no third-party signal we can watch. We would find out from the price.
**Unknown-unknown class: opaque-counterparty program risk** — undisclosed customer and program concentration behind classified or foreign-defense contracts, where the disclosure regime itself prevents monitoring.
**A second untripwired class, which is ours, not the company's: unrun-detector blindness.** Eleven detectors matched this name in dispatch. *Zero were actually executed* — no shell was available, no satellite or permit or safety-record pull ran, and the broker contract-search call was permission-denied. Ten of the eleven look spurious on inspection (no consumer app, no clinical program, no facilities in the permit jurisdictions), and the one that fired — the supply-calendar detector — fired by hand-reading. But "looks spurious on inspection" is not "ran and returned nothing." We do not know what the physical-evidence layer would have said about Edge Autonomy's production sites.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- *Our size versus the market's:* we hold nothing, and a starter would be 0.25–0.50% of the book — call it low-to-mid seven figures against a ~$2.9B market capitalization. **We did not measure average daily volume this session** (the capacity tool was unavailable). Given the share count, the market cap, and the heavy retail participation, a sub-1% starter is almost certainly a small fraction of a day's volume — but that is an assumption we are stating, not a measurement we made. It must be measured before any order.
- *Predicted flow — and this is the trap:* there is **$350.4M of live, unused at-the-market equity capacity**, roughly 12% of the current market capitalization. The company sold its previous program out completely at an average of $14.59 and sold into the last one at $16.05. It stopped when the stock fell. Our rule is never to bid into flow we ourselves predict. Right now the flow is *above* us — $11.68 spot against a $16.05 last clearing price — so the rule does not bar an entry today. **But the reflexivity is exact: the condition under which our thesis works is the condition under which the company restarts selling stock into us.** If we are right and the stock goes to $15, we should expect the seller to reappear. Any long here is implicitly short the issuer's own option to print equity into strength, and that option is worth roughly 12% of the cap.
- *Doctrine interactions:* (i) Our rule against shorting names with retail-momentum characteristics blocks the only view we actually hold with a mechanism — the 3% bearish gap. So our best-supported opinion is unexpressable, and "flat" is not neutrality, it is a forced abstention. (ii) Our default of taking a minimum starter out of a clean court does *not* apply, because this court is not clean: there are named, specific kills (space down 17.8% then flat, 2.3% segment margin, 5.1x sales on no profit, and a bearish variant estimate). Flat is earned here, not defaulted into. (iii) No integrity or disclosure taint was found, so no valuation haircut on that basis — we are not applying one.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we keyed our re-entry to the space segment while the company's value was actually being set by drone backlog conversion — so we either sat out the entire move, or bought back in late, above $14.59, at precisely the price where the company restarts selling $350.4M of stock into our bid.**
