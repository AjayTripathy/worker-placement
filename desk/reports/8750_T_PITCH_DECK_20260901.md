# PITCH — 8750.T — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED  — BUY_SMALL_ACCUMULATE_ON_WEAKNESS (conviction 6/10): no kill survived either bench, but nothing about today's price is attractive — buy a small starter only on weakness at ¥1,750 or below, nothing at the current ¥1,868.5.**

*Dai-ichi Life Holdings, Tokyo Stock Exchange. Tape 2026-09-01 close: ¥1,868.5. 52-week range ¥1,052–¥1,959 (we are 4.6% below the high and 77.6% above the low). We own none of it and have no orders working.*

---

## What we believe

Dai-ichi is a Japanese life insurer trading at roughly two-thirds of its own stated embedded value — about ¥1,868 a share against an embedded value near ¥2,720 a share (0.686x, and the share count behind that ratio was independently re-verified: 3,621,895,219 issued less 2,864,286 in treasury = 3.619bn shares). It pays out at least 50% of group adjusted profit, has guided the dividend from ¥54.5 to ¥72 a share, and finished a ¥100bn buyback in March 2026.

We believe two specific things, and no more than that:

1. **The rate move is real in earnings but has not shown up in shareholder value.** Japanese long rates rose; the insurer's investment income rose with them (positive spread up sharply year over year, yen bond interest up ¥9.5bn). But when you strip out the equity market's contribution, the embedded value barely moved — roughly ¥286bn of Dai-ichi Life's embedded-value gain is attributable to equities, not rates, because the gain on assets was washed out by the matching rise in liability value. This finding is the single strongest thing in the whole file, and — importantly — **neither bench attacked it.** It survives by default, not by having been tested.
2. **The real clock is not the next earnings print; it is the plan.** The current three-year management plan ends with fiscal 2026 (March 2027). A successor plan — with a new capital ratio target and a new capital-return policy — lands around May 2027. That, not the November results, is the event that would force the market to re-price the company.

What we believe about *price*: this is a good business at a mediocre entry. A small position taken on weakness is defensible. A position taken here is not.

## What the market believes

The market believes the steepener trade already happened and has been paid for. The stock is up about 14% in three months and about 16% in six, and the 52-week high of ¥1,959 was set intra-June during the bond rout. That is the opposite of an ignored story.

More pointedly: **every sell-side price target we could reach is below the current price.** The aggregator consensus is 10 analysts, average 12-month target ¥1,361, highest ¥1,560 — the *highest* reachable target is 17% below the tape. (This is aggregator-grade data with one internally stale quote, so treat it as directional, not precise.) The professional consensus is not "cheap and overlooked." It is "already run."

The market also appears to believe capital return is safe. It half is. The dividend is being ratcheted up. The buyback is not: the ¥100bn program was the *plan-period* program, it completed on 2026-03-09 (79,682,200 shares for ¥99.99bn), and **no fiscal-2026 replacement has been authorized.** Management's stated policy is to decide on repurchases "nimbly and flexibly, taking into account HD cash levels, the investment pipeline situation, ESR, the company's stock price." Both of the named inputs currently point away from a buyback.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the capital ratio correctly, and almost nobody does.** The company's disclosed target for its economic solvency ratio is a **range of 170%–200%** ([Medium-Term Management Plan](https://www.daiichilife-group.com/en/about/management-plan.html), FY2024–FY2026). The ratio is currently about 206% — *above the top of its own range*, which is the definition of excess capital. But it fell from 220% to 206% in a single quarter. The distance from here to the top of the range is roughly 6 points, and it is closing at about 13–14 points a quarter. One more comparable quarter and there is no excess left to return. That glide path — not the level, not the direction of rates — is the variable that decides whether the buyback comes back.
- **We know the rate benefit doesn't reach shareholders.** Most write-ups on Japanese lifers this year run "rates up → lifers win." At the earnings line that is true. At the embedded-value line, for this company, this quarter, it was not. Owning that distinction is a genuine information advantage over the headline version of the trade.
- **We have a mechanism most coverage inverts.** Under Japan's economic-value solvency regime, rising rates *increase* the required capital held against mass-lapse and surrender risk. So the solvency ratio can fall in the very quarter the duration-gap benefit lands. The company said as much: the drop was attributed to "higher mass lapse risk and an increase in goodwill arising from M&A transactions."

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: mostly, it probably is.

- **The tape paid already.** +16% in six months, 4.6% off the high, 77.6% off the low. This is a crowded, largely-realized trade, not an undiscovered one.
- **Every reachable analyst target is below us.** If the external anchor were above the price, the "cheap at 0.686x embedded value" argument would have a second leg. It doesn't. The one external anchor we could pull cuts against us.
- **The discount to embedded value is chronic, not new.** Japanese lifers have traded below embedded value for years. A structural discount that never closes is not a mispricing; it is a valuation convention. We have no evidence that this time is different, and the successor plan in May 2027 is a *hope* for a catalyst, not a scheduled one.
- **The excess capital that would fund a re-rate is being spent, not returned.** Some of the ratio decline is goodwill from acquisitions — capital deployed into M&A rather than into shareholders. That is a real use of the money and it is not coming back.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the excess capital quietly disappears (monitorable).** The solvency ratio glides from 206% into the 170–200% range at the half-year print. No fiscal-2026 buyback is ever authorized. The dividend keeps rising, which is nice but is already in the price, and the thing that would actually re-rate the stock — a large discretionary return of surplus capital — never arrives. We are left holding a chronically cheap insurer with no catalyst until May 2027. *Tripwire: the half-year solvency ratio, and any Tokyo Stock Exchange repurchase notice. Both observable.*

**Failure narrative 2 — the steepener unwinds and the multiple goes back (partly monitorable).** Japanese long rates stabilize or fall, the "rates up, lifers win" trade reverses, and the stock retraces toward the analyst targets in the ¥1,360–¥1,560 area — a 17–27% drawdown from today, and still 10–20% below our ¥1,700 add. We were early into a mean-reverting narrative rather than into a business change. *Tripwire: 30-year Japanese government bond yield. Weak, though — we have no reliable mapping from the yield to the multiple, and the original brief's attempt to build one ("30-year to 4.5% is thesis-negative") rested on a number the company never decomposed. Treat this tripwire as directional only.*

**Failure narrative 3 — the number we are trading on is a model output we cannot audit, and it moves for reasons that leave no public trace (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).** The economic solvency ratio is not a fact; it is the output of an internal model with regulator-negotiated parameters — mass-lapse calibration, the ultimate forward rate, the final shape of Japan's incoming capital standard. Any of those can be re-set between disclosures, moving the ratio 10–20 points with no preceding public signal, no filing we can watch, and no price action to read. The same is true of the underlying risk itself: a genuine surrender run in single-premium savings products has no early public tick — we would learn about it from the same disclosure everyone else does. **Unknown-unknown class: unauditable internal-model parameter shift under regulatory regime change.** Compounding it, a second class: **single-source extraction fragility** — our entire read of the quarterly deck (pages 3, 12, 29) comes from *one* bench's proxy-fetched extraction. The second bench tried twice and got binary garbage back. If that extraction is wrong, every number in this deck downstream of it is wrong, and nothing in our process would tell us.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- *Our size against the market's:* irrelevant, in both directions. A 0.25% starter is a few thousand dollars against a ¥6.76 trillion company. We will not move the price and we cannot learn anything from our own flow. We are a pure price-taker here — which also means we get no information edge from execution.
- *Predicted flow:* we would be buying the same thing the domestic capital-return crowd already owns. This is a crowded name near its high, not a discovery. We should size as a follower, not as a discoverer.
- *Doctrine interaction — the resting order problem.* Our proposed entry is a resting bid at ¥1,750/¥1,700 with the half-year results around 2026-11-13. A resting bid into a print is an accidental short-volatility position: the only way it fills near the print is if bad news gaps the stock into us. **The gate must be cancelled before the print and re-posted after, or explicitly converted into a deliberate "buy the bad print" decision.** Right now it is neither.
- *Doctrine interaction — currency.* This is a yen-denominated equity whose whole thesis driver is Japanese fiscal and rate stress. The equity gain and the currency loss can be the *same event*. The ownable metric is dollar total return, and neither bench modelled it. Neither did the original brief.
- *Doctrine interaction — the yield is smaller than the screen says.* The ¥72 forecast dividend is 3.85% gross at today's price. Japanese withholding of 15.315% takes it to **3.26% net** — and on dividends actually paid to date, 2.47%. Anyone quoting the gross figure is quoting a number we will never receive.
- *Doctrine interaction — we may not be able to trade it.* IBKR contract lookup for 8750.T has been permission-denied on three consecutive runs. We have never confirmed we can buy this security at all.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid a full price for excess capital that was already being spent — the solvency ratio glided back inside its 170–200% target range, no new buyback was ever authorized, and the rate-driven re-rate we thought we were buying had already been paid out to the June sellers.**
