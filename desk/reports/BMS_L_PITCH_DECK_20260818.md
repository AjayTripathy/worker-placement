# PITCH — BMS.L — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-06 — BUY A STARTER, 0.4× a normal single-name position, bought only on limit orders inside the bid/ask, underwritten as a dated shipping-rate trade with a management-quality discount — not as the cheap compounder the original memo described.**

Conviction 6/10. Braemar plc, London listing, 227p on the pack's tape (last trade 228p, bid 216 / ask 226, close 223p on 6-Aug-2026), £73.7M market value on 33,067,603 shares. We hold none of it today.

---

## What we believe

Braemar is a shipbroker. It earns commission on chartering ships and on buying and selling them. Its revenue is a direct function of freight rates and vessel-transaction volume, both of which are currently at levels not seen in decades.

Three things have to be true together, and we think they are:

**1. Braemar's fiscal year ends on 28 February, and that is the same day the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed.** Everything in the last published accounts (year to 28-Feb-2026) was earned *before* the event. The rate explosion sits entirely in the year now running. This is the single fact that makes the case, and — worth saying plainly — it did not appear in the original bull memo at all. Both benches found it independently.

**2. The rate tape is extreme and verified.** As of 6-Aug-2026: the dirty tanker index at 2,575, up 38% in five weeks and 156% year-on-year; clean tankers +112% year-on-year; the dry bulk index at 3,057, +52%. The benchmark Middle East–China tanker route was earning roughly $423k/day against $22k a year earlier. First-half newbuilding orders +66%; a record first half for ship sales — over 1,200 vessels, over $35bn, tanker sales +58%, supertanker sales +163%. Braemar's larger listed peer Clarkson reported first-half revenue +39% and underlying pre-tax profit +56%, describing itself as "materially ahead of market expectations."

**3. Revenue recognition puts the peak inside the current half.** Braemar books voyage commission when the cargo discharges, roughly one to two months after the fixture. The March-to-mid-June peak therefore lands inside the half ending 31 August 2026, and revenue stays elevated for about two months after any reopening.

Meanwhile the order book has turned: $72.5M at 28-Feb-2026 → $77.9M at 30-Apr-2026, +7.5%. And the published forecast for the current year (£139.7M revenue / £14.2M profit) was set on 2-Jul-2026 — the exact local low in tanker rates, before the July collapse of the US–Iran framework re-ramped them. The number in the market does not contain the last five weeks.

What we do **not** believe is the original memo's arithmetic. It called this 5.7× profit and a 12% cash yield. Both are wrong, and we say so below.

## What the market believes

Mostly, the market is not thinking about Braemar at all. This is the load-bearing observation.

- Research coverage is two houses: Canaccord (the company's own broker, notes not distributed outside its client list) and Edison (paid by the company, ~£60k a year). Edison's estimates have not been revised since **25-March-2026** — before the event.
- There is no disclosed short interest, and there never has been. Nobody is arguing the other side.
- Median on-book trading is **£13.7k a day** across 57 sessions, with a 4.5% gap between bid and ask. There are no listed options, so the position cannot be hedged.
- On 3-Aug-2026 Clarkson gapped up 10.2% on its results. Braemar closed **down 1.3%** the same day, and is down 9.7% from its 10-July peak. Clarkson is +30.8% year-to-date at 52-week highs; Braemar is +6.05% and mid-range.

So the market has no view, not a contrary view. That matters for how we read the flat share price: it is not evidence that someone knows something bad. There is no mechanism by which the peer's news transmits into this share price. The gap closes only when Braemar itself prints numbers.

## Why we might have edge

1. **A calendar accident nobody has priced.** The fiscal year-end falling on the event date means the last audited accounts describe a different world. Anyone screening on trailing numbers sees a company whose profit fell 31% and whose tanker desk shrank 22%. That is a true description of a pre-event year and a useless description of the current one.
2. **Frozen estimates in an orphan stock.** The only two forecasts in existence are stale by five months and one of them is paid for by the company. There is no institutional machinery here to update the number quickly.
3. **We have quantified the upside conversion, rather than assuming it.** From Clarkson's own first half, each extra £1 of revenue dropped £0.19 to underlying pre-tax profit. Applying 19% to Braemar at +20% revenue (well below Clarkson's +39%, which was partly acquisitions) gives underlying operating profit of about £18.6M against £13.18M — roughly 5.6× earnings and 4.6× enterprise value to operating profit at today's price. On today's rating (10.5×), that level of earnings would be worth roughly 418p a share. That is arithmetic from the benches' numbers, not a target we defend to the penny.
4. **The peer read-across is being actively declined by the tape**, which means the re-rating, if it comes, comes in one step on a specific date we can name.

## Why it might be priced in — and the four reasons it might simply not work

Honest inventory of the bear case that survived both benches:

- **The upside mostly belongs to the brokers, not to us.** Broker bonuses are a percentage of personal and desk revenue with **no cap on individual awards**. The proof is in the last accounts: one broker, Gundy, took £1,143k, up from nil, in a year when statutory operating profit fell 31% — roughly 22% of the entire group's underlying operating profit to one person. Total pay ran 73.8% of revenue, and cash pay per head *rose* 3.2% in a year revenue fell 4.4%. Central costs rose 29%. In a windfall year the uncapped pool is the first claimant, which is exactly why we model 19% conversion and not the 50%+ operating leverage a normal broker would show. (Braemar's own downside conversion was 56% — it loses more on the way down than it keeps on the way up. That asymmetry is the honest core of the bear case.)
- **The valuation is not what the memo said.** After adding leases (£7.96M) and net debt (£2.9M), enterprise value is about £84.6M, so 6.4× underlying operating profit — and 11.9× the statutory number, because "specific items" were £5.5M, not the £3.95M claimed. Over five years, adjustments equal 34% of cumulative underlying operating profit. Free cash flow after lease principal and the £4.1M spent buying its own shares to fund broker awards is 5.0%, not 12% — and 2.0% stripping out a one-off working capital benefit. Earnings multiple is 10.5× on fully diluted underlying earnings (21.28p), not 9.2×.
- **A management-narrative problem that is real, though not fatal.** Detailed below. Short version: the National Crime Agency put a freezing order on a company account; the company was on notice from late January 2025; the accounts to 28-Feb-2025 were signed on 28-May-2025 stating there were no significant post-balance-sheet events; fourteen days later the company announced the freezing order as price-sensitive information. £1.9M, since paid, no solvency question — but the bull case requires trusting this management's word "in line" against a peer saying "materially ahead," and this is a reason to trust it less.
- **We cannot size it and we cannot hedge it.** £13.7k a day of volume and a 4.5% spread mean entry friction alone eats about 4.5%, accumulation takes weeks, and there is no options market. A 0.4× position is not caution; it is the largest thing that can actually be built and exited.
- One more, unresolved: **Hormuz may reopen sooner than the trade needs.** Transits were near zero from March to mid-June, now running 15–20 a day against 60–80 before the conflict. Clarkson expects "a gradual reopening" in the second half. An Iran–Oman route deal is "agreed in principle." A confirmed reopening starts the clock running out on the thesis.

## What remains unverified

- **The September date is a pattern, not a commitment.** The company calendar says "no upcoming events," and Edison — after meeting the CFO — said the next catalyst is November. We are going with 25-Sep because the company issued its HY update on 25-Sep-2025. That is inference, and our own blue bench got this wrong before reversing.

Every number in this deck traces to a bench reading a PDF or an RNS. It has not been cross-checked against a structured feed, because none exists in our pipeline for this listing.
- **Prices are 6-Aug-2026 and this is being written 18-Aug-2026.** Twelve sessions unobserved. Re-poll live before staging any order.
- **19% conversion is borrowed from Clarkson, not measured at Braemar.** It is the best available proxy and it is a different company with a different pay structure. Braemar's uncapped broker bonus could make its number materially worse; we will only learn the true figure at the November interims.
- **The forecast in the market may not be what we think.** Canaccord's note is not distributed and we have not read it. We are reasoning off Edison's frozen numbers and the company's own 2-Jul "in line" statement.
