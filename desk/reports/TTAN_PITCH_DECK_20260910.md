## What we believe

ServiceTitan sells software to home-services contractors (HVAC, plumbing, electrical). It trades at $56.285, down 52.9% from its 52-week high and only 3.9% above its 52-week low, after the September 8 print. Our belief, stated plainly:

1. **The subscription business is not broken.** Subscription revenue was $212.4M in the July quarter, growing ~22%. Contracted revenue due within the next twelve months (cRPO) grew roughly +23% year over year — faster than reported revenue (+21%) and much faster than the guided next quarter (+14.8%). A business whose customers were actually leaving cannot have a twelve-month forward book growing 23%.
2. **The damage is real but localized.** It sits in the transaction-linked "usage" line (payments/GTV) and in a deliberately shrinking professional-services line that carries a **-158% gross margin** — i.e., the company loses money on it on purpose and is winding it down ([10-Q, 2026-09-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001638826/000163882626000094/ttan-20260731.htm)).
3. **The price now reflects a much worse business than the one in the filings.** At ~95.4M shares and $56.285, market cap is ~$5.37B against implied FY27 revenue of ~$1.14B — **4.70x sales** for a company still growing ~18% for the full year with positive free cash flow (~$50.5M in the quarter, ~$56.4M six-month operating cash flow).

So: **reduced damage-arriving**, not "nothing happened" and not "the business is failing."

## What the market believes

The market believes growth is stepping down permanently and that management just told you so. Its case is not stupid:

- Guided next-quarter growth is **+14.8%**, versus **+25.0%** in the same quarter a year ago. That is roughly 1,000 basis points of deceleration in four quarters, and the comparison was *not* harder — last year's Q3 actually grew faster than last year's Q2.
- The implied fourth quarter (~$293.9M, +2.8% sequential, ~+13.9% year over year) means the full-year guide **back-loads no recovery at all**. Nobody is being asked to wait for a reacceleration; there isn't one in the numbers.
- Gross transaction volume grew +17% — slower than the software — and management did not explain the gap.
- The company simultaneously announced a strategic pivot toward its AI product ("Max") over broader trade expansion, on the same day as a chief revenue officer change. The market read that as "the old growth engine is being replaced," which is a re-rating event, not a quarter event.
- Retention is disclosed as a **band** — "greater than 110%" — not a number. A move from 118% to 110.5% would look identical in that disclosure ([8-K EX-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001638826/000163882626000093/ttan-ex99_1.htm)).

## Why we might have edge

- **We decomposed the guide correctly and the loud bear case did not.** The headline bear argument compares a *conservative forecast* to a *reported result*, which manufactures a fake decline equal to the company's habitual beat. Guide-to-guide is $285M → $286M, **+0.35%**. Apply the demonstrated beat (+2.74% last quarter, +3.7% on the year-ago Q1 guide) and next quarter lands near **$293.8M, +0.35% sequentially** — a modest seasonal break versus the prior year's +2.9%, not a collapse.
- **We separated the two things inside the "non-subscription" bucket.** Strip out professional services (shrinking on purpose, negative margin) and the implied usage line is about **-9.0% sequentially**, or **-7.4%** after adjusting for the one fewer business day management named. That is a genuine dent. It is not the -16% to -22% the bear decomposition produced.
- **We ran the bear's own falsification test.** The bear said "if cRPO grows ≥22%, my backlog argument collapses." Using the company's disclosed RPO and its next-twelve-month percentage, cRPO is ~$263.7M versus ~$214.2M, **+23.1%**. The test was run and it failed for the bear. (Caveat below — the percentages are rounded, and that matters.)
- **We are not carrying the position.** Book is flat, no orders. We can choose our entry rather than defend one.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetrically, here is the honest list of reasons this is *not* edge:

- **4.70x sales is not obviously cheap for a ~14% exit-rate grower.** The "cheap" frame is priced off a 21% grower. If the run-rate is 14%, 4.7x is roughly fair, not a gift. This is the single strongest argument against us and we do not have a rebuttal beyond "the forward book says 23%."
- **Duration is quietly extending.** The share of the backlog due within twelve months slid **53% → 52% → 50%** across three consecutive quarters. Longer contracts bought with price concessions look like backlog strength and aren't. It's a ~300 basis point effect, not a 1,570 basis point illusion — but it is real and it moves the same direction three quarters running.
- **Dilution is genuine.** Diluted shares rose 1.50% in a single quarter. Even discounting the post-IPO normalization distortion, the share count is a headwind nobody is being paid for.
- **The AI-pivot story is management-declared, not inferred.** That makes it the most credible bear leg on the table, and it is precisely the kind of thing that takes a multiple down and keeps it down for three or four quarters.
- **The tape has not absorbed anything.** Pinned 3.9% off the low with a -52.9% drawdown is not a stabilized dislocation. Neither of our benches could pull a single price bar to measure absorption; we are eyeballing this.
- **We cannot see the street's number.** No consensus pull succeeded on either bench. "The market overreacted" is being asserted against an anchor we cannot see.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative #1 — The usage line is the whole story and it keeps going (most likely).** The implied ~-7.4% sequential usage decline, adjusted for calendar, is not phasing; it is contractors doing less volume, or a lower take on that volume. It compounds for two more quarters, GTV growth falls under 12%, and the software line follows because usage and seats are correlated at the same customer. *Monitorable:* yes — usage revenue and GTV disclosed at the 2026-12-03 print; Austin and Bernalillo County trade-permit counts are readable monthly and independently of the company. **Both permit checks are currently unrun. That is our own gap, not the company's.**

**Failure narrative #2 — The AI pivot is a repositioning that eats two years.** Management deprioritizes trade expansion for the Max product, sales leadership turns over, the new motion takes four to six quarters to produce bookings, and the multiple compresses to 3.5x while we wait. *Monitorable:* partly — cRPO and net dollar retention at each print, plus sales-leadership Form 4s and 8-K personnel items. But if the market simply decides to pay less for the same numbers, no tripwire fires.

**Failure narrative #3 — THE ONE WITH NO TRIPWIRE: the payment processor gets repriced.** The 10-Q carries a third-party payment-processor concentration disclosure sitting directly under the usage line. If that processor renegotiates the economics — a take-rate split change, a term change, a migration — revenue on that line falls and **we will learn about it only when it prints.** There is no filing, no permit series, no satellite, no app store, no alt-data vendor that observes a private bilateral contract renegotiation. **Unknown-unknown class: PRIVATE BILATERAL COUNTERPARTY-TERMS RISK — economics governed by a confidential commercial contract between two private counterparties, with no public observable and no leading indicator, disclosed only ex post through the revenue line it damages.** We are naming it rather than pretending a sensor covers it. A second class sits alongside it: **BANDED-METRIC MASKING** — retention is disclosed as ">110%," so deterioration from 118% to 111% is invisible by construction, and no monitor can see inside the band.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** Our own size is not the problem. At an assumed $250M book, the full 0.60% authorization is ~$1.5M, roughly 26,600 shares — against an estimated 2.5–4.0M share average daily volume, that is **0.7% to 1.1% of a day**. We move nothing and we can exit in an hour. (Volume is an estimate; neither bench pulled a bar. It is item 5 in the unverified list.) The real structural risks are two:

- **Predicted flow against us.** Four Rule 144 notices in eleven days plus three Form 4s in August 2026 is continuous insider supply. We do not know the share counts or whether they are pre-set 10b5-1 sales — the "they knew something" version is unverified and we are not asserting it. What we *are* asserting is that there is a seller in the tape and we would be adding into it.
- **Crowded gates.** Our four go/no-go conditions at the December print (cRPO, GTV, usage sequential, retention band) are the same four every analyst will publish. If they fail, everyone exits through the same door at the same second and our "exit, don't add" rule executes 15% below where we decided to use it. Our staging rule also makes us a **momentum buyer by construction** — we only add on confirmation, which means we pay up after the news is out and we are structurally guaranteed to have our smallest size at the low.
- **Doctrine interaction.** We are leaning on the "guide-versus-actual artifact" pattern (bear decompositions that treat a conservative forecast as a real sequential decline). That doctrine is correct in general and is exactly the doctrine that will talk us out of a genuine demand break the one time it is genuine. It cuts both ways and we should expect it to cost us here at some point.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___** — the usage/GTV line kept deteriorating for reasons governed by a private processor contract and by contractor demand we never instrumented, while the market re-rated a ~14% grower down toward 3.5x sales and our confirmation-only staging rule left us buying strength that reversed.

**Tax overlay.** The loss branch of this table (scenarios 3 and 4, 40% of the mass) resolves at the December 3 print. In a harvest year that loss must land in **tax year 2026**. Stock entered now satisfies this — we can realize before December 31. If the trade is later expressed in options, **only the 2026-12-18 expiry qualifies**: it covers the December 3 catalyst *and* the loss branch settles inside 2026. **Any January or February 2027 expiry defers the loss into the following year and is excluded during a harvest year**, notwithstanding that the later expiry buys more time for scenario 1 — the edge here (+1.5% EV) comes nowhere close to numerically dominating the deferral cost, so the exception does not apply.
