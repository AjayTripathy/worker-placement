# PITCH — JEN.BR — PROPOSED VERDICT: WATCH, NO POSITION — re-enter only at ≤10× rolling trailing earnings (~€60 today) or on a Q3 order-intake recovery Nov 4 (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

## What we believe

Jensen-Group (JEN.BR), the family-controlled Belgian maker of industrial laundry equipment, just printed a second quarter that looks strong on the surface (revenue +19.4%) but is deteriorating underneath. Operating profit fell 7.1% versus the same quarter last year, the operating margin dropped from 13.9% to 10.8%, and earnings per share fell 27%. More importantly, new orders in Q2 came in 8.8% below last year — the company booked only €110.5m of new orders against €161.9m of revenue shipped — and management itself said it is "cautious for the second half seen the increasing market uncertainty and lower order intake in Q2 2026." An associate company in the same end market (Inax/Tolon) saw its profit contribution fall 64%, which independently corroborates that the cycle is turning, not just one company stumbling.

We believe the business itself is a quality, conservatively run compounder — the objection is cyclical and price-based, not a fraud or balance-sheet case. Nothing in the filings was found to be misleading; the company prints its own bad quarter plainly in its half-year release ([H1 2026 press release, p.1–2](https://www.jensen-group.com/fileadmin/Documents/Investor_Relations/Press_Releases/20260806_JEGR-PressRelease_H1_2026_ENG.pdf)).

## What the market believes

The stock has already repriced: €72.2 versus a 52-week high of €89.8 (down ~19.6% from the high, though still ~29% above the 52-week low of €56). The post-earnings drop of roughly 15% came on about 17× normal daily volume — that is the market reading the same tables we read, not a panic in a thinly traded name. At €72.2 the company trades at roughly 6.7× enterprise value to trailing operating cash profit (EBITDA) and about 12× trailing earnings per share of ~€6.01. Both benches concluded the current price is approximately fair: our own corrected mid-case fair value lands near €70 against a €72.2 tape, with the direction of earnings pointing down. In short: the market believes what we believe, and has mostly paid for it already.

## Why we might have edge

- **The headline order collapse is overstated, which sets up a contrarian opportunity later.** The scary "orders at only 68% of revenue" ratio is partly an artifact: revenue was unusually high this quarter because a record backlog was being converted. Measured against last year's revenue base, the same order number is 82%, and the real decline in orders is −8.8%. If the market is anchored on the 0.68 headline, a decent Q3 order number (≥€135m at the Nov 4 update) could reprice the stock as a quality compounder again — that is our defined re-entry trigger.
- **We understand the buyback better than the headline.** The 963,140-share buyback authorization sounds like ~10% of the company, but the courts established it was authorized on May 18, 2026 — the same board day as a related-party acquisition (Inwatec) that is "partly financed with JENSEN-GROUP shares." Historical execution is only about 1.2% of shares per year, and the existing treasury stock already covers the acquisition's share component 2–3 times over. We will not be fooled in either direction: we watch actual weekly buyback disclosures, not the authorization.
- **A possible pull-forward pattern nobody has priced.** Q1 orders were a record (+22.7%) and Q2 broke down, with trade tariffs named in the company's own risk factors. If Q1 demand was borrowed from Q2 (customers ordering early ahead of tariffs), the underlying trend may be less bad than the sequence suggests — Nov 4 resolves this.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: it largely is, and that is why the proposed verdict is no position. Every load-bearing bear fact comes from the company's own regulated press releases, published August 6, and the stock fell 15% on heavy volume the same day. The prosecution bench's own conclusion was that "nothing here is mispriced — the market is reading the same tables." Trailing multiples (~6.7× EV/EBITDA, ~12× earnings) are computed on peak-cycle earnings that the order book says are rolling over, so the stock is cheaper-looking than it is. Fair value ≈ €70 versus price €72.2 means there is no margin of safety and no identified edge at today's price — only a plan for the prices and dates at which edge would appear.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

The courts say **no entry today**. These are the reopen bands, taken from the benches' own math:

- **Price reopen gate: ≤ ~€60, defined as 10× rolling trailing-four-quarter EPS — not a static level.** Today that's 10 × €6.01 ≈ €60; the level must be recomputed at every quarterly print because the earnings base is falling (if Q3 EPS declines, the gate moves down with it). A touch of the gate triggers a fresh court review, **never** an automatic buy (falling-knife discipline).
- **Fundamental reopen gate: Q3 order intake ≥ €135m at the November 4, 2026 trading update.** That would show Q2's order weakness was a one-quarter dip (or tariff pull-forward payback), and flips the file to a quality-compounder re-entry case at the then-current price.
- **Float-shrink reopen gate: cumulative buyback execution > 150,000 shares net of any shares issued for the Inwatec acquisition, evidenced in the weekly buyback disclosures between September 1 and year-end.** Execution, not authorization, is the signal; note share-financed acquisition payments *add* shares back.
- **Sizing:** none pre-gates. Any position after a gate fires requires (a) a live Interactive Brokers two-sided quote (currently unobtainable — permission denied), and (b) a fresh court pass. No pre-earnings position: November 4 is ~59 trading sessions out, and the order-trend direction is management-confirmed negative.
- **Exits/trims:** not applicable (no position).
- **Catalyst dates:** buyback program starts **September 1, 2026**; Q3 trading update **November 4, 2026 (evening, company-confirmed)**; Inwatec purchase-price allocation in the **2026 annual report (~spring 2027, date not yet announced)**.
