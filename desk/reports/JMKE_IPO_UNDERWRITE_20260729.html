Jersey Mike's (JMKE): a good sandwich business, offered at the wrong price, through an adverse structure

S-1/A of 2026-07-20 (CIK 2127043); 43.5M Class A at $21–25, NYSE, pricing ~July 30. Mode A read the filing forensically; Mode B built the outside view from third-party data first. They converged — and where they differed, the filing was worse than the press.

DECLINE at range ($21–25). At the $23 midpoint: fully-exchanged equity $7.31B, EV ≈ $9.0B = ~26.5× FY25 adjusted EBITDA, ~133× GAAP net income — for same-store sales that have decelerated every year and every recent quarter (8.4% FY23 → 2.0% H1-26, price/mix-driven) into a QSR category with traffic down 3%. Deserved band 18–22× → $16–19/share generous, $14–16 strict. The ask is 25–40% above it. WAIT band $15–18; realistic paths there are the ~Jan-2027 lockup or the first sub-2% comp print. A first-day pop (13.7% float, beloved brand — the CAVA pattern) would not be disconfirmation.

The structure: every choice monetizes the window

  • SEV 8The recap-to-proceeds circle, in the filing's own words. Five months before this IPO (Feb-9-2026), the securitization issued $760M of new notes whose proceeds funded, among other things, "a dividend to our Sponsor" (amount not disclosed — a named residual). The IPO's entire ~$295M of net primary proceeds repays a portion of those same notes. The sponsor extracted cash via debt in February; the public repays it in July.
  • SEV 8The tax asset leaves with the sellers — at 90%. The tax receivable agreement pays pre-IPO owners 90% of realized tax benefits (85% is the customary figure); the filing's own early-termination estimate is $1,447M — 4.9× everything the company itself raises in this offering.
  • SEV 7The equity is a levered stub. $2.1B of whole-business securitization debt (issuer leverage tested >5.0× in December, triggering a mandatory paydown); ~5.3× gross pro-forma; the notes' anticipated repayment starts 2029 — a refi wall at whatever rates then prevail.
  • SEV 768.3% of the deal is secondary (Blackstone + ADIA, ~$742M at mid) and the greenshoe is 100% secondary. Post-IPO the sponsor keeps 76.5% of votes under the controlled-company exemption, and a Blackstone affiliate is a declared FINRA conflicts-of-interest underwriter in its own deal.
  • SEV 5Adjusted-EBITDA asymmetry. The $339M metric adds back the cash cost of buying out the legacy Area Director layer ($44M FY25, $28M in Q1-26 alone — a quarter that was a GAAP loss) while keeping the resulting savings, plus founder-era discretionary and ad-fund-timing items.
  • SEV 3–4Definitional grooming, disclosed but flattering: comps and AUV exclude stores younger than 425 days (~14 months — longer than the customary ~12–13); the cover's "50% cumulative 2020–25 same-store sales growth" is a sum of six annual prints; the "~42% cash-on-cash" uses franchisee-self-reported build costs excluding pre-opening.

The quarter-by-quarter comp series is the load-bearing fact: FY25 3.2% → Q2-26 2.3% vs 3.6% prior year; H1-26 2.0% vs 4.2%. Systemwide sales growth (+10–13%) is carried by unit count (8.1–8.5%, itself down from 12%), and the filing concedes the comp is substantially price/mix. Roadshow language ("accelerating transaction growth through the quarter") emphasizes intra-quarter momentum while the year-over-year series halves. At ~27×, the first negative comp print is a −30–40% event — the Krispy Kreme / Portillo's path, both franchise-story IPOs now 70–80% below offer.

What the disconfirming pass could NOT kill

CLEAN The franchise system is genuinely healthy, and the bear case dies at the store level: AUV $1.37M (~3× Subway), store-level EBITDA ~16% ≈ $224k on a ~$515k build — roughly 40% cash-on-cash even haircutting the self-reported build cost. The 1,600-store domestic pipeline is 90% existing franchisees — the people with the best information are re-upping. Saturation is more nuanced than the category chart suggests: Jersey Mike's is capturing share from Subway's structural retreat (−8,345 US units since 2016, with 18.7k remaining at ~$500k AUV — a large donor pool). Twenty consecutive years of positive comps; top-tier syndicate; Deloitte; one-vote-per-share both classes. This is why the verdict is WAIT with a band, not AVOID: the business deserves ownership at a franchise multiple, not at a scarcity multiple.
