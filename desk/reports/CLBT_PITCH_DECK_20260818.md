# PITCH — CLBT — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no order, size zero; both benches land on reject-and-wait, and the original bull case died on its own two load-bearing numbers.

---

## What we believe

Cellebrite sells digital-forensics software to police forces, prosecutors and government agencies — the tools that pull evidence off a seized phone and organise it into a case file. Almost all of the money is recurring subscription revenue, which the company reports as ARR (annual recurring revenue).

On 13 August 2026 the stock fell 29% in a day. The company cut its forecast for how fast that recurring revenue would grow this year — from roughly 18–19% down to 14–16% — and on the same day the CEO left. The shares now sit at about $10.51, which is 48% below the 52-week high of $19.98 and only 9% above the 52-week low of $9.58.

Our original argument was that the market had wildly overreacted: a 29% price move for what looked like a small trim to a single year's forecast. We went and checked that argument against the company's own published numbers, and **we no longer believe it.** What we believe now, stated plainly:

- The business is decelerating for real, but by about **2 percentage points**, not the 6 points the tape appeared to price. The apparent 6-point break is an artefact of an acquisition (Corellium, closed 1 December 2025) sitting inside last year's reported growth rate. Stripping it out, growth goes from 17% to ~15%, not 21% to 15%.
- Customers are still expanding, but less than they used to. Net retention — how much existing customers grow their spend — is 117%, down from 120% a year ago.
- Cash generation has deteriorated sharply in the first half of this year, and nobody has yet shown whether that is a timing problem or a structural one.
- There is a very large, undated block of stock overhanging the shares: Sun Corporation, a listed Japanese company, owns about 47% of Cellebrite (~119 million shares, ~$1.25bn at today's price), and an activist investor is publicly pressing Sun to monetise it.

Net: this is a decent, cash-generative software business that got cheaper for mostly real reasons, with a supply problem attached. It is not a dislocation we can size today.

## What the market believes

The market believes growth stepped down permanently and re-priced the company's *future* years accordingly — not just this year. That is a defensible read even after our corrections, because the forecast cut was to the recurring-revenue line, which leads reported revenue by roughly a year.

Where consensus sits: analysts were at roughly $568M of 2026 revenue before the print; the company guided $555–561M; consensus has settled around $558M. **Our own number is within 1% of theirs.** We do not have a different forecast. Whatever case exists here is an argument about what multiple the business deserves, not about what it will earn.

At $10.51 the market is paying an enterprise value of about $2.26bn (252.8M diluted shares, less $141.3M cash and $257.7M marketable securities) — roughly 4.05x this year's revenue and 14.5x adjusted EBITDA. That is not an expensive multiple for a 15%-growing subscription business with net cash. It is also not obviously wrong.

## Why we might have edge

Three narrow places, honestly stated:

1. **The acquisition-contamination correction.** The company disclosed once, in a single line of its FY25 results release, that $16.1M of its year-end ARR was purchased with Corellium, and that organic growth was 17% ([PR Newswire, FY25 results](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cellebrite-announces-record-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-302684465.html)). Every reported figure since then blends the acquisition in. Anyone comparing this year's organic guide against last year's blended actual — which is the natural, lazy comparison, and the one *we* made first — overstates the deceleration by about 4 points. If the sell-side made the same mistake, the market has priced a break that is a third as large as it looks. We cannot confirm the sell-side made it.
2. **Nobody has published a 2027 number.** The de-rating is logically about 2027, and no bench, including ours, produced a street 2027 estimate to test against. The gap we would need to find lives in a year nobody has tabled.
3. **The Sun block cuts both ways.** A 47% control holder under activist pressure to monetise is normally a reason to stay away. It is also the single most likely source of a discrete, datable re-rating event — a sale, a merger, a marketed placement — and those events resolve at a known price.

## Why it might be priced in

- **We agree with consensus on this year's revenue to within 1%.** There is no forecast edge.
- **The deceleration is real even after our correction.** Organic ARR growth was ~17% entering the second half against a 14–16% full-year exit guide — a further organic slowdown, not a floor.
- **The forecast requires the second half to accelerate.** Second-quarter revenue grew 16%; the full-year guide of 17–18% implies roughly 19% in the second half, while recurring revenue decelerates. That is a demanding shape for a CEO on day one. (Flagged as plausible, not proven — one input was derived rather than sourced.)
- **Cash generation is down a third.** First-half free cash flow was $31.4M against $47.5M a year ago (−34%); operating cash flow $37.5M against $53.5M (−30%). The attractive "28% cash margin" we originally cited is a trailing-twelve-month figure carried by one strong quarter.
- **Stock compensation is growing twice as fast as revenue.** ~$59–61M annualised versus $44.9M last year, against 16–17% revenue growth — and the "raised" EBITDA guidance excludes it. The $4M EBITDA raise is a quarter the size of the compensation increase it leaves out.
- **The chart says distribution, not basing.** 48% off the high but only 9% off the low, five days after the event, with no bounce.
- **This looks exactly like a cohort we have already graded 0-for-7:** buying a big earnings gap-down where the headline *was* the news.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry. There is no order, no starter, and no size.** What follows are the levels and conditions at which the name reopens for a fresh court — not orders.

**Current sizing: 0.0% of book. Authorised sizing on any reopen: 0.0% until re-courted.** The Sun block alone (~119M shares, ~$1.25bn) is a sizing veto independent of the fundamental argument: we do not have this stock's average daily volume verified, so we cannot state how many days of supply that block represents, and until we can, any position we build is a position we may not be able to exit alongside a control-block placement.

**Reference math (both benches', at $10.51):** EV $2.258bn = 252.8M diluted shares × $10.51 less $399.0M of cash and securities. FY26E revenue $558M (guide midpoint). Net cash is $1.58/share.

**Price levels, derived from that math:**

| **$9.25 – $9.67** | 8–12% discount to $10.51 — the customary clearing range for a marketed block | The band a Sun secondary would likely price in. **This is the preferred re-entry zone**, because it converts the overhang from an unknown into a completed, dated event with a known clearing price |

**Reopen triggers (any one of these forces a new court; none of them alone is a buy):**

1. **A Sun/Cellebrite structural resolution** — a merger, a sale, or a marketed secondary with a published clearing price. Converts the overhang into a dated event.
2. **Q3'26 organic net-new ARR above $26M with net retention ≥118%.** ($26M is roughly 24% above last year's organic Q3 addition of $20.9M.) This is the number that decides the name, and it is fully derivable from the release on the day.
3. **Net retention back at ≥120%** — re-crossing the year-ago level rather than drifting in the 115–117% band.
4. **Second-half free cash flow restoring trailing FCF above $160M with second-half conversion ≥30%** — proof the first-half collapse was collections timing, not structure.
5. **A disclosed federal or defense contract quantifying the delayed large deals as booked.** Management's "no deals are lost" is a transcript claim with no counterpart in any filed document.
6. **A live IBKR print.** Hard prerequisite. No order stages without it, at any price.

**Catalyst dates:**

- **~2026-10-21 to 2026-10-28** — expected date-announcing press release. The company's practice is to name the quarter date 2–3 weeks in advance. **Unconfirmed.**
- **~2026-11-11 (data-feed derived) to 2026-11-18 (broker calendar)** — Q3'26 results. **Unconfirmed by any company release.** Roughly 60 trading days out, so there is no print-proximity urgency to this decision either way.
- **~2027-02-13** — Hogan's six-month advisory period ends (derived from the 6-K's stated six months from 13 August 2026). Not a trading catalyst; a governance marker.
- **Undated** — any Sun Corporation or ValueAct action. This is the one that actually moves the name and it has no calendar.

## What remains unverified

- **The live price.** Everything above rests on a web quote of $10.51 (2026-08-18). No broker print was obtainable by either bench; the tool permission was denied.
- **The cause of the cash-flow decline.** Neither bench decomposed the −34% first-half free cash flow into collections timing versus structural margin. This is the difference between a wait and a buy, and it was not done.
- **The street's 2027 numbers.** The de-rating is about 2027. No consensus 2027 revenue or ARR figure was produced by anyone. The re-rating case is therefore untested, not disproven.
- **Defense and intelligence ARR.** Claimed to be growing 25%, with no dollar base disclosed anywhere. It could be 5% of the company or 25%; we cannot tell, and the answer changes the multiple.
- **Who is selling.** Six Form 144 filings in six weeks with no confirmed filer identity.
- **Sun's current disclosed stake.** The ~47% figure is reported, and the activist filing is confirmed; the current, filed stake and any lock-up terms are not.
- **Whether the second-half revenue guide really implies ~19% growth.** One input in that derivation was calculated rather than sourced.
- **Whether the sell-side made the same Corellium error we did.** The whole "why we might have edge" case turns on this, and it is an assumption.
- **The F-3 shelf's current effectiveness.**
- **Whether Cellebrite's average daily volume can absorb any position we might take alongside a 119M-share control block.** Not measured.

One process note, stated plainly because it bears on how much weight this deck deserves: the original bull case on this name was wrong on both of its load-bearing facts, the red bench that killed it was wrong on the size of the break it claimed, and neither bench got a live price. The conclusion — no position — is the same one all three roads lead to, which is the only reason to have confidence in it.
