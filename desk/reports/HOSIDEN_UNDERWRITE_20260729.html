The thesis in plain language

Hosiden assembles the Nintendo Switch 2. FY26 (ended March) was the launch year: sales +81% to ¥448B, record net profit ¥16.2B, and the balance sheet is a vault — ¥78.4B of cash against ¥10.2B of (zero-coupon) debt. Management returns cash for real: a 30% payout policy, plus a ¥4.2B buyback fully executed and cancelled. The market pays 8.6× peak earnings because it sees a console cycle at its top.

What the courts established that the multiple misses: the trough is observed, not modeled. In FY24–25, with the old Switch dying and the amusement segment at ¥125–145B, Hosiden still earned ~¥13B of operating profit — mobile, automotive, and components are a real floor (with the honest caveat, found by the red team in the company's own words, that those years were partly rescued by the weak yen: FX-adjusted trough EPS is ~¥150–170). And the feared assembly-share loss to Sharp didn't happen: +¥209B of amusement growth in the launch year is major-assembler scale.

The risk is not the multiple — it's the single name on the purchase orders. The customer note, verified verbatim: Nintendo is 78.0% of total revenue (57.4% even in the trough year), on orders at will, with no backlog disclosure. Everything else is commentary.

What the market believes vs what we believe

The position
Market believesPE 9 on a console-launch year is a value trap: Nintendo itself guides year-2 hardware −17%, so the assembler's earnings just peaked; a company that is 78% one customer deserves a structural discount; and the cash pile is Japanese cash — it sits on the balance sheet forever and earns no credit in the price.
We believeEach leg is half right, and the halves are priced twice. The cycle is real — but the trough is observed (~¥150–170 FX-adjusted EPS with the console segment at bottom), so mid-cycle is a step down, not a cliff. The concentration is real — but it survived the share-loss test this cycle at major-assembler scale. And the cash-never-returns premise is now factually wrong: a completed-and-cancelled buyback plus a 30% payout policy is behavior change, ~6.5% trailing yield. At the band you pay ~5× ex-cash for the risk the market is right about, and get the behavior change for free.
Who's mispricing, and whyThe market prices the cycle correctly and the capital-return regime change slowly — small-cap Japan re-rates lag behavior changes by design (no coverage, no index bid), and this one is only two quarters old. A second, mechanical reason price discovery is muted: the ¥10B convertible strikes at ¥2,682.7, so convert-arb supply caps rallies above it — which suppresses the price and is why our band sits below it.
Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrongIf the Japan-discount on cash persists (we credit it only 50–70% in the FV band for exactly this reason) and Nintendo cuts builds toward its own −17%, then PE 9 was a fair price for a shrinking, concentrated assembler and the trade earns the dividend and little else. The 78% concentration cannot be analyzed away — only the kill triggers manage it, and a Nintendo order shock would gap through any limit.
Mechanism falsifierThe ~Aug-7 Q1 print's reconciliation: Hosiden's −5.9% amusement guide vs Nintendo's −17% unit guide. Evidence of share/content gains (or the ~20M build-order nuance proving real) sustains the thesis; silence means the guide is optimism and the pack stands down.
Edge typeRISK_PREMIUM at the band with a capital-return re-rate option attached. Not an edge claim at spot — spot is fair; the band is where the concentration risk is over-paid-for.

The Q1 question (prints ~Aug-7): one reconciliation

Nintendo guides year-2 hardware at 16.5M units, −17%. Hosiden guides its amusement segment −5.9%. Holding that gap requires assembly-share or content gains the filings don't evidence — the print must show them, or the guide is optimism. One favorable, unverified nuance on the watch list: supply-chain reporting puts Nintendo's production orders near 20M against the 16.5M sell-through guide, and an assembler is paid on builds. The reaction driver is this reconciliation plus any FX-assumption revision — never the translation beat (the ¥155/USD guide assumption vs ~¥163 spot is a built-in cushion that means nothing for volume).
