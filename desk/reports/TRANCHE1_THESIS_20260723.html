The Rotation Sleeve, Tranche 1: seven positions from four courts

July 23, 2026 · IBKR sleeve · nine order envelopes staged (ids 100–108), ~$71k at limits, all below market · nothing submitted without a human tap

The household's real fragility, measured at 99% coverage on a five-factor model, is not a growth tilt — it is a lot of market exposure carrying too little of the market's most profitable, durable businesses. This tranche buys that missing quality directly, in three places a US-concentrated portfolio doesn't reach: cash-rich small Japanese value, specialty insurance underwriting, and low-breakeven energy carry. Every name passed two examinations — its claims verified against primary filings, then an independent attempt to kill it — and every entry rests below the market price. The refusals below are as much the product as the purchases.

What the tranche does

Three blocks, three jobs. Japan value (~$27k) buys businesses trading near or below the value of their net cash — exposure that moves on Tokyo's reform cycle, not America's AI cycle. Specialty insurance (~$24k) buys underwriting profit and float income — businesses that get bid in a flight to quality and earn more as rates stay high. Energy carry (~$20k) buys the destination of a growth-to-real-assets rotation, restricted to names that keep paying at $55 oil. On the engine's stress tests, the completed construction roughly halves the sleeve's per-dollar loss in a growth→value rotation and turns the software→energy rotation positive; this tranche is the first ~$71k of that build, inside a $700k interim cap that keeps margin far from any forced-sale zone before the October capital arrives.

Japan — cash-rich value with the traps re-tried

Okumura Engineering (6229) Deploy — lead

1,200 sh @ ¥1,560 limit (−2.1% vs last) · ~$11.5k · valve & fluid-control maker (LNG, ships, semis)

The stock fell 32% from its March peak, and the court was sent to find the corpse — an impairment, a lost customer, a downward revision. It found the opposite: two upward revisions into the peak, record operating profit (+65%), and then a deliberately conservative first-time guide for next year (−18%) issued the exact week the stock de-rated. This is a sell-the-news round trip of a momentum spike, not a broken business. It trades at 0.65× book with net cash equal to 38% of its market value, the family stake is stabilizing, and the structure is clean for a US taxable holder.

Risk: the −18% guide turns out to be honest demand weakness, not sandbagging. Tripwire: the August 14 first-quarter report — tracking the guided path or better earns the add; a genuine downward revision halts it.

Nakayama Steel (5408) Deploy — smaller

2,100 sh @ ¥628 limit (−2.3%) · ~$8.1k · the patient ballast leg, most liquid of the three

A genuine Graham bargain — the market price is below the company's current assets minus all liabilities, at roughly 0.36× book — but the court's decisive finding is why it's sized down: the ¥14.7B of net cash is already spoken for, funding a ¥105.5B electric-furnace joint venture that doesn't operate until about 2030, while next year's income is guided down 58%. The court also corrected our own file: a recorded "dividend cut ¥40→¥14" never happened — the dividend went ¥13→¥14 and is guided to ¥20, rising. Cheap, safe, liquid, and probably dead money for a while: bought as asset-protected ballast, not a catalyst.

Risk: the furnace build swallows the cash cushion via new debt while the earnings trough deepens. Tripwire: a buyback authorization or treasury-share cancellation upgrades it; new debt for the venture downgrades it.

Kuwazawa Holdings (8104) Deploy — smaller, pullback only

1,900 sh @ ¥672 limit (−2.7%) · ~$7.8k · Hokkaido building-materials distributor

The most interesting verdict in the batch: this name was previously killed in our files as a "working-capital illusion" — cash that looked like surplus but was really suppliers' money. The court re-tried it against the audited results and overturned the kill: receivables and payables offset almost exactly, borrowings are trivial, so the ¥7.5B of net cash — 65% of the market value — is genuine surplus. And it is finally moving: the dividend has gone ¥18→¥26→¥30 (a 4.4% yield) with buybacks and a +20% profit guide. The honest caveat is timing — the stock sits at a 52-week high after a 34% run on a thin tape, so the limit sits below market on purpose: this position is only taken on a pullback.

Risk: buying a closing discount late; a take-under offer below book from a strategic holder. Tripwire: a major-holder sale lifts the overhang (add); any management-buyout approach forces a tender/fight decision.

Insurance — underwriting quality into a softening cycle

Kinsale Capital (KNSL) Deploy

37 sh @ $322 limit (−1.1%) · ~$11.9k · pure excess-&-surplus-lines underwriter

Down 36% from its high, and the court's job was to decide whether that is a franchise crack or a contained soft patch. The evidence says contained: the decline is concentrated in one division — commercial property, where standard carriers re-entered and Kinsale is walking away from mispriced business — while the other 71% of the book keeps growing with pricing intact. Strip the property division and premiums still grew 6% last quarter. Crucially, the underwriting is profitable this accident year (~80 combined ratio before any reserve help), returns on equity run near 29%, and book value grew 33%. The elite franchise is now priced at roughly fair — which is exactly when it becomes buyable.

Risk: the soft market spreads from property into casualty, the core book. Tripwire: premium growth outside the property division turning negative across the next two quarterly filings.

W.R. Berkley (WRB) Deploy

165 sh @ $71.50 limit (−1.2%) · ~$11.8k · diversified specialty & commercial lines

The strongest investment-income engine in the group — roughly 80% of net income now comes from the float portfolio, and new money still reinvests above the book yield, which is precisely the "paid more as rates stay high" exposure this sleeve wants. It passes the test that killed a peer trade last quarter: it trades at only a ~10% premium to its own five-year valuation history (the peer we faded carried +23%), and the current multiple only requires ~14–15% returns from a company delivering ~19.7%. The court did not hide the blemish: recent-year casualty reserves developed modestly adverse ($44M last year, social-inflation driven) — disclosed honestly, graded as the real risk, and sized against.

Risk: the scissors — adverse reserve development accelerating while pricing decelerates below claims inflation. Tripwire: insurance-segment adverse development above $75M in any quarter (or $150M cumulative this year), or the accident-year loss ratio pushing past ~64%.

Energy — carry that survives $55 oil

ExxonMobil (XOM) Deploy — anchor

64 sh @ $155 limit · ~$9.9k · the quality anchor of the block

The court graded the whole complex on one question — who keeps paying if oil falls to $55? — because after a year in which the sector re-rated 28–75%, nothing is cheap and the only durable edge is survivorship. Exxon wins it outright: the lowest breakeven of the majors (~$40 Brent, trending toward $35), net debt at just 13% of capital, a 43-year unbroken dividend-growth streak, and positive free cash flow through the cycle trough. Bought as fairly-priced carry with the price exposure attached — not as a bargain, and sized accordingly.

Risk: paying +31% year-to-date pricing; a recession de-rates the multiple even while the dividend holds. Tripwire: net debt above ~20% of capital, or a "market conditions" suspension of the buyback — either means the cycle turned.

Suncor (SU) Deploy — ladder

52 @ $64 / 53 @ $62.50 / 54 @ $61, all limits into weakness · ~$9.8k total

The oil-sands pick on the same survivorship test: breakeven falling from US$42 toward $38 (lowest of its peers), a clean balance sheet, and roughly 9% of the market value returned to holders annually. What earns it the second slot over purer plays is structural: its integrated refining arm profits from the crack spread when crude weakens — a built-in shock absorber for exactly the drawdown this sleeve is constructed against. The two classic killers of the oil-sands trade were checked and have genuinely receded: the heavy-oil discount is the tightest in years post-pipeline, and the emissions-cap threat is being dismantled.

Risk: record refining margins mean-revert in a demand-soft recession, giving back the bonus. Tripwire: buyback guidance cut below ~CAD 3.5B, or the breakeven stalling above $42 at the next investor update.

What would change our mind

Each position carries its own pre-registered exit or add condition, listed above — none requires judgment in the moment. At the sleeve level: a genuine break in the "quality gets bid" premise (profitable underwriters de-rating faster than the market in a selloff), a Japan governance-reform reversal, or oil-complex balance sheets releveraging into buybacks would each send the relevant block back to court. The construction itself is falsifiable: the engine's stress numbers are recomputed as fills land, and if the growth→value loss doesn't shrink as designed, the allocation — not the narrative — gets revised.
