# PITCH — CIFR — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Do not buy at today's price. Both benches land on the same answer for the same reason: even if everything the company promises comes true, on time, fully funded, the equity is worth roughly $14.5/share against a tape of $17.3–18.5. This is a price rejection with a dated re-opening, not a permanent kill.

---

## What we believe

Cipher Digital (CIK 1819989) is a bitcoin miner converting its Texas power sites into leased high-performance computing shells. It has signed a genuinely large book of business: about 907 MW operating or contracted, anchored by two Amazon leases (Black Pearl, ~300 MW / 15 years, and Stingray) and one lease to Fluidstack, a private AI cloud company, partially backstopped by Google. The company says this book will throw off about **$793 million of average annual net operating income** over the base lease terms and about **$11.4 billion of contracted revenue**. Delivery is running ahead of schedule — both benches accepted that, and neither tried to knock it down.

What we believe, after the court, is narrower and less exciting than that story:

1. **The build is real and the money is in the bank.** Roughly $831.8M of unrestricted cash and $3.728B of restricted cash sit against $4.543B of project-level notes (Cipher Compute $1.733B at 7.125%, Stingray $810M at 6.0%, Black Pearl $2.0B at 6.125%). Interest expense of $66.7M in the quarter is offset by $35.9M of interest income on the cash pile — net carrying cost of about $31M a quarter, already funded. **This is not a company at risk of going broke, and we say that against our own initial instinct.**
2. **The share count is far larger than the headline.** Two convertible bond tranches are in the money and about 24M warrants were handed to Google as payment for backstopping the Fluidstack lease. Real fully-diluted count is roughly **559 million shares, not the 414–415 million** that shows up on quote screens — 26% more shares than the story is usually told with.
3. **On the company's own promised income, at the same capitalization rate the most-cited sell-side shop (KBW) uses for the sector, the whole thing is worth about $14.5/share.** The stock trades above that. There is no discount to buy.
4. **The legacy business no longer pays the bills.** Mining revenue is ~$24.8M/quarter and falling (−43% year over year); power sales add $2.3M. Corporate overhead runs near $235M/year. Every dollar of head-office cost is now funded by financing, not by operations.

## What the market believes

The consensus is Strong Buy with an average target of **$32.18**, about +74% from here. Working backwards, that target implies roughly $18 billion of equity value, which means either:

- a **~4.0% capitalization rate** applied to the promised $793M of income — richer than any listed data-center landlord trades at, let alone a first-time merchant developer in West Texas with bullet-maturity debt and one non-investment-grade tenant; or
- roughly **$5 billion of credit for the 4.4 GW of pipeline that is not yet under contract.**

The second is the more interesting tell. KBW marked peer companies' unsigned pipeline (HUT, WULF) to **zero** in July, then published a Cipher target that only works if Cipher's unsigned pipeline is worth billions. That is a live inconsistency in the street's own model, and it is the mechanism for the gap between their number and ours. We are not rejecting this because analysts like it — we are rejecting it because the specific number carrying their target is one they refuse to apply to comparable companies.

## Why we might have edge

- **Nobody is counting the shares.** The 38.8M shares from the 2030 converts (224.9213 shares per $1,000 on $172.5M, ~$4.45 strike), the 81.1M from the 2031 converts (62.3733 per $1,000 on $1.3B, ~$16.03 strike), and the ~24M Google warrants add 144M shares — a third more than the screen shows. The build everyone is excited about was *funded by issuing those shares*. Investors are paying for the delivery twice: once in the enthusiasm, once in the dilution that paid for it.
- **The Google warrants are a hidden price for credit quality.** Cipher effectively bought its anchor lease's credit rating by giving equity to the party providing the backstop. That makes headline income per megawatt look like peers' while income per *actual* share is structurally lower. This is a repeatable pattern worth naming and screening for.
- **A blended valuation across three very different tenants flatters the bulls.** Amazon on a 15-year lease and a venture-funded AI cloud with a partial backstop are not the same credit. One capitalization rate across both is an error, and it errs upward.
- **The insider record around the top.** Nine Form 4 and 144 filings between June 16 and July 10, including a CEO 144 for 112,500 shares around $21.19 on July 8 — into the $30.14 high, in the same window the company raised $2.0B of Black Pearl notes. Concentrated insider selling plus maximum external financing at the same moment is a recognizable pattern, and neither the market nor our first pass weighted it.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetrically, here is why we might be wrong and the street right:

- **We valued a 15-year Amazon lease with a perpetual capitalization rate, which is the wrong tool.** A term-matched discounted-cash-flow model — $793M of income escalating over 15 years, discounted at 9–10%, with a conservative terminal value — plausibly lands in the **low-to-mid $20s**. **Neither bench ran it.** This is the single unrun calculation that could flip the verdict, and it is the honest reason our conviction is 7–8 out of 10 rather than 9.
- **We assumed the $3.728B of restricted cash is exactly consumed by remaining construction spending.** Neither bench read the commitments note (exhibits R68–R70). If restricted cash meaningfully exceeds what is still owed to contractors, free equity value exists that our table gives zero credit for, and the whole valuation shifts up.
- **We charged $150M/year of head-office cost against a stabilized rental income stream.** That is too harsh for a finished portfolio — development-stage overhead shrinks once you stop developing. The $10.54/share figure that treatment produces overstates the bear case.
- **Delivery genuinely is early**, and a developer that ships on time earns a better multiple than one that doesn't. We concede that and it is not in our numbers.
