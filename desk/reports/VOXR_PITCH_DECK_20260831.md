# PITCH — VOXR — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Both benches say do not buy at $5.625; the proposal on the table is a *conditional* one: no position today, and a two-part reopen test at $2.10–$3.15.

---

## What we believe

Vox Royalty is a small mining-royalty company. The idea that put it in front of us was simple: royalties on gold mines should hold up — or gain — when the broad market falls, because the royalty owner collects a slice of revenue without paying mining costs. That makes it a candidate for the part of the book meant to survive a drawdown.

After reading the company's own filings, we no longer believe that description fits this company.

1. **The cash is mostly not gold.** In the first half of 2026, royalty revenue was $3,743,878. A single asset — Kanmantoo, a **copper** mine in South Australia — produced $1,970,000 of it, 53%. In the second quarter alone it was 68%. Copper falls when the economy falls. It is the opposite of the ballast we were shopping for.
2. **The second-biggest historical earner is an iron ore mine that is being switched off.** Wonmunna's royalty fell from $3,000,000 to $720,000 because the operator moved its ore feed to a different mine (Lamb Creek). That single change more than explains the whole year-over-year revenue decline.
3. **Strip out those two, and the rest of the "70+ asset portfolio" halved** — $2,095,339 down to $1,053,878, a 49.7% decline. What is marketed as a diversified book of 70+ royalties is, in cash terms, one big asset at a time, each one wasting, sitting on top of a shrinking remainder.
4. **Most of the reported revenue isn't royalty income at all.** About 83% of first-half receipts came from gold *purchase-and-resale* agreements — the company buys ounces at a formula price and sells them at market, keeping the spread. That is a trading spread, not a royalty. It consumes cash before it produces cash, and the deliveries are capped in total ounces, so it runs out.
5. **The price already exceeds the best case we can construct.** At $5.625, the enterprise value is about $359.5M. If we hand the company everything it wants — the stalled Los Filos mine restarts tomorrow at 100% certainty, the royalty stub earns a full multiple — we still only get to $3.14–$5.30 per share. Today's price sits at or above the top of that range.

**Our belief: this is a fairly-to-richly priced, pro-cyclical royalty trading company being valued as a counter-cyclical gold royalty compounder. Not a buy at $5.625. Possibly interesting under $3.15, and only if the shrinking part stops shrinking.**

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a growing royalty compounder in a strong gold tape:

- The company guided full-year 2026 receipts to **$32–37M**; the analyst consensus is **$33.555M**, sitting neatly inside that range. Nobody disputes the number.
- The company raised its dividend and markets "up to 90% receipts growth in 2026."
- It just announced another acquisition — two Australian copper/gold royalties from Latitude 66 for up to A$3.4M, announced 2026-08-20 ([SEC 6-K, exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1907909/000165495426007768/)). The story is "serial accretive acquirer in a Tier 1 jurisdiction."
- The stock is 16% below its 52-week high but **67% above its 52-week low**. This is a near-high tape, not a dislocated one.

So the disagreement is not about the forecast. **We agree with the consensus number. We disagree about what that number is worth**, because we think the number is made of the wrong stuff: capped trading spreads and pro-cyclical base-metal royalties, not durable gold royalty income.

## Why we might have edge

Three things we found that we could not find published anywhere else:

1. **Nobody decomposes the royalty line by asset.** The press release the market reads gives a blended "receipts" figure. The concentration in Kanmantoo (53% of royalty revenue), the Wonmunna wind-down, and the halving residual are visible only in the management discussion buried in exhibit 99.2 of the quarterly filing ([primary source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1907909/000165495426007526/voxr_ex992.htm)). Marketing metric and audited metric disagree, and the difference is the whole thesis.
2. **The company gave us its own valuation anchor and it is far below the tape.** In June 2026 Vox sold its i-80 gold stream — roughly 100,000 ounces of remaining deliveries — for **$4.8M in shares**. At the company's own realized spreads of $83–94 per ounce, that stream was worth $8.3–9.4M undiscounted. It cleared at about **55 cents on the undiscounted dollar**. Apply that same ratio to what's left (Greenstone ~226koz, Los Filos ~588koz) and the entire purchase-agreement book is worth roughly **$37M** — against a $359.5M enterprise value. This is not our multiple assumption; it is a price this issuer transacted at, this quarter, for this exact asset class.
3. **The Kanmantoo royalty steps down 80%.** The filing says the rate is 2.5%, "stepping down to 0.5% after >85Kt copper produced." At guided production of 12,750–14,000 tonnes a year, that is six-plus years away even counting from zero — so it is not an imminent problem, but it means the single asset carrying half the royalty income has a written expiry on its economics that no summary mentions.

The edge, if we have one, is **exclusion**: we can say precisely why this is not the asset the cohort thinks it is, and we can put a number on the best case.

## Why it might be priced in

Honest concessions:

- **The guidance already prices the second-half decline.** First-half receipts were $22.19M against a $32–37M full-year guide. That implies second-half receipts of $9.8–14.8M — down 33% to 56%. The company told everyone. This is not a hidden landmine; it is disclosed and consensus.
- **The 2027 "cliff" we initially thought we found does not exist.** The existing Greenstone agreement delivers up to 58,500 oz/year until March 1, 2027 — but a replacement was signed in January 2026 at 29% of refined gold, minimum 63,600 oz in 2026, and it is *larger* annually than the one it replaces. There is a total-ounce cap (~226koz, roughly 3.5 years of deliveries), not an expiry cliff.
- **The stock-based compensation critique is well-known.** First-half stock comp of $3,775,725 slightly exceeds first-half royalty revenue of $3,743,878, and fully diluted shares (74,383,536) exceed outstanding (69,452,471) by 7.1%. Any holder can see this in the same filing; it is a reason to size down, not a secret.
- **Gold royalty companies have traded at premiums to net asset value for a decade.** "Expensive versus NPV" has been true of this whole sector for years and has not been a shorting signal. Our valuation kill is a reason not to own it; it is not, on its own, a reason to expect the price to fall.

The genuinely novel parts (per-asset decomposition, the i-80 sale as an anchor, the trading-spread mechanics) are novel *because nobody bothered*, not because they are hard — which means they can become consensus at any time, or never.

## What could go wrong

*(This is the pre-mortem for the conditional position — a starter bought in the $2.10–$3.15 reopen band. Today we own nothing, so today's risk is purely the risk of being wrong to stay out.)*

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy the "ballast" and it behaves like a cyclical.**
We take the starter at $3.15 in a general drawdown. But the cash flow is copper (Kanmantoo, 53%) and iron ore (Wonmunna's successor assets), both of which fall with industrial demand. The purchase-and-resale gold book requires paying cash for ounces *before* selling them, so in a fast gold decline the spread compresses or inverts and the book consumes working capital exactly when we wanted it to generate cash. We end up holding a levered cyclical in the slot reserved for the thing that pays us during the crash.
*Tripwire exists:* the per-asset royalty split in each quarterly management discussion, plus copper and iron ore spot. Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — The band is a value trap, and we fire mechanically into it.**
The price reaches $3.15 not because the market got scared but because the residual royalty book keeps halving — another operator relocates feed, another single-mine annuity retires, and the "70+ assets" continue to produce almost nothing. We buy a declining stub at a lower multiple of a smaller number and it is still expensive.
*Tripwire exists, and it is why the entry test is deliberately conjunctive:* the price band alone must not trigger a buy. It must be accompanied by a printed quarter in which royalty revenue **excluding Kanmantoo** grows year-over-year. If the price gets there and that number does not, we do not buy.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE. The royalty deeds themselves.**
Every economic term that matters here lives in private contracts we cannot read. We know the Kanmantoo royalty steps down after 85,000 tonnes of copper — but **the company has never disclosed how many tonnes are already counted**, or whether the counter includes the historical open-pit era or only the 2024 underground restart. The same opacity applies to deduction definitions in each net-smelter-return royalty, area-of-interest clauses, operator buy-back rights, and dispute provisions. An operator could reinterpret the deduction basis, a counter could turn out to be nearly exhausted, or a deed could be challenged — and the first we would learn of it is a revenue line that came in wrong, or a lawsuit. There is no filing, no satellite image, no data feed that reveals a number the issuer does not publish.
**Unknown-unknown class: undisclosed private-contract mechanics in a non-operating asset holder.** We own a claim on someone else's mine, defined by a document we have never seen, administered by a counterparty whose concentration the company declines to quantify. (The filing's only counterparty language is qualitative — "subject to the credit risk and performance of the counterparties who own and operate the mines" — with no operator-level concentration disclosure, despite Equinox spanning both Greenstone and Los Filos. The absence of that note on a two-asset-dependent book is itself the finding.) We accept this as unhedgeable and it is a direct argument for a small size and against ever making this a core holding.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own footprint, our own flow, and our own rules.**

- **We do not know if we can trade this.** The executability check was never completed: broker calls were permission-denied to both benches, and no shell was available. **We have no verified average daily volume, no bid-ask spread, no borrow data.** This is a $390M dual-listed micro-cap with two separate order books (NASDAQ and TSX), which usually means each one is thinner than the combined figure suggests. **No size may be set until this is closed.** Stating a share count today would be inventing a number.
- **We are predicting the flow we would be bidding into.** Our own thesis says the second half brings a 33–56% receipts decline and a shrinking residual book. If we place a standing limit order at $3.15 and walk away, the most likely way it fills is *on our own predicted bad news*. A resting order underneath a quarterly report is an accidental bet that nothing bad happens — the opposite of what we believe. Any entry here must be worked deliberately after a print, not left resting through one.
- **Doctrine interaction, stated plainly:** our house default is that a clean adversarial review with no named killer produces at least a starter position. That default does not apply here, because we have named killers: the ballast premise is falsified by the company's own revenue decomposition, and the best-case valuation sits below the current price. Going flat is the exception being properly invoked, not the lazy answer. Counter-argument we accept: we have been wrong before by demanding a discount to a self-built NPV in a sector that has structurally traded above NPV for a decade.
- **Cost of being wrong to stay out:** if gold keeps rising and the acquisition machine keeps working, this compounds away from us and we own none of it. That is a real, unbounded-upside miss, and the reopen band may never print.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a pro-cyclical copper-and-iron-ore royalty stub in the slot reserved for crash protection, and it fell with the market at the exact moment we needed it to hold.**

2. **Cumulative copper tonnes at Kanmantoo, and the counter's start basis.** Undisclosed. Determines whether the 80% royalty step-down is six years out or much closer. The company has never published it.

7. **The Q3 report date.** Vendor-derived, not company-announced.
8. **Whether the Latitude 66 acquisition closes**, and what the two new royalties are actually worth. The A$3.4M price is small; the disclosure gives resource figures but no revenue path before Austral restarts Rocklands in mid-2027.
