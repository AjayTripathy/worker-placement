# PITCH — CHA — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no orders, no entry before the August 28 print; a conditional starter is proposed only if four specific numbers land, and the price cap we must respect may make even that unfillable.

---

## What we believe

Chagee is a Chinese premium tea-drinks chain (7,531 shops, ~95% of sales in Greater China) that listed on Nasdaq in April 2025 at $28 and now trades at **$9.99** — down 58% from its 52-week high of $23.90, and 12% above its low of $8.98.

What we think is true, after two benches argued it out:

1. **It is genuinely cheap on the reported numbers, but not as cheap as the original write-up said.** The company holds roughly **US$1.15 billion of cash and short-term deposits against a US$1.91 billion market value**, so the operating business is being valued at about **US$757 million** — roughly **5.5x** the last twelve months' reported earnings, or about **3.6x** if you add back the one-time stock-compensation charge taken at the IPO. The original 3.5x claim was wrong; so was our red bench's 6.9x correction.
2. **The business is shrinking where it matters most.** About 78% of revenue is wholesale sales *to* franchisees. The Greater China franchised shop count peaked at 6,836 in September 2025 and has fallen two quarters in a row to **6,603**. Sales per shop are down ~18% year over year. That is the core problem and neither bench disputes it.
3. **The first-quarter "turn" is partly real.** Average sales per shop rose 5.5% quarter over quarter. Our red bench said that was purely an accounting-mix effect from converting weak franchised shops into company-owned flagships. That explanation does not hold up: the mix shift was smaller than the prior quarter's, when sales per shop *fell* 10.9%. And the metric the original write-up actually relied on — same-store sales, which is immune to mix by construction — improved by 9.4 percentage points.
4. **The most serious live problem is where the cash sits.** Roughly RMB7.1 billion (~US$1.03bn) of the cash is inside China. The company's own filings state that **no Chinese subsidiary has ever paid a dividend up to the Cayman parent, and no cash has been transferred in either direction** ([F-1, SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2013649/000110465925033664/tm246985-29_f1a.htm)). Meanwhile the parent has already spent ~US$176 million on a December 2025 special dividend and authorized a US$150 million buyback — together about **US$326 million of the ~US$385 million net IPO proceeds**. Completing the buyback probably requires moving money out of China for the first time. Until that happens, most of the "cash" backing the low multiple is not demonstrably available to shareholders.
5. **It is very hard to trade.** Only **17.2% of shares are freely floating** (32.8M of 191.1M), on about **US$4.5 million of average daily volume**. Under our own rule of never being more than 1% of daily volume, a $250,000 position takes roughly five and a half trading days to build and the same to exit.

**Net belief: a cheap-looking, cash-rich, structurally declining franchise business with a possible seasonal tailwind into the next print, an unproven ability to get its own cash out of China, and a float too thin to size meaningfully. Interesting; not yet ownable.**

## What the market believes

The market is not confused, and that is the single biggest problem with this idea.

- Analysts model **US$0.35 of earnings per share for the coming quarter** — about RMB461 million of net income, versus RMB630 million a year earlier. That is a **26% year-over-year decline already in consensus.** The Street is not fooled by the optically collapsed prior-year reported figure (which was low only because of an RMB552.5 million one-time IPO stock charge).
- Published targets bracket the current price: **Goldman Sachs Neutral $13.60, CLSA Hold $10.00.** Nobody is calling it a bargain and nobody is calling it broken.
- The market believes the cause of the decline is external and ongoing: a Chinese food-delivery platform subsidy war plus weak catering spending. It is right about that.
- Screening tools are showing an **8.71% dividend yield** on this stock. That is an artifact — there has been exactly **one** dividend (ex-date December 8, 2025, US$0.92 per share) and vendors are annualizing a one-time special as if it were quarterly. Any yield-driven buyer here is buying a data error.

**Where the market may be wrong:** it appears to be extrapolating the current quarter-to-quarter decline forward as the normal seasonal shape. Reconstructing 2024 from prior-year figures disclosed inside the Q3 2025 release shows **the third quarter is the seasonal peak and the first quarter is the trough** — the opposite of what our own red bench assumed. If sales per shop simply hold flat from Q1 to Q2, the year-over-year comparison is about **−11.9%**, not the ~−17% our red bench treated as neutral. That would produce non-GAAP net income of roughly **RMB450–560 million against consensus of ~RMB461 million** — a modest possible upside surprise.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly assessed, our edge here is narrow and mostly analytical rather than informational:

1. **We read a document nobody else in the argument had read.** The Q3 2025 results release ([EX-99.1, SEC.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2013649/000110465925116606/tm2532281d1_ex99-1.htm)) contains the prior-year comparatives that let you rebuild the pre-shock 2024 quarterly shape. Without it, you cannot tell seasonality from decline in a company with only one year of public history. Both the original write-up and our red bench got the seasonal direction wrong for exactly this reason.
2. **We can separate mix from same-store.** Most commentary on franchise-to-company-owned conversions treats the per-shop average as a same-store proxy. It isn't. We sized the mix effect (company-owned shops would need 3.75x the volume of franchised shops to explain the lift) and checked it against a quarter with a *larger* mix shift and a *falling* average.
3. **We separated the cash drain from the cash burn.** Cash fell RMB1,249.5 million between September and December 2025. That is the special dividend, essentially to the decimal — not deteriorating operations.
4. **We caught the yield artifact** that will mislead any screen-driven buyer.
5. **We noticed the sequencing of the buyback and the shelf.** The company began repurchasing on June 1, 2026 and filed a resale-capable shelf registration on **June 5, 2026 — four days later** — against a register where 83% of shares are insider-held. The authorization is 46% of the entire public float at today's price. That is enough to absorb a large insider exit without a marketed offering. We are not alleging anything; we are noting that the company's own bid may end up being the counterparty to the pre-IPO register's exit, which is a reason to be small, not a reason to be short.

**What is not edge:** the valuation. Our estimate of next quarter's earnings overlaps the Street's. Per our own rule that a difference of opinion, not a difference of research effort, is what creates a tradeable gap — there is barely a gap here.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because this is the stronger side of the argument today:

- **Consensus already carries the decline.** US$0.35 for the quarter is a −26% year-over-year number. There is no "the Street hasn't noticed" story.
- **5.5x earnings on a business with a shrinking store base is ordinary, not dislocated.** Cheap multiples on declining wholesale volumes are the normal price of declining wholesale volumes.
- **The cash discount may be rational, not a mistake.** If you give zero credit to the RMB7.1 billion sitting in China — which is what the filings' own language invites, since none of it has ever moved — the operating business is being valued at roughly **13.8x** trailing earnings, not 5.5x. *(That sensitivity is our derivation from the benches' cash-transfer finding, not a figure either bench computed.)* The market may simply be pricing trapped cash at a large haircut, which is defensible.
- **Both prosecuting and defending benches ended at the same position: flat into the print.** They disagreed about nearly every number and agreed about the action. That is usually a sign the action is right.
- **The float is the answer to "why is it cheap."** 17.2% float, 4.74% institutional ownership, $4.5 million a day. Illiquid stocks with an effective shelf overhead trade at discounts because the discount is the compensation for the exit risk. Getting paid for illiquidity is not the same as finding a mispricing.
- **The legal overhang is unresolved.** Three plaintiff firms are soliciting claims tied to the $28 IPO and the August 29, 2025 −13% disclosure day. Neither bench checked the federal docket to see whether an actual complaint has been filed. Under our own rule, an unresolved integrity question means the name can never be treated as fairly priced and must be capped below our fair-value estimate.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Pre-print: NO ENTRY. Flat. No resting orders.** Both benches independently reached this, for different reasons. The original write-up's pre-authorized trigger of "beat −8% on same-store sales" is **withdrawn** — it was set against a mis-derived baseline and is not a meaningful line.

**The reopen conditions (all four must land in the Q2 2026 release, expected 2026-08-28):**

1. Greater China **same-store sales better than −11%** year over year (flat sales per shop = −11.9%; better than that means genuine stabilization, not seasonality)
2. Greater China **franchised shop count ≥ 6,600** (holding the base; last reported 6,603)
3. **Non-GAAP net income ≥ RMB500 million** (consensus ~RMB461M — this is a real beat, not a rounding win)
4. **Actual repurchased share count disclosed** — executed buying, not a re-stated authorization

**If all four land — proposed starter:**
- **Size: ≤0.5% of the book**, and no more, on first entry
- **Pace: ≤1% of daily volume ≈ 4,500 shares / ~$45,000 per day.** A $250,000 starter therefore takes about **5–6 trading sessions** to build. Budget the same to exit.
- **Absolute price ceiling: $8.70.** Derivation, stated so it can be argued with: a stabilized business at 8x trailing earnings gives a fair value of ~$11.84 per share with full credit for the Chinese cash, or ~$10.21 with a 30% haircut on that cash for withholding tax and transfer friction. Because the legal overhang is unresolved, our own rule caps us at 0.85x fair value → **$10.06 with full cash credit, $8.68 with the haircut.** We use the haircut number, because the filings say the cash has never moved. **Never pay above $10.05 under any reading.**

**Flag this openly: those two conditions may be mutually unfillable.** A print good enough to clear all four triggers will very likely gap the stock above $8.70 on the open. In that case the correct action is **stand down and wait for a pullback**, not to relax the cap. We have made this mistake before with paired conditions that can never co-occur, and we are naming it in advance rather than discovering it at the fill.

**Add band (second tranche, ≤0.5% of book more):** only after the Q3 2026 release (expected ~2026-11-27) shows franchised shops ≥6,600 **and** executed buybacks running **>US$40 million in the quarter**, and only at or below the then-current 0.85x cap.

**Kill / do-not-reopen conditions:**
- Greater China franchised shops **below 6,500**
- The Q2 release is **again silent on moving cash out of China** — at that point the low multiple is unsupported, because the cash backing it is unreachable
- A **secondary offering or resale takedown** under the June 2026 shelf into this float
- A **filed securities complaint** (as opposed to solicitations) — that hardens the cap and, at the extreme, removes the name

**Upgrade-to-real-size conditions (would change the whole thesis):** a filing disclosing that a Chinese subsidiary has actually **declared and remitted a dividend** to the Cayman parent. That single event converts RMB7.1 billion from a footnote into an asset and is worth more than any quarter of same-store sales.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **2026-08-28 — Q2 2026 results.** *Not company-confirmed.* Derived from a filing cadence that is consistent to the weekday: five consecutive releases on Fridays (2025-05-30, 2025-08-29, 2025-11-28, 2026-03-31, 2026-05-29). Treat as high-likelihood, unconfirmed.
- **~2026-11-27 — Q3 2026 results.** Same cadence basis (2025's landed on the Friday after Thanksgiving). Unconfirmed.
- **No date** attaches to a shelf takedown. It became possible on June 5, 2026 and remains possible any day.

## What remains unverified

- **Whether a securities complaint has actually been filed.** Solicitations are not a case. Nobody checked the docket. This determines whether the price cap binds.
- **The June 5, 2026 shelf registration's contents.** We could not read the document. Whether it permits insider resales — the crux of the supply concern — is asserted by neither bench with evidence.
- **The FY2025 annual report's current cash-transfer language.** We are relying on the earlier IPO-prospectus wording. If the annual report updated it, our strongest objection may already be stale — in either direction.
- **The 2024 quarterly figures** that overturned the seasonality argument are derived from comparatives disclosed inside later releases, with bounds — not printed quarterly statements. The direction is well-supported; the precise magnitudes are not primary.
- **Live price, float, average volume, and the price path around the May 29 release.** Vendor data only; the broker rail was unavailable to both benches. Our sizing math rests on it.
- **Whether the shop counts are real.** No independent verification of 7,531 locations. Everything about unit trends comes from the company.
- **Greater China demand in the current quarter.** No visibility at all, for the structural reason above.
- **The buyback's actual execution.** We know the authorization. We do not know whether a single share has been repurchased, or with what money.
- **Whether the seasonal improvement thesis survives the subsidy war.** The reconstructed 2024 shape says Q3 is the peak. But 2024 was a pre-shock year. If the subsidy war overwhelms seasonality, the 2024 shape is the wrong template — and that is precisely the mistake we caught our own red bench making in the opposite direction.
