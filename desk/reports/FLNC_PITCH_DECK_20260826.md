# PITCH — FLNC — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — minimum starter (0.25–0.50% of book) on a price gate *below* today's $11.20, explicitly not owning the November print; no immediate buy.

---

## What we believe

Fluence Energy builds and sells grid-scale battery storage systems — large containerized battery installations that utilities and data centers use to store power. It also licenses software to run them. Revenue is roughly $3B/year. The stock is $11.20, down 67% from its 52-week high of $33.51, but already up ~70% from its $6.60 low.

The whole stock right now hinges on one quarter. The company's own fiscal-2026 guidance requires its closing quarter (July–September 2026, reported in November) to do $1.31–1.51B of revenue against roughly $650M in the prior quarter, and to swing adjusted EBITDA from −$29.3M to somewhere between +$60.8M and +$100.8M.

Two things we believe, both of which run against where the original bear brief and the red bench landed:

**1. That quarter is not unusual for this company.** Both benches initially treated a 2.0–2.3× step-up as extraordinary. It isn't. In fiscal 2024 the closing quarter was $1,227.6M — **2.54× the prior quarter and 45.5% of the full year**, computed off the evidence pack's own quarterly series (Dec-23 $364.0M + Mar-24 $623.1M + Jun-24 $483.3M = $1,470.4M; FY24 total $2,698M). The bear case's headline number was *less* demanding than what the company actually did two years ago. The brief's own table contained its own refutation.

**2. The margin recovery everyone treats as required is not required.** The bear case says the guidance secretly assumes gross margin snaps back to ~13%, and that a repeat of the recent weak margin blows up the EBITDA guide. Work the operating-expense bridge: last quarter's adjusted gross profit was $38.6M against adjusted EBITDA of −$29.3M, so adjusted operating expense is about $68M. At the **10.6%** margin the company already printed net of its two named one-time charges, and holding opex flat:

| Q4 revenue | Gross profit at 10.6% | Less ~$70M opex | Required range |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1.31B | $139M | **+$69M** | +$60.8M to +$100.8M ✓ |
| $1.41B | $149M | **+$79M** | ✓ |
| $1.51B | $160M | **+$90M** | ✓ |

It clears at every point of the company's own revenue range. Management does not need a margin recovery. It needs the margin it already has and revenue anywhere inside its own guidance.

**3. Nobody priced it.** Neither bench produced a fair value. At $11.18 the enterprise value is about **$2.10B** (184.60M shares — 143,163,588 Class A plus 41,432,781 Class B-1 — plus $400M of convertible notes at face, less $365M of cash and restricted cash). Against roughly $3.3B of contracted work the company says converts over the next twelve months, that is **0.64× enterprise value to sales** — for a business with a $6.4B contracted order book, a stated $33.1B pipeline, orders up 3× year-over-year, and ~$850M of data-center wins.

So: a cheap, contracted, unloved industrial, where the two named reasons to stay away both broke when we checked the arithmetic. That is worth a small position on a lower price — not a conviction buy at the current price, and not through the print.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a company whose margins are structurally broken and whose big closing quarter is a promise, not a fact. The stock trades below 0.7× sales because the market is pricing the risk that the quarter slips, that the "one-time" charges keep recurring, or that fixed-price contracts signed at old prices get delivered against costs that moved.

There is also a large short position — reportedly about 23% of the float with roughly 9.4 days of average volume to cover. **This figure is from a secondary source and we could not verify it against FINRA.** If it is right, the pessimistic view is not a lonely one; it is the consensus expression, and it is crowded.

Published analyst estimates for the quarter are **$1.53B of revenue and +$0.39 of earnings per share** — above the top of the company's own revenue guidance and, on our arithmetic, above what the company's own EBITDA guidance can arithmetically produce (+$0.39 × 184.6M shares ≈ +$72M of net income, against an adjusted EBITDA ceiling of ~$101M *before* depreciation, interest on $400M of notes, and tax). We think that consensus print is stale or on a mismatched basis. We flag it, and we do not lean on it.

Finally: the market does *not* appear to have noticed that fiscal-2024's closing quarter was even more concentrated than the one now required. That is the specific belief we think is wrong.

## Why we might have edge

- **Arithmetic nobody redid.** The fiscal-2024 base effect (2.54×, 45.5% of the year) is in the evidence pack's own quarterly series. It takes three additions. It refutes the loudest bear line and we have not seen it anywhere in coverage.
- **An operating-expense bridge nobody built.** The finding that a 10.6% margin clears the EBITDA guide across the *entire* revenue range is derived from two numbers in the earnings release. Both the original brief and our own red bench got this wrong in opposite directions — the brief said it fails, red said it barely passes at the low end only. It passes everywhere.
- **A share-count trap nobody sees.** Fluence has an "Up-C" structure: the founders (AES and Siemens) hold units in an operating subsidiary rather than ordinary shares. In May 2026, AES converted **10,066,414** of those units into ordinary Class A shares; its non-controlling stake fell from 28.19% to 22.45%. Total economic ownership did not change — so **no dilution occurred** — but about 10 million newly sellable shares appeared, followed by Form 144 sale notices on 2026-06-15 and three more on 2026-06-22. This also explains why the evidence pack's diluted-share series alternates between ~129M and ~184M: that is two different accounting bases, **not dilution**. Standard screens see nothing here. We filed this as a new reusable pattern.
- **A pricing gap.** The court reached a "stay flat" instinct without ever computing what the business is worth. 0.64× sales on a contracted book is a number that has to be argued with, not ignored.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly assessed, the edge is narrower than the above makes it sound.

- **The stock has already doubled off the low.** At +70% off $6.60, this is not an undiscovered dislocation. Somebody has already re-rated it. Anchoring on "down 67% from the high" flatters the setup; the low-anchored number is the honest one.
- **The bear thesis is a well-populated trade.** A ~23% short position (unverified) means the pessimistic case is not proprietary. Neither is ours: "cheap on contracted backlog, closing quarter is seasonal" is a readable thesis, and it is available to anyone who reads the 10-Q.
- **The two things that actually decide the outcome are unknowable before the print.** Whether the one-time charges were genuinely one-time, and whether the contract-manufacturing line ships enough units — neither resolves from public documents. We have an arithmetic edge on what the guide *requires*. We have no edge on whether it is *met*.
- **The genuinely bearish leg is real and dated.** Management says its Houston contract-manufacturing line reaches full production in **fiscal Q1 2027 — after the guided quarter.** That constrains units shippable, which no backlog or deferred-revenue argument can fix. This is the strongest thing in the bear file and it survived the whole court.
- **Coverage exists.** This is not an orphan security. There is published consensus and sell-side attention. The "nobody is looking" premium does not apply.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — Revenue lands, margin doesn't, because operating expense scales with the ramp.**
Our central result (the margin recovery isn't required) holds operating expense flat at ~$70M while revenue more than doubles. That is a real weakness and we should say so plainly. If opex runs at $85M instead — perfectly plausible in a quarter shipping 2.3× the prior quarter's volume — then at $1.31B and 10.6% margin the EBITDA lands at **+$54M, below the +$60.8M floor**, and the guide misses. The entire "no snapback needed" conclusion is one operating-leverage assumption away from inverting.
*Tripwire: partial.* The quarterly report itself. Nothing before it.

**Failure narrative 2 — The line doesn't ship.**
The Houston constraint is supply-side and dated after the quarter. If throughput comes in short, revenue slips into fiscal 2027, the $783.0M of inventory sits — that inventory currently pre-stages 58–67% of the required quarter's cost of goods, which is bullish if it ships and a write-down vector if it doesn't, since those are fixed-price contract cells against a falling cell-price curve. Meanwhile the liquidity story degrades: recognizing the $956.5M of customer deposits into revenue is **non-cash**, so the quarter that saves the income statement drains the balance-sheet cushion. Nine-month operating cash flow was −$366.5M *while* deposits built $316M; excluding that build, the cash burn was roughly $683M.
*Tripwire: exists but currently broken.* Customs bill-of-lading data on cell and module import cadence for July–October is the only external read. Our pull returned zero consignees for a company carrying $783M of imported-cell inventory — that is a failed query, not a clean negative, and it must be re-run.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE. A private counterparty change.**
Three customers are **48% of nine-month revenue** (up from two customers at 40%), and the founder-shareholders AES and Siemens are themselves related-party customers responsible for **$272.1M of that revenue**. Any of the following would move the stock and would produce no filing until it is already in the numbers: a bilateral contract renegotiation on price or schedule; a customer deferring delivery to manage its own capital budget; a founder-shareholder making a strategic decision about its stake or its purchasing relationship; a cell supplier changing terms. We do not even know who the cell supplier is — the company disclosed a $15M battery prepayment without naming the counterparty, so we cannot screen it.

**Unknown-unknown class, named: *private bilateral counterparty action.*** This is the category of risk where the deciding event happens inside a commercial negotiation between two parties, produces no public artifact, and becomes visible only as an aggregated number in a quarterly filing. Concentration is what makes it lethal here: with 48% of revenue in three counterparties, one private conversation is a thesis-level event. There is no sensor for this. There cannot be one. It is the single best argument for keeping the position small.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- **Our own size is not the problem, and saying so is not a compliment to us.** At 0.25–0.50% of book against a ~$2.1B-capitalization name, our order is a rounding error on any plausible daily volume. (We did not verify FLNC's average daily volume — it is on the unverified list.) We cannot blame execution if this loses money. It will lose money because the analysis was wrong.
- **We would be creating the exact structure our own doctrine forbids.** A resting good-till-cancelled buy at $9.60 sitting into a November earnings report is not a value entry — it is an accidental bet that the stock *won't* move violently. It fills automatically on a gap-down, at the worst possible information moment, before we know why. **Any gate placed under this pitch must be cancelled before the print date and re-entered by hand afterward.** This is the single most likely way to convert a disciplined plan into an undisciplined position.
- **We would be bidding beneath predicted forced flow.** AES's ~10M newly sellable shares are supply we have identified and dated. Putting a bid under a supply event we ourselves predicted means we are the liquidity for it.
- **Doctrine interaction, and it cuts against us.** This position exists because *no one named a surviving kill* — the desk's default is a minimum starter when a clean court produces no named kill. That is a procedural reason to own something, not a positive reason. It is materially weaker than conviction and should be treated that way in sizing. Separately, the desk's record on owning earnings dislocations is **0 for 7**, which is precisely why the plan does not own the print.
- **Two housekeeping interactions to clear before any buy:** check the Parametric separately-managed sleeve for an existing FLNC holding (a duplicate creates a wash-sale entanglement we would be creating on purpose), and note that any position held under a year is taxed at ordinary income rates on exit.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we correctly established the company *can* hit its guidance without a margin recovery, and it missed anyway — because operating expense scaled with the volume ramp, the Houston line shipped late, or one of three concentrated customers quietly moved a delivery — and we discovered all three at once, from the same press release, having already been filled at our lower gate on the opening gap.
