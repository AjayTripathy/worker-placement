# PITCH — RKT — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Rocket Companies is a mortgage lender and loan-servicer whose earnings are, in the near term, close to a mechanical function of the 30-year mortgage rate. The stock is down about 46% from its 52-week high and sits roughly 7% above its 52-week low ($13.075 vs. a $12.18–$24.35 range in the pack we received).

Our belief, stated plainly:

1. **The cheapness is an illusion created by the company's own preferred earnings measure.** At the pack price the stock trades at roughly 20x the "adjusted" earnings the company guides on, but roughly **40x** the actual GAAP earnings it reports. The gap between the two is mostly (a) amortization of intangible assets created by paying $14.2B in stock for Mr. Cooper, and (b) stock compensation. Neither is a made-up cost. Amortization is non-cash, but the goodwill it sits next to was struck at the acquirer's peak price. Stock comp is a real transfer from shareholders.
2. **The bull case's designated hedge does not reach the P&L the guidance is given in.** The $19.4B of mortgage servicing rights (the right to collect fees on a $2T, ~9.1M-loan book) is real and is ~52% of market cap. But the company reconciles the change in that asset's value *out* of adjusted revenue and adjusted EPS, and reports it **net of hedges** — Q2'26 was $(23)M, not a several-hundred-million-dollar rate windfall ([Q2'26 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001805284/000162828026054577/rkt-20260630.htm)). So the asset that is supposed to rescue you cannot show up in the number the market grades.
3. **The forward pipeline drained in Q2.** New rate locks ($47.0B) came in **below** closings ($49.1B), a $2.1B / −4.3% drain ([Q2'26 earnings 8-K ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1805284/000180528426000082/rkt-063020268xkex991earnin.htm)). That is a leading indicator and it points the same way the Q3 guide points.
4. **But we do not have an edge.** After both benches, the score is zero findings that the street doesn't already have. Everything that survived — the GAAP/adjusted gap, the pipeline drain, the guide-versus-street gap — was public on August 6 and has had five weeks and ~10% of price to be absorbed. Being right about a widely-held view is not a trade.

**Net: the business is worse than the adjusted numbers suggest, the stock has already fallen a lot for that reason, and we cannot demonstrate that we know something the market doesn't. Therefore: flat, with pre-committed reopen conditions.**

## What the market believes

The market believes two things at once, and both are defensible:

- **The bull half:** Rocket is now the largest US mortgage platform after absorbing Mr. Cooper and Redfin; it has a $2T servicing book that earns fees regardless of origination volume, $11.2B of liquidity, and material cost synergies still to be realized. If mortgage rates fall, origination volume is a coiled spring and the stock is a high-beta call option on that. Consensus for Q3 was **$2.91B** of revenue — above the company's own $2.5–2.7B guide — which tells you the sell side is still modelling acquisition revenue accretion faster than the company is modelling market shrinkage ([StreetInsider summary of the guide vs. consensus](https://www.streetinsider.com/Earnings/Rocket+Companies+(RKT)+Misses+Q2+EPS+by+1c,+Offers+Guidance/26884197.html); secondary source — flagged below).
- **The bear half:** rates are not cooperating. Freddie Mac's weekly survey had the 30-year at **6.71% on 9/3/26**, described as the highest in 13 months, versus 6.50% a year earlier ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). Short interest is only ~5.4% of the public float, so this is not a crowded short — it is a name people have simply stopped paying for.

The tape's verdict — a 46% drawdown with a small short base — reads as *disillusionment*, not *panic*. That matters: panic can be bought; disillusionment usually needs a catalyst.

## Why we might have edge

Honest answer: **thin, and mostly in things neither bench finished.**

- **The gap between the guide and where the street actually is, today.** The $2.91B consensus figure is dated the day of the Q2 print. Consensus almost certainly re-based downward toward the guide over five weeks. If it re-based *all the way* to $2.6B, the "10.7% below street" bear point is already gone; if it only re-based to $2.8B, there is still a live negative-revision cycle ahead. **Nobody pulled the current number.** That is a real, cheap, drainable edge and we have not drained it.
- **The tax receivable agreement (TRA).** Rocket is an "Up-C" structure — the pre-IPO owners hold a separate class of shares (1.85B Class L, ~65% of the economics) and typically have a contract routing ~85–90% of the company's future tax savings to *them*, not to public shareholders. That is a permanent senior cash claim that does not appear in a P/E multiple. The blue bench raised it; nobody has priced it. If it is large, the effective multiple on Class A is higher than anything quoted in this deck.
- **The stock-comp run rate.** Our own audited pack data stops at $40.0M/quarter (3/31/25, pre-acquisitions). The red bench assumed $90M/quarter. That single assumption swings the cash-adjusted multiple from **23.1x to 26.3x** and swings the "valuation reopen" price from **$11.34 to $9.92**. A five-minute XBRL pull resolves it. Nobody did it.
- **Cash flow.** Neither bench mentioned operating cash flow at all, despite it being in the pack: −$797M (Q1'25), −$3.01B (Q1'24), −$1.33B (Q1'23), versus +$8.8B and +$6.3B in the 2021–22 boom quarters. For a lender, negative operating cash flow is usually just loans-held-for-sale building on the balance sheet, not distress — but it is load-bearing for any liquidity argument and neither bench tested it.

So the potential edge is **diligence we haven't done**, not insight we already have. That is exactly why this is a proposal and not an order.

## Why it might be priced in

Strongly, and we should say so:

- Every surviving bear finding is from the August 6 print or earlier. The market has had five weeks.
- The blue bench's audit is blunt: after review, **0 novel kills, 4 consensus-class kills**. When both of our own benches independently produce only things the wires already carried, the base rate says it is in the price.
- The 46% drawdown is itself the evidence. A stock does not fall by half on a 1-cent EPS miss; it falls that far because the market has already re-rated the earnings power.
- Short interest of 5.4% says nobody is paying up to be short either. Flat positioning on both sides is the signature of a *fairly-priced* disagreement, not a mispricing.
- The one thing genuinely not in the price is the FOMC outcome on 9/16 — and that is a coin-flip on a macro variable, which is a lottery ticket, not an edge.

## What could go wrong

**(the pre-mortem, v1.7)**

### Failure narrative 1 — We buy the reopen band and rates keep going the wrong way (MONITORABLE)
We enter at, say, $11.30 after a valuation-driven flush. The Fed holds or signals higher-for-longer; the 30-year survey rate goes to 7.0%+; Q4 origination guides down again; the "cheap on adjusted earnings" case dies because the E falls faster than the P. At beta ~2.2, a 10% move in the rate-sensitive complex is a 20%+ move in this name. We lose 25–35% from entry.
**Tripwires (monitorable, and they exist):** weekly Freddie Mac PMMS print each Thursday; FOMC 9/16 and 10/27–28 decisions and dot plot; the quarterly rate-lock-versus-closings line in the earnings release.

### Failure narrative 2 — The adjusted-earnings bridge breaks in public (PARTIALLY MONITORABLE)
The company took $14.2B of stock-funded acquisition onto the balance sheet at its own peak price and is now 46% below that high. That is a textbook goodwill-impairment trigger. A multi-billion-dollar write-down is non-cash and gets adjusted out — but it *also* forces auditors and the market to re-examine the intangible amortization that is currently making adjusted EPS look twice as good as GAAP EPS. If the market decides to price GAAP instead of adjusted, the multiple goes from 20x to 40x overnight with no change in the business.
**Tripwire:** an 8-K or 10-Q impairment disclosure — but note this arrives *simultaneously* with the price move, so it is a confirmation, not a warning. Partially monitorable at best.

### Failure narrative 3 — The controlling holder does something we cannot see coming (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE)
The Up-C collapse converted the founder/affiliate stake into **1,848,879,455 Class L shares with economics identical to Class A** — roughly 65% of the company's economics in one hand. No lockup calendar tracks this, because there is no lockup. There is no filing that precedes the decision. A pledge, a large structured secondary, a swap into the TRA, a take-private proposal at a price we consider unfair, or a governance change can all appear as a fully-formed 13D/A or 8-K on a random Tuesday. By the time the sensor could fire, the gap has already happened.
**Unknown-unknown class, named: NON-CALENDARED CONTROLLING-HOLDER CAPITAL-STRUCTURE ACTION.** This is the class of risk where the information generating the loss is (a) private until disclosure, (b) disclosed at the same instant as the price move, and (c) generated by a party whose incentives are not aligned to the public float. We have **no instrument** that gets ahead of it, and we should not pretend otherwise. Sizing is our only defence: this is why the hard cap below is 2% of book and not larger.

### The reflexive / structural leg
- **Our size versus liquidity:** average daily volume for Class A is **UNVERIFIED** — the blue bench estimated 23–33M shares/day, which at ~$13 is $300–430M of notional. A 1.5%-of-book position would be a small fraction of one day's volume on any plausible book size. **We are not the flow here and we cannot move this stock.** Our risk is the opposite: we are a price-taker in a name where the marginal seller may be a 1.85B-share holder.
- **Predicted flow:** the Up-C collapse took Class A public float from ~151M shares to ~980M shares. That mechanically raises the name's weight in float-weighted indices (a one-time buy) while permanently increasing the tradeable supply (a persistent sell-side pressure). We have not measured either. If we enter just before a float-driven index rebalance we could be right for the wrong reason and then give it back.
- **Doctrine interactions — the honest one:** three of our own doctrines fired against this name (the valuation-kill doctrine, the venue-consistency detector on "record quarter" marketing versus a triple miss, and the new non-GAAP-add-back mechanism). They look like three independent confirmations. **They are not.** All three are downstream of a single variable — the mortgage rate path — and of a single accounting choice — the adjusted-earnings bridge. If rates fall hard, all three unwind together. We should treat our conviction as roughly *one* view expressed three ways, and discount it accordingly. This is the most likely way our process, rather than the world, hurts us.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be **that we mistook three correlated expressions of one rate-and-accounting view for three independent confirmations, sized to the apparent stack of evidence rather than to the single underlying variable, and got run over by a rate move or a controlling-holder action that no sensor in this deck could see coming.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say NO ENTRY. Position is FLAT, order book empty. What follows are reopen bands, not entries.**

**Why no entry now, in one line:** at 20x adjusted / 40x GAAP / 23–26x cash-adjusted into a quarter the company itself has guided below the street, with a 2x-beta rate exposure and a Fed decision four sessions away, we are being paid nothing to take the risk — and we have produced no view that differs from consensus.

**Why no short either:** 5.4% short interest means no squeeze fuel but also no crowd to run with; a $2T servicing annuity with $11.2B of liquidity is a bad borrow-and-wait; and the asymmetry is flat, not negative.

### Reopen band A — VALUATION (price-gated, no new information required)
- **$11.34 or below** — 20x cash-adjusted earnings *if* the Q3 10-Q confirms stock comp near the pack-audited $40M/quarter run-rate (cash-adj EPS $0.567).
- **$9.92 or below** — 20x cash-adjusted earnings *if* stock comp is confirmed at the $90M/quarter run-rate red assumed (cash-adj EPS $0.496).
- **The band is therefore $9.90–$11.35, and which end binds is decided by one XBRL pull.** Drain item #5 must be closed before any order goes in. Until it is, use the conservative end: **$9.92**.
- **Starter size at the band: 0.75% of book.**

### Reopen band B — EVIDENCE (price-permissive up to $15.00)
All three must print in the Q3 release, together:
1. Adjusted revenue **above $2.70B** (i.e. the top of the guide or better — proof the guide was sandbagged), **and**
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin **≥28%**, **and**
3. Net rate locks **exceeding** closed volume (reversing the $2.1B Q2 pipeline drain).
- **Starter at up to $15.00: 0.75% of book.** Add a further **0.5%** if realized Mr. Cooper synergies step from ~$100M to **>$250M** annualized in the same release.

### Reopen band C — MACRO (confirming, never initiating)
- Freddie Mac 30-year survey rate **below 6.25% on two consecutive weekly prints** *and* an actual FOMC cut. This band **upgrades sizing within A or B by 0.5%**; it does not open a position on its own. We will not buy a rate call dressed as a stock.

### Hard rules
- **$12.18 (52-week low) is a tripwire, not a buy.** A decisive break requires a fresh re-look and explicitly does **not** authorize an average-down.
- **No initiation into 9/16 or 10/27–28.** If a reopen band triggers within two sessions of an FOMC decision, wait until after.
- **The 10/28 vendor earnings date collides with the 10/27–28 FOMC.** Any late-October plan straddles both events. Treat late October as a no-initiation window until the company confirms its date.
- **Maximum position: 2.0% of book, ever**, given ~2.2 beta (secondary-sourced), the rate-factor correlation with the rest of the book, and the un-sensored controlling-holder risk in the pre-mortem.
- **Exit / trim discipline for any future starter:** exit in full if (a) the Q4 guide lands more than 5% below then-current consensus, or (b) a goodwill impairment above $2B is announced alongside any change to liquidity or facility covenants. Trim half on a close below the entry-band low minus 12%.
