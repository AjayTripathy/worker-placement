# PITCH — VELO — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — not a dislocation entry (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

---

## What we believe

Velo3D is a metal 3D-printing equipment maker whose business turned in 2026 and whose balance sheet is the best it has ever been, while the stock trades as if something broke. Revenue grew 52.3% year over year in Q2'26 ($20.664M vs $13.572M — recomputed from the pack's own XBRL and the Q2 10-Q, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001825079/000119312526344560/velo-20260630.htm). Cash is $91.1M against $8.2M of debt. The stock is 63.5% below its 52-week high and still 303% above its low.

The core belief has two legs:

1. **The 63.5% drawdown is a financing unwind, not damage.** The company established an at-the-market share-sales program in May 2026 inside a short squeeze, sold $59.4M of stock in Q2 plus $50M at $14.00, and refilled the treasury. The squeeze *was* the fundraise. A chart that looks like collapse is mostly the issuer selling into its own spike.
2. **The real cash loss is much smaller than anyone (including both of our own benches, first pass) believed.** Headline H1 operating cash outflow was −$39.5M, but $27.0M of that is a one-time working-capital unwind (prepaid −$11.2M, contract assets −$10.1M, accounts payable −$8.2M, accrued −$4.3M). Net loss $18.5M less $6.0M non-cash equals roughly **$12.5M of recurring cash loss per half — about $6.25M per quarter**, not the ~$20M/quarter both benches originally carried. On $91.1M that is ~14 quarters of runway before capex, not four.

Corrected for the cash the first bench forgot, enterprise value is **$262M = 3.75x** the midpoint of the company's own $65–75M FY26 guide, on a business printing +52% growth. That is not a screaming bargain, but it is not the 5.0x the reject case was built on either.

We do **not** believe this is a table-pounding buy at $11.58. Our own probability table (below) says fair value is ~$11.27 — i.e. the tape is already about right. The pitch is for a **small, gated, price-disciplined starter below the tape**, not for the position size the original brief wanted.

## What the market believes

The market holds two contradictory views at once, and that is the interesting part.

- **Sell-side believes a lot.** Consensus is Strong Buy with an average target of $23.75 (+153% from $11.58, per S&P Global data via https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/velo/statistics/). Analysts are *more* bullish than we are.
- **The tape believes little.** Price is $11.58, short interest is 5.54M shares = 18.59% of shares outstanding, and the stock has given back nearly two-thirds of its high.

So the market's operative belief is not "Velo3D is going to $23.75." It is: *the good Q2 was real but was already paid for in the squeeze; the company will keep selling stock; the H2 margin promise (>30% gross margin, versus 21.5% actual) will not be met; and growth is one country and possibly one customer.* The market believes the supply of shares beats the improvement in the business over the next two quarters.

Consensus and tape agree on one number: FY26 revenue of $65–75M, which is just management's guide repeated back. There is no independent street FY27 build we could find.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly, thinner edge than the original brief claimed. What survives:

1. **The burn correction.** If recurring cash loss is ~$6.25M/quarter rather than ~$20M, the "they must keep diluting or die" premise weakens materially. This came out of the 10-Q MD&A cash-flow bridge, not from a screen. Both of our own benches had it wrong before the audit. If two dedicated adversarial reviews got it wrong from public filings, it is plausible the marginal seller has it wrong too.
2. **The enterprise-value arithmetic.** A large share of the market conversation quotes market cap over revenue and ignores that 26% of the market cap is cash. Our own red bench made exactly that error — booked the $8.2M debt and dropped the $91.1M cash it had cited two findings earlier. That is a cheap, repeatable error for others to make.
3. **Backlog nobody priced.** Entering backlog of ~$31M covers 101.5% of the low end of the implied H2 revenue ($30.5M). Neither bench priced it. It converts the "guide is soft" complaint from a demand question into a shipment-timing question.
4. **Structure over story.** The "issuer refills the tank inside a squeeze, then the chart looks like distress" pattern is a recognizable mechanism, not a narrative. Recognizing it is what lets us separate an −63.5% chart from an impaired business.

## Why it might be priced in

Also honestly stated:

1. **Everything we like was published on 2026-08-11.** Q2 revenue, the 980bp margin gain, the doubled backlog, the raised guide — all in one 8-K, in public, four weeks ago. The tape has seen it and sold it. There is no secret.
2. **The street is already twice as bullish as we are.** Being long a Strong Buy / +153%-target name at half a starter is not a divergence trade. If anything we are the bear relative to consensus.
3. **The "raise" contains no growth.** H1 revenue of ~$34.5M against a $65–75M guide implies H2 of $30.5–40.5M, or $15.3–20.3M per quarter — at or *below* Q2's $20.66M. The guide raise embeds zero required sequential growth. That is a knowable, already-known fact.
4. **Supply is a standing fact, not a surprise.** A live ATM program, a 964,474-share CEO option grant (3.24% of shares out), plus convertible notes and four vintages of warrants in the capital structure. Everyone can see the share count is going up.
5. **Our fair value is roughly the price.** Our own scenario table produces $11.27 against a tape of $11.58. When your model prints the market price, "edge" is a word you should use quietly.

## What could go wrong

*(PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)*

**Failure narrative 1 — Share supply beats the fundamentals (monitorable).**
The ATM is refreshed or upsized after Q3, the convertible notes convert, and BEPO/2022/2023/2024 warrants exercise. Share count goes from ~29.8M toward 36M+. Every good quarter is met with a print of stock. Price grinds from $11.58 to $8 while revenue rises. *Tripwires exist:* 424B5 prospectus supplements, 8-Ks, the 10-Q cover-page share count, block prints. This one we can watch.

**Failure narrative 2 — The working-capital correction runs backwards (partly monitorable, badly lagged).**
Our best new finding cuts both ways. The $27.0M "release" partly came from paying down accounts payable and burning prepaid — a one-time harvest funded by the raise. If contract assets and inventory rebuild into an H2 shipment push, cash burn re-accelerates toward the number both benches originally assumed, and the runway story we are leaning on collapses back to the consensus one. *Tripwire is quarterly and lagged by up to 90 days* — we would learn in the Q3 cash-flow statement, after the damage.

**Failure narrative 3 — A single private customer changes its mind (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).**
97.7% of Q2 revenue was Americas ($20.193M of $20.664M); Europe fell from $2.748M to $0.357M, −87%. The growth is one geography and, on the short-seller's allegation, plausibly one or two aerospace/defense/space customers. If a large private counterparty decides to in-source printing, re-qualify a competitor's machine, or simply slip a capital budget, we find out when the revenue does not appear. There is **no filing, no permit, no satellite image, no alt-data feed** that reveals a private buyer's internal capital plan before it becomes a printed number. **Unknown-unknown class: PRIVATE-COUNTERPARTY STATE CHANGE — an opaque, non-reporting customer's intent, unobservable by construction.** A secondary unknown-unknown class also sits here: **DOCTRINE-INDUCED BLINDNESS** — we stripped the reject verdict partly on a procedural rule (that "the street already agrees" is a sizing instrument, not a rejection). If that rule is simply wrong for a Strong-Buy microcap, our whole overturn is procedure, not evidence.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our size versus the tape:* we do not have average daily volume in the pack. Estimated at 1.5–4.0M shares/day (~$20–45M notional) based on post-squeeze microcaps with ~19% short interest at a $345M cap. At a 0.75%-of-book starter we are a rounding error — we cannot move this stock and we will not be trapped by our own size. This is one of the few clean facts here. **But the estimate is unverified and must be drained before any add.**
- *Predicted flow is against us:* an ATM sells into strength by design. Our upside case is exactly the condition that triggers issuer supply. We are buying a stock whose largest predictable seller gets more motivated the more right we are.
- *Crowding:* 18.59% short interest means violent two-way moves on the print. Squeeze risk works for us on a good number and against us on entry timing — if we chase a pop, we are buying from the ATM.
- *Doctrine interactions:* the verdict rests on three doctrinal rulings, and each one being wrong flips a piece of the case. (a) "Divergence-not-coverage" stripped the FATAL consensus tag. (b) The prize-table mandate was declared non-binding by *both* benches on the grounds that Velo3D is revenue-generating — we override that below, because the load-bearing unknowns are genuinely time-gated. (c) Both benches' detector dispatch was mis-keyed: consumer-goods and wrong-city permit connectors fired, while the one physically checkable claim (Livermore capacity and permitting, the gate on >30% H2 gross margin) has **no** connector at all. We are trading with a known blind spot in the exact place the margin promise lives.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be **issuer-side share supply (ATM refresh plus convertible and warrant conversion) grinding the price down through our bands while the business improves exactly as we expect — and if not that, then a single undisclosed private customer's order slipping, which we cannot see coming.**

**Vol read — and the honest limit on it.**

**No options chain was supplied with the evidence pack.** We therefore *cannot* report chain-level midpoint IV, IV percentile, realized vol, or the true variance risk premium, and we will **not** substitute the underlying aggregate IV feed — that is precisely the TSSI 71-vs-101 error. The chain pull is Unverified #7 and is armed as a missing sensor.

What we *can* compute is the table's own implied dispersion, and what it says about any option we might be offered:

- **σ_table = $3.98 = 34.3% of spot** over a ~0.36-year horizon (to mid-January 2027) → **≈57% annualized**.
- **Test to run the moment the chain arrives:** if chain midpoint IV > ~57%, the market is paying more for movement than our table thinks the movement is worth → **do not buy calls; route to the premium-selling gates.** Given a stock that went $2.87 → $31.75 → $11.58 in twelve months, realized vol is almost certainly well north of 57%, so the prior strongly favors selling premium over buying it.
- **Expression math, priced off the table itself:**
 - January $12.50 call: C_ours = Σ p·max(S−K,0) = 0.20 × $5.00 = **$1.00**.
 - January $15.00 call: C_ours = 0.20 × $2.50 = **$0.50**.
 - At ~57% vol and 0.36 years, a near-the-money call on an $11.58 stock is worth roughly $1.6–2.2 on the chain. **Our table says calls are expensive. Do not buy calls.**
 - December $10.00 put: P_ours = 0.30 × $1.25 + 0.15 × $4.85 = **$1.10**. If the chain bid is above $1.10, selling it is positive-EV on our own numbers, and assignment puts us long at an effective **$8.90** — inside our Add-1 band.
- **Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated on the Q3 print — branch A/B (margin test passes) versus C/D (it fails) are two separate humps, not one smooth distribution. A single-volatility model will misprice this. Use the discrete branches, not the smile.
- **TAX OVERLAY:** assuming this is a harvest year, the loss branch must land in 2026. **Any expiry after 2026-12-31 defers the loss into 2027 and is excluded.** That kills the January call ideas outright — and the edge does not numerically dominate ($1.00 of table value against a ~$2 offer is *negative*, not dominant). The December-18 expiry works and conveniently sits after the expected print. Note the conflict honestly: the Livermore Q4 catalyst may resolve after the December expiry, so the short put may be assigned before the second catalyst lands. We accept that, because assignment at $8.90 is an outcome we want.

3. **Excluded: long calls of any expiry** — our table values them below the likely offer.
