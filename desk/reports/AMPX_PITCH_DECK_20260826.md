# PITCH — AMPX — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

---

## What we believe

Amprius makes high-density silicon-anode battery cells. Revenue is real and growing fast: $62.6M in the first half of 2026 versus roughly half that a year earlier, gross margin up from 8.9% to 27.3%, and management raised full-year guidance twice to at least $140M.

A short-seller report in May 2026 ("Exaggerated Orders, Hollow Manufacturing") argued the orders were inflated and the factory was a story. The second-quarter results answered part of that: the revenue showed up, the cash gap narrowed, and the company signed a lease extension on its Fremont, California plant running to 2034.

Our starting belief was that the stock still carried a fraud discount the numbers no longer justify — a mispricing we could buy.

**After the court, we believe something narrower and less flattering.** The second-quarter print did not change the price. The stock closed at $10.12 the day before the results, spiked to $11.72 two days later, and gave it all back — it sits at roughly $10.41 today, up 2.9% from where it started, after a quarter that doubled revenue and raised guidance. So the market genuinely has *not* re-rated on the news. That part of the thesis survives.

What does not survive is the idea that this is a cheap stock. At ~146.1M shares and ~$10.12, the company is worth about $1.48B; strip out $74.5M of cash and the enterprise is ~$1.40B, or about **10.6× what Wall Street thinks it will sell this year** — on a business that still loses money. That is a growth multiple, not a discount.

And one thing we did not know when we opened the case: **Amprius is not really building its own factory.** In January 2026 it paid $20M in cash to walk away from a 774,155 sq ft, 15-year lease in Brighton, Colorado, wrote off $19.1M and $4.7M of construction-in-progress, and scrapped $3.5M of Fremont production equipment. It spent only $2.4M on plant and equipment in the whole first half of 2026 — 3.8% of revenue. Its own filing says it makes cells "by leveraging Berzelius' existing production line," a plant in Nanjing, China, owned by a company it describes as a *former* affiliate. Fremont is a 10 MWh pilot line for "quick turn prototypes."

**Net belief: the mispricing is real but small, the business risk is larger than we assumed, and the price is wrong for entry today.** Verdict is WAIT, gated on price.

---

## What the market believes

Ten analysts cover it. Their consensus is $132.1M of revenue this year and $207.9M next year — note they are *below* the company's own ≥$140M guidance by about 6%, so the street is already discounting management. Median price target is $22, range $18–30, roughly double the current price.

So the market is not calling this a fraud. The market believes it is a real, fast-growing supplier that will roughly double again next year, and it is paying accordingly. What the market has *not* done is pay up for the second-quarter proof point — the price is flat since the print. The most defensible reading is that buyers already assumed the growth and sellers already assumed the risk, and the quarter moved neither.

There is also a short-seller thesis in the market that the manufacturing is hollow. On the evidence, that thesis is substantially correct as a description of the facts, even though the revenue is real.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The post-print flat-line is genuine and specific.** The whole-year "up 51% off the low" statistic is misleading — that move happened *before* the quarter. On the correct window, revenue doubled and guidance rose and the stock went nowhere. That is the kind of gap worth watching.
2. **We read the company's own money, not its slides.** The abandoned Colorado factory, the $2.4M of capex, and the Nanjing production line are all in filings that most of the ten sell-side models appear not to have re-underwritten. The gross margin everyone quotes is, in substance, a price negotiated with one offshore supplier.
3. **We found the disclosure hole and know it will never close.** Because Berzelius is a *former* affiliate rather than a current one, it is not a related party, so its share of cost of goods will never appear in the related-party note. The company also still claims "emerging growth company" status four years after going public, which legally thins its disclosure. That is a structural blind spot we can name and others may be assuming away.

---

## Why it might be priced in

- The short report is public and was published in May. Ten analysts cover the name. The stock is 57% below its 52-week high. Nothing here is undiscovered.
- The "hollow manufacturing" finding is the short report's *title*. We did not find it; we confirmed it. That is diligence value, not information edge.
- The customer-concentration and Ukraine-exposure risks are disclosed in the 10-Q in plain text.
- If the price is flat because informed buyers and informed sellers netted out, then there is no discount to collect — just a fairly-priced high-variance asset. That is the honest base case.

## What could go wrong

**The pre-mortem. Three ways this loses money, worst-understood first.**

### 1. The margin was a gift, and we cannot ever check — *no monitorable tripwire*

Almost all cost of goods runs through one Chinese supplier that was, until recently, an affiliate. If that supplier is selling to Amprius below cost — as a legacy accommodation, a strategic subsidy, or a wind-down of an old arrangement — then the 27.3% gross margin is not a property of the business, and the multiple built on it is fiction.

**There is no sensor for this.** Not "we haven't built one" — there is no data source. Berzelius is a former affiliate, so it never appears in the related-party note; the emerging-growth-company exemption keeps the surrounding disclosure thin; and Berzelius is private and Chinese, so there is no counterparty filing to read. The one re-entry condition the bear bench proposed — "the FY26 10-K quantifies Berzelius at under 15% of cost of revenue" — **can never fire**, because that disclosure is not required and will not exist.

The only observable proxy is slow and indirect: if the company ever moves volume to Fremont and gross margin holds at 27%+, the transfer price was probably fair. If margin compresses as it insources, it was a subsidy. That test resolves in 2027 at the earliest, long after the multiple has re-rated in one direction or the other.

**Unknown-unknown class: *economics held entirely by a counterparty with no disclosure duty to us.*** This is the general family — off-balance-sheet supplier economics, undisclosed transfer pricing, single-source cost structures behind a private foreign entity. We should treat any thesis whose core exhibit is a margin produced by one undisclosed counterparty as un-underwritable at a growth multiple, regardless of how good the margin looks. That is the reusable lesson from this name.

### 2. The customers stop paying

Two customers hold 54% of receivables. Days-sales-outstanding is 109. That implies roughly $40.7M of receivables, of which ~$22.0M sits with two names — about 30% of the company's entire $74.5M cash balance — against a bad-debt reserve of $0.3M, or 0.7%. Ukraine was $20.5M of $62.6M of first-half revenue (33%); Europe/Middle East/Africa together was 64%. Finished-goods inventory rose 87% to $11.2M.

A ceasefire, a funding lapse, an export-license change, or one slow-paying defense buyer converts a growth story into a write-off. **This one has tripwires** — DSO, the two-customer receivable share, the reserve ratio, and the Ukraine revenue line all print quarterly.

### 3. The multiple simply compresses

10.6× this year's expected sales on a loss-maker, with a 52-week range of $6.67–$24.22, is a beta-heavy instrument. A risk-off tape, a growth de-rate, or a single missed quarter takes this to $6 without any new information. The subtle danger is that **our own $8.44 buy gate fills on the way down** — we get the price we asked for precisely because the thesis broke, not because the market blinked.

### The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own flow, our own rules

- **Size versus liquidity.** A 0.5%-of-book starter in a ~$1.4B NYSE name is very unlikely to move the tape. But **average daily volume was never verified in this court** — neither bench pulled it. The ≤1% ADV constraint we hold ourselves to is presumed satisfied, not measured. That is a gap, not a clearance.
- **We would be bidding into our own predicted flow.** We think the risks are Ukraine collection and a growth de-rate. Both push the price *down*. Putting a resting buy at $8.44 means we are queued to buy from exactly the sellers we expect to be right. The gate must therefore be an *event-conditional* entry, not a passive limit that catches falling knives.
- **A resting order through the print is an accidental bet against a big move.** The next results are around November 5. If a limit order sits at $8.44 through that date, the only way it fills is a bad print — we would be short volatility without having chosen to be. **The order must be cancelled before the print and re-armed after.**
- **Our own rules remove the obvious workaround.** The natural way to get paid to wait at $8.44 is to sell a put. We do not sell premium in the taxable account, so that route is closed. Entry has to be an outright purchase.
- **Tax.** Any exit within twelve months is taxed at ordinary rates. At starter size the dollar impact is trivial, but a fast stop-out here is a fully taxed loss of time, and the position offers no harvesting value at this size.
- **Short interest is unverified.** Neither bench got a feed. We do not know whether a decline would be cushioned or accelerated by short positioning, and we should not assume a squeeze in either direction.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the 27% gross margin we underwrote was a price set by one former-affiliate supplier in Nanjing rather than a property of the business — and we will not be able to prove that until it shows up as margin compression a year or more after the multiple has already broken.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry at today's price.** No position, no orders. Below are the reopen conditions, taken from the benches' own arithmetic.

**Current price:** ~$10.41 (vendor close 2026-08-26; broker gateway was unreachable — **re-poll before any order**).

**Fair-value ceiling for entry.** Both benches agree the current price is above any defensible value here. The bear's gate is $7.00, derived as roughly 7× consensus 2026 sales. The bull's gate is $8.44 — the July 29, 2026 close, a level the tape traded four weeks ago. **We propose $8.44 as the working gate**, because $7.00 requires a 33% decline and is effectively an order that never fills. $8.44 is about 8.6× consensus 2026 sales.

**Starter tranche — 0.5% of book.**
- Condition: price ≤ $8.44 **and** no adverse news explaining the decline (specifically: no customer-loss disclosure, no receivable write-down, no export-control action against the Nanjing supplier).
- This is *not* a resting limit order. It is a conditional entry checked manually, because a passive order at $8.44 buys from exactly the sellers our own pre-mortem says are right.
- **Any order must be cancelled before the Q3 results date and re-armed after.** A resting bid through a print is an unintended bet against a large move.

**Second tranche — additional 0.5%, maximum total 1.0% of book.**
- Only after the Q3 report, and only if *all three* hold:
  1. Quarterly revenue ≥ $38.7M (the run-rate needed for the ≥$140M full-year guide)
  2. Days-sales-outstanding ≤ 105 (from 109)
  3. Fremont capital spending steps up materially — the bear proposed >$15M in the second half as evidence the domestic line is real
- Absent evidence on the supplier-margin question, **1.0% is a hard cap.** Neither bench would size this larger, and the bear's position is that no price justifies more than a starter while the cost structure is unverifiable.

**Exits and trims.**
- **Immediate exit** on any export-control designation, entity-list addition, or sanctions action touching Berzelius (Nanjing) — that is a revenue cliff, not a drawdown.
- **Exit** if gross margin compresses below ~22% while Fremont volume rises. That is the subsidy test failing: if the transfer price was a gift, insourcing reveals it.
- **Exit** if two-customer receivable concentration exceeds 60% while DSO exceeds 120 days and the bad-debt reserve stays under 1%.
- **Trim** into any move above ~$18 (bottom of the analyst target range) — that is the market paying us for the growth case before the margin question resolves.
- Note: any exit inside twelve months is taxed at ordinary income rates. At ≤1% sizing this is immaterial, but it means there is no tax reason to hold a broken thesis.

**Catalyst dates.**
- **~2026-11-05 — Q3 2026 results. UNCONFIRMED.** This date is derived from a data vendor. No company press release names it. Q2 printed Aug 4, so early November is *consistent*, not *verified*. Confirm from an investor-relations announcement before treating it as a date.
- **December 2026 — claimed start of domestic NDAA-compliant production. UNCONFIRMED, and the filing language is weaker than the claim** ("research and adapt our supply chain," not "begin production").
- **~February–March 2027 — FY2026 annual report.** Will *not* quantify the Nanjing supplier's cost share. Do not build a trigger on it.
