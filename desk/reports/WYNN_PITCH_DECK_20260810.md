# PITCH — WYNN — PROPOSED VERDICT

**NO POSITION at $102.50 — reject the buy case as pitched; keep Wynn on a watch list with specific price and data triggers. PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION.**

## What we believe

Wynn's second-quarter 2026 "beat" was manufactured almost entirely by luck at one property. At Wynn Palace in Macau, the casino kept 29.7% of what mass-market players wagered, versus 22.3% a year earlier — a win-rate swing that added roughly $40–50M of quarterly profit without any underlying growth in customer demand. Every other part of the business declined: the older Macau peninsula property was flat ($95.5M vs $96.5M), Las Vegas profit fell 8.3% ($215.2M vs $234.8M), and Boston fell 12% ($56.1M vs $63.9M). Macau's market-wide July gaming revenue was down 8.4% year over year ([DICJ data](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-drops-8-4-year-over-year-in-july)).

Meanwhile the balance sheet is entering its most demanding stretch: $1.57B of cash against $10.72B of debt, $525–650M of equity still owed to the UAE project (now costing $5.7B after a $600M overrun, opening September 2027), Macau capital spending set to roughly double in 2027, a $600M convertible bond at the Macau subsidiary that holders can put back in March 2027, and only $326.1M left on the share buyback authorization. The stock's main support mechanism runs out of ammunition exactly when the cash calls arrive.

One more thing we believe because management said it on the earnings call: the company normalizes (adjusts out) lucky results in the VIP segment, where luck hurt them this quarter (−$8.6M), but presents the mass-market segment — where luck helped enormously — unadjusted. Their CFO's words: *"We tried to normalize for mass hold... nobody liked it. We reverted back to normalizing for VIP"* ([call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-wynn-resorts-tops-q2-2026-estimates-shares-rise-93CH-4835984)).

## What the market believes

The market treated the quarter as a genuine beat — shares rose on the print. Sell-side coverage is heavy and bullish: 17 analysts, average price target ~$123 (high $135, low $101), with 17 of 19 ratings at Buy or better ([consensus](https://www.investing.com/equities/wynn-resorts-ltd-consensus-estimates)). At $102.50 the stock is 24% below its 52-week high but the consensus view is a recovery story: Macau demand returning, Vegas resilient, and the UAE resort as a 2027 growth option. That consensus is anchored, at least in part, to a quarter whose profit was inflated by a win-rate coin flip.

## Why we might have edge

- **The decomposition is arithmetic, not opinion.** We took the quarter apart property by property from the [company's own press release](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/WYNN/wynn-resorts-limited-reports-second-quarter-2026-8fob0mlrj0tu.html). One property's win-rate anomaly is the entire beat; strip it and the run-rate sits roughly 6% below what consensus is capitalizing.
- **The asymmetric-adjustment finding is confirmed from management's mouth**, not inferred. When a company adjusts out bad luck but not good luck in the same report, the headline number is biased upward by construction. This is a repeatable pattern we are adding to our detection library for all casino-sector work.
- **The buyback-exhaustion fact appears under-appreciated.** $326.1M of remaining authorization against $525–650M of UAE equity calls, doubled 2027 Macau capex, and a 2027 convertible put is a specific, dated collision that the "return of capital" leg of the bull case ignores.
- **A dated, falsifiable test arrives September 1**: Macau's August revenue release either shows volume-led recovery or it doesn't, and we've defined in advance what counts (see entry bands).

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the other side, and our own second bench (the cross-examiner) reduced the bear case in three places:

- **The give-back may be smaller than the bear math says.** Some of the elevated 29.7% win rate may be *structural* — a shift toward premium mass-market customers mechanically raises the blended win rate. The theoretical "normal" rate isn't disclosed, so the ~$48M/quarter reversion figure is a model assumption, not a fact.
- **The consensus anchor is $123, not $133** as the original bear brief claimed — the gap between price and target is ~20%, not ~30%. The mispricing is smaller than first framed.
- **Vegas wasn't actually flattered by luck this quarter** — management disclosed luck *cost* Las Vegas $3.6M vs theoretical. The Vegas decline is real, but the "hold-flattered decline" amplifier was wrong.
- July's Macau weakness was partly one-off (typhoon, World Cup), and revenue was +9.4% month over month. An August rebound is plausible.
- With 17 analysts covering, this is a heavily-studied name; the market has had the same press release since August 4.

Net: the *long* is rejected on confirmed evidence, but the *short* is also unproven — reversion size is uncertain, the options market hasn't been checked, and the next earnings date is unconfirmed. Flat both directions.
