## What we believe

KKR is a fee-collecting asset manager whose stock is down about a third from its high (pack tape $101.73 vs $151.25 high; $82.54 low) while the visible, filed parts of the business are still printing records. Our read, after both benches:

1. **The fee engine is real and filed.** The Q2'26 numbers the bull case leans on ($1.32 of fee-related earnings per share, $1.68 of total operating earnings per share, $72bn of uncommitted capital at roughly 90 basis points of fees) are not a podcast claim — they sit in the earnings release furnished with the 2026-07-30 8-K (accession 0001404912-26-000022). The red bench's "transcript-only" attack shrank to a labeling complaint once blue traced the accession red itself cited.
2. **The big August exit is mostly the company's own money, not client money.** USI Insurance Services sits in KKR's balance-sheet ("Strategic Holdings") bucket after KKR bought out more than half of its co-investor's stake in 2023. So the Aon sale is a one-time gain that largely lands at KKR & Co. — reported around $2bn of adjusted net income — rather than the thin carry slice the bear case assumed.
3. **The scariest live leg — investors pulling money out of the retail private-market funds — already cooled.** K-FIT repurchase requests were 1.65% of shares in Q2 and were met in full, versus 6.3% requested and only ~80% filled the quarter before.
4. **What we are actually being paid for is one unknown:** the credit marks inside Global Atlantic's insurance general account, which is consolidated and which the filings do not break out. Everything else is either public or small.

At $101.73 the stock is about 19.3x annualized Q2 fee-related earnings and 15.6x annualized adjusted net income on roughly 920mm adjusted shares (~$94bn market value). That is a mid-range multiple for this group, not a distressed one. **So this is a starter-size idea, not a fat one.**

## What the market believes

The market believes the earnings quality is worse than the headline and that the discount is deserved:

- Growth is being part-funded with expensive hybrid paper: $2.59bn of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred (51.75mm shares, priced 3/2025, converts ~2028-03-01) plus $590mm of 6.875% subordinated notes due 2065 against legacy 4.625% 2061 notes — a 225 basis point step in cost of capital, about $13mm/year on that one deal, and ~$162mm/year of preferred dividends that get paid before common.
- With the stock below the ~$120.77 initial conversion price, holders get the **maximum** 0.4140 ratio: ~21.4mm new shares, about 2.3% dilution, already scheduled.
- Private-credit stress is visible in KKR's own listed vehicle: FSK non-accruals 7.1% at cost / 3.8% at fair value, NAV down 2.8% in a quarter to $18.30, debt/equity 1.27x.
- Most damning: **the tape did not pay for the record print.** On results day the stock closed *down* 0.53% at $98.75, even against a consensus beat ($1.63 vs $1.43 expected). Six weeks later it is only 2.9% higher. The good news is public and the discount persisted.
- The company also pulled formal EPS guidance while touting $72bn of dry powder and 30+ product launches.

## Why we might have edge

- **One genuinely unpriced variable.** Nobody in either bench could quantify Global Atlantic's general-account below-investment-grade and asset-based-finance marks. It is the only leg capable of explaining why the market ignored a record print. If the November release shows those marks bounded, the mechanism for the discount disappears and the multiple has room.
- **A live mis-attribution we can arbitrage in analysis.** The standard sell-side reflex on an alt-manager exit is "that's fund money, we only get carry." That reflex is wrong here because the asset migrated onto the balance sheet. Our own bear bench made exactly that error, which is decent evidence the street can make it too.
- **A stale fear.** The redemption scare is one quarter out of date (6.3% prorated → 1.65% met in full). Desks that stopped updating after the bad quarter are carrying the wrong prior.
- **Timing.** The Nov 3–7 release is ~37 trading days out, so we are not buying into a print we can't underwrite, and we have time to arm staged adds rather than chase.

## Why it might be priced in

- 19.3x forward-ish fee earnings is not cheap-for-a-reason territory; it is roughly where this cohort trades. The honest version of the bear case is **"we simply agree with the street at 19x."** Red flagged that and it survives as a sizing constraint, not a kill.
- The market had the same $1.32 and the same beat we have, and sold it. Absent the Global Atlantic mechanism, we are just paying for a drawdown.
- The dilution, the preferred dividends, the coupon step and the FSK credit damage are all in filed documents. None of it is secret.
- The $72bn "fee annuity" is smaller than it looks: $72bn × 90bp = $648mm **gross**; after a 25% compensation share and 21% tax it is about **$384mm**, roughly 0.41% of market value — and it only shows up if the capital actually gets deployed, which is the exact thing a frozen-exit market prevents.
- The USI gain is one-time and sits in the low-quality earnings bucket the bull case elsewhere disparages. You cannot capitalize it.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the hidden one, with NO monitorable tripwire.** Global Atlantic's general account takes credit damage that never appears as a discrete, datable event: it bleeds through reserve assumptions, level-3 marks, and reinvestment spread over several quarters, disclosed only in aggregate and only at prints. There is no 8-K, no covenant, no ratings action we can sensor — the loss is *inside a 100%-owned insurance balance sheet that reports on its own schedule*. We cannot arm a tripwire for it; we can only re-underwrite four times a year.
**Unknown-unknown class named: CONSOLIDATED-OPACITY RISK** — a loss housed inside a fully-consolidated subsidiary whose risk disclosures are coarser than the thesis needs. Second-order sibling class: **DOCTRINE/REGIME RISK** — a rule change on retail access to private credit (suitability, NAV-pricing, redemption gating standards) that re-rates the whole distribution engine with no issuer-level filing to watch.

**Failure narrative 2 — we are right about the facts and wrong about the price.** The November release quantifies Global Atlantic, the marks are fine, and the stock does nothing, because 19.3x was always fair and the drawdown was a rate/credit-cycle de-rating of the entire group, not a KKR error. We then own a mid-multiple financial into a widening credit cycle. *Tripwire: exists* — post-print, if the release is clean and the stock fails to hold $100 within five sessions, the mechanism is dead and we cut to zero.

**Failure narrative 3 — the funding channel keeps getting more expensive.** Another hybrid deal at 7%+, or the preferred dividend stack growing, tells us the fee annuity is being bought with dilutive capital. Combined with the locked-in maximum 0.4140 conversion ratio, the per-share arithmetic degrades even if the business grows. *Tripwire: exists* — any new 424B5 for preferred or ultra-long subordinated notes at a coupon ≥150bp above the prior comparable issue.

**Reflexive / structural leg.** Our own size is not the problem: KKR trades an estimated 4–6mm shares/day (~$450–600mm notional; **estimate, not fetched — item 9 below**), so a full 1.8%-of-book sleeve is a low-single-digit fraction of one day's volume and we are a price-taker, not a price-maker. The structural flow that *does* matter is not ours: ~21.4mm shares of scheduled mandatory-convertible supply into 2028-03-01, with convertible-arbitrage desks already short against it — that shorting dampens rallies and is unrelated to fundamentals. Doctrine interactions cut against us in one specific way: under **bands-rest-by-default**, our adds at $95/$91/$87 do *not* fire unless explicitly armed, so a fast flush after a clean print can pass through all three rungs and leave us with only the starter — the doctrine that protects us from catching a falling knife is the same doctrine that leaves us underweight if we are right. The print-proximity doctrine also gives us no cover: the 5-day pre-print trigger does not fire at 37 trading days, so nothing forces a reconstruction between now and November.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that Global Atlantic's general-account credit marks were the market's real objection all along, they are worse than our comp-based estimate, and they surface too slowly and too coarsely for any sensor we can arm to get us out first.
