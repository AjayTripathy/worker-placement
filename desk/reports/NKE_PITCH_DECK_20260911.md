## What we believe

1. **The reported numbers are distorted in both directions, and both benches distorted them further.** Nike's FY26 gross margin of 42.9% includes about 213bps of one-time tariff-refund benefit ($986M / $46.4B revenue). Strip the credit *and* leave the matched tariff cost in, as the red bench did, and you get a fake collapse (40.8% vs 42.7%). Strip both, and FY26 is roughly flat versus FY25 (~42.9% vs 42.7%) — which is also what the matched quarter says (40.2% vs 40.3%, −10bps). Our working view: **margins are flattish on a tariff-neutral basis, not collapsing**.
2. **Earnings are down, but by single digits, not 27%.** FY26 EPS $2.10 includes $0.52 of benefit → $1.58 "ex-benefit." That is not comparable to FY25's $2.16, because FY25 carried roughly two months of tariff cost and FY26 carried nine-plus. Adding back the disclosed tariff cost after tax (~$0.51–$0.80/share) puts tariff-neutral FY26 EPS near **$2.10–$2.35**. At $36.885 that is **~16–18x**, not 23x.
3. **China is a real demand problem that is smaller than the headline.** Q4 China: digital −25%, stores −9%, wholesale −19%; FY26 China comp store sales −6%. Deliberate channel cleanup shows up in digital and wholesale; **store comps are the least-mediated read, and they say roughly mid-single-digit true demand erosion**, amplified to −17% constant-currency by Nike's own actions.
4. **We do not yet have a number worth betting.** Neither bench published an estimate for the period the trade turns on. Street FY27 is ~$1.72; JPMorgan is $1.55. Without our own tariff-neutral FY27 EPS and a named mechanism, this is a valuation opinion, not an edge.
5. **Management's own behavior argues against "dislocation."** At a 52-week low, FY26 buyback was **$123M / 1.8M shares** against a ~$2.4B dividend, with cash+short-term investments $9.0B (down $0.1B) and operating cash flow — including ~$0.3B of one-time tariff cash — more than consumed by dividends and capex. People who think their stock is mispriced usually buy more of it.
6. **Our scenario table says the stock is above fair value.** Probability-weighted EV is **$30.53 vs $36.885 spot (−17%)**. That, not rhetoric, is the reason for no entry.

## What the market believes

- FY27 EPS of about **$1.72**, with the bear desk (JPM, Underweight) at **$1.55** on China plus rising competition, against management's "flattish H1 FY27 EPS" and "slightly positive" Q1 gross margin.
- That the refund masked the operating story: the masking was the *headline* of print coverage in June 2026, not a discovery. The red bench filed it as a NOVEL kill; the kill-class audit reclassified it and four others as **CONSENSUS (0 novel / 5 consensus)**.
- That the brand is structurally weakened in China and in performance running, and that the S&P 100 deletion (effective before the open 2026-09-21) is a fair marker of lost stature.
- Net: the market believes in a fairly-priced, slow, self-managed decline. **We largely agree with the market's number.** That is the honest headline of this deck.

## Why we might have edge

- **The decomposition nobody did.** Both benches argued about aggregates; neither built a tariff-neutral FY27 bridge. The inputs exist in primary text (10-K segment note, disclosed tariff cost, refund accrual split). If our tariff-neutral FY27 EPS lands ≥$1.98 (+15% vs consensus) with a stated mechanism, the multiple is ~15–16x on a stabilizing base, and consensus is anchored to a distorted FY26 print.
- **The supply-vs-demand split is resolvable with disclosed data,** and it is the crux. Store comps (−6%) versus digital (−25%) is a test the street is not running because the headline constant-currency number is easier to quote.
- **A real optionality item with a date:** ~$686M of the $986M is an *unreceived* accrual; the revoking order did not address refunds. Cash receipt is a discrete, checkable event that cuts both ways.
- **The tariff leg has statutory sunsets that the bear case misread.** Section 122 caps at 15% for not more than 150 days absent an Act of Congress ([congress.gov CRS IF13199](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13199)); it took effect 2026-02-24 and lapsed 2026-07-24 absent legislation, and the Court of International Trade struck the Section 122 tariffs in May 2026 (government appealing). If the successor regime is materially lighter than FY26's ~$1.0–1.5B run-rate, FY27 cost relief is mechanical.

## Why it might be priced in

- The refund-masking narrative was public on print day. Nothing in our margin work is secret; it is arithmetic anyone can do from the press release.
- Sell-side has already cut. A $1.55 low estimate means the bear case is on the tape, not ahead of it.
- New lows, a Dow/S&P 500 member, ~$54.6B cap, exhaustive coverage: this is the least likely place for an information edge. Our edge claim is *framing* (tariff-neutral), and framing edges are thin and short-lived.
- Our own detector stack came back mostly unusable (reviews = bot wall / baseline-only; app review velocity unresolved; thermal miscalibrated for distribution centers). We are left with public filings — the same inputs everyone else has.
- The one thing we could not out-argue: management, holding the best information, is buying $123M of stock at the low.

## What could go wrong (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

**Failure narrative 1 — "Tariff-neutral" was never the right frame (monitorable).** We normalize away a cost that is permanent under a substituted legal authority. The Section 122 surcharge lapses, and Section 232 or a negotiated replacement lands at equal or higher cost, so tariff-neutral EPS is a number no shareholder ever receives. *Tripwire:* Federal Register / USTR primary text on the successor regime; Q1 FY27 gross margin ex-tariff not up year over year; any 8-K quantifying FY27 tariff cost above ~$1.0B.

**Failure narrative 2 — Demand-lost, not supply-withdrawn (partly monitorable).** Store comps deteriorate from −6% toward −10% and beyond, confirming the search-heat detector's read (NIKE CN 34.9 DECAYING; Jordan 11.8 DECAYING) rather than management's "marketplace health" framing. Earnings power resets to $1.30–1.55 and 13–15x gives $17–23. *Tripwire:* China store comps and digital split in the Q1 FY27 release and 10-Q.

**Failure narrative 3 — Brand-relevance decay with NO monitorable tripwire.** The shoes stop being what people want, gradually, in a way no filing, comp, refund or index event reports until it is in the revenue line two to six quarters later. There is no disclosure line item for "cultural relevance," our reviews and app-velocity detectors are dark, and search heat is jointly caused by fewer launches — so we cannot distinguish deliberate scarcity from fading desire in real time. **Unknown-unknown class: slow-moving consumer preference regime change — a taste shift with no reporting surface ("silent substitution"). We name it and accept we cannot sensor it.** Partial mitigation only: cap size, and require two consecutive quarters of store-comp improvement before adding.

**Reflexive / structural leg.** Our own footprint is not the risk here. A starter of 1.5% of book on any plausible book size is tens of thousands of shares — well under 1% of Nike's daily volume — so we cannot move this tape and cannot be trapped by our own exit. The predicted external flow is also smaller than the red bench implied: the S&P 100 deletion touches only S&P-100-dedicated assets, while NKE stays in the S&P 500, the Dow and the NYSE. On $15–60B of S&P-100-tracking assets at a 25–40bps weight, forced supply is roughly **1.0–6.5M shares ≈ 0.1–0.5 days of volume** — sentiment, not mechanics. The genuine reflexive risk is the *other* side: short interest reported up ~20% to ~68.8M shares (red's figure, unverified) means a squeeze on any good Q1 headline could force us to buy our reopen band at a worse price than our math allows — and the doctrine interaction is that our own "don't chase, wait for the gate" rule guarantees we miss that move. We accept missing it. A second doctrine interaction: DIVERGENCE-NOT-COVERAGE stopped a rejection here, so a WATCH can persist indefinitely on a name we never build an estimate for. Mitigation: the reopen gate has a date and dies if unmet.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a cheap-looking tariff-neutral multiple on a brand whose consumer demand was still quietly eroding, and the tariff relief we normalized into the model was replaced by a successor duty regime we never verified.
