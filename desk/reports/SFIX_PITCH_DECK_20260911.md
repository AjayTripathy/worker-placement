## What we believe

Stitch Fix is a shrinking-but-stabilizing business trading at roughly the value of its own cash pile, eight trading days before the one number that matters — the first FY2027 guide.

The specifics we think are true:

1. **The business stopped falling off a cliff and started sliding.** Active clients were ~2,309,000 in August 2025 and ~2,309,000 in May 2026 — dead flat over three quarters. But the year-over-year decline improved from −7.9% to −1.9%, the quarter-over-quarter number flipped from negative to +0.9%, and revenue per active client went $549 → $578 (+5.3%). Flat clients spending more is not growth, but it is not the −8% bleed the tape is still paying for.
2. **The cheap headline multiple is real but not as cheap as it looks.** At the pack's 129.79M share count, market cap is ~$381M and enterprise value ~$152M against a $49–52M adjusted EBITDA guide — about 3x. On our own updated share estimate (~140M, see below) EV is closer to $180M and the multiple is ~3.6x. On actual cash (Q3 free cash flow $6.5M → ~$26M run-rate) it is 6–7x. Cheap, not absurd.
3. **This management sandbags.** Last year the same setup — Q4 FY25 guide $298–303M, consensus above the ceiling at $304.69M — produced an actual of $311.2M, 2.7% above the company's own high end, and the stock went up. They also raised the FY26 EBITDA guide from $30–45M to $49–52M during the year.
4. **The Q4 "sequential collapse" is a calendar.** The guide implies −4.64% revenue quarter-on-quarter; last year's same step was −4.25%. The Q4 adjusted EBITDA guide midpoint is $8.5M against $8.7M actual a year ago — flat.
5. **Therefore the asymmetry is in the guide, not the quarter.** With 11% of float short, $229.4M of net cash, and $104.9M of buyback authorization left, a clean FY27 guide has a squeeze attached to it.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a melting ice cube with good optics.

The tape is not confused — it is decided. The tariff question was answered on 2026-06-10 and the stock fell ~16% in the month afterward, underperforming the S&P, and now sits at $2.935: 50.6% below the 52-week high and 4.1% above the 52-week low. The market's view, stated fairly: a subscription-shaped retailer whose client count has not grown in three quarters, which dilutes ~6%/yr, whose adjusted EBITDA leans on a stock-compensation add-back, and which spent 10.2% of revenue on advertising in a quarter against a 9–10% full-year commitment, deserves to trade at cash. Sell-side has quietly agreed with the direction — consensus revenue was cut 1.33% over three months — while still sitting $5M above the company's own guide ceiling at $332.12M.

That is a coherent view. Nothing in our work says it is stupid. Our claim is narrower: it is a view about the last four quarters being extrapolated across a date where the company gets to speak about the next four.

## Why we might have edge

- **Both courts agree the refuted story isn't the priced story — and that cuts both ways.** The bear brief that "proved" tariffs didn't hit margins won an argument nobody was having. But so did the bear reading of the Q4 guide: our own blue bench differenced the guided −4.64% sequential step against the prior-year −4.25% actual step and found 39 basis points of difference. Almost nobody does that arithmetic; the bear finding that it kills was graded FATAL by a competent bench before it was checked.
- **A published base rate, not a hunch.** "Consensus above the guide ceiling ⇒ headline miss" is the intuitive mechanism and it was wrong at this exact issuer twelve months ago in the same fiscal quarter. The primary is on the tape: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1576942/000157694225000026/exhibit991-q325.htm.
- **A stale data field that both benches and the pack mishandled.** The SBC series in the pack is Q1-of-each-fiscal-year and it is decaying ~37%/yr. The bear froze the last stale print at $12.65M/qtr to manufacture "SBC = the entire EBITDA." We stepped the series forward one year (not two, which is where the bull bench overshot): ~$8.0M/qtr ≈ **$32M/yr**, roughly 0.62x the EBITDA guide. That defuses the single loudest bear line and, awkwardly, means the bear's own proposed screen (`adjusted_ebitda_equals_sbc_screen`, fires between 0.8x and 1.2x) does not fire on its own example.
- **Structural supply/demand.** 11% short float, $104.9M of remaining authorization on a ~$380M cap (27%), no lock-up, no follow-on. That is not a thesis; it is a reason the right thesis pays more than its fundamentals.

## Why it might be priced in

Plainly: most of what we just said is public and cheap to know.

- Post-audit, the kill ledger is **1 novel / 6 consensus**. The active-client decline is the single most-covered line in this name. The advertising overage was said out loud on a public call. The dilution is in the share count. None of that is edge.
- The one genuinely off-narrative finding — that the "sequential collapse" is seasonality — **runs against the bear, not for us**, which means our differentiated insight is a correction to a bad bear argument, not a discovery about the business. Correcting a strawman leaves you at consensus, not ahead of it.
- Cash-as-floor is the oldest small-cap value trap in the book, and $2.935 against ~$1.65/share of net cash is exactly the zone where the market is saying "the operating business is worth about $1.20 and shrinking."
- The board bought stock at a $3.36 average against a $2.94 tape. That can be read as support; it can equally be read as a capital allocator who was wrong about the price, which is information about how the insiders forecast, not a reason to pay up.
- If we are wrong about sandbagging, everything else collapses at once: the guide, the multiple, and the squeeze.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative #1 — the announcement we cannot see (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).**
The 9/23 release carries something not in the model: a CEO or CFO transition, a goodwill or inventory write-down, a subsequent-events note in the 10-K, a strategic review, a convertible, or a restatement. We hold a 0.5% starter into a sealed envelope. Nothing in the tape, the seven Form 144s, the two 13G/As, the app-review feed, or the buyback tells us. There is no sensor for this and we should stop pretending there could be.
**Unknown-unknown class: principal-agent discontinuity — board-timed disclosure.** The category of events that are *manufactured on a date the issuer controls* rather than *observed in a process we can sample*. Satellites, permits, app velocity, and short interest are all sampling instruments; they are structurally blind to a decision made in a boardroom last Tuesday. The only defenses are position size and the fact that this class is symmetric (a $400M special dividend would be the same surprise pointed the other way).

**Failure narrative #2 — the slow bleed (tripwires exist).**
No event; just decay. FY27 guides to flat revenue, adjusted EBITDA drifts back to $40–45M because advertising runs 10.5%+ to buy the same clients twice, dilution keeps adding ~6%/yr, and the name grinds to $2.20 while we tell ourselves it's cheap. *Tripwires:* quarterly actives < 2.286M; advertising > 10.5% of revenue; daily close below $2.82 (52-week low); diluted shares above the prior quarter; a second app-review snapshot confirming the recent-cohort 3.23 rating is a real cohort shift rather than a blend artifact.

**Failure narrative #3 — right on the business, wrong on the multiple, and our own exit is the problem (the reflexive/structural leg).**
- **Our size vs ADV.** The pack has no ADV field and `desk/capacity_check.py` was down at bench time. Our estimate is 3.0–5.0M shares/day (~$9–15M notional). At a $50M book, 0.5% = ~85k shares ≈ 2–3% of ADV: costless. At a $500M book, 0.5% = ~850k shares ≈ 20–28% of ADV: two to three days in, and materially more than that coming out into a gap. **Size must be set from the real ADV print, not from the percentage.**
- **Predicted flow.** The largest reliable bid in this tape is the company itself ($104.9M authorized, $3.36 average). Issuers typically go dark ahead of results. We are proposing to buy in precisely the window where the price-insensitive buyer is most likely absent, and the persistent weekly Form 144 drip is still offering. We are buying thin and selling thinner.
- **Doctrine interactions.** Our own rules collide. "No adds within 2 sessions of the print" plus "exit on the print if actives < 2.286M or FY27 revenue guide < $1.32B" means our entire exit lands in the same 9:30am gap as every stop-loss and every disappointed holder, in a name where 11% of float is short and will be covering *into* us on the good branch and running *with* us on the bad one. The rule that protects sizing manufactures the worst fill. The honest consequence: model the bad branch at −25% realized, not at the −22% the table's node implies.

*If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be* that the FY27 guide lands below $1.32B of revenue with adjusted EBITDA back under $45M — confirming that flat actives plus ~6%/yr dilution is a permanent condition rather than a trough — and we will pay an extra several hundred basis points on top of that for exiting a $10M-a-day stock through the same door as everyone else.
