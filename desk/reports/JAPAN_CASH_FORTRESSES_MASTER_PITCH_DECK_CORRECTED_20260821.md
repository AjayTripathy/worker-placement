## The honest thesis

We are buying profitable, effectively debt-free Japanese manufacturers at **0.37×–0.81× book**
where net cash is a large fraction of the market cap — verified name by name, not asserted. The
return comes from three places, in order of reliability:

1. **The balance sheet floor** — real, audited, and the reason downside is bounded;
2. **Idiosyncratic re-rating events** — MBOs, buybacks, parent squeeze-outs, activist stakes.
   The four precedents the original cited are all genuine (Citizen's 25.6% cancellation, DNP's
   ¥300B buyback, the Taisei Oncho MBO, Dai-ichi's Benefit One topping bid);
3. **Modest carry while waiting** — the sleeve-weighted dividend is **~2.7%**, not 3.8%.

What the thesis is **not**: a +7.3% carry trade (see corrections), and not a mechanical TSE
forcing function. The TSE regime is comply-or-explain disclosure pressure — strongest on Prime
(where 7239 lives), weakest on the Standard-market microcaps that make up most of this cohort.
The precedents are real but skew Prime/large-cap; for the microcaps, the catalyst is the
controlling family's decision, and the position is priced so that waiting is cheap.
