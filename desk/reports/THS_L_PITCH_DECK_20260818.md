# PITCH — THS.L — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-06 — STARTER, SMALL, GATED: buy 0.35% of the book at or below ~120p; conviction 5/10; no adds until the mid-October production report clears the price gate, and a Zimbabwe funding close without a written right to take dollars out makes the stock *worse*, not better.**

---

## What we believe

Tharisa is a South African open-pit mine that produces two things at once from the same rock: platinum-group metals (about 46% of revenue) and chrome concentrate (about 54%). It is listed in London and Johannesburg. At ~118p the whole company is worth about $478M against roughly $209M of annualised operating earnings — a little over two times. That is a price that says the market expects something to go badly wrong.

Our view is narrower than "it's cheap":

1. **The existing South African business alone justifies the price.** Chrome, the larger revenue line, has risen for five straight quarters — $235/t trough in the March 2025 quarter to $306/t in the June 2026 quarter, +30%. Reserve life at the mine was reassessed upward from 74.5Mt to 109.8Mt. The underground extension is fully funded ($179M arranged, peak funding requirement cut by about $45M). None of that requires anything to be believed about Zimbabwe.
2. **Zimbabwe (Karo) is effectively free at this price.** The market is paying close to nothing for it. Our red team's central argument was that the Zimbabwe asset is about to be written down; we tested that against the actual accounts and it does not hold up yet (details below).
3. **The two things that would actually break the thesis are both measurable, dated, and near.** Cash burn and the price Tharisa actually *receives* for its metals. Both get reported in mid-October and again in late November.

That is why the position is a starter and not a real position: we think the price is wrong, and we think the two ways we'd find out we're wrong report inside 100 days.

## What the market believes

The market believes a version of this that is not stupid:

- **Metal prices are rolling over.** The basket Tharisa actually realised went $3,038/oz (Mar-2026 quarter) → $2,681/oz (Jun-2026 quarter) → roughly $2,412/oz quarter-to-date. That is a two-quarter decline, 21% off the peak. Our own blue team initially argued the forward market "prices no decline" and was wrong — a forward curve above spot is a financing cost, not a forecast.
- **Chrome's rise is built on something unhealthy.** South African chrome exports went 17.8 → 20.6 → 23.8Mt (2023→2025) and are running near 29Mt annualised. The reason isn't demand — it's that South African ferrochrome smelting collapsed from 3.2Mt to 1.6Mt, so ore that used to be smelted at home is exported raw instead. Chinese port stocks passed 5Mt in July, +68% year over year. Spot has already softened to roughly $283/t — approximately the $285/t that Tharisa itself uses in its own project economics.
- **The company is building two mines at once with a shrinking cash pile.** Net cash fell from $54.7M to $10.7M in a single quarter. Underground capital spending is heavily back-loaded ($16.3M of $76.7M spent in the first half), while Zimbabwe is funded concurrently on fiscal terms that have never been agreed in writing.
- **Zimbabwe doesn't pay you your dollars.** The central bank kept its 30% surrender requirement at the February 2026 policy statement, and unpaid local-currency counterpart balances owed to miners reached $228M industry-wide by June 2026 (Zimplats $78M, Unki roughly $100M). One peer shelved a $400M expansion indefinitely.

Roughly two times earnings is what those five facts, taken together, price.

## Why we might have edge

Four specific places where we did work the market does not appear to have done:

1. **Nobody has independently challenged the underground risk.** Tharisa has never mined underground. It is committing to do so while its open-pit life has been quietly walked down from 2041 (2021 guidance) to "end 2032" (2026 decks) — nine years of life removed in under five years. The one July 2026 presentation page says both "8+ years" and "end 2032." Published analyst coverage (Peel Hunt, BMO, Berenberg) treats the build as affordable and stops there. We found no third-party critique of the execution risk itself. That is a real coverage gap.
2. **We sized the Zimbabwe cash problem; the sell-side asserts it.** The distinction that matters is not "will Karo get built" but "if it is built, can the parent get the dollars home." At a maintained 30% surrender and an industry-wide arrears balance of $228M and rising, a fully funded, fully built Karo still faces a standing haircut on remitted cash. That inverts the usual logic: a funding close on the wrong terms is a *negative* event.
3. **Karo is not a small Tharisa, and both the company's framing and the market's shorthand treat it as one.** The metal mix is materially different — Karo is roughly Pt 43.5 / Pd 38.3 / Au 8.6 / Rh 3.8 / Ru 3.8 / Ir 1.9, versus Tharisa's own Pt 52.9 / Ru 16.0 / Pd 15.9 / Rh 10.5 / Ir 4.5 / Au 0.2. Karo is levered to palladium — the weakest metal in the complex. Applying Tharisa's realised basket to Karo's economics overstates them. Neither our own initial thesis nor our red team caught this until a second pass.
4. **The chrome scare that isn't there yet.** The "25% South African chrome export tax" that circulates in coverage has never been enacted. What exists is a consultation on an *export permit* regime — [Government Gazette Notice 6712, 3 October 2025](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202510/53477gon6712.pdf), with the comment period extended to 4 March 2026 by [Notice 7139](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202602/54157gn7139.pdf). The 25% figure comes from a leaked draft, not an instrument. Budget 2026/27 and the July 2026 draft tax bills are silent on it. It survives only as a target line in an industrial strategy document. If it is priced in as a certainty, it is priced wrongly — though see the concession below.

## Why it might be priced in

We hold these against ourselves honestly:

- **The stock is not undiscovered.** It is covered by three brokers, it is up 35% year over year, and — critically — **it did not reprice on a single Zimbabwe event**. Half-year results went up; the stock rose 4% after Act 6/2026. If the market were mispricing Zimbabwe risk, we would expect the tape to show surprise somewhere. It shows none. That is genuine evidence against our edge.
- **The chrome tax may be priced as an expectation, and expectations can be right.** Tharisa's own investor-relations head said on record he is "unsure if Tharisa's chrome production will attract the proposed tax." Concentrate *would* be captured by the tariff heading in question (HS 2610.00 covers "ores **and concentrates**"). Our finding is that it hasn't happened — not that it won't.
- **Cheapness at two times earnings on a two-quarter declining revenue line is not obviously cheap.** If the realised basket keeps falling at the current rate, the multiple expands on its own without the price moving.
- **The single most load-bearing assumption in the whole plan is unvalidated and nobody has needed to argue about it.** Tharisa's underground study assumes $40.8/t mining cost against a current open-pit cost of $49.8/t per reef tonne, which is itself up 29.4% year over year. That is an assumed unit-cost *decrease* going underground. We searched for a single South African Bushveld precedent of an operator cutting unit mining cost by going underground and found none. Every comparable benchmark runs the other way (Booysendal R1,350/t, Two Rivers R1,489/t and +58% in four years, Zondereinde R3,636/t). The market may simply be right to disbelieve this.
- **The underground precedent record is worse than the overrun percentages suggest.** Cost overruns at comparable South African builds were only +9% to +28%. But two of the five projects we examined — Two Rivers Merensky and Bokoni — were mothballed mid-build having spent most of the budget, with zero production. The percentage understates the damage. And Tharisa's construction contract with Cementation is open-book cost-plus: the contractor is de-risked, Tharisa carries the overrun.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**A note on provenance, stated plainly:** the adjudication returned the sizing instruction (starter, small, 0.35% of the book, gated) but its "reopen gates" block came through **empty**. So the bands below are assembled from the two teams' own numbers, and every level is labelled with where it came from. Nothing here is invented from a chart. All prices in pence.

**Initial entry — do this now**
- **BUY 0.35% of the book at or below 120p** (last 118.0p; bid 117 / ask 119). This is the adjudicated size. Our blue team argued for 40–60bps after its first pass and 40–60bps after its self-correction; the adjudicator cut it further to 35bps. Take the adjudicated number.
- Basis: at this price the market values Zimbabwe at approximately zero, and the South African business alone — annualised operating earnings ~$209M, chrome +5.5% quarter over quarter, underground fully funded at $179M, reserve life reassessed 74.5Mt → 109.8Mt — carries the price.
- **Do not chase above 120p.** The entire argument is the multiple. Above ~145p the "South Africa alone carries it" claim stops working at any earnings level we can defend, and the position must be re-adjudicated rather than added to. *(Deck arithmetic from bench inputs, not a bench-issued level.)*

**Add — first opportunity, 13 October 2026 (Q4 production report)**
- **Add up to +25bps (taking the position to 0.60%) only if the achieved basket for the quarter is ≥$2,400/oz.** This is our blue team's gate 4 read forward: below $2,400 for two consecutive quarters is an exit, so a single quarter at or above it is the minimum condition for adding.
- Second condition on the same add: net cash must not have fallen materially below the $10.7M reported. A further large draw with capital spending still back-loaded ($60.4M of the $76.7M FY26 underground budget still to spend) means the funding buffer is gone.
- If the basket prints between $2,300 and $2,400: **hold, do not add, do not sell.** One weak quarter inside a known downtrend is not new information.

**Add on weakness — conditional, not automatic**
- If the price falls to **≤100p with no gate below tripped**, add +25bps. At 100p the market capitalisation is roughly $405M, or about 1.9× annualised earnings, on a business whose larger revenue line has risen for five straight quarters. *(Deck arithmetic from bench inputs.)*
- **This add is void if any exit condition has fired.** A fall driven by a tripped gate is information, not a discount.

**Exits and halts — from the benches verbatim**
1. **Net cash turning materially negative at FY26 results with no funding close → halt** (no further capital, position frozen).
2. **No Karo financial close and no explicit deferral by the FY26 results → do not add**, at any price.
3. **The FY26 audit must reconcile the 12.8% vs 14.3% discount-rate discrepancy and disclose headroom.** An unexplained restatement, or a Karo impairment booked on an *unchanged* metal basket → **exit in full**.
4. **Achieved basket below $2,400/oz for two consecutive quarters → exit in full.** (Tightened from $2,300 after the second pass, given the two-quarter downtrend.)
5. **Any Karo funding close that does not carry an explicit, enforceable right to repatriate US dollars → treat the funded case as WORSE than the deferred case and do not add.** This one is counterintuitive and it is the reason the position stays small: good news on funding can be bad news on value.

**Sizing summary:** open at 0.35%. Hard ceiling 0.60% without re-adjudication. There is no path to a full position on the current evidence.

**Catalyst dates**
- **~13 October 2026** — Q4 FY26 production report (quarter ending 30 September). Carries the achieved basket and chrome price. *Date is our estimate from the company's own reporting rhythm — unconfirmed by the company.*
- **24 or 30 November 2026** — FY26 results. Our two internal systems carry two different dates for this and neither is company-confirmed; last year's equivalent announcement came on **1 December 2025**, which argues for the later end. Treat the whole late-November-to-early-December window as live.
- **By FY26 results** — the deadline for a Karo financing close or an explicit deferral. Not a company-announced date; it is our own gate.
- **Undated, live** — the South African chrome export-permit regime. Comment closed 4 March 2026; no final notice as of 22 July 2026. Could gazette at any time.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, including things we tried to check and could not:

- **Red's $3,230/oz and $2,305/oz derivations.** No company source for either figure exists. We could not reconstruct them and they should not be repeated.
- **The Q4 basket estimate of ~$2,412/oz is our own arithmetic on 5 of 13 weeks**, blended 80/20 July/August. It reproduces correctly, but the company has disclosed nothing. Label it an estimate everywhere it appears.
- **Iridium and ruthenium — about 24% of the basket by value — have no futures market.** Prices are dealer-posted. Tharisa's own site carries iridium at $7,290 and ruthenium at $1,523 against Umicore's $7,900 and $1,750: a ~$60/oz swing in the basket from source choice alone. There is no price history to check and no way to hedge. By value this is a rhodium-and-minor-metals book (Pt 34.4% / Rh 33.3% / Ir 13.3% / Ru 10.5% / Pd 8.2%), not a platinum book, and it is being valued off platinum headlines.
- **The Salene carrying value ($2.3M) is last-disclosed, not current** — the asset has dropped out of the impairment note.
- **Zimbabwe's chrome export ban has not been lifted** and was tightened by a 22 May 2026 declaration. Whether the 25 February 2026 raw-mineral export suspension remains in force is unconfirmed.
- **Chrome-specific policy after 10 July 2026 was not checked.**
- **Chinese demand-side series were never retrieved** — stainless production by month, ferrochrome mill tender prices, stainless export volumes, and the in-force status of European, Indian and Indonesian trade defences. We have partial primary data only: global stainless 64.2Mt in 2025 (+2.1%), China 40.8Mt of it; Chinese high-carbon ferrochrome output +36.8% year over year in the first half; Q3 2026 European ferrochrome benchmark 156 US cents/lb. **The entire demand side of the chrome argument is therefore unverified.**
- **The 2.8× versus 2.29× valuation bridge**, and the $27.7M figure appearing twice (Karo commitments and bond carrying value).
- **The 50–60 year mine life claim rests on resource, not reserve.** Proven and probable reserves of 30.4Mt support roughly 3.0Mtpa against a 5.6Mtpa plant. We have not resolved how that squares with the 74.5Mt → 109.8Mt reassessment; the two figures are on different bases and were never reconciled in one document.
- **Whether the underground unit-cost assumption has any precedent anywhere.** We searched South Africa and found none. We did not search other jurisdictions.
