# PITCH — UPB — PROPOSED VERDICT: NO POSITION — DO NOT BUY AT $7.25 INTO THE AUG 12 REPORT; RE-OPEN ONLY ON THE TRIGGERS BELOW (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

## What we believe

Upstream Bio is a clinical-stage biotech (Waltham, MA; CIK 2022626) developing one drug, verekitug, an antibody against the TSLP receptor, for severe asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps. The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $33.68 to $7.25 — down 78.5%, though already 24% off the $5.85 low.

What we believe after the court, stated plainly: **the science is real but the stock is not a buy here.** The clinical floor is genuine — two Phase 2 primary-endpoint wins, including a 56% reduction in the annualized asthma exacerbation rate at the 100 mg every-12-weeks dose, and that tested dose exists as a fallback if the more aggressive plan fails. But three things break the buy case at $7.25:

1. **The "upcoming reveal" we were waiting for already happened.** Our original brief treated the Phase 3 dose announcement as the single biggest piece of undisclosed information. It was disclosed on March 26, 2026 — "a high-dose quarterly regimen of verekitug of up to 400 mg, administered in a single injection," both indications, first patients dosed in Q1 2027 ([company press release](https://investors.upstreambio.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upstream-bio-highlights-phase-3-development-strategy-verekitug)). The stock fell roughly 50% *after* that disclosure. The market saw the plan and de-rated anyway.
2. **The pivotal dose was never actually tested.** ClinicalTrials.gov shows the Phase 2 arms were 100 mg every 12 weeks, 400 mg every 24 weeks, and 100 mg every 24 weeks. The Phase 3 plan — 400 mg every 12 weeks — is a combination bridged only by pharmacokinetic modeling, and FDA sign-off on it is still pending. No Phase 3 is registered yet.
3. **Whatever the drug is worth accrues mostly to future investors, not to today's buyer.** The company burned $46.9M of cash in Q1 2026 (from $341.5M at year-end 2025 to $294.6M at March 31), putting mid-year cash near $248–255M. Running two broad-population Phase 3 programs to filing will plausibly cost more than the entire current market cap (~$394M on 54.42M shares). A large equity raise is coming; the likeliest moment is into any strength around the FDA alignment news. Good news triggers dilution — the bull catalyst is the bear trigger.

Both adversarial benches — the one attacking the thesis and the one auditing the attacker — landed in the same place: reject the entry, hold no position through the earnings report expected August 12.

## What the market believes

The market has already priced most of what we know. At $7.25 the market cap is ~$394M and the enterprise value (market cap minus cash) is only ~$137M — the market is saying the drug's risk-adjusted value net of the coming dilution is small. The stock fell in two legs: the first from the highs, the second (~50%) *after* the March Phase 3 strategy was published — meaning the market digested the untested-dose risk, the 2030-31 launch timeline, and the funding gap, and marked the stock down through them. Meanwhile the competitive backdrop hardened: Amgen/AZ's Tezspire is the entrenched TSLP incumbent, Dupixent is everywhere, and GSK's Exdensur (depemokimab) was FDA-approved in December 2025 as the first twice-yearly biologic ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-approval-gsk-twice-yearly-asthma-drug-depemokimab-exdensur/808121/)) — an approved competitor that is dosed *less* often than verekitug's planned four shots a year, though only for the eosinophilic sub-population. The stock has coverage (JPMorgan and others), so this is not an overlooked name; and a new 13G (a passive >5% ownership disclosure) was filed July 30, which with the bounce off $5.85 says some buyer already stepped in at the lows.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: less than our original brief claimed, and the court cut it down further. What survives:

- **The clinical floor is under-discussed.** Two positive Phase 2 primary endpoints plus a *tested* fallback regimen (100 mg every 12 weeks, the arm that produced the 56% exacerbation reduction) bound the downside of an FDA non-alignment. If the aggressive dose is rejected, the company is not at zero — it has a de-risked regimen to fall back on.
- **The runway is better than both benches first assumed.** The blue bench caught something the red bench and our own brief both got wrong: the "cash into 2027" guidance was issued *in the same press release* as the Phase 3 plan, so it already contemplates funding the two Q1 2027 trial starts. A *forced* raise is further away than the bear math implies; any near-term raise would be discretionary, into strength.
- **The one genuinely unpriced branch:** a non-dilutive partnership or royalty deal that funds the pivotal program. Nothing in the tape prices that, and it is the single event that would flip this story.
- **Exdensur does not close every door.** The red bench's claim that "the differentiation residual is negative" was reduced on audit: Exdensur's label is restricted to eosinophilic asthma, while the TSLP class is biomarker-agnostic — the low-biomarker segment Tezspire serves is untouched by GSK's approval.

## Why it might be priced in

This is the stronger side, and it is why the proposed verdict is no-position:

- The Phase 3 strategy — the supposed information edge — has been public since March 26 and the stock fell ~50% afterward. What remains undisclosed (the exact dose within "up to 400 mg," and FDA alignment) is controlled entirely by management timing.
- The funding math is visible to everyone: ~$250M of cash against a pivotal program plausibly costing $600–800M (an estimate, not a tool-verified figure — see the table) means the market is rationally discounting dilution, not mispricing the asset.
- The stock is 24% off its low with a fresh 13G on file — the "buy at the low" moment, if there was one, was in June.
- Insiders, a register heavy with sophisticated crossover funds, have made zero open-market purchases at these prices. The only Form 4 activity is automatic tax-withholding sales at $6.10.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

The court's verdict is **no entry** — do not buy at $7.25, and specifically do not buy in the two days before the earnings report, because the bullish branch (FDA alignment announced) is precisely what triggers a dilutive raise into the pop. Per doctrine, here are the **reopen bands**, all from the benches' own math:

- **Price reopen: below ~$4.70.** Projected mid-2026 cash of $248–255M over 54.42M shares is $4.56–4.69 per share. Below that, the buyer pays less than the cash in the bank and gets the drug for free; the dilution objection inverts. (Secondary marker: a clean break of the $5.85 52-week low on no new bad news warrants a fresh look, not a buy.)
- **Event reopen #1 — the flip trigger:** a non-dilutive partnership or royalty financing that covers the pivotal budget, at any price. This is the one branch the tape does not price; it converts the funding gap from the bear case into someone else's checkbook.
- **Event reopen #2:** an equity raise actually prints and clears. Once the overhang is realized and absorbed, the "future money captures the value" objection is spent, and the clinical floor can be bought.
- **Event reopen #3:** a cluster of insider open-market buys (not tax-withholding activity) from the crossover-heavy register.
- **Soft positive marker:** June 30 cash reported at ≥$260M with runway guidance reaffirmed — above the benches' $248–255M projection, it would mean the burn deceleration the guidance implies has finally shown up in the numbers.

**Sizing:** zero now. If a reopen trigger fires, the position returns to the court for sizing — the surviving thesis (clinical floor + fallback regimen) supports at most a small starter, not a conviction position.
