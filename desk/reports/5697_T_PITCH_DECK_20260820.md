## What we believe

Sanyu (TSE 5697) is a small Japanese steel-products maker selling into the auto supply chain. It trades at roughly **half of its own book value**, holds **about ¥4.0 billion of net cash against a ¥5.0 billion market value** — meaning cash alone is ~81% of what the whole company costs — carries **no debt**, and pays a dividend of about **3.6%**. Strip out the cash and the operating business is priced at roughly one times a year of operating profit.

We believe that is too cheap even for a company with real problems, and that the business is currently getting better rather than worse: the quarter it reported on **August 4, 2026** showed operating profit of **¥356 million versus ¥201 million a year earlier — up 77%**, with net profit up 81%. Neither the case-for nor the case-against had that number when they argued. It arrived after them, and it favors the buy side.

So: a small starter position, bought patiently, sized so that being wrong costs us very little and being unable to sell quickly is an inconvenience rather than a problem.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a permanently cheap company, and it has decent reasons.

- **The cash is not ours.** Japanese small caps with a large corporate parent-like shareholder — here a **33.4% holder** — have historically sat on cash for decades. The market prices that cash at a discount because it may never be paid out or deployed.
- **Auto suppliers are squeezed.** Tier-two steel and parts suppliers face price pressure from customers above them. Today's earnings jump may be a cyclical or one-off recovery, not a new level.
- **It is too small to matter.** At roughly **$32 million of market value and about $0.5 million of daily trading**, professional money physically cannot own it in size. Nobody is coming to re-rate it.
- **Cheapness has been stable.** The stock is up ~51% from its 52-week low (¥547) and is only ~6% below its 52-week high (¥879), so the market has already given it *some* credit. It hasn't ignored the name; it has just refused to pay book value for it.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We re-checked the numbers against the source and they held.** The case-for was argued outside our shop by a different system. We did not take it on faith — we pulled the company's data ourselves and graded each load-bearing claim. Everything that mattered survived. That is rare; usually verification subtracts.
2. **We have a fact that is 16 days newer than the argument.** The August 4 quarter (operating profit +77%) postdates both the buy case and the sell case. The sell case's core claim — that this is a structurally pressured supplier — is running against fresh evidence it never saw.
3. **We tested the tax trap harder than the buy case did and it still passed.** For a US owner, the fear with a cash-heavy foreign company is being taxed as a passive foreign investment company (PFIC), which is punitive. Under the market-value asset test in the US code ([26 U.S.C. §1297(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1297)), passive assets are ¥4.02B against a base of market value plus liabilities (¥5.03B + ¥11.0B ≈ ¥16.0B) = **about 25%**, well under the 50% line. Cash being 81% of *market cap* is not the same as cash being most of the *assets* — there is ¥21.4B of assets sitting under a ¥5B price tag.
4. **Our edge is willingness, not information.** We can own a $32M company. Almost nobody who could analyze it can buy it.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because these are real:

- **The discount may be permanent and correctly permanent.** "Cheap Japanese small cap with a big strategic shareholder" is one of the most durable value traps in existence. Being right about the arithmetic and wrong about ever getting paid is the standard outcome.
- **One good quarter is one quarter.** +77% off a low base in a cyclical supplier is not a trend. The next print can undo the entire story.
- **The 33.4% holder controls the outcome, and we do not know what it wants.** If it is content, nothing happens. If it moves, it may move in a way that is bad for minority holders (a low-priced buyout at a fraction of book is a genuine risk in this exact setup).
- **The market is not asleep.** Up 51% off the low means someone already noticed. We are not early to an unknown; we are late to a known cheap thing that stayed cheap.
- **We have no catalyst we control or can date with confidence.** Everything here is "wait and see if something happens."
