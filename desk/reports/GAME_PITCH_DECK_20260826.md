# PITCH — GAME — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no entry today; both benches reject, and the deck below carries reopen conditions instead of a buy.

## What we believe

GameSquare (Nasdaq: GAME) is a small esports and creator-media company in Frisco, Texas — the FaZe Clan brand, an ad/agency business, and a creator software tool called TubeBuddy. Alongside the operating business it holds a treasury of 14,840 Ether tokens, most of it pledged as collateral against borrowings.

The idea that brought it to court was this: the stock fell about half over a year, the last audited balance sheet marked the Ether at a low point, Ether has since recovered, and the reported revenue growth looked strong (+137%). Put together, that reads like a company whose balance sheet quietly got better while nobody was looking.

**After working it, we do not believe that.** Our own analysis says the growth number is an accounting artifact, the balance-sheet recovery is real but already in the price, and the thing that actually explains the share-price decline is share issuance, not a mistake by the market.

Specifically, what we now believe:

- The company's revenue grew about **17%**, not 137%. The 137% compares this year's revenue to a prior-year base that had been shrunk by removing a divested business, while this year's number *adds* two acquisitions.
- **Revenue per share fell about 55%** over the same twelve months, because the share count went from 39.0 million to 102.3 million (+162%) before a 1-for-8 reverse split. The stock fell 49%. The stock is tracking the dilution almost exactly. There is no mispricing to collect.
- The Ether recovery is real (the tokens are worth roughly **$36.6M** at $2,468/ETH versus a **$23.3M** carrying value), and it will produce a roughly **+$13.3M non-cash gain** in the next quarter. But anyone with a browser can compute that from the spot price. It is not information.
- The company had **$2.1M of unrestricted cash** and burned **$9.2M of operating cash in the first half**. The Ether is not spare value on top of the business — it is what pays for the business.

We believe the correct action is **no position**, and that the name is worth re-examining only on the specific conditions listed under Proposed Entry Bands.

## What the market believes

Nobody covers this stock. There is no published analyst estimate for the current period that we could find, no institutional sponsorship of consequence, and about **$663,000 of stock trades per day** — roughly 190,000 shares. That matters for how we read the price: with no professional anchor, "the market believes X" is a weak statement here.

What the tape shows: the stock is **$3.48**, up **85% from its 52-week low of $1.88**, and it closed the prior session at **$3.65**, which was its highest close in six months. It is down about **49%** from its 52-week high. A 1-for-8 reverse split took effect **August 24, 2026**, so every price here is on the new, post-split basis.

The most defensible reading of that price action: buyers have already responded to the Ether recovery and to the removal of the delisting threat (the stock only needed to hold $1.00 to cure a Nasdaq minimum-price deficiency; at $3.48 that is settled). The market is not ignoring the balance sheet. It priced it, and then some.

We flag one honest limitation: our red-team originally claimed the stock "outran" Ether by comparing the stock's gain from its 52-week low against Ether's gain since June 30. Those are different starting dates and the comparison does not hold as stated. We could not compute the stock's return since June 30 because our price feed was unavailable. So "already priced in" is our judgment, not a measured fact.

## Why we might have edge

Stating the strongest version of the bull case, because it is not zero:

1. **Nobody is looking.** $663K/day, no coverage, a company that just did a reverse split — this is exactly the kind of name where a careful read of a 10-Q can beat the market, because almost nobody reads it.
2. **A mechanical gain is coming.** Under current accounting, crypto is marked to market through income. At today's Ether price the next quarterly report carries a roughly **$13.3M** gain and likely flips working capital positive. Retail investors who do not model this will be surprised by the headline.
3. **The balance sheet did de-risk.** Pledged collateral is **11,320 ETH = $27.9M** against **$12.1M** of borrowings — **231% coverage**, versus a 130% requirement. Ether would have to fall to about **$1,390** (−44%) before a margin call is in play. The "they get liquidated" version of the bear case is currently dead, and our red-team conceded that outright.
4. **Reverse splits create forced selling.** Some holders sell mechanically after a split, and index/screen exclusions reset. That can create a price unrelated to value.

## Why it might be priced in

And the honest counterweight, which is where we land:

- The stock is at a six-month high and up 85% off its low. Whatever the Ether recovery is worth, buyers have had two months and a live, continuously-quoted collateral price to act on it.
- The coming $13.3M mark-up is computable by anyone from the spot price and the token count that was filed on August 13. Information that is trivially derivable is not edge.
- Back out the crypto and the cash and add the debt, and the market is paying about **$21.4M** for the operating business — roughly **0.3x** annualized revenue. That is not an obviously distressed price for a business that burns cash, is assembled from acquisitions, and grows about 17% organically-plus-bolt-ons.
- The delisting overhang is gone at $3.48. That is not something to pay for; it is something already collected.

## What could go wrong

*(This is the pre-mortem. Because we are not entering, read these as: "if we ever do enter on the reopen bands below, here is how we lose.")*

**Failure narrative 1 — The dilution treadmill simply continues, and the reverse split reset the ladder.**
The share count went 39.0M → 102.3M in twelve months. The company burns ~$1.5M/month and holds $2.1M of unrestricted cash. The 1-for-8 split just took the stock from ~$0.43 to ~$3.48, which restores the ability to issue stock at a "respectable" price — that is very often the actual purpose of a reverse split. We buy at $3.48, the company sells shares into the recovery, and revenue per share keeps falling on schedule. *Monitorable:* yes — an S-3 or 424B5 registration, an at-the-market program announcement, an 8-K under Item 3.02, and the share count on the cover page of the next 10-Q.

**Failure narrative 2 — Ether falls and the whole structure inverts.**
At 231% coverage the company is safe today. But the equity is a leveraged claim on Ether: a 44% Ether decline puts pledged collateral at the 130% maintenance line and the company would have to post more, sell tokens, or repay. At a 100% coverage point (~$1,070/ETH) the equity behind the loan is gone. Crucially, the *stock* will move more than Ether does on the way down, because the operating business is also burning and the balance sheet is the only thing holding it up. *Monitorable:* yes — Ether spot is quoted continuously; the trigger prices are computed above.

**Failure narrative 3 — Something in the loan agreement that we have never read decides the outcome. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
Neither bench retrieved the actual credit agreement behind the $12.1M of Ether-secured borrowings. We do not know: whether excess collateral can be withdrawn at the company's option or only at the lender's; whether there is a material-adverse-change clause; whether there is a cross-default to other obligations; whether the lender can re-set the margin requirement unilaterally; or who the lender is and how it is funded. We inferred "auto-60-day renewal" at the September 30 maturity from secondary description, not from the contract. **There is no data feed, price, or filing cadence that reveals the state of a private bilateral agreement before it acts.** We would learn about it from an 8-K published *after* the consequence.
*Unknown-unknown class: undisclosed-exhibit counterparty discretion* — the family of risks whose terms live only in a non-filed or unretrieved private agreement, where the counterparty's option is invisible until exercised. Closely related and equally untripwired here: related-party dealing at a nano-cap and the quality of an audit we have not examined.

**The reflexive / structural leg — our own size, our own flow, and our own rules.**
This is the part that binds regardless of whether the analysis is right.

- The stock trades **$663K/day**. Our publishing doctrine caps participation at **1% of average daily volume**, which is about **$6,600 per day**. A $250,000 position takes roughly **38 trading sessions to build and 38 to exit** — and volume in a cash-burning nano-cap disappears precisely on the days you need it. The realistic outcome of being wrong here is not a stop-loss; it is being unable to leave.
- **We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predicted.** The bull case includes "the November print carries a big mark-up." If we accumulate ahead of that, we are buying into the same catalyst everyone else can compute, and then trying to exit into whoever is left afterward, through a $6,600/day straw. Our own rule against bidding into our own predicted flow applies directly.
- **Publishing is one-way.** Our exits publish permanently. In a name this thin, our own disclosed exit is itself a price event.
- **Doctrine interactions:** no tax-loss-harvest or wash-sale entanglement (we hold nothing here and it is not a pair for anything we hold). We would not sell options against it in the taxable account. And the FaZe brand carries genuine retail-attention characteristics, which is precisely the profile our short-side rules exclude — so the trade is long-only or nothing.
- Finally, the honest cost side: this court consumed real analyst time on a position whose maximum sensible size is capped below the value of the work. That is a structural reason to pass even if the analysis were bullish.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a shrinking per-share claim — the company funds its burn by issuing stock faster than the business grows, so revenue, Ether, and the story can all keep rising while the value attached to each share we own keeps falling, and a $663K/day tape leaves us no way out once that becomes obvious.
