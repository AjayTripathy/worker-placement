# PITCH — MNSO — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position now; the file stays open only through the August 28 print, and re-opens only on one specific test inside that print.

---

## What we believe

MINISO (NYSE: MNSO, HKEX: 9896) is a Chinese-listed global variety retailer — ~8,565 stores, two banners (MINISO and TOP TOY). The stock is $10.68, 0.4% above its 52-week low of $10.64 and 60% below its 52-week high of $26.74.

We believe three things, and only three:

1. **Sales are still growing; profits are not.** On August 14 the company itself pre-announced the first half of 2026: revenue RMB 11,450–11,550M (up 22–23%), but adjusted net profit excluding currency effects of RMB 1,210–1,230M — **down 1–3%**. Revenue compounding in the low twenties while earnings go backwards is the whole story.
2. **The June quarter alone is much worse than the half suggests.** Subtracting the already-reported March quarter from the guided half gives a June quarter with revenue up 16–18% and **operating profit of roughly RMB 99–139M against RMB 836M a year ago**. Strip out the one investment mark that distorts it and operating profit is still down **12–17%** on 17% higher revenue. Reported net profit for that quarter is a **loss of about RMB 298M**.
3. **At $10.68 this is roughly 8–9× the earnings we think it can actually make.** That is not obviously cheap and not obviously a trap. It is priced approximately correctly for a business whose earnings are flat-to-falling.

Conclusion: we do not own it, and we do not buy it before the print.

## What the market believes

Two markets disagree with each other.

- **Sell-side research still believes the old story.** Consensus FY26 earnings sit at $1.56 per share with a mean price target of $19.71 and a "Strong Buy" rating — a target 85% above the current price, on a stock sitting on its 52-week low. Our own estimate of what the company can earn is $1.15–1.40. Those forecasts have not been revised for the August 14 pre-announcement.
- **The actual tape does not believe it.** The stock fell roughly 11% (from about $12.04 to $10.68) in the sessions after the pre-announcement. The market read the release, did the same subtraction we did, and marked the stock down for it.

So the market has already priced the *event*. What it has not done is update the published forecasts — and those cuts are still owed.

## Why we might have edge

- **We opened the second exhibit.** The August 17 filing (accession 0001104659-26-097571) has two attachments. The first is a logistics notice: *"we will report on Friday, August 28, 2026."* The second is a **complete first-half profit alert** with revenue, operating profit, net profit, EPS and the investment mark. Our initial write-up cited the filing for the print date and never opened the second exhibit — it then spent its effort *estimating* numbers the company had already published. One of our review benches caught that. It is a repeatable failure mode and we are writing it up as a permanent check: on any foreign-issuer 6-K with more than one exhibit, open all of them before doing any reconstruction.
- **We computed the quarter, not the half.** The company guided the half-year only. Every half-year number reads positive (+22–23%, +4–6%, +5–7%). Every derived quarterly number is negative (a net loss, and operating profit down 83–88%). Choosing to pre-announce on the half-year basis is itself information. Neither the published research nor our first pass did this subtraction.
- **We separated the investment mark from the operating business.** MINISO holds a stake in an AI limited partnership carried at fair value. It added RMB 875M to the March quarter and subtracted roughly RMB 598M in the June quarter. That swing dominates reported profit and tells you nothing about stores. Working with the operating line instead is what produces the real finding.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly — most of it probably is.

- The stock fell 11% on the pre-announcement. The information is public, filed, and decoded.
- ~8–9× adjusted earnings is roughly what a retailer with flat earnings deserves. There is no obvious gap between price and value here.
- The one thing genuinely not priced — the sell-side cuts from $1.56 toward $1.30 or below — is a reason to **wait**, not a reason to buy. Buying ahead of forecast cuts means bidding into selling pressure we ourselves predict.
- We have a poor track record on this exact setup. Our internal record on "buy the stock that fell on an earnings event" is 0-for-7. When the headline *is* the news, there is usually no dislocation to harvest.

## What remains unverified

- **Gross margin and operating expense detail for the June quarter.** Absent from the pre-announcement. This is the one number the entry decision turns on, and we will not have it until the print.
- **Balance sheet.** Net cash, debt, and any convertible securities are not pulled. Share count is derived from profit ÷ earnings-per-share, not read from a filing. Every valuation multiple in this deck is therefore equity-value-based, not enterprise-value-based.
- **Which AI partnership this is.** The filing names no company. The identification circulating internally is an inference from a listed stock's price movement, not a document. It does not change the conclusion — the amounts are ~2% of market value — but it should not be repeated as fact.
- **Where the investment mark sits in the income statement.** Our key finding (operating profit down 12–17% excluding marks) assumes the mark sits below the operating line. That was inferred from a divergence in the March quarter's numbers, not read off a presented income statement. The August 28 report settles it.
- **Currency treatment across periods.** The company's "adjusted, excluding foreign exchange" figures for the March quarter and the half are not fully reconciled to each other.
- **Consumer interest data.** The TOP TOY reading (0.0) contradicts reported 51% segment growth and is probably a broken feed. We are not using any of the three consumer-interest signals as evidence in either direction.
- **Supplier base behind the 43.3% gross margin.** Never examined. Import/shipping records on MINISO's trading entities would test whether margin pressure is sourcing cost or price competition.
- **Price basis.** Every multiple here uses the $10.68 quote supplied with the research pack, not a live broker quote. Re-price before any order.

**Where the two reviews landed:** the first review recommended rejecting the name outright; the second overturned that in part — the demand-collapse case was overstated, the AI-investment case was immaterial, and the stale-forecast case is about timing rather than a reason to reject. What survived, and got stronger, is the operating-profit decline. Both reviews agree on the action: **no position into August 28.** They disagree on whether the file should close now (first review) or after one more data point (second). This deck follows the second: one test, one date, then either a small starter or the file closes for good.
