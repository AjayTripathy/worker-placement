# PITCH — OI — VERDICT: PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION — do not buy at $6.62; no position, no orders, no size; reopen only on a fundamental or price gate below.

*O-I Glass, Inc. (NYSE: OI) — CIK 812074. Tape $6.62, 52-week range $6.58–$16.91, down 61% from the high and sitting 1% off the low. We hold none of it and have no orders working. Written from the red/blue court of 2026-08-18 on the machine-built evidence pack of 02:21Z.*

## What we believe

O-I Glass makes glass bottles and jars. It is the biggest one in the world, and its European business just fell apart: segment operating profit dropped by about $85M year over year, margin went from roughly 12% to under 1%, and the company wrote off **all** of Europe's goodwill — $873M — plus took a $96M valuation allowance against European deferred tax assets.

The original idea was that this is a cyclical trough in European bottle prices. Buy the most levered operator into the bottom of a price cycle, and you get paid double when price mean-reverts.

**After the court, we do not believe that.** Here is what we believe instead:

1. **The European problem is mostly O-I's own, not the industry's — but it is a timing problem, not a permanent one.** Two listed European competitors reported the same quarters and did better. That kills the "industry price war" framing. But the reason they did better is that they *finished* the plant-closure programs O-I is still in the middle of. Verallia said so directly: its European margin expanded on footprint cuts and a 3.2% reduction in net cash costs. O-I is running the same program ("Fit to Win"), booked $53M of benefit in Q2, and has spent ~$700M of cumulative charges getting there. That is a lag. Lags close. It is not proof of a permanent cost disadvantage — nobody in this court produced an actual unit-cost comparison.

2. **Management has signed a statement that Europe will not recover soon.** A deferred-tax valuation allowance requires management to assert it is more likely than not that the taxable income will *not* show up. You do not take one on a business you think is at a cyclical bottom. This is the single most credible bearish fact in the file, because it comes from the people with the furnace-level data and it carries auditor sign-off.

3. **The balance sheet makes this a stub, not a stock.** 153,659,747 shares at $6.62 is $1,017M of market value against $4,647M of net debt (long-term $4,793M + short-term $193M − cash $339M). Debt is 4.6x the equity. Small moves in earnings become enormous moves in the share price, in both directions.

4. **The decisive reason not to buy is none of the above.** It is that we do not actually disagree with anyone. Our own valuation math on management's guidance midpoint produces **$7.34/share**. RBC's price target is **$8**, BofA's is **$11**. Our number sits *inside* the street's range and *below* its lowest published target. There is no number where we differ from consensus with a named mechanism for the difference. Buying here would score our fair value against theirs with nothing between them. That is a reject, and it is worth saying plainly that **neither of our benches led with this** — blue only reached it as an afterthought finding.

5. **It is not permanently uninvestable.** There is no covenant cliff, no forced seller, no dilution pending. The option does not expire. That is precisely why the answer is "wait for a price or a fact," not "avoid forever."

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we believe, which is the problem.

At $6.62 the stock is priced at about **$1,030M of forward EBITDA** on the same 5.5x multiple both benches used — essentially management's own guidance midpoint, no better and no worse. The market is not pricing a disaster and it is not pricing a recovery. It is taking the guide at face value.

Around that, the sell side is still moving down: BofA to Underperform ($11 target), RBC to Sector Perform ($8, citing competitor oversupply and free cash flow "barely breakeven" in 2027), Citi cut. Short interest is 5.62% of float and rising (+1.93% month over month), about 5 days to cover. Three weeks after the print the stock is 1% off its 52-week low — there has been no absorption of the bad news, and downgrades are still landing.

Two things follow. First, we would be buying into flow we can see coming. Second, the fact that the stock has not bounced is itself information: nobody with a research budget has found the trough thesis compelling enough to step in front of it.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thinner than it looked going in. What survives:

- **The peer comparison is not what the bear case says it is.** Red's kill used O-I's *European segment operating profit* margin (0.9%) against Verallia's *group adjusted EBITDA* margin (20.7%) — two different measures at two different perimeters. On a like-for-like basis the gap is roughly **8% vs 15.8%**, about 2x, not 23x. Most of the desk will read the headline comparison, not the corrected one.
- **The industry price event is real, in the quarter that matters.** Red used Verallia's first-half numbers, where margin expanded. Verallia's *Q2 standalone* adjusted EBITDA fell 5.6% to €192M with margin down 116bps to 21.4%; Vidrala's organic revenue fell 3.8% on softer price *and* volume. The trend turned inside the half that red used to argue it hadn't.
- **The downside case is not zero.** Red's own table showed a wipeout at $0.90B of EBITDA — but only by applying 5.5x to the good case and 5.0x to the bad one. At a consistent 5.5x, the $0.90B case is **$1.97/share**, a 70% loss, not a 100% one. Consistent inputs change the shape of this from "uninvestable" to "high variance," and high variance is a sizing question.
- **The option does not expire.** $1.11B undrawn on a $1.25B revolver, $339M cash, and the only financial covenant is *Secured* leverage ≤2.50x against a debt stack that is mostly unsecured. There is no mechanism that forces this company to sell equity or hand the keys over in the next several quarters. We can wait for our price. That is a genuine structural advantage over a levered name where the clock runs against you.

## Why it might be priced in

- **Our fair value is inside the street's range.** $7.34 vs targets of $8 and $11. There is no gap to harvest.
- **Management already told everyone.** The impairment and the valuation allowance are in the filings; the guidance cut is public; EPS guidance was withdrawn. None of this is our discovery.
- **The stock has not stopped going down.** 1% off the low, three weeks post-print, with short interest rising. If there were a trough thesis with legs, this is where it would be visible.
- **The success case is still cash-flow negative.** Capex is guided to $450M (up from $432M) against free cash flow of **−$50M to −$150M**. Hitting guidance perfectly means the equity stub does not deleverage this year. There is no self-help path that pays the shareholder in 2026.
- **The bear case has a second barrel.** $608M of goodwill remains on the books, all of it in the Americas, against $539M of total book equity. Another write-down is available.
- **No dated event forces a re-rate.** The next print is ~49 trading days out. Nothing between here and there resolves the question.

## What remains unverified

- **Europe's D&A, and therefore the entire corrected peer comparison.** The ~$50M figure is a pro-rata allocation of $120M total quarterly D&A by revenue share. Segment D&A is not disclosed. If Europe is more capital-intensive than the average of the portfolio — plausible for the older footprint — the real margin gap is worse than 2x and red's structural verdict gets stronger. **This is the single most important unverified number in the deck.**
- **The 9.500% 2033 notes' secondary price.** 144A/Reg S; no public mark. Both benches tried. The credit market's read on this company is simply unavailable to us, and one third of the original conviction score rested on it.
- **Unit cost per tonne, O-I vs Verallia vs Vidrala.** Nobody produced it. "Restructuring lag" and "permanent cost disadvantage" are indistinguishable without it, and they have opposite investment conclusions.
- **The 2026-10-27 release date.** Inferred from a pattern, not confirmed by the company.
- **Verallia and Vidrala Q3 reporting dates.** Not verified. Two of our three reopen gates depend on them.
- **One of our own citations does not match its label.** Blue cited a Verallia URL as the "H1'26 PR" — `https://www.verallia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/PR_Verallia_first_quarter_results_20260422.pdf` — but the filename and date say *first quarter*, April 2026. The Verallia Europe-segment margin of 15.8% should be re-sourced from the actual H1 document before it is relied on again. Flagging our own sloppy citation rather than passing it through.
- **Which tape price is real.** $6.62 or $6.686 — the two benches held different packs and live broker re-pull was blocked in both sessions. Immaterial at 1%, but we do not currently know our own price of record.
- **Whether the Americas is repeating Europe.** Segment profit +22% on volumes −7% is price harvesting into falling volume — which is exactly Europe's configuration one year before its price line broke. No external anchor was run against this. It is a plausible tail, not an established finding, and it is the question the next court should open with.
