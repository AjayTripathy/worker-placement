## What we believe

eToro's stock fell about 14% on its August 11 quarterly report and has not recovered — it now trades at $28.03, roughly 42% below its 52-week high of $48.28 and about 13% above its 52-week low of $24.74. The market treated the report as proof that eToro is a crypto-cycle stock whose cycle has turned.

We believe the filed income statement says something different, and that the number that matters is not in any headline.

eToro books crypto trading on a gross basis: it reports the full customer notional as revenue and the matching cost of buying that crypto as cost of revenue. In the quarter just reported, those two lines were $1,346.4M of revenue against $1,353.6M of cost — meaning eToro's crypto spot business contributed **negative $7.2M**. A year earlier the same two lines were $1,914.8M against $1,877.1M, a **positive $37.7M**. So crypto swung roughly **$45M against the company year over year**.

And total net contribution still grew, from $210M to $229M. Back the crypto leg out and the non-crypto business — equities, commodities, currencies, the fee and interest lines — grew from about **$172M to about $236M, roughly +37%**. That is the thesis in one sentence: *the crypto damage already happened, it is bounded because the segment is now approximately zero, and the rest of the business grew 37% straight through it.*

The price you pay for that: the company holds about **$1.23B of its own cash and short-term investments against $455M of total liabilities**, of which only $148M is money owed back to customers — so roughly **$775M is genuinely the company's own**. Against a $2.57B market value that is an enterprise value of about **$1.80B**. On the most conservative earnings base available (this quarter's adjusted EBITDA of $78M, annualized to $312M) that is **5.8x**. On the first-half run-rate it is 4.8x. Adjusted earnings put it near **10x**.

## What the market believes

That crypto is 84.5% of eToro's revenue and crypto revenue just fell 29.7%. That is a true and checkable statement, and it is the number a screen or a headline reader picks up. It is also the gross number, and the gross number for a principal-booked business is close to meaningless — a firm that buys $1,346M of crypto and sells it for $1,346M shows enormous "revenue" and earns nothing.

The market also believes, correctly, that:
- Adjusted EBITDA and net contribution both came in **below analyst consensus**, which is why Cantor Fitzgerald cut its price target to $53 from $61.
- July's crypto activity was brutal: trades down ~73%, average size down ~50%, implying notional down about 86.5% — and none of that has hit a reported quarter yet.
- The company simultaneously announced a **$231M acquisition** of a US broker, TradeZero, which does not close until the first half of 2027 and is accretive only to *adjusted* earnings.
- Management repurchased **$189.1M of its own stock in the first half** at an average around $37.80 — about 35% above today's price.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The netting.** The paired cost-of-revenue line sits directly beneath the crypto revenue line on the same filed statement. Anyone quoting 84.5% without it is comparing a gross number to a net one — a 17-fold difference in basis. Our own red bench made exactly this mistake, twice, in two full-text passes.
2. **A positive turn inside the KPI table the market sold.** Capital-markets money invested per trade went **$197 in April (−48% year over year) to $207 in July (−23%)** — up 5% sequentially with the annual decline cut in half. Neither of our benches read this correctly on the first pass; both mismatched it against the crypto line.
3. **Direction of the crypto loss.** Crypto spot contribution was **−$18M in Q1 and −$7.2M in Q2** — the loss *narrowed 60%* as volume fell, because the cost line is variable, not fixed. There is less left to break than either the bull or the bear case assumed.
4. **The balance sheet is unusually clean for the sector.** Customer money owed out is $147.8M against $1.23B of liquid assets; equity is $1.36B; property and equipment is $9.4M. There is no hidden leverage to discover.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because several of these are strong:

1. **Costs are growing into a slowing top line.** Net contribution +9%, adjusted EBITDA +8.3% — margin went the wrong way. Sequentially, adjusted EBITDA fell **28%** versus roughly **11%** in the same quarter last year. This is the real bear argument and *neither bench prosecuted it*. Our blue bench flagged it against its own recommendation.
2. **No catalyst for about three months.** eToro publishes monthly operating metrics only inside quarterly releases. There is no interim data between now and the next report — expected around **November 9, but unconfirmed** (derived from a data vendor; no company press release names it). Buying now is buying a blind quarter.
3. **The acquisition is unpriced and has an integrity problem.** TradeZero America and its co-founder Daniel Pipitone — quoted by name in eToro's announcement and staying on — settled SEC charges in May 2022 for **deceiving customers about the January 2021 meme-stock trading halts** (Securities Act §17(a)(2) and (3); $100k firm / $25k individual penalties; a two-year independent compliance consultant). The SEC found new-account applications ran ~200x the daily average off that misrepresentation. eToro is paying up to $231M — about 2.9x revenue — for a franchise whose growth is partly an artifact of a sanctioned misstatement, and the deal requires regulatory approval.
4. **Supply.** Eleven Form 144 notices of intended insider sale in 2026, including one on **August 12, the day after the crash, at the low**. We have not pulled a single share count, so we cannot say whether this is meaningful or trivial.
5. **This template's track record is bad.** Our own scoring of "buy the earnings-day dislocation" setups is **0-for-7**. The base rate argues for half-size at most.
