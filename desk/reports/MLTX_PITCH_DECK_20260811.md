# PITCH — MLTX — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: BUY a half-size starter (~0.5% of book) near $16.59; no adds until the FDA accepts the filing (~end November 2026); exit if the FDA refuses the file or the trial's placebo-adjusted benefit comes in under 15 points at unblinding.** (Red bench voted no-position, conviction 6/10; blue bench partially overturned to this staged half-starter, conviction 7/10.)

## What we believe

MoonLake Immunotherapeutics is a clinical-stage biotech whose lead drug, sonelokimab, just reported a successful Phase 3 trial (IZAR-1) in psoriatic arthritis: the company says it met all endpoints, with 42.1% of patients reaching a 50% symptom improvement (ACR50) at week 16, rising toward ~60% by week 24. We believe:

1. **The approval path is likely intact.** The company reports the FDA sees "no remaining gaps" ahead of its marketing application, guided for submission around the end of September 2026, with an acceptance decision around end of November.
2. **The drug is probably at least as good as the incumbent.** The trial was placebo-controlled, and published history says placebo response rates in psoriatic arthritis run a stable 10–15%. So a 42.1% absolute response implies roughly a high-20s-percentage-point benefit over placebo — about parity with UCB's Bimzelx, the closest competitor, whose landmark trial showed 44% response versus 10% placebo ([Lancet, BE OPTIMAL](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/piis0140-6736(22)02302-9/fulltext)). This bounding argument was the blue bench's decisive contribution — the exact benefit is still hidden (see below), but it can't plausibly be terrible.
3. **The price already punished the stock for worse than this.** Shares trade at $16.59, down 74% from the 52-week high, 11% below where they sat two days before this *positive* result, and 18% below the $20 level where institutions bought $230 million of stock in June.
4. Week-52 durability data (67.2% of completers holding a high response) reads like-for-like with the incumbent's published figures — weak but directional support.

## What the market believes

The market sold this good news (−11% over two days). The likely read: the company announced *that* the trial succeeded but not *by how much* — the trial stays blinded until week 52, so the placebo-adjusted numbers won't be public until the first half of 2027 ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06641076](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06641076) — primary completion 2027-02-04). After this same company's earlier trial in a different disease failed precisely on the placebo-adjusted margin, "trust us, it worked" earns a discount. The market also sees: the CEO, CSO, and CFO together sold about $5 million of stock at ~$20 in July; the company filed paperwork enabling a new share sale the same day as the good news; and a $400M credit facility plus a 2027 launch build mean more capital will be raised before revenue arrives. In short: the market believes the headline but is pricing the withheld number, the coming share supply, and management's own selling.

## Why we might have edge

- **The withheld number is bounded, and the market may be treating it as unbounded.** Psoriatic arthritis placebo rates are historically stable (~10–15%), unlike the volatile placebo rates that caused the prior failure. The red bench treated the two diseases as equally foggy; the blue bench showed that's wrong. If the crowd is extrapolating from the earlier failure, the fear is miscalibrated.
- **Two dated, company-guided events land in the buyer's favor within ~4 months** — the filing submission (~Sept 30) and the acceptance decision, possibly with priority review (~end Nov) — against a price 18% below where a $230M institutional book cleared in June.
- **The insider selling is weaker evidence than it looks:** the sale plans were adopted in March 2026, after the crash, and executed at $20+, ~20% above today's price. Pre-planned selling at higher prices is a ceiling on management enthusiasm, not a signal at $16.59.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated symmetrically, because the red bench's case partly survived:

- **We cannot verify the one thing that matters most.** Differentiation versus the incumbent is a number the company possesses and will not show until H1 2027. Parity (the floor-bounded case) may not be enough for a challenger against an entrenched competitor.
- **"Approvable" rests entirely on the company's own paraphrase of a private FDA meeting.** No FDA text is public; the application isn't even submitted yet, so a refusal-to-file is a live, downside-only tail.
- **Share supply is telegraphed:** an armed shelf, a $400M credit facility, a commercial launch to fund, and cash runway (~$537M against ~$62M/quarter burn) that runs only to mid-2028 *before* launch spending. A raise before launch is near-certain.
- **Management sold ~$5M at the very level the bull case cites as "institutional support."**
- The week-52 durability figure counts only patients who finished (396 of ~840 randomized), so it flatters — as does the incumbent's equivalent figure.
