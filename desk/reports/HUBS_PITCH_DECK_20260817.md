# PITCH — HUBS — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-11 — TRIM HALF THE POSITION, HOLD THE REST, ADD NOTHING; conviction 6/10, position cap cut from 1.2% to 0.5% of the book, and the remaining half is on a documented kill trigger at the Q3 report.**

## What we believe

HubSpot is a genuinely good business that we own at a price that requires it to re-accelerate, and on August 5, 2026 the company itself told us it will not re-accelerate soon.

The specifics:

- Management guided to **5,000–6,000 net new customers per quarter "for Q3 and beyond,"** down from the 7,000 it just delivered and well below the 9,000–10,000 the market had been carrying.
- It guided **net revenue retention flat at 102%** — meaning the average existing customer spends only 2% more next year, before churn is netted out. There is no expansion engine visible to offset slower new-customer growth.
- Reported revenue growth still looks respectable (Q3 guide of **$924–925M, roughly +14%**), but roughly **3 percentage points of that is a weak-dollar tailwind**, not business. Strip currency out and the exit rate is about **11% growth in constant currency**.
- Against that, the stock is priced on accounting profit at roughly **55x operating income** (about $43.3M of GAAP operating profit in Q2, annualized to ~$173M, against an enterprise value near $9.6B). The widely-quoted "12.6x" multiple uses the company's own adjusted numbers, which exclude very large stock compensation. Owner-level economics are close to break-even once you charge that cost.
- The buyback is drawing down the balance sheet rather than being funded from operations: **$536.4M of repurchases in Q2 against $167.9M of free cash flow**, $742.9M in the first half, against $1,383.2M of cash and investments. That is roughly 2.6 more quarters at this pace. It is a real bid, but it is finite and it is not free.
- The margin pressure is self-inflicted and deliberate: in April the company cut prices on its agent products and added cheaper entry tiers. That is a strategy, possibly a good one, but the payback is unproven and the market has now re-rated the stock twice on it (down ~11.9% on May 7, down ~21% after hours on August 5).

Conclusion: this is a **business risk, not a solvency or fraud risk**. The company is profitable, carries no debt, and its convertible notes matured and are gone. So the correct response is to make the position smaller, not to leave. We cut half.

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we do, and has already acted on it. The stock is **59% below its 52-week high of $525.51**, at **$215.17**, and only 27% above its 52-week low of $169.63. Two separate post-earnings de-ratings this year have taken the multiple down hard. Around 40 analysts cover the name, so nothing here is undiscovered.

Where the market may still be too generous: it is largely quoting the adjusted profit figure, which makes the stock look like it trades at a mid-teens multiple. On reported accounting profit it does not.

Where the market may be too harsh: it has priced a growth deceleration while the company's committed future contract value — signed business not yet recognized as revenue — is growing about **twice as fast as revenue** (see below). If that backlog is real and not a one-time artifact, today's price is too low.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We separated currency from business.** The company's guide looks like 14% growth; underlying growth is about 11%. Most commentary quotes the headline. Our whole case for cutting rests on the currency-adjusted number, and it is derived from figures both sides verified.
2. **We charged the real cost of stock compensation.** Using the company's own audited operating profit instead of its adjusted profit changes the valuation from "reasonable" to "demanding." Consensus commentary mostly does not do this.
3. **We looked at the backlog, and it argues against our own conclusion.** Our second bench pulled the quarterly filing and found that contracted revenue with more than a year of original duration went from **about $1.3B to about $1.7B year over year — roughly +31%** — against 15% constant-currency revenue growth. That is the one forward-looking indicator available, and it points up. It is the single strongest reason we kept half rather than selling everything.
4. **We treated the buyback as an actor, not a floor.** The authorization is about $42M per month. That is under one day's trading volume. Anyone modeling the buyback as price support is overestimating it, and we can see the drawdown in the filing.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: much of it probably is. This is the weakest part of the case, and we should say so.

- The stock fell ~21% on the news we are reasoning from. We are not early to it.
- With ~40 analysts and a 59% drawdown, there is no neglected-security story here. We could not run our crowding check this session (the tool was unavailable), so "the market hasn't noticed" is an assertion we did not test.
- The backlog growth above is a real argument that the selling overshot.
- The price cuts could be exactly right — buying volume with price is a standard and often correct software playbook. We do not have cohort data either way.

The honest framing is not "we know something." It is: **at ~55x accounting profit with the company guiding down, the payoff is not worth full size, and we can wait one quarter for evidence that costs us nothing.**
