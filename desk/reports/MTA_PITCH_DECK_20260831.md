# PITCH — MTA — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No entry. Metalla Royalty & Streaming (NYSE American: MTA) at $10.97 is priced for a business roughly four to five times the size of the one the company itself forecasts, the buyer who drove the stock up 109% off its low stopped buying about seven weeks ago, and the multi-year growth target that would justify the price was quietly withdrawn — so we stay flat and reopen on evidence, not on a lower price.

---

## What we believe

Metalla owns royalties: contractual rights to a slice of the revenue from mines that other companies build and operate. It runs nothing itself. That is a genuinely good business model — 90% gross margins, no capital calls, no labor — and the first half of 2026 was the company's first profitable half ever ([6-K filed 2026-08-13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1722606/000106299326004314/exhibit99-1.htm): revenue $8.29M, net income $1.33M).

We believe four things about it:

**1. The growth everyone is quoting is mostly the gold price, not the company.** The circulating headline is "+94% revenue." That is one quarter, in dollars. Over the full half-year, the company delivered 1,833 gold-equivalent ounces versus 1,468 a year earlier — **up 24.9%**. The realized price per ounce implied by the filed income statement went from $3,008 to $4,522 — **up 50.3%**. Multiply those and you get the +87.7% half-year revenue growth exactly. So roughly two-thirds of the revenue growth is the gold price, which we do not control and are not paid for owning through this vehicle.

**2. The volume story has stalled, and the company stopped saying otherwise.** Metalla delivered 3,436 ounces in 2025 against its own guidance of 3,500–4,500 — a miss of the low end ([company PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/metalla-announces-preliminary-2025-geo-deliveries-record-annual-revenues-and-corporate-updates-302685681.html)). It then re-issued the **identical** 3,500–4,500 range for 2026. Separately, a December-2023 corporate strategy publicly targeted **">30,000 ounces annually by 2030"** ([PR](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/metalla-provides-three-year-corporate-strategy-and-highlights-near-term-catalysts)). That target appears in neither the 2025 results release nor the current management discussion. It was not restated lower. It was dropped.

**3. The price already pays for the target that was dropped.** Our own arithmetic, on the same model both benches used: 30,000 ounces a year by 2030, at $4,500 gold, 90% cash margin, $10M of annual corporate cost, valued at 20× cash flow and discounted back four years at 10%, works out to roughly **$10–12 per share** depending on whether you tax it at 25% or at the 40% rate the company actually paid this year. The stock is $10.97. The market is not paying for the 4,000 ounces management guides to; it is paying, in full and undiscounted for risk, for an aspiration the company no longer repeats.

**4. The bid that produced the move has gone quiet, and what is left is an overhang.** Tether-affiliated entities went from 7.8% of the company in January to 13.5% (12,587,333 shares) by mid-July, filed across five amendments in four months. The most recent amendment ([2026-07-15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1722606/000110465926083957/primary_doc.xml)) reports **no open-market purchases** since 6/16 and states the purpose of the transaction as **"Not applicable"** — no control intent, no plans. Nothing has been filed in the 47 days since. Under the prompt-amendment duty that attaches to this kind of filing, that means less than 1% has been added. The stock has not corrected. So: the support is finished, the position remains, and there is no takeover option embedded in it.

**Our conclusion:** this is a good business at a price that requires a step-change nobody has committed to in writing, with its marginal buyer withdrawn. Flat.

## What the market believes

The market believes Metalla has crossed over. The story is coherent and not stupid:

- 2026 is the first profitable year after a long build; the balance sheet is clean ($10.7M cash, $12.3M drawn on a $40M facility with a $35M accordion, maturity 2028, no equity issuance program);
- the portfolio has 46 royalty interests including slices of very large future mines (Taca Taca, Copper World, Côté) — call options that cost nothing to hold;
- gold at ~$4,589/oz makes every one of those options more likely to be exercised by their operators;
- a large, sophisticated, price-insensitive buyer accumulated 13.5% of the company this year;
- the three analysts covering it model **$8.79M of 2026 net income** and a Buy rating.

The market is also, quietly, valuing this the way royalty companies get valued: on net asset value and cash flow multiples that assume the pipeline converts. Comparable royalty companies traded at a median **1.68× net asset value** and **23.6× 2026 cash flow** in the Royal Gold merger banker analysis ([DEFM14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/85535/000008553525000132/rgld-20250902.htm)). Apply 1.68× to today's ~$1.03B enterprise value and the market is implying the royalty portfolio is worth about **$617M** — against $255M carried on the balance sheet at cost. That is not an absurd implication for a 46-asset book. It is just an unverified one.

## Why we might have edge

Three specific places where we did work the tape has not:

**We decomposed the growth into ounces versus price, over the right period.** The +94% figure is a single quarter measured in dollars. The company's own half-year ounce growth is +24.9%. That decomposition is in the management discussion; it is not in the headlines. Anyone underwriting +94% as operating momentum is underwriting the gold price and calling it execution.

**We rebuilt the earnings from the filings instead of taking consensus.** Starting from guidance (4,000 ounces at the $4,522 the filings actually imply): $18.1M revenue, 89.9% gross margin, less $6.0M general costs, $3.6M stock compensation, $1.5M interest, at the 40.4% tax rate the company actually paid in the first half → **about $3.1M of 2026 net income**. Consensus is $8.79M. To get there you need roughly 6,200 ounces — *above the top of the company's own guidance* — or you have to ignore stock compensation and the cash tax rate. We think the sell-side model is running a revenue line the issuer does not guide to. We cannot read their model, so this is a reasoned inference, not a proof.

**We read the ownership filings as a time series, not a snapshot.** Everyone can see "Tether owns 13.5%." What matters is that the buying stopped on a specific date, that the stated purpose is "Not applicable," and that the silence since has a legal meaning (under 1%). A stake that was a tailwind through June is inventory in August. That reversal is only visible if you diff the amendments.

Honestly, the edge here is a *negative* one. We are not claiming to see value nobody sees. We are claiming the bull case rests on a number the company stopped publishing, and that this is checkable and mostly unchecked.

## Why it might be priced in

We should be symmetric about this. Several reasons our case may already be in the price, or simply wrong:

- **Royalty companies always look expensive on current cash flow.** 103× enterprise value to cash flow and 4.0× book are the normal appearance of an option portfolio early in its life. Franco-Nevada looked like this once. A high multiple on a pre-ramp royalty book is not evidence of mispricing; it is the shape of the asset class. Our prize table is essentially an argument that the option value is too big, which is a much weaker claim than "the earnings are fake."
- **Nobody computed net asset value — including us.** This is the single decisive question and it is open. If an asset-by-asset valuation at spot gold comes back at $617M or more, today's price is fair on peer multiples and our entire case collapses to "we did not do the work." This is a coverage gap, not a finding.
- **The withdrawn target may have been withdrawn for good reasons.** Companies drop stale multi-year targets all the time when the asset mix changes. Absence is suggestive, not damning.
- **The analyst price targets ($8.08–$10.69) are probably stale** — they predate the stock doubling. "The stock trades above the highest target" sounds damning and is not; we struck that argument.
- **Gold at $4,589 and rising covers a lot of sins.** If gold goes to $6,000, the realized price does the work again and the ounce stall stops mattering for another two years.

## What could go wrong

*(This is the pre-mortem. We hold no position and have no orders, so these are the failure modes of the decision — including the decision to enter later at a reopen level.)*

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy the dip and it is a gold trade wearing a company costume.** We reopen at $6, gold retraces from $4,589 to $3,600, and realized price per ounce falls 20% while ounces grow the guided 2–13%. Revenue goes flat-to-down, the "first profitable year" reverses, and the multiple compresses on top of it. We lose 40% and learn that we bought a levered gold proxy at a 4× book premium instead of buying gold. *Tripwire exists:* spot gold, and the realized price per ounce disclosed each quarter in the management discussion. Monitorable, and we would see it coming.

**Failure narrative 2 — The overhang unwinds and we are the exit liquidity.** The 13.5% holder is a crypto-treasury entity. Its reason to sell has nothing to do with mines: a drawdown in its own book, a redemption cycle, a regulatory event. That stake is roughly **43 days of average daily volume** on a thin stock. It can sell down toward 5% before the market gets a full accounting, and each amendment tells us what already happened. *Tripwire is lagging and partial* — we can watch crypto prices as a proxy and ownership amendments after the fact, but we cannot see the seller's balance sheet, and the trigger is not in our information set. Practically: by the time we know, we have eaten the print.

**Failure narrative 3 (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE) — the royalty contracts do something we never see coming.** Metalla operates nothing. The economics live in contracts with third-party operators that are not public documents, on mines whose disclosures appear in *other companies'* filings, some of them private or non-US. A royalty can be diluted by a buy-down clause, disputed over an area-of-interest boundary, subordinated in an operator restructuring, or rendered worthless when an operator re-sequences a pit. None of that appears in Metalla's filings until after it is settled. We cannot build a sensor for it because the primary documents do not exist in any feed we have. **Unknown-unknown class: counterparty-plane opacity** — the value driver's disclosure happens on someone else's plane, on someone else's schedule, in venues we do not index. Every detector we ran against this name (pollution, permits, app reviews, hazard exposure) came back UNCHECKABLE or NOT-FIRED for exactly this reason: there is no facility, no product, no site that belongs to the issuer. We are structurally blind on the asset side of this company, and no amount of reading Metalla's filings fixes that.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own predicted flow, our own book:**

- *Size versus liquidity.* Average daily volume is roughly 293,000 shares, but that figure is from July 2025 and **neither bench could verify it** — broker market-data permissions blocked both attempts. At $11, a 0.5%-of-book position is on the order of 90,000 shares, which at the stale figure is about 31% of a full day's volume and would take a week or more to build at a responsible participation rate — and considerably longer to exit into stress. Any sizing built on 293,000 shares is built on an unverified number that is 13 months old.
- *Bidding into flow we ourselves predict.* Our central structural finding is that a 13.5% holder is a probable future seller. Standing a bid underneath a supply we forecast is precisely the trade we have told ourselves not to do. Either we believe finding 4 and do not bid, or we do not believe it and should stop citing it.
- *Adverse selection on the seller.* A dated, mechanical forced sale is something we can price. This is not that. It is a discretionary seller with better information about its own liquidity than we have, no lock-up and no standstill. Discretionary sellers sell when it hurts most.
- *Correlation with what we already own.* This name arrived as part of a real-asset ballast cohort alongside other gold royalty names. Adding MTA is not diversification; it is a third helping of the same gold-price factor at the highest multiple in the group and the worst liquidity. If gold rolls over, the whole sleeve moves together, and this is the position that cannot be sold.
- *A live dilution vector.* A **US$500M base shelf** went effective 2026-07-28. It is not a secondary offering and there is no lockup population, but it is a standing authorization to issue equity into strength — from a company whose market value is ~$1.03B.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we paid for a five-fold ounce ramp that the company itself never promised in a filed document, and the gold price stopped covering for the fact that the ramp was not arriving.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry at any price today.** Both benches reached flat independently, and we concur. Note explicitly: **a price level alone does not reopen this**, because the arithmetic below shows the entire current price is pipeline option value, and the pipeline target has been withdrawn. But the bands are stated concretely so the decision is mechanical if the tape gets there.

**The valuation ladder (our arithmetic, on the benches' model: 90% cash margin, $10M annual corporate cost, valued at 20× cash flow, discounted back to today at 10%, gold at $4,500):**

| What you have to believe by 2030 | Implied share price (25% tax / 40% tax) |
|---|---|
| Guidance only — 4,000 ounces forever, no ramp | **$1.03 – $1.25** |
| 10,000 ounces (one-third of the withdrawn target) | **$2.80 – $2.30** |
| 15,000 ounces (half the withdrawn target) | **$5.56 – $4.42** |
| 30,000 ounces (the full 2023 target, on time) | **$12.20 – $9.70** |

Today's $10.97 sits in the bottom row. That is the whole pitch in one table.

**Reopen bands — price condition (necessary, not sufficient):**
- **$6.00 or below** — the level at which the market is crediting roughly half the withdrawn 2030 target rather than all of it. This is where we re-open the file and do the net asset value work.
- **$5.25 or below** — the 52-week low; the price at which half-credit holds even at the 40% cash tax rate the company actually pays. A pure-discount entry becomes arguable here *if* the evidence gate below is also met.
- **No add ladder is proposed** because there is no starter.
- **$11.60 or above** (52-week high) with still no published multi-year outlook: do not chase; log it as expectations extending further beyond anything filed.

**Reopen bands — evidence condition (any ONE required, in addition to price):**
1. A **multi-year ounce outlook of 12,000+ published in the management discussion or an audited-venue filing**, with named assets and operator-confirmed start dates. Not a slide deck. Venue matters here precisely because the missing target lives in deck venue and the guidance lives in filing venue.
2. **An asset-by-asset net asset value at spot gold** — done by us — showing the portfolio is worth more than ~$617M, which is what today's price implies at the peer 1.68× multiple. *This is work we owe and have not done. It is the fastest way to be proven wrong.*
3. **A new ownership amendment showing the large holder at 18–20%+ with a stated control or strategic purpose.** That converts an overhang into a floor and inverts the sizing logic entirely.
4. **A readable sell-side model** that reconciles $8.79M of 2026 net income to 3,500–4,500 ounces. If their revenue line is right and ours is wrong, our second finding dies.

**Sizing if both conditions are met:** starter of **0.25–0.50% of book**, built at no more than **1% of average daily volume per day**. At the stale 293,000-share figure that is ~2,900 shares/day and roughly two to four weeks to build — which is itself an argument against the position. **Do not commit sizing until average daily volume is verified from the broker.** Treat the current number as unreliable.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **2026-11-12** — third-quarter results (vendor-derived, **UNCONFIRMED** — no company release names this date). ~50 trading days out. Watch: nine-month ounces against the 3,500–4,500 range, and whether a multi-year outlook reappears.
- **Early January 2027 (estimated from the prior year's pattern, UNCONFIRMED)** — preliminary full-year ounce deliveries release. **This is the decisive date.** Last year this release carried the guidance miss. Full-year ounces below 3,500 for a second consecutive year, against an identical re-issued range, would end the growth story on the company's own numbers.
- **Any day** — an ownership amendment from the 13.5% holder, in either direction.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because two of these are load-bearing:

1. **The net asset value of the 46-asset royalty portfolio. Never computed, by anyone, in this court.** Today's price implies roughly $617M on peer multiples against $255M carried at cost. If the real number is $617M or better, this deck is wrong and the name is fair. This is the highest-value open task on the file.
2. **Average daily volume.** Broker market data was permission-blocked for both benches. Everything we say about exit risk, the "43 days of volume" overhang, and our own participation rate rests on a July-2025 third-party figure of 293,000 shares. Do not size off it.
3. **The consensus figures.** $8.79M of 2026 net income, three analysts, $8.08–$10.69 target range — all secondary sources. We could not read a single sell-side model. Our claim that consensus ignores stock compensation and the cash tax rate is an inference from arithmetic, not something we verified.
4. **The 2026-11-12 earnings date.** Vendor-derived. No company release confirms it.
5. **Why the 2030 target was withdrawn.** We confirmed the absence across two venues. We did not find any statement explaining it, and we did not ask the company.
6. **Everything on the asset side.** Individual royalty contract terms, operator mine plans, buy-down and area-of-interest clauses, and operator financial health are all outside our information set. See failure narrative 3 — this is structural, not a to-do item.
7. **The gold price deck.** All valuation math above uses $4,500/oz. Spot was ~$4,589 on 2026-08-27. Every number in the ladder moves roughly proportionally with gold.
