# PITCH — AAP — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — start a 0.25% position at ~$43.61 with a second 0.25% gated on the Q3 print; the red bench dissents to FLAT, and its dissent now rests on one surviving objection (the sales trend), not on the valuation argument it led with.

---

## What we believe

Advance Auto Parts is a 4,311-store auto-parts retailer trading at $43.61, down 32.6% from its 52-week high and 16.3% *above* its 52-week low of $37.50. The company is roughly two-thirds of the way through a multi-year turnaround after selling its Worldpac business, and it sits on $3.12B of cash against $3.39B of long-term debt — essentially no net financial debt.

The core belief is simple and it is a **margin** belief, not a sales belief:

At today's price, the enterprise is worth $2.885B ($2.62B of stock at 60.0M shares, plus $3,387M debt, less $3,120M cash). Management guides fiscal 2026 to $8.485–8.575B of sales at a 3.8–4.5% adjusted operating margin. At the midpoint that is **$354M of operating profit, so the enterprise trades at 8.1x operating profit**. Put differently: if you believe a healthy specialty retailer is worth 10x operating profit, today's price is paying for a **3.4% operating margin forever — below the bottom end of the company's own current-year guidance.**

The sensitivity, which is the whole case:

| Sustained operating margin | Implied share price | vs. $43.61 |
|---|---|---|
| 3.4% (what the price pays today) | $43.61 | — |
| 4.15% (this year's guided midpoint) | ~$54 | +24% |
| 4.9% | ~$65 | +50% |
| 6.0% | ~$81 | +85% |
| 8.0% (the 2021 level) | ~$109 | +150% |

Every 100 basis points of operating margin is worth about $1.03 per share of after-tax earnings, or roughly $14 of share price at 10x. **We do not need the turnaround to work. We need it to not go backwards from the level already guided.**

## What the market believes

The market believes the sales trend is deteriorating faster than the margin plan can absorb, and it has a real point.

The last quarter's headline comparable-sales figure was −0.5%, but it was not evenly distributed: the first 8 weeks ran about +1%, which means the final 4 weeks ran about **−3.5%**. Management said the current quarter was tracking "slightly ahead" of that, implying roughly −2.5% to −3% today. Half of the quarter's margin expansion (130 of 260 basis points) came from **$26M of one-time tariff refunds**, stated verbatim in the earnings exhibit — not from operations.

The market also believes the guidance raise was hollow, and it is right about that too. Management raised adjusted EPS guidance from $2.40–3.10 to $2.60–3.30 while leaving sales guidance and operating-margin guidance **completely unchanged**. The entire raise came from interest income on the cash pile — "approximately $100 million of interest income, an increase of $20 million." After tax that is $1.21 per share, **41% of the $2.95 midpoint**. Sell-side consensus for the year is $2.92 against a $2.95 guided midpoint: the street is sitting exactly on the guide and cut estimates after the print.

So the market's view is: negative and worsening sales, a margin beat that was half tariff refunds, and an EPS raise that contains zero operating improvement. That is a coherent bear case and it is why the stock is where it is.

## Why we might have edge

Three things, in descending order of confidence.

**1. The price is below the company's own guided floor.** This is the one that matters. To lose money from here on a three-year view, AAP has to sustain an operating margin *below* the low end of a guide it reaffirmed 5 days ago while beating on the quarter. That is not impossible, but it means the current price contains no margin of safety on the upside and a meaningful one on the downside of the *guided* range. Both benches litigated the sales trajectory in detail; **neither bench priced this until the blue bench raised it in its final section.** The market is trading a comp-sales print; the asset is a margin option.

**2. A reporting-calendar artifact makes the year look worse than it is.** Fiscal 2025 was a **53-week year** (2024-12-29 through 2026-01-03); fiscal 2026 is 52 weeks (16+12+12+12). Q4 loses a week worth roughly $160M of sales versus the year-ago quarter. That means reported full-year sales growth will look approximately **flat-to-negative even on a +1–2% comparable-sales result** — a mechanical headline that reads as deterioration to a screen and is already inside the company's unchanged guidance. This is a genuinely non-consensus observation and it is verifiable from the filing's own period tags.

**3. The most widely-repeated bear datum does not survive arithmetic.** The circulating claim from the earnings call is that DIY comparable sales fell "low double digits" while Pro rose "low single digits," summing to −0.5%. Solve it: with Pro at +3%, Pro would have to be **73% of net sales** for that to arithmetically work. It is about 58–60%. At a 60% Pro mix the implied DIY comp is **−5.75%**, not −11%. And critically, the *audited* earnings exhibit filed with the SEC declines to quantify DIY at all — it says only that "tighter household budgets constrained spending more than we anticipated." The "low double digit" figure exists only in transcript coverage, and a second transcript read of the same call says DIY declined **low single digits**. The most likely reconciliation is that −LDD referred to DIY *transactions/units*, offset by commodity price inflation on the same items. Someone is trading a unit decline as if it were a dollar decline.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly and plainly:

- **Our two strongest sales-side objections are the street's objections.** The exit rate and the tariff-refund quality of the beat were both in the post-print analyst notes. We have no differentiated forecast for this fiscal year — and we structurally cannot, because this year's EPS is anchored to the guide by construction. Any divergence we have is on fiscal 2027+ operating margin, where we have not yet built a number.
- **A 32.6% drawdown in a widely-covered $2.6B NYSE name is not an information gap.** Nobody is failing to look at this. What we are claiming is a *framing* edge (margin option vs. comp print), not a facts edge.
- **The margin sensitivity table is not proprietary.** Anyone can run it. The reason the market does not pay for it is that it does not believe the margin, which is a legitimate disagreement rather than an oversight.
- **We have zero alternative-data instrumentation on this name.** Every non-filing detector we consulted was either not applicable or has no tested link to auto-parts comparable sales. We are trading on the same public filings as everyone else.

## What could go wrong

*(The pre-mortem. Three failure narratives, then the structural leg.)*

**Failure 1 — The recovery is not cyclical; it is share loss, and margin goes down, not up.** AutoZone and O'Reilly have out-executed AAP for a decade. If DIY weakness is AAP-specific rather than category-wide, the 3.8% guided floor is not a floor — it is a way-station, and the operating-leverage math runs symmetrically: a −3% comp held through the second half puts the 3.8% guide itself at risk. The price already pays for 3.4%; there is no cushion beneath the guide, only above it. **Tripwire: monitorable.** Q3 comparable sales, the direction of the operating-margin guide, and AZO/ORLY DIY comps for the same period.

**Failure 2 — The cash is not ours and the interest income evaporates.** The $3.12B of cash sits against **$3,126M of accounts payable on $3,844M of inventory (81%)**. This is the float of a negative-working-capital model — supplier money, not owner surplus. Negative comps are exactly the condition under which vendor terms tighten, and the $100M of interest income carrying 41% of guided EPS would go first. Separately, the filing carries a 2027 unsecured notes context: the cash may be pre-funding that maturity, in which case repayment cancels both the $100M of income and roughly $186M of interest expense. **Tripwire: partially monitorable and lagging.** The AP/inventory ratio prints quarterly, one quarter late; a notes redemption would come via 8-K. A private renegotiation of supplier terms or a trade-credit insurer pulling coverage would be invisible to us until it showed up in the numbers.

**Failure 3 — Execution inside 4,311 stores goes wrong and we cannot see it. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** The entire thesis is a margin-recovery option, and margin recovery at a physical retailer is produced by supply-chain consolidation, distribution-center transitions, store-labor models, and pricing systems — none of which emit an observable signal between quarterly reports. **Unknown-unknown class: internal operational state at a physical-footprint retailer with no third-party observability.** We checked our entire connector inventory against this name and every one came back not-applicable or untestable: app-review velocity has no backtested link to auto-parts comps, product-heat and review connectors measure brand noise rather than comparable sales, permit connectors have no disclosed projects, and the customer-concentration detector correctly did not fire (the concentration disclosure is by product category, not customer — a DIY/Pro retailer has no undisclosed anchor customer to triangulate). **We have literally no instrument on this business between prints.** A distribution-center transition failure, a senior-management departure, or a botched systems cutover would reach us at the same moment it reaches everyone else, at whatever price that moment sets. A second, adjacent unknown-unknown class: the **private trade-credit channel** — the terms AAP gets from suppliers are negotiated in private and disclosed only in aggregate, one quarter in arrears.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- **Our size is immaterial to the tape and this is not a risk.** A 0.25% starter against the deployable book is roughly $8,500 — about 200 shares against a name that trades well over $100M a day. We cannot move this stock and we will never have a liquidation problem. *(Average daily volume is an estimate; we did not verify it — see unverified.)*
- **Our own add rule bids into flow we ourselves predict, and that is a real conflict.** We forecast a weak Q3. Our proposed second tranche triggers at $38. A weak Q3 is the single most likely thing that takes the stock to $38. That means the price gate as written would have us buying exactly the flow our thesis anticipates — which is the failure mode our own value-ladder discipline exists to prevent. **Resolution built into the bands below:** the $38 add is conditional on the *cause* of the decline, not the price alone. If $38 arrives with the operating-margin guide cut below 3.8%, the thesis is broken and the add is cancelled, not triggered.
- **Doctrine interactions.** (a) This is a taxable account and a new lot, so any exit before roughly August 2027 is short-term — the 24% upside case to $54 is worth materially less after tax if it arrives early, which argues for the tranche schedule rather than a full-size entry. (b) Premium-selling against the position fails our own gates: a cash-secured put here would have a break-even floor above a strike we cannot defend while comparable sales are negative, so no options overlay. (c) A 0.25% starter is small enough that its main cost is not capital but attention and instrumentation — and per the point above, we currently have none to give it. (d) This competes for a slot against the index-first deployment plan, and it should be judged against that, not against cash.
- **The benches disagree and we are proposing the blue bench's answer over the red bench's.** That is a live risk in itself: red recommended FLAT, and while its lead argument was overturned on arithmetic, its surviving argument (the exit rate) is the one most likely to be right in the next 90 days.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the −3% exit-rate sales trend persisted into the second half, the 3.8% guided operating-margin floor was cut rather than defended, and the stock re-rated to price a structurally lower margin — i.e., the red bench's timing objection turns out to have been a margin objection all along.**

**Trims and exits** (from the margin table above, at 10x enterprise value to operating profit):
- Trim one-third at **$54** — the guided-midpoint 4.15% margin, fully valued on management's own current-year plan.
- Trim one-third at **$65** — 4.9% margin, 75bp above guide mid.

**Thesis kill — exit in full, do not average down:**
1. Adjusted operating-margin guide cut below **3.8%**, or
2. Two consecutive quarters of negative comparable sales *with* operating margin printing below 3.8%, or
