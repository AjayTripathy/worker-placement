# PITCH — FIG — PROPOSED VERDICT: NO ENTRY (and no short) — stay flat into the ~Nov 4, 2026 earnings report; reopen only on the triggers below — **PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

## What we believe

Figma is a genuinely good business that we should not buy today, because the forward-looking demand numbers in its own latest filing just broke, and a huge block of newly sellable insider stock is still working its way through the market.

The good-business part is not in dispute: roughly 84% gross margin, $53.2M of free cash flow in the June quarter, $1.667B of cash and securities against zero debt, and revenue guided to grow 36–39% this fiscal year ([10-Q, filed 2026-08-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579878/000162828026053348/fig-20260630.htm)).

The problem is what the same filing says about *future* demand. The metrics that look backward (net dollar retention of 136%, deferred revenue up 44.7% year over year) still look healthy. But every metric that looks forward broke in the June quarter, all at once:

- **Deferred revenue** (cash collected for services not yet delivered — a forward-demand gauge) *fell* by $0.9M during the quarter, while revenue grew 11% sequentially. The same quarter a year earlier it *grew* by $26.5M.
- **Remaining performance obligations** (total contracted future revenue) grew only 0.7% sequentially against that 11% revenue growth.
- New bookings ran slightly below revenue recognized (book-to-bill just under 1) — the company delivered more than it signed.
- Management's own guidance encodes the slowdown: the third-quarter midpoint implies roughly +1% sequential growth, and the full-year guide implies second-half growth of about +32% versus +47% in the first half.

On top of that, a final block of **77.7 million shares** came out of lock-up around August 7 — about five full days of the stock's entire trading volume — and insider sale notices (Form 144s) kept arriving through August 10. Some of that supply may already be absorbed, but we can't verify it yet.

Our conclusion: this is a company one bad leading-indicator quarter into a possible demand slowdown, at a valuation (8.1x–9.5x this year's guided sales) that is fair-to-full for the trajectory actually printed. The right position is none.

## What the market believes

The market appears to have already read the same filing the same way. The stock fell **14.9%** on the earnings day itself (Aug 5 close $28.15 → Aug 6 close $23.97, on 53M shares), then closed down *again* on the actual unlock day, and sat at **$25.42** on August 14 — down 68.5% from its 52-week high of $80.75, but still 53% above its low of $16.60. Short interest stands at about 66 million shares.

In other words: the market is treating the earnings report as a demand disappointment, not merely a supply event. This is a heavily covered, high-profile recent IPO — there is no neglected corner here where the crowd might simply not have looked.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: on the original long thesis, we found we don't. The thesis argued the market was confusing lock-up selling pressure with real demand damage, and that demand was fine. Our own checks refuted both halves — demand indicators broke *and* the selling pressure isn't finished.

Where edge could still exist, and what this deck actually preserves:

1. **We now know exactly which two numbers resolve the question.** If the Q3 report (≈ Nov 4) shows deferred revenue building again by more than +$40M in the quarter, or contracted backlog growing more than +8% sequentially with net dollar retention holding at 134% or better, the June quarter was a blip and the long case reopens — likely before the crowd re-underwrites it.
2. **The supply overhang may clear faster than assumed.** About 175M shares traded between Aug 6 and Aug 14 against the 77.7M unlocked block, with 66M shorted shares as a standing pool of buyers who must eventually cover. If the next published short-interest figures show covering completed and the price holds for three weeks on normal volume, the flow objection dies.
3. **We did the share-count work most won't.** The honest valuation range is 8.1x (basic) to ~9.5x (diluted) guided sales — not the thesis's 8.0x and not our own red bench's initial 9.8x. Having the correct number means we'll recognize genuine cheapness (~$16.90, see entry bands) if the market overshoots.

## Why it might be priced in

Because the simplest reading is that it already is. The 14.9% single-day drop landed **on the earnings release**, a day before the unlock — the market punished the demand numbers, not the share supply. The forward-looking metrics and the tape point the same direction; there is no gap between the story and the numbers for us to arbitrage. And at $25.42 the stock is not cheap enough to pay us for the unresolved questions: 8.1x–9.5x guided sales is a full price for growth that management itself guides to decelerate from 47% to 32%. The one claim that could rescue the long — "the price already assumes catastrophe" — was asserted by the thesis and never computed by anyone, including our own skeptics.
