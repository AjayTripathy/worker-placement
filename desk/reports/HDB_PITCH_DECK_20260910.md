## What we believe

HDFC Bank is a solvent, over-capitalised deposit franchise (CET1 17.4%, ROA 1.85%) that is having a governance year, not a solvency year. But the thing that is actually deteriorating — the funding mix — is deteriorating in the same place the lawsuit points. CASA (cheap current/savings money) has gone from ~38% in Sep-23 to 34.8% FY25 to 34.1% FY26 to **32% at Jun-26**, and period-end CASA balances fell **−3.3%** in absolute rupees to ₹10,253bn. Net interest margin walked 3.48 → 3.34 → 3.26. The bank replaced the missing cheap money with wholesale: a **$1.75bn senior unsecured GIFT-City bond** ($500m 3y + $1.25bn 5y, 26-Aug-26). That is a franchise substituting expensive funding for cheap funding while its CEO leaves (26-Oct-26), no successor is named, the RBI has pulled a supervisory examination of the MSRDC deposit arrangements forward from the post-April-2027 cycle, and a securities class action runs to a 13-Oct-26 lead-plaintiff deadline.

We believe the price does **not** yet pay us for that. At ₹687 / $21.83 the stock trades at ~**1.87x trailing book** (₹368/share; $11.64 per ADS at 3:1 and USD/INR 94.83) — which is essentially the marginal *bull's* own target multiple. BofA cut its target multiple to **1.80x forward** P/B on leadership and governance uncertainty. You are not buying a discount to the bull case; you are buying the bull case, with the file open.

So: **DAMAGE-ARRIVING, priced roughly fairly, wait for the October evidence.** We are flat because the reward for being early is small and the left tail is undated.

The tape is −40.7% in USD from the 52-week high (−32.7% in local rupees; the ~8pp gap is the currency, not the bank), it printed a fresh 52-week low ₹687.10–₹690.65 on 09-Sep-26, and the *downgrading* analyst — HSBC, cutting to Hold — still carries ₹830 / **$26.1**, about 21% above the tape, on "deposit-growth concerns" rather than fraud. The market also believes the RBI is being accommodative — the 30-Jul-26 bulk-deposit amendment was widely headlined that way.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We read the operative words of the RBI amendment instead of the headline.** The amendment *kept* the no-discrimination clause — deposit rates "shall be uniform across all branches and for all customers and there shall be no discrimination" — and only added LCR-run-off-category flexibility for ≥₹3cr bulk deposits, **plus daily publication of bulk rates, effective 01-Oct-26**. Nothing in it legalises ₹45cr routed to a single state depositor through a marketing line. Read correctly it is a *forward constraint* arriving exactly when wholesale/bulk is the growth engine — it raises the cost of the marginal procured deposit into Q2/Q3 FY27.
2. **We refuse the bank's own metric.** Management markets **average** deposits +13.3% YoY. When the alleged misconduct *is* deposit procurement (6.01% paid to MSRDC vs 3.50% savings, a ~2.5% gap booked as road-safety sponsorship), deposit growth stops being a damage detector and becomes the exhibit. The honest series are cost of deposits, CASA%, and government/institutional deposit share — and the two we can see both print damage.
3. **We caught a corporate-action trap that would have flipped our posture.** A vendor book-value series unadjusted for the 1:1 bonus (record 27-Aug-25) against a bonus-adjusted price manufactures a fake 1.15x book. Recomputed off the issuer's own net worth (~₹566,201cr at Jun-26) it is ~1.87x, corroborated independently at 1.89x. That single number was the entire FLAT→BUY argument.
4. **Conduct duration.** The MSRDC arrangements date to **2017 and 2021** and were board-characterised as "business overreach." A six-to-nine-year arrangement is a wider amended-complaint surface than a one-off.
5. **The ESOP tell.** The GNRC granted **58,695,020 options + 15,373,400 RSUs** (plus small tranches) at ₹712.10 on 06-Sep-26 — at a −34% trough, seven weeks before the CEO's exit, vesting 15/20/25/40% from grant date, recipients not disclosed. Primary document: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144967/000119312526384277/. Total ≈ 74.4M shares ≈ **0.48% of ~15.4bn shares** — economically trivial dilution, but it is retention economics that mechanically favours *continuity*, which cuts against any "external mandate for a balance-sheet reset" story.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, the bear case here is not secret:

- **HSBC's downgrade rationale *is* our thesis.** Deposit growth and margin compression are the published reason the marginal seller sold. Our CASA/NIM finding is **consensus, not novel** — we graded it as such.
- **The multiple already moved.** BofA cut 1.95x → 1.80x forward on exactly "leadership, strategic direction, corporate governance." The tape sits at that cut multiple. The de-rating we are predicting has largely happened.
- **The margin slide predates the scandal.** FY25 3.48 → FY26 3.34 was running before 27-May-26. Attributing the 9bp Q1 slip to MSRDC mislabels merger-legacy mix drift as fraud damage. Our own Red bench made that error and Blue reduced it.
- **Domestic absorption is real and one-way.** LIC to 9.99% plus MF accumulation against an exhausted FII unwind means there is a large price-insensitive bid under the stock. Being flat into that is a real cost.
- **The alleged payment is 0.24% of quarterly PAT** (₹45cr / ₹19,060cr). On the direct axis this is a rounding error, and the market is right to treat it that way.
- **Capital is not in question.** CET1 17.4%, no equity raise, the August money was bonds.

If the October update is fine and a credible successor is named, we will have been flat through a 20% move and the entire pitch will have been an expensive act of prudence.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The re-rate happens without us (highest probability).** Early-Oct provisional numbers show period-end deposits +12–14%, CASA stabilises, an internal successor with continuity credibility is named by 26-Oct, and the stock closes the 21% gap to HSBC's *bear* target. We enter our starter 15–20% higher, or not at all. *Tripwire: monitorable* — the provisional update and the successor announcement are both dated, and our gates are written against them.

**Failure narrative 2 — We buy the gate and the file is wider than MSRDC.** We arm the ¼ starter on a clean October, and in Nov–Feb an amended complaint or the RBI examination reaches conduct outside MSRDC — other state entities, other years, or accounting that touches the deposit line. Both benches agreed this restores FATAL. The 2017/2021 duration makes it more likely than a one-off would. *Tripwire: partially monitorable* — docket and 6-K watch catch it after it prints, not before.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** The loss comes from something with no public precursor: a whistleblower letter, a regulator's private finding, a counterparty (state or institutional depositor) quietly pulling balances, or a second internal-vigilance matter that never reached a filing. We cannot see it, and no sensor in our manifest reaches it. **Unknown-unknown class: private-file / unobservable-counterparty conduct tail** — adverse information held inside a supervisory process or a non-public counterparty relationship, where the first observable is the outcome itself. There is no early warning to arm; the only defence is size and the fact that we hold no position today.

**Reflexive / structural leg.** Our own footprint is not the problem: a ¼ starter at 0.5% of book is roughly $1.2M on a $250M book against an ADR that we estimate trades ~$90–110M/day, i.e. ~1% of ADV — a non-event, and the *local* line (39.2M shares in a session) is deeper still. Exit liquidity is genuinely ample. The structural risks are the other way round:
- **Predicted flow is one-directional and price-insensitive.** LIC lifting toward 9.99% plus domestic MF absorption means we may be trying to buy weakness that the domestic bid removes before our gate arrives — the classic "gate never fills" failure.
- **The 01-Oct-26 daily bulk-rate publication regime is itself reflexive.** Once every bank's bulk rates are public daily, bulk deposits become more price-elastic and stickier-to-competition; the funding-cost path could worsen faster than the quarterly cadence shows, or the transparency could compress *everyone's* spreads, in which case our HDB-specific thesis is really a sector thesis with no relative edge.
- **Doctrine interactions.** Two doctrines pulled hard in opposite directions here and nearly produced a fabricated entry. KILL-CLASS doctrine ("below FV, no FATAL, all kills SIZING ⇒ MINIMUM STARTER not FLAT") is only as good as the "below FV" input — and that input was a bonus-unadjusted vendor BVPS. Had the `bonus_issue_book_value_desync` check not run, doctrine would have mechanically produced a buy at a 60%-overstated discount. We are flagging that as a live process risk, not a solved one: **doctrine that auto-converts a valuation number into a posture must never run before the corporate-action audit of that number.**
- **Scale sensitivity cuts both ways.** 10bp on ~₹30,115bn of average deposits ≈ ₹30bn pre-tax ≈ ~3% of annual PAT. That is the right axis for this story — but it is a *scale* statement, not an attribution, and if we let it become an attribution we will overpay for a mix-drift problem we mislabelled as a fraud problem.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ — and the fill we would write today is: *we paid for a governance file we cannot see inside, on a book-value number we already had to correct once.*
