# PITCH — SYM — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — WATCH — band $30 (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Symbotic builds and installs warehouse automation systems. It is a real business with real revenue (~$2.8bn run-rate, revenue +22% y/y, 77 systems in deployment) and the demand-damage story people fear is **not** visible in the numbers. We looked hard for it and did not find it.

What we believe instead is narrower and less exciting: **the business is fine and the price is not.** At $42.51 on the full share count (129.9m Class A + 71.4m V-1 + 403.6m V-3 = 604,801,708 units as of 8/3/26, per the 10-Q cover page — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001837240/000183724026000043/sym-20260627.htm), the equity is ~$25.7bn and enterprise value ~$24.0bn. Against a street FY26 adjusted EBITDA of ~$340m that is **~71x**. Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock compensation, and stock compensation is running ~$200m a year ($101.3m in the first six months of FY26 — https://ir.symbotic.com/node/11011/pdf), so the cash number is closer to ~$140m and the multiple is **~172x**.

Then the growth. We do not argue with the street's growth forecast. We adopt it. Revenue compounding 26% and EBITDA compounding 72% from FY25 to FY28 gets you roughly $0.8bn of EBITDA in FY28 — which is **~30x EBITDA three years forward, with no discounting and before the ~$200m of stock comp that never shows up in the adjusted number.** We agree with the bulls and still are not paid for the risk.

And one thing has genuinely stopped working: new orders. Backlog was $22.7bn at Q2 FY26 and $22.5bn at Q3 FY26 on $721m of revenue burned, which implies roughly **$0.5bn of gross new bookings — a book-to-bill near 0.72.** Backlog has been flat for three quarters. Only ~15% of it converts inside twelve months. One customer, Walmart, was **over 84% of FY25 revenue.** About $7.5bn of the backlog is the GreenBox joint venture, which Symbotic owns 35% of and books equity-method losses for — so part of the backlog points back at itself.

Believed, in one line: **good company, wrong price, orders flat, one customer, and a multiple that already pays the bull case.**

## What the market believes

The market believes two different things at once, which is why this is worth writing down.

The bullish half looks at the same $22.5bn backlog and ~$24bn enterprise value and calls the stock cheap at **under 7x forward sales** (https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/01/symbotics-backlog-sits-at-x-billion-heres-the-cust/). Twenty-four analysts carry an average target of $65.67 — roughly 55% above the tape. That view uses *the same inputs we use* (26%/72% CAGRs, the correct ~604.8m unit count) and reaches the opposite conclusion. We take that seriously; it is not a strawman.

The bearish half is already loud on the obvious things: Walmart concentration has been written about repeatedly (https://finance.yahoo.com/news/investors-worry-symbotics-walmart-exposure-153400886.html), insider selling was covered in late August, the stock is down ~51.6% from its 52-week high, and Zacks has it ranked #5 (strongest sell). Sentiment is not evidence, but it tells us the *narrative* bear case is priced.

What the market does **not** appear to hold: the decomposed book-to-bill (everyone quotes the $22.5bn headline; almost nobody differences it against revenue burned), and the stock-comp-corrected multiple (~172x cash EBITDA rather than ~71x).

## Why we might have edge

1. **Decomposition, not headlines.** "Backlog $22.5bn, still strong" is the headline. Differencing three consecutive prints against revenue recognized gives book-to-bill ≈ 1.0 over three quarters and ≈ 0.72 in the latest quarter. That arithmetic is public and, as far as we can find, unpublished.
2. **The stock-comp correction.** The pack's stock-comp series was stale (FY25 vintage, $26.8m/quarter → $107m/yr). The actual FY26 first half is $101.3m, i.e. ~$200m annualized. Adjusted EBITDA of $340m therefore excludes **roughly 59% of itself.** Anyone anchoring on 71x is using a number that is off by more than 2x on the cash line.
3. **Correct capitalization.** Screens and many write-ups use the Class A count (109–130m shares) because that is what the XBRL `dil_sh` tag returns under the Up-C structure. The real unit count is 604.8m. Getting this wrong makes the stock look roughly 5x cheaper than it is. Our own evidence pack had this error, and our red bench half-corrected it (598m) before blue fixed it properly.
4. **Backlog quality, not size.** The $7.5bn GreenBox slug is a 35%-owned, SoftBank-funding-dependent affiliate. The 400-APD Walmart commitment that bulls cite ("could increase … by more than $5.0 billion") is **contingent and explicitly outside backlog.** The filings treat it as optionality; write-ups treat it as support.
5. **We ran the adversarial court and lost half our own bear case.** That is edge too: we know which bear legs are fake, so we will not be shaken out of the flat by a rebuttal we already made ourselves.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: quite a lot of it.

- **The drawdown already happened.** The stock is −51.6% from its high and **+12.8% off its 52-week low.** The clean −53% entry no longer exists. Whatever we are "discovering," a 50% de-rate suggests the tape has discovered something similar.
- **Concentration and insider selling are in mainstream coverage.** Our own blue bench re-tagged both from "novel" to "consensus." We should not pay ourselves for those.
- **7x forward sales is not an absurd number for a 26%-grower.** If the terminal multiple is anything above ~20x EBITDA on FY28's $0.8bn, the bull math works and our 30x-forward complaint becomes a quibble about the discount rate.
- **Stock comp may be transitory.** A de-SPAC with heavy founder/retention grants can see stock comp fall as a share of revenue fast. We are annualizing a two-quarter number ($44.1m then $57.2m — note it *rose*), which is exactly the kind of extrapolation that burned our red bench on cash flow.
- **Book-to-bill of 0.72 in one quarter is one quarter.** Lumpy, multi-hundred-million-dollar capital-equipment orders do not arrive smoothly. Three flat quarters is suggestive; it is not yet a trend, and the Q4 guide is a sequential *raise*.

## What could go wrong (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

We currently hold nothing, so "losing money" here has two shapes: the flat is wrong (we miss the move), or we take the reopen band at ~$30 and the reopen band is a trapdoor. Both are covered.

**Failure narrative 1 — The flat is wrong because one order fixes everything (monitorable).**
Walmart converts a meaningful piece of the 400-APD commitment into disclosed remaining performance obligation, or a named non-Walmart, non-GreenBox anchor signs. Backlog prints $24bn+, book-to-bill snaps back above 1.3, and the market stops arguing about multiples entirely. The stock retraces toward the $65.67 street target and our careful 172x arithmetic reads as pedantry. **Tripwire: backlog > $23.5bn on the FY26Q4 or FY27Q1 print, or an 8-K naming a new anchor customer.** This is our largest regret risk and the reason the reopen band is written as an *up*-gate as well as a down-gate.

**Failure narrative 2 — We buy $30 and it is a value trap (monitorable).**
Price reaches $30 (~48x FY26 adjusted EBITDA, ~117x cash EBITDA, ~20.6x FY28 street EBITDA), we take the starter, and then book-to-bill goes to 0.5 because Walmart's automation capex cycle is simply past peak. Revenue growth decelerates from 22% toward single digits in FY28, the street's 72% EBITDA CAGR is cut, and the stock is a $20 stock on a lower base. Our entry logic was a multiple, and multiples do not hold when the denominator forecast breaks. **Tripwire: two consecutive quarters of book-to-bill < 0.9, or contract liabilities declining sequentially, or Walmart capex commentary turning down.**

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: a private single-counterparty decision, learned only in the print.**
Over 84% of revenue is one customer and ~$7.5bn of backlog is a 35%-owned affiliate funded by SoftBank. The decisions that matter most — Walmart's internal deployment-pace and vendor-strategy reviews, and SoftBank's funding posture toward GreenBox — happen in rooms we have no window into and generate no filing, no permit, no satellite signature, and no price signal until the quarter is already reported. Our own detector sweep proves the blindness rather than curing it: `plant_thermal` and `sentinel2_buildout` both came back **UNCHECKABLE** because revenue-bearing construction is at *customer* distribution centers and we do not have the site list for the 77 in-deployment systems. There is no instrument we can arm that fires before the fact.
**Unknown-unknown class named: SINGLE-COUNTERPARTY STRATEGIC INTENT (off-tape, off-filing, affiliate-funding-coupled).** This class also covers a related-party or affiliate-funded financing action inside the unexplained $427.0m financing inflow — we know the amount and not the source. This is the leg we cannot sensor, and it is the leg that would hurt worst.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- **Our own size vs volume.** Our average daily volume figure for SYM is **not verified** (item 11 below; estimated ~5–9m shares/day, ~$210–380m notional, from large-cap de-SPAC robotics comps). If that estimate holds, a 1.0–1.5% book starter is a small fraction of one day's volume and we are a price-taker with no meaningful footprint. But if the estimate is wrong by 3x — plausible, since it is a comp-based guess, not a feed — a starter placed into a post-print liquidity hole becomes self-impacting. **We should not size off an estimate; pull the real number before any order.** This is a live structural risk created by our own unverified input, not by the market.
- **Predicted flow against us.** The 13D/A and six Form 144s in thirty days mean scheduled affiliate supply is arriving through the open market rather than through a marketed deal (`lockup_expiration_calendar` did not fire; there is no S-1 in the inventory). Persistent 144-channel supply caps rallies and means our $30 gate could be *reached* by mechanical selling rather than by fundamental news — we would be buying the flow, not the discount. Conversely, a Zacks #5 rank plus 51% drawdown is the classic setup for a violent short-covering squeeze on any backlog beat; our crowding read is a **coverage gap in both benches** (short interest unavailable), so we genuinely cannot size that.
- **Doctrine interactions.** Three of our doctrines fought each other here and we should say so. Up-C/TRA doctrine correctly forced the 604.8m unit count — but red then applied the non-controlling-interest haircut *on top* of the full unit count, double-counting the structure and overstating the kill; blue struck it. The `contract_liability_burn_inversion` mechanism our red bench proposed as a new knowledge-graph candidate was **falsified by the issuer's own balance sheet within the same court** (contract liabilities *rose* $1.367bn → $1.860bn). And the year-to-date-differencing habit that produced "Q3 OCF −$147m, decoupled from EBITDA" is now itself logged as an anti-pattern detector (`fiscal_q3_ocf_trough_misread`), because the prior-year same quarter was −$196.5m. Our doctrine stack generated two confident, wrong, novel-tagged kills in one sitting. If we are wrong on the surviving valuation leg, the most likely mechanism is the same one: a compelling decomposition with no base-rate comparison behind it.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that Walmart's private deployment decision — which we have no tripwire for — turns *up*, converts the 400-APD commitment into backlog, and we spend the entire re-rate sitting flat while re-deriving a stock-comp multiple that nobody else is willing to price.

**Reopen band B — FUNDAMENTAL (can reopen the court above $30, but only in this exact conjunction).**
