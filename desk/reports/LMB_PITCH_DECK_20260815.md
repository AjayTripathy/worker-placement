# PITCH — LMB — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION): REJECT — do not buy Limbach at $45.54; the margin break is company-specific and still unfolding, and no entry price is proposed until one of four reopen conditions fires.

## What we believe

Limbach Holdings (building mechanical/electrical contractor, ticker LMB) has fallen about 65% from its high and sits ~3.5% above its 52-week low. The original idea was that this crash overshoots: that the gross-margin collapse mostly reflects the lower-margin Pioneer Power acquisition mixing into the numbers (an effect that lapses after July 1, 2026), plus a large shareholder's exit temporarily weighing on the stock.

After both of our internal review teams worked the evidence, we no longer believe that. We now believe the margin break is Limbach-specific execution and bid-underwriting failure, for three reasons drawn from primary filings:

1. **Management's own guidance contradicts the "acquisition mix will roll off" story.** In the August 4 second-quarter release, the company *cut* full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance from $90–94M to $78–84M while *raising* revenue guidance from $730–760M to $760–790M — and that cut applies to a second half that is entirely past the acquisition anniversary and explicitly excludes the newly bought CYMCOR business. More revenue with meaningfully less profit means management itself expects no mix rescue. Management also said Pioneer's margins will take "two to three years" to reach company average. Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1606163/000162828026052611/a20260630ex991pr.htm
2. **Direct competitors printed the opposite result in the same quarter.** Comfort Systems — the closest mechanical-contracting peer, bidding the same data-center labor markets — expanded gross margin from 23.5% to 25.9% and raised guidance. EMCOR's gross margin also ticked up. Limbach's compressed ~650 basis points. This is not industry-wide cost inflation.
3. **Even the "good" segment is going backwards in dollars.** Owner-direct gross profit fell to $30.8M from $31.6M in Q2 (and $53.7M from $57.8M for the half) despite revenue growing 15–18%. Growth is arriving with negative incremental profit.

Adjusted EPS fell 31% year over year ($0.64 vs $0.93) on 22% revenue growth. That is a real earnings decline, not a quirk of expectations.

## What the market believes

The market has already repriced this as a broken story: $45.54 against a $128.57 high, with the bulk of the decline coming *after* the August 4 earnings report — i.e., in response to public information. Capital Group, which held 7.1% of the company, sold its entire stake to zero before that report; notably, the stock absorbed that supply roughly flat and then fell ~40% on the earnings themselves, which tells you the decline is fundamental repricing, not selling pressure. The stock trades around 7x expected EBITDA, which looks cheap against the peer group — but that multiple sits on a guidance number management has already cut once and on margins still falling.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: not much, and the deck should say so. The original pitch claimed its edge was that "no outside party can currently decompose acquisition mix from structural margin decline." The review process showed that test has effectively already run — the peers reported, and the answer went against us. What little residual edge exists:

- **The Pioneer allocation fork.** The 10-Q reports Pioneer's $54.5M revenue contribution only at the total-company level, not by segment. Nobody outside the company can compute Pioneer's standalone margin, so nobody can yet *prove* how much of the legacy business decayed. A future disclosure could resolve this in either direction — that is a genuine informational catalyst we are positioned to read fast.
- **A specific, pre-committed re-test.** We know exactly what would distinguish "industry problem" from "Limbach problem" at the next round of reports (Comfort Systems' and EMCOR's Q3 mechanical margins), and most observers won't run that comparison systematically.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost everything here is public and already reacted to: the guidance cut, the peer outperformance, the earnings decline, and the shareholder exit are all in filings and press releases, and the stock fell ~40% on the report. The name has sell-side coverage; consensus already reflects the bear case. Cheapness on a multiple is not edge when the denominator is still falling — 7x EBITDA is an anchor, not a floor. That is precisely why the verdict is reject rather than "buy the dip."

**Reopen conditions (any one suffices to re-run the case):**
1. **Disclosure resolving the mix fork favorably:** Pioneer standalone gross margin shown to be ≥18% *while* legacy owner-direct margin holds ≥27%. This would rehabilitate the "core is intact" arithmetic.
