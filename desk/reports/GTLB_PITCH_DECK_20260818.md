## What we believe

GitLab sells software that helps engineering teams write, review, test and ship code. It charges mostly per developer seat, and it is trying to add a second, usage-based revenue stream (AI agents that consume "credits") on top of those seats.

We believe three things about it, and we hold them with different confidence:

**1. The business is not breaking.** The parts of the fundamental case that sounded alarming turned out, on the primary documents, to be either misread or already known. Revenue guidance for the year was *raised*, not cut ($1,099–1,118M → $1,112–1,118M). Profit guidance was *raised* ($129–137M → $135–141M). The most recent quarter beat its own revenue guide by ~4% and its own profit guide by ~14%. On the strict accounting measure that fully charges the cost of paying employees in stock, the loss has shrunk four years running (−32.3% of revenue → −18.8% → −7.4% → −6.0%), meaning roughly 37 cents of every incremental revenue dollar is dropping through. Balance sheet: $1.36 billion of cash and investments, zero debt, no converts, no preferred.

**2. The thing that would justify the price cannot be verified.** The stock's valuation requires the AI/consumption business to become large. GitLab publishes no revenue figure for it — not in the annual report, the quarterly report, either earnings release, or the investor slides. The only number that exists is an internal, self-described "evolving" metric ("consumption run-rate," CRR) of roughly $15M as of April 30 and "surpassed $20 million" as of June 30, against a ~$1.1 billion revenue guide. GitLab's own words in an SEC filing: CRR "is not material to the Company's financial performance at this time." We cannot underwrite the central claim from primary sources, and unverifiable is not the same as clean.

**3. Three fundamental problems are real and neither bench could break them.** Net dollar retention — how much existing customers grow their spend — has fallen one point per quarter for five straight quarters (122 → 121 → 119 → 118 → 117%) with no floor visible. Contract length is shrinking: total contracted backlog grew 18% while near-term backlog grew 24%, and that gap widened from 4 points to 6 points in one quarter. And GitLab itself disclosed on a slide that "headwinds persist for 20% of ARR."

**The synthesis, and the reason we are waiting:** GitLab's own current metrics arithmetically imply faster growth than management is guiding to. Customer count +7% multiplied by retention of 117% produces roughly +25% run-rate growth; reported growth was +23.1%; the full-year guide is +16.4–17.0%. That is a 6–8 point gap. Either retention falls to ~110–112% by year-end, or the guide is deliberately conservative. **That fork is the whole thesis, it resolves at the next earnings print, and it is not worth paying up ahead of.**

## What the market believes

The market is not asleep on this name and we should not pretend otherwise. At the time of the court, options implied roughly 79–82% annualized volatility — the 81st-to-87th percentile of the past year — and the September straddle marked a ~24% move over six weeks, with about $0.72 of genuine downside skew after adjusting for intrinsic value. Traders are pricing a real, two-sided, event-driven outcome.

The consensus posture reads as: *seat-based developer tools are the category most exposed if AI agents replace developer headcount, so the multiple has come down and stays down until GitLab proves the consumption business is real.* The stock is −30% from its 52-week high and was roughly flat year-to-date at the time of the court.

The market is right that the proof is absent. Where we think the market is imprecise: it is reading the *guide* as the trajectory, when the delivered numbers have beaten and the guide has been raised twice.

## Why we might have edge

- **We decomposed the price chart instead of taking the headline.** The stock was +95.5% off its 52-week low, which reads like a completed recovery. It is not. The $18.73 low was a one-week print during the April-2026 market liquidation. Against normal reference points the stock was +0.7% versus January, −10.8% versus December, −24.9% versus November and −30.1% from the high. Only the crash round-tripped; the underlying re-rating from November onward — which happened *before* the market break — never reversed. Our own first bench got this wrong and the second bench overturned it.
- **The guide-versus-metrics arithmetic above.** It is a clean, falsifiable fork that resolves on a known date, and it is not how most models are framed.
- **The incremental margin.** Focusing on the measure that fully charges stock compensation shows ~37 cents of incremental profit per revenue dollar, which contradicts the popular "they're spending faster than they're saving" reading.
- **We caught an accusation against the company that was false.** Our own screen flagged GitLab for quietly cutting a marketed metric by 25%. The primary filing shows the opposite: GitLab volunteered the cut in a dedicated SEC filing, quoted its own prior figure verbatim, gave both reasons, and had pre-warned that the definition would change. Not scoring that as dishonesty — when the screen said it was the single highest-weighted finding — is itself a form of edge.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: mostly, it is.

- **The stock has moved against us since adjudication.** The court ran at $36.62 on 2026-08-07. The tape as of 2026-08-18 is **$40.52** — up 10.7% with no new disclosure in between. At $36.62 the second bench's own scenario set required roughly a **18–27%** chance of the bull case to break even. At $40.52 the same scenarios require roughly **37–49%**. Reward-to-risk fell from about **1.39:1 to about 0.87:1**, and the price is now **above** that bench's own base-case fair value of ~$35.60. The setup the court adjudicated has materially decayed, which is the strongest possible confirmation that waiting was right.
- **The core unknown is unknowable from filings.** GitLab bundles AI into the seat price ($29/user/month Premium including $12/user/month of credits; Ultimate includes $24), so only overage is separable — and overage *is* the CRR figure. A separate "AI revenue" line may never exist. Anyone waiting for it may wait forever.
- **The valuation still requires a lot.** Reverse-solving the enterprise value over a fading-growth path implies a terminal free-cash-flow margin, after charging stock comp, of roughly 19–23%. Mature application-software companies do reach that (Adobe 33%, Salesforce 26%, ServiceNow 20%). Developer-tools companies mostly have not: Atlassian 1.0% and falling, Datadog 4.8%, Zscaler 2.4%, MongoDB −2.0%, Snowflake −10.2%.
- **At least a quarter of the originally marketed consumption figure was money GitLab gave away.** The restated April number excludes "certain one-time credit incentives granted to paying customers," and separately the company broadened AI access to *free-tier* users. Some of the AI traction is company-funded, not customer demand.
