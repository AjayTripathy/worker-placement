# PITCH — CMPX — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO POSITION, NO ORDERS. Both benches independently reached "do not buy." Re-examine only at the ESMO data (~Oct 24, 2026) or a BLA filing; if no filing happens by Dec 31, 2026, treat the thesis as dead.**

## What we believe

Compass Therapeutics ($2.15) is a Boston clinical-stage biotech whose lead drug, tovecimig, hit its main goal in a randomized trial in second-line biliary tract cancer: response rate 18% (statistically significant vs. paclitaxel alone) and progression-free survival about 2.1 months better. But the headline survival comparison across all enrolled patients showed no benefit (hazard ratio 1.05) — and 54% of the control patients crossed over to receive tovecimig after progressing, which muddies that comparison in both directions. A subset analysis showing 12.8 vs. 6.1 months of survival is statistically biased (patients had to survive long enough to cross over) and the FDA will ignore it. The honest read: the drug's true survival effect is currently **unknown**, and the analysis that would answer it — a crossover-adjusted survival calculation — is expected in the full dataset at the ESMO conference around October 24.

Two things the original thesis claimed are dead. First, "the market carries the pipeline for free": at $2.15 on ~187 million shares, the company is worth ~$402M against ~$180M cash, so the market is already paying **~$222M** for the drug portfolio. Second, "a binary resolves in ~7 weeks": the guided Q3 "FDA feedback" is a private meeting whose minutes are never published — bad news would be silently absorbed into schedule slippage, not announced. The real public decision points are the ESMO data, then whether a BLA gets filed by year-end, then a likely FDA decision date in second-half 2027.

The company says its cash lasts into 2028 (~$180M vs. ~$64–70M/yr spend), but it funds itself by issuing stock — share count grew ~35% in the past year.

## What the market believes

The stock is down 69% from its 52-week high of $6.88 (the collapse followed the April survival miss) and has bounced 34% off the $1.61 low. At ~$222M above cash, the market treats tovecimig as a long shot with real residual value — neither priced for approval nor priced at zero. A new 13G filed August 7 turned out to be State Street at 5.5% — index-fund plumbing, **not** evidence that active buyers are re-rating the stock (our red bench initially misread this; see table). Next earnings ~November 5, unconfirmed. Source: [Q2 2026 results release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/06/3340156/0/en/Compass-Therapeutics-Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Financial-Results-and-Provides-Corporate-Update.html); trial design at [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05506943](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05506943).

## Why we might have edge

- **The crossover-adjusted survival number is the whole game, and it isn't public yet.** Few investors in a thinly covered ~$400M name model the difference between the biased subset analysis and a proper adjusted analysis. If ESMO shows a legitimate adjusted survival benefit, the regulatory case strengthens materially and we'd know exactly why.
- **Silence is readable.** Because FDA meeting feedback is private, a BLA that quietly fails to appear by year-end *is* the disclosure of bad feedback. We've encoded this as a tripwire; most holders will just experience drift.
- **Precedent work cuts both ways and is genuinely open.** All three approved biliary-tract-cancer drugs (pemigatinib, futibatinib, zanidatamab) went through on *accelerated* approval with single-arm response rates of 36–42%. Compass has a lower response rate but a *randomized* package — a stronger evidence type per point. Nobody has cleanly priced which matters more.

## Why it might be priced in

- $222M above cash already pays for a meaningful probability of success; the "cheap option" framing was arithmetic error on our side, now corrected.
- The null overall-survival result is fully public since April; everyone can see the ESMO calendar too.
- The blue bench rejected red's claim that the stock is "fairly priced" as *unproven in either direction* — no valuation work supports it. We genuinely do not know whether $222M is cheap or expensive for this package, which is itself a reason not to own it yet.
- Downside asymmetry of information: because bad FDA feedback stays private, holders learn bad news last, via drift and slippage.
