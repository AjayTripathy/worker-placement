# PITCH — 6824.T — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — buy a starter position of 0.30% of the book in New Cosmos Electric (TSE 6824) at ~¥4,235, worked in over four sessions, but only after we confirm we can actually trade Tokyo at our broker; the original reasons for owning it were both destroyed in court, and what survives is a different, weaker, still-positive case.

## What we believe

New Cosmos Electric makes gas alarms — the small devices that detect a gas leak in a home or a factory. It is a 92-year-old Osaka manufacturer, listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, worth about ¥51.5 billion (~$325 million at ¥158.86/$).

Three things are true about it at the same time, and we think the market is pricing the first two and ignoring the third:

**One — it is a cheap business hiding behind an expensive-looking balance sheet.** At ¥4,235 the whole company costs ¥51.5bn. But ¥20.4bn of that is cash sitting in the bank, against only ¥3.8bn of debt. Strip the net cash out and you are paying about ¥39.0bn for the actual operating business — roughly 6.5 times the operating profit the company has guided to for this year, and 5.3 times what it earned last year. About a third of the market value is cash. On the money actually at work in the business, the company earns about 11% a year, not the 7.6% you get if you naively divide profit by total book value.

**Two — this year's guided profit is a step down, and the market has punished it.** The company guided to ¥6.0bn of operating profit for the year ending March 2027, down from ¥7.35bn last year. The stock is 30.7% below its 52-week high of ¥6,110. That much is understood by everyone.

**Three — the step-down guide looks reachable, and one dated regulatory event sits inside it that management has said out loud it has not yet put in the numbers.** New York City's Department of Buildings confirmed on 15 June 2026 that every dwelling unit in the city must have a natural gas alarm by **1 January 2027** — that is primary, we read the notice ourselves ([nyc.gov DOB service notice](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/buildings/pdf/natural_gasalarm-sn.pdf)). That is a legally fixed, non-negotiable purchase by roughly a million-plus New York dwelling units, landing in the exact half-year the guide depends on. New Cosmos sells the DeNova Detect alarm into that market.

So: pay ~6.5× cash-adjusted operating profit for a debt-free manufacturer whose earnings floor is being set by law, with an 11% return on the capital actually employed.

**What we do *not* believe, and want stated up front:** we no longer believe this is "trading below book value" (it is at 0.98× — essentially exactly at book), and we no longer believe the North American profit collapse was a temporary mix problem that snaps back. Both were central to the original write-up. Both were killed. See the self-check table.

## What the market believes

The market believes this was a boom-and-giveback story and is now pricing the giveback.

New Cosmos had a very good year: revenue up 18.8%, net income up 54.3%, operating profit ¥5.16bn → ¥7.35bn. The stock is up 44% over the past twelve months and sits 42–48% above its 52-week low of ¥2,852. It is not a washed-out, forgotten name — it is a stock that already re-rated once and is handing part of it back.

Then the first quarter of this year (April–June 2026, reported August) came in badly. Sales fell 1.6%. Gross margin — the profit left after the cost of making the product — dropped from 48.5% to 43.4%, a 5.1 percentage-point fall. Operating profit fell from ¥1.60bn to ¥0.78bn, roughly in half. North American sales fell 28.6%. Management attributed the margin damage to inflation in materials and wages, in plain Japanese: 「昨今の物価上昇と人件費アップにより製造原価および販売管理費が増加」 — *recent price rises and wage increases pushed up manufacturing costs and SG&A*.

So the market's view is coherent and reasonable: a cyclical alarm maker had a peak year, its biggest growth market just fell 29%, its costs are permanently higher, and it is worth about one times its book value on a mid-single-digit return. Nothing about that story is stupid.

The market also, importantly, believes the New York deadline is not worth much to *this* company — because the same DOB notice that sets the deadline also certifies "at least 4 distinct manufacturers" of approved battery-powered alarms. A mandate you share with three or more competitors, sold into landlord procurement departments that buy on price, is not obviously a windfall.

## Why we might have edge

Four specific places where we think we know something the price does not reflect, ranked by how much we trust them.

**1. The guide is reachable without any margin recovery — and we can show the arithmetic.** This is the strongest thing we have, because it is pure arithmetic off the audited quarterly filing ([TDnet tanshin, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.release.tdnet.info/inbs/140120260806512250.pdf)).

The lazy read is: Q1 operating profit was ¥776m, times four is ¥3.1bn, the guide says ¥6.0bn, so the guide needs *double* the current run rate — impossible. Our own red-team bench made exactly this argument and it is wrong, because Q1 is New Cosmos's seasonally weakest quarter (last year Q1 was only 21.7% of full-year operating profit).

Do it properly. The remaining nine months need ¥40.19bn of sales (¥51.0bn guide minus ¥10.81bn already booked) — that is only **+2.8% growth** versus the same nine months last year. Now hold this quarter's *depressed* 43.4% gross margin constant — assume zero margin recovery — and hold operating expenses flat in yen at ¥3.91bn per quarter. You get ¥17.44bn of gross profit minus ¥11.74bn of costs = **¥5.70bn of operating profit against the ¥5.23bn the guide requires.** It clears by ¥474m with nothing going right.

The jump from a 7.2% margin to a 13.0% margin is not a margin snapback. It is a fixed-cost factory running ¥13.4bn of quarterly volume instead of ¥10.8bn.

**2. The balance sheet is being read wrong in both directions, and the second correction is bigger than the first.** The original write-up said "trading below book at 0.91×." That was an error — it divided by total net assets including ¥4.1bn belonging to minority shareholders of subsidiaries. True price-to-book is 0.978. Fine, that leg is dead.

But the correction people then reach for — "so it's at book on a 7.6% return, that's a fair price for a mediocre business" — is a bigger error. Because a third of the market cap is cash. On the equity actually deployed in the business, the return is 11.0%, and the enterprise (what you pay for the business itself) is 6.5× the *guided down-year* operating profit. Both benches in our court initially missed this; only one of them caught it, and only on re-derivation.

**3. Currency is running 6% in the company's favour versus its own assumption.** The guide assumes ¥150 per dollar. Spot on 21 August 2026 was **158.86** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/japan/currency)). With 45.6% of sales overseas, that is a meaningful translation tailwind to the yen numbers that the ¥51.0bn guide does not contain. (Caveat: this mostly does *not* accrue to us — see the self-check table.)

**4. Management has said the New York deadline is not yet in the guide.** In the Q1 briefing, management described the January 2027 deadline as creating demand "potentially outside initial planning" for the second half, and said developments were being monitored before adjusting guidance. If true, the guide has an option attached to it that the guide does not price.

We hold this one loosely and you should too. We reached that language through a machine translation of a Japanese briefing transcript, and the *same* fetch mis-scaled ¥51.0bn as "¥510bn" in the same response. We are relying on the structure of the statement, not the quote.

## Why it might be priced in

**The strongest argument against us, stated at full strength:** the best-informed party in the world on this question looked at the New York deadline and did not change a single number.

The company set its ¥6.0bn guide on **15 May 2026**. The DOB determination landed **15 June 2026**. The company then published its quarterly results on **6–10 August 2026** — roughly eight weeks later, with management naming the June determination explicitly in prepared remarks — and republished the **identical** operating profit guide and the **identical** dividend. Management did not think a legally mandated purchase by a million-plus New York dwelling units, landing in the exact half-year its guide leans on, was worth one yen of upward revision.

Our whole "edge" claim was that the tape has not repriced 15 June. But the tape doesn't reprice things — informed parties do. And the single best-informed party, publishing *after* the event, didn't either.

There are two honest rebuttals, and neither fully closes it. First, Tokyo Stock Exchange rules only *force* a guidance revision at a 30%-or-greater deviation in operating profit — more than ¥1.8bn here — so Japanese small caps routinely sit on good news for a quarter or two. Silence one quarter in is structurally weak evidence. Second, 15 June didn't *create* the mandate; it declined to push it back to 2029 under the fallback in Local Law 102/2025. It removed a downside risk. A guide that already assumed the deadline would hold looks exactly like a guide left unchanged.

**Second-strongest:** we cannot quantify New Cosmos's share of the New York channel. The DOB notice itself certifies at least four approved manufacturers. A mandate split four-plus ways into price-competitive landlord procurement can produce a revenue bump that is real and completely immaterial. The claim circulating that DeNova has shipped "250,000 units since 2019" is **unverified — we could not locate a primary source for it and are not relying on it.**

**Third:** cost inflation is real and is roughly 60% of the profit damage, not a mix artifact. We tested this properly. To explain the ¥641m gross-profit decline through mix alone, the ¥866m of Japanese domestic sales that backfilled the North American shortfall would have to carry roughly zero gross margin — arithmetically impossible. At a realistic 40% domestic margin, mix explains about ¥259m of ¥641m. The rest is input costs and wages, and those do not un-happen when volume returns.

**Fourth:** the stock is up 44% in a year and 42% off its low. The "trough earnings" we are buying are trough only relative to an inflated peak.

**Fifth, and unaddressed by anyone in the court:** after 1 January 2027, New York's mandate is satisfied. A million-plus alarms installed in 2026 come off a 5-to-10-year replacement clock together. There is a demand cliff on the other side of this catalyst, in fiscal 2028, and neither our bull case nor our bear case put a number on it.

## What could go wrong

Three ways we lose money, most likely first. One of them has no tripwire at all, and we name it.

**Failure narrative 1 — The mandate arrives and we get a sliver of it (highest probability).** New York's January deadline converts into real alarm purchases, exactly as we expect. Four-plus certified manufacturers bid into building-management procurement. First Alert and the incumbents, with existing distribution into US property channels, take the volume; DeNova takes 10–20% at compressed pricing. North American revenue recovers to flat instead of surging, the ¥6.0bn guide is met but not beaten, and the stock does nothing for four quarters while we hold a position we cannot re-underwrite. We lose nothing dramatic; we lose the year.
*Tripwire: Q2 North American sales below ¥4.0bn (reported ~early November). Also: DOB approved-device counts by manufacturer, and any Con Edison or NYCHA award announcement.* Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — Operating costs are more variable than we assumed and the guide misses (second-highest, and it directly attacks our best argument).** Our central claim is that the nine-month guide clears by ¥474m holding operating expenses flat in yen at ¥3.91bn per quarter. That is a big assumption: it requires SG&A to stay flat while revenue rises 24% quarter-on-quarter. Run the break-even: the guide fails the moment quarterly SG&A rises more than **4.0%** above the Q1 level. Given SG&A already grew 4.8% year-on-year on *falling* sales, and given selling costs, freight and commissions scale with volume, our ¥474m cushion is about one percentage point of cost-elasticity wide. If SG&A is only 84% fixed rather than ~100% fixed, our arithmetic edge evaporates and the company misses its own down-year guide — which, after a 50% profit decline in Q1, would be read as confirmation that the whole franchise has re-based lower.
*Tripwire: SG&A as a percentage of sales in the Q2 report, and the gross margin bridge in the supplementary materials.* Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 3 — the one with NO tripwire. The Japanese domestic base was itself a pull-forward, and it reverses.** In Q1, Japanese domestic sales grew **+16.2%** and quietly did the work of holding total revenue near flat while North America fell 28.6%. Nobody in this court — neither bench, not us — established *why* domestic grew 16% in a mature home-alarm market in a shrinking-household country. We assumed it was a healthy base. It could equally be a Japanese replacement cycle, a utility procurement batch, a distributor stocking ahead of a price increase, or a domestic regulatory refresh cresting right now.

**There is no monitorable tripwire for this.** New Cosmos reports as a **single segment** — 「ガス警報器事業の単一セグメント」 — so the audited filing contains *no* regional breakdown, no channel breakdown, and no product-line breakdown. The regional numbers we are quoting exist only in a voluntary investor briefing that management can stop publishing at will. There is no filing, no regulatory series, no third-party dataset, and no external anchor that would reveal a domestic pull-forward before it reverses. The first evidence we will ever get is the revenue decline itself, in a print, after the fact.

**Unknown-unknown class: single-segment reporting opacity — undisclosed demand provenance.** We are underwriting a revenue base whose drivers the issuer has never been required to disclose and has chosen not to. Any thesis built on a company reporting one segment carries this class, and we should tag every such name in the book the same way. The corollary is that the inventory build we noticed (inventories +¥685m versus +¥22m last year, operating cash flow down 60% from ¥2.32bn to ¥0.93bn) is genuinely two-sided: it is either pre-building for a legally dated compliance window, or it is unsold product backing up in a channel where demand already peaked. We cannot tell, and neither could either bench.

**The reflexive / structural leg — how our own machinery hurts us here.**

*Our minimum trade exceeds our own liquidity rule.* Average daily volume is roughly 8,500 shares — about ¥36m, or $225–245k. Tokyo trades in 100-share lots, so the smallest position we can take is 100 shares = ¥423,500 ≈ **$2,666**, which is **1.2% of a day's volume**. Our own publishing discipline caps ladder participation at 1% of ADV. **We cannot open this position at all without breaking our own rule** — even at the minimum size. Neither bench computed this; both wrote sizing plans in percentages without checking whether those percentages correspond to a tradeable number of shares. On a $1.0M book, one lot is already 0.27% and the "work it in over four sessions" instruction is arithmetically impossible — the whole position is one click.

*Our doctrine manufactured this position.* Under our clean-court minimum-starter rule, going flat requires a *named* surviving kill. The red bench named several; the blue bench overturned the two load-bearing ones; therefore no surviving kill; therefore a starter is mandatory. But look at what happened underneath: the thesis that entered court ("cheap below book, plus an unrepriced regulatory catalyst") is *not* the thesis leaving court ("6.5× enterprise value to operating profit, net-cash manufacturer, guide clears on arithmetic"). Both original legs were destroyed. The position survives because a new rationale was constructed mid-proceeding by the defending bench. That is thesis drift wearing survival's clothes, and our doctrine has no detector for it. If we own this, we own it knowing the rule, not the reasoning, is what put it in the book.

*We are double-counting yen weakness.* We credited a 6% currency tailwind to the guide because the dollar is at 158.86 against an assumed 150. But we are a dollar-denominated book. The same yen weakness that inflates New Cosmos's reported yen revenue *deflates* our translated dollar return roughly one-for-one. For us the currency point is close to a wash, and we presented it as a clean positive. That is our error, not the company's.

*Flow:* at this size we predict no flow of our own and there is nothing to front-run. The structural risk runs the other way — an ADV this thin means an *exit* on bad news is 2+ sessions minimum, and in a gap-down it is worse.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the January 2027 New York mandate arrives exactly as we predicted and New Cosmos captures too small a share of it to matter, because we never verified — and had no way to verify — that this company is the one the mandate pays.
