# PITCH — CUV.AX — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED  — NO ENTRY at A$10.60; watch-listed with a hard reopen band at A$8.60 and below, because the blue bench's own RP_TAINTED cap (≤0.85×E[fv] ≈ A$7.80–8.60) prices below the tape, and the red bench's REJECT survives on cost-ramp and disclosure grounds even after three of its four "fatal" findings were shown to be bench errors.**

---

## What we believe

Clinuvel sells SCENESSE, an implant that lets people with erythropoietic protoporphyria — a rare inherited condition where sunlight causes severe pain — tolerate light exposure. It is the only approved treatment, it is profitable, and it has been profitable for nine straight years. The company holds A$233.0M of cash and term deposits against 50,123,630 shares and no debt, so roughly A$4.65 of the A$10.60 share price is cash. You are paying about A$5.95 per share for the actual business.

We believe three things:

1. **The business is cheaper than it looks, but not as cheap as the pitch claimed.** On FY25 actual results (revenue A$105.3M, net profit A$36.2M), stripping out the interest the cash itself earns leaves roughly A$29.4M of operating profit — about 10× the cash-adjusted price. On a declining FY26 that becomes 15–19×. So the "10× and profitable" headline is defensible on last year's numbers and stretched on this year's.
2. **The monopoly has a dated expiry, not a hypothetical one.** A rival oral drug, dersimelagon, hit its Phase 3 primary endpoint (announced 20 January 2026), filed for US approval in June 2026, and on 18 August 2026 was licensed worldwide to LEO Pharma for $435M. That is a dedicated global dermatology sales organisation buying the channel. Approval is plausible around Q2 2027. This is the whole thesis.
3. **The company's own US securities filing says it has no competitors.** Clinuvel's SEC registration statement states SCENESSE "is the only approved treatment… therefore, we currently have no direct commercial competitors," and does not name dersimelagon, MT-7117, bitopertin, Tanabe or Disc Medicine anywhere ([SEC registration statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001290877/000149315225028243/filename1.htm)). Amendments were filed *after* the rival's successful Phase 3 and that language stayed. We do not think this is fraud. We think it is a management team that does not want to describe the thing that is about to happen to it — and by our doctrine that permanently caps what we will pay.

Net: a real, profitable, debt-free franchise, honestly cheap on last year's earnings, run by people who will not say the competitor's name, in the last four quarters before the competition arrives. Ownable — at a price that is roughly 20% below where it trades today.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a slow, dated story, not a surprise. The stock is A$10.60, down 24% from its A$14.00 high but up 27% from its A$8.35 low — mid-band, not dislocated. When the rival's positive Phase 3 was announced on 20 January 2026, the stock fell from 11.73 to 11.06 (−5.7%) in a day and was back at 11.86 within a week. That is a market that read the news, priced a 2027 event, and moved on.

Street expectations sit near A$21M net profit for FY26 — we have not been able to cite a source for that number and are carrying it as unverified. The market appears to value the vitiligo programme (CUV105) at something above zero; we value it at zero.

The market also has not obviously punished the disclosure gap, because almost nobody reads a newly-registered foreign issuer's US filing when the primary listing and the analyst coverage are Australian.

## Why we might have edge

- **A documented disclosure asymmetry with an exact control group.** The other company developing an EPP drug — Disc Medicine — names its competitors in four consecutive annual reports. Clinuvel names none, in a document amended after the competitor's Phase 3 succeeded. Our framework treats the *exclusion of candidates who won't describe their own risk* as the durable edge, not the prediction itself. This is that signal, cleanly instrumented.
- **A channel-capture mechanism most people are not modelling.** EPP is a small population treated through a handful of accredited centres. Competitor trials and their open-label extensions have engaged several hundred of exactly those patients (roughly 440 after correcting for double-counting, see below), and both oral programmes enrol from age 12 while US SCENESSE is adults-only. Patients leave at the enrolment desk, not on the approval date. Revenue erosion can begin before any regulator acts.
- **An arithmetic discipline the sell-side framing hides.** Interest income on the cash pile is ~36% of half-year net profit. Any "X times ex-cash earnings" figure that subtracts the cash but keeps its interest income in the earnings line is double-counting. Applied correctly the multiple is still fine; applied lazily it flatters the name by roughly a third. We now have this codified as a reusable detector.
- **A cross-check anchor.** LEO paid $435M for worldwide rights to an unapproved pill. Clinuvel's whole business, net of cash, is US$193M. Read one way that says the incumbent is cheap. Read the other way it says a serious commercial organisation just paid up to take this market. Both readings are legitimate; we are not going to pretend only the bullish one is.

## Why it might be priced in

Substantially, it probably is.

- The decisive news is public and dated: positive Phase 3 (January), AAD presentation (March), NDA filed (June), LEO deal (August). None of this is ours alone.
- The market's reaction to the January readout was 6% for one week. The stock at A$10.60 is only ~5% below its pre-readout level. This is not an unpriced binary; it is a known 2027 approval with a known incumbent.
- The disclosure flag is only "hidden" to someone reading the US filing. Australian coverage of a nine-year-profitable ASX name is not thin. Our informational advantage here is narrow and mostly about *quality of management candour*, not about facts nobody has.
- The cheapness leg is ordinary. 10× cash-adjusted earnings for a decelerating single-product orphan drug facing a funded competitor is not obviously a mispricing. It may simply be the right price.

The honest summary: our edge is a **character judgement plus a mechanism**, not an information monopoly. That justifies a small, capped, price-disciplined position — not a conviction position, and not one at today's price.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The erosion is faster and quieter than any filing will tell us. (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)**
Prescribers at accredited centres start defaulting to the oral option for new and adolescent patients from the day it is available — possibly before, via expanded access. Nothing about this appears in a registry, a filing, or the tape. Clinuvel reports revenue twice a year with no patient-count or new-start disclosure; by the time a half-year number shows it, two or three quarters of drift have already happened and the multiple has already re-rated. We cannot build a sensor for this because the underlying variable is never published.
*Unknown-unknown class: unobservable-until-aggregated demand-side drift* — a state change in physician and patient preference that leaves no trace in filings, registries, or price until it surfaces, late, inside a lagging aggregate. Related classes that would sit in the same bucket here and are equally unmonitorable: a payer or reimbursement-channel repricing at the accredited centres, and an EU/Australian label change for the competitor that never touches an SEC form.

**Failure narrative 2 — We buy the reopen band and the band was the erosion.**
Our entry only triggers 20% below the current price. The realistic paths to A$8.60 are: a bad 27 August print, a bad H1 FY27 print, or the FDA approving dersimelagon. In every one of those, the price fell *because the thesis got worse*. We would be buying a cheaper multiple on an earnings stream that is being confirmed as impaired. The cash floor of A$4.65/share limits how wrong this can go, but the operating business could compress from A$5.95/share of value toward A$3/share and the "cheapness" would keep looking better the whole way down. This is the classic value trap shape and we should say so before we own it, not after. *Tripwire exists:* H2 FY26 sales growth, and the first half in which sales growth goes negative.

**Failure narrative 3 — We are wrong about the competition and it costs us the upside, not the downside.**
The mirror risk. If dersimelagon's approval slips, or its label excludes pediatrics, or LEO's launch is slow, or Clinuvel's implant retains patients on efficacy and convenience grounds, then the name re-rates from 10× toward 16–18× cash-adjusted earnings and never revisits A$8.60. We will have been precisely, expensively right about the disclosure flag and wrong about what it predicted. Our RP_TAINTED cap is a permanent handicap that guarantees this specific failure mode: we systematically cannot buy names whose management is evasive, even when the evasion turns out to be about something that doesn't matter. *Tripwire exists:* FDA action date, label text, LEO launch disclosures.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**
- **Our size is not the problem; our bid placement is.** A 0.5% starter on a book this size is tens of thousands of dollars against an ASX line with a market cap over A$500M. We will not move this stock. Average daily volume is **UNVERIFIED** — we have not pulled it — and that is a gap we should close before any order, but no plausible ADV makes our fill impactful.
- **We are a structural non-buyer into strength and a forced buyer into weakness.** The RP_TAINTED cap means we mechanically only get filled when the price falls. That is adverse selection by construction: the only sellers who will hit our bid are ones reacting to news we said would hurt us. Our value-ladder discipline exists exactly to stop us bidding into flow we ourselves predicted — and here, we are.
- **Print proximity is a live structural risk.** The FY26 result lands 27 August, three days out. The 27 February 2025 half-year print moved this stock 12.53 → 10.65, −15.0%, on seven times normal volume. A resting GTC bid across that print is an accidental short volatility position with no premium collected. No resting orders through 8/27, full stop.
- **Doctrine interactions.** Our clean-court default is to take a minimum starter rather than stay flat, and staying flat requires a *named* kill. Named here: (a) the RP_TAINTED price cap is unmet by A$2.00/share, and (b) the disclosure omission is unresolved. Our dogfood pay-up allowance explicitly does not apply, because fairness here is contested, not verified. Our tax-adjust rule notes that any position taken in the reopen band and trimmed inside twelve months is taxed at short-term rates, which materially weakens the case for a trade-around and argues for either owning it through the 2027 FDA date or not owning it.
- **Instrument risk.** Entry is ASX-only. There is a new Nasdaq line (a June 2026 registration of existing shares, no capital raised, no lockup — checked and clean), but it is thin and newly-discovered. Trading the US line would give us a worse fill on the same risk, and the AUD/USD exposure is unhedged either way.

Filled in, honestly: *we bought a cheap-looking multiple on a monopoly that was quietly ending, and the cheapness was the market correctly discounting revenue we could not watch erode.*
