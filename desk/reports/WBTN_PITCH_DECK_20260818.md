# PITCH — WBTN — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-17 — WATCH**

*WEBTOON Entertainment Inc. (NASDAQ: WBTN), CIK 1997859. Last price $8.22 (pack tape; both benches worked off the $8.285 Friday close). We hold nothing and have no order working. This draft creates the first ledger row for this name.*

---

## What we believe

WEBTOON is a digital comics platform — readers pay per episode to unlock chapters early, and the company owns or licenses the underlying stories, some of which (notably *Solo Leveling*) become anime, games and merchandise.

Three things about it are true and checkable:

1. **It is genuinely cheap on the numbers, and cheaper than our own opening brief claimed.** 135,711,070 shares outstanding as of 2026-07-31 ([SEC XBRL cover-page series](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001997859/dei/EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding.json)) at ~$8.28 is a $1.12B company. It holds $583.1M of cash plus $11.2M of short-term deposits, has **zero borrowings**, and carries only $22.6M of operating leases ([10-Q balance sheet, 2026-08-10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1997859/000199785926000089/R2.htm)). Strip the cash out and the operating business is valued at about $541M against roughly $1.35B of annual revenue — **0.40x sales**. Half the market value is cash in the bank.
2. **The business is currently not making money, and the company has told us the next quarter will be about the same.** Second-quarter adjusted profit (EBITDA) was $5.5M against $9.7M a year earlier; the company guided the third quarter to **$0.0–5.0M** — a guide whose floor is zero. Operating cash flow was **negative $6.3M** versus positive $5.7M a year ago.
3. **The damage is concentrated in Japan, and it is the bad kind of damage.** Japan is 49% of revenue and the company's highest-spending market. Paying users there fell **9.5%**, and the share of readers who pay fell to **9.4%** against a street expectation of 10.4%. Critically, average spend *per* paying user was **up 2.9%** in constant currency. So this is not "we raised prices and lost people" — it is people leaving at unchanged prices. That is real demand loss, not a pricing mistake we could expect the company to reverse.

Our position: this is a cheap, debt-free platform with a real operating problem in its largest market and a governance problem sitting on top of the cash. We are not buyers today. We want it on the sheet with instruments armed, because the reopen case is asymmetric and costs us nothing to wait for.

## What the market believes

The stock is down **63%** from its 52-week high ($22.47) and sits **4.7%** above its 52-week low ($7.85). The market is pricing WEBTOON as a business whose largest market is structurally shrinking and whose cash will be spent rather than returned.

Two market-structure facts cut against the pure-despair read:
- **Disney owns about 2%** (2,666,757 shares, per the [2026 proxy statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1997859/000199785926000055/)). A strategic holder, not a fund flow.
- **The tradeable float is tiny and heavily shorted.** NAVER holds 59.67%, LY Corp sits in a 20%+ voting tier, Disney ~2% — roughly 85% of shares are locked up, leaving a float of about 15–18M shares. Reported short interest of 2.75M shares is only ~2% of shares outstanding but **~15–18% of the actual float**, at about 4.4 days to cover. Any genuinely good news has fuel behind it.

Note what we could *not* establish: nobody has extracted what the street actually models for Q3 revenue or profit. We know a street number exists for this company — the 10.4% Japan paying-ratio consensus proves it — but we never pulled the revenue and profit consensus. **So we cannot presently say whether we disagree with the market or only with our own opening brief.** That is a hole in this deck, and it is a gate before any bid.

## Why we might have edge

- **The valuation is real, not an accounting illusion.** We verified share count, cash, and the absence of debt directly from the filed balance sheet and the SEC's own XBRL feed, from two independent fetch paths that agreed on every load-bearing figure. A debt-free business at 0.40x sales with half its value in cash has a genuine margin-of-safety shape *if* the cash belongs to shareholders.
- **We correctly diagnosed the mechanism of the Japan break where the surface numbers invite the wrong read.** Falling paying users with rising spend per user is attrition, not a price-lever failure. That distinction also weakens the "Korea will become Japan next" fear: Korea's growth is price-led (spend per user +14.8% versus paying users +10.4%) in a market whose users are still paying — a different regime, not an earlier stage of the same one.
- **We found the thing the price does not obviously reflect.** Within days of the print, and with the stock 5.5% off its 52-week low, the company agreed to buy up to 60% of RI Games Holdings for **KRW 150bn (~$100M)** from Kevin Tail Han — who founded RI Games *and* Redice Studio, the studio behind *Solo Leveling*, the flagship property this entire thesis rests on. Plus up to KRW 50bn of further commitments. No buyback anywhere. That is the controller telling us, in public, what the cash is for.
- **Size is not a barrier.** At roughly 628,000 shares a day, 1% of daily volume is about $52,000. A 1% position in our ~$1.03M book is about $10,300 — a fraction of a single day's participation. Nothing here is un-ownable on liquidity.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The cash floor is not our floor.** NAVER controls 59.67% of the votes. Minority holders cannot direct that $583M, cannot force a buyback, and just watched ~$100M of it go to an insider-connected seller at the equity low. A net-cash floor that minorities do not control is optionality for the controller, not protection for us. Under our own response framework this is a governance and business-risk finding: **it caps how much we can own; it does not by itself refute a 0.40x-sales platform.**
- **The cheapness may be a measurement artifact.** At a 1.6% profit margin and a guided-zero next quarter, "0.40x sales" and "27–54x profit" describe the same company. Neither multiple settles the question. The honest statement is that margin is unresolved in both directions.
- **We have run this play before and lost seven times out of seven.** Our post-earnings "it's cheap now" template is 0-for-7. The headline *is* the news; the market is not slow to a printed miss.
- **Nothing forces resolution for about 60 trading days.** The next report is roughly 2026-11-16, and that date is derived from a data vendor — no company announcement confirms it. We would be paying for the option to wait, into a quarter management has already told us could earn nothing, against a controller whose information we do not have.
