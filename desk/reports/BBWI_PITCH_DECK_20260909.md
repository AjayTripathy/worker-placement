## What we believe

Bath & Body Works is a cheap, shrinking, levered specialty retailer — and it is meaningfully less cheap than the first pass through this name claimed.

1. **The core business is contracting, not resting.** FY26 Q2 (ended 2026-08-01) net sales were **$1,514M, −2.3% y/y**, against $1,549M a year prior. The four-year Q2 track is 1,559 → 1,526 → 1,549 → 1,514. The company guides FY26 net sales **−4% to −2.5%** and Q3 **−5% to −2.5%** against a $1,594M base. Source: 8-K Ex-99.1, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000701985/000070198526000025/ex991bbwi-20262qearningsre.htm
2. **A quarter of the headline cash is not repeatable.** Roughly **$168M of the ~$650M FY26 free-cash-flow guide is one-time**: ~$80M of tariff refunds sitting inside *reported and adjusted* Q2 (adjusted EPS $0.62 → **$0.31** ex-refund), plus an **$88M pre-tax interchange-settlement receipt booked as a reduction of operating expenses in Q1** — cash, in G&A, therefore inside operating cash flow and inside the guide (Q1 8-K Ex-99.1: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000701985/000070198526000012/ex991bbwi-20261qearningsre.htm).
3. **So the honest valuation is:** recurring FCF ≈ 650 − 88 − 80 ≈ **$482M**, which is **13.3%** of the $3.62B equity cap (203.4M diluted × $17.80) and **~7.5%** on ~$6.4B enterprise value. Underlying adjusted EPS ≈ $2.70 − $0.31 = **$2.39**, i.e. **7.4x** — not 6.6x. Dividend cover is **2.9x**, not ~4x.
4. **Earnings are going backwards.** FY26 adjusted EPS of $2.60–2.80 is **−16% y/y** versus $3.21 — **−26%** if you strip the refund.
5. **The balance sheet is genuinely improving.** Debt principal fell to **$3,632M** at 2026-08-01 from $3,916M at 1/31/26, and a further **$250M of the 7.5% 2029 notes was redeemed 2026-08-19**, taking principal to roughly **$3.38B** — about **$534M retired this fiscal year**. The ABL leverage covenant was **not tested** ("As of August 1, 2026, we were not required to maintain this ratio," 10-Q: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000701985/000070198526000029/bbwi-20260801.htm).
6. **The mix is deteriorating in the expensive channel.** US/Canada stores **−5.4%**; the growth is in lower-margin wholesale (Ulta/Amazon) and direct **+3%**, plus international/other **+24.9%**. The fixed-lease base sits under the shrinking leg.
7. **The buyback — the whole basis of the old "cash cow" story — is off.** Management states no share repurchases are assumed in the outlook. The ~8%/yr share-count shrink that made the return math work is paused prospectively.

Net: this is worth owning at a price, and $17.80 is not obviously that price. It is a **WATCH with a measurable gate**, replacing the brief's gate, which could not be measured at all.

## What the market believes

The market and we do not disagree about earnings — we disagree about the multiple, and about which earnings are real.

- Sell-side price targets cluster **$20–22** (JPM $21, MS $20, DB $22), i.e. **5–7x forward** — the street is not modeling a recovery, it is modeling a cheap decliner.
- The street's Q3 adjusted EPS sat near **$0.26–0.27** against a company guide of **$0.07–0.12** — a >55% gap. That gap was *pre-announced on 2026-08-26*, so it is tape-known, not a hidden landmine. Management's disclosed drivers: ~$30M of H2 tariff/input-cost pressure, ~$35M of incremental "Consumer First" spend, and the non-repeat of Q2's refund.
- Screens are looking at the wrong line. FY26 **GAAP** EPS is guided **$3.13–3.33**, *above* adjusted $2.60–2.80, because of the one-time recovery. A GAAP screen prints **5.5x** and flags "deep value." That is a settlement being capitalized as run-rate.

## Why we might have edge

- **Basis, not forecast.** Our edge, if any, is that we separated recurring from non-recurring cash before assigning a multiple. A screen sees 5.5x GAAP; the consensus write-up sees 6.6x adjusted and an 18% FCF yield; the filing-true number is **7.4x and 13.3%**. That is a ~35% error in the headline yield, and it is available to anyone who reads the Q1 and Q2 releases side by side — which almost nobody does, because the refund is disclosed in one quarter and the settlement in another.
- **Two reusable patterns fell out of this case** and are now in the desk's library: (a) *one-off cash inside an FCF guide* — an issuer raises the guide in the same release that discloses non-recurring receipts; (b) *GAAP-above-adjusted inversion* — whenever guided GAAP EPS exceeds guided adjusted EPS by more than ~5%, a one-time gain is hiding there. Both fired here and both are mechanical to check next time.
- **A third pattern is about our own process:** *pre-resolved conjunctive tripwire* — a watch-list gate whose legs are already answered by public filings and guidance at the moment it is written. The original brief's gate had a debt leg already cleared, an FCF leg already guided through, and a comps leg pointing at a metric the company no longer reports. That gate could never open. We now screen our own gates for this before arming them.
- **Deleveraging is real and under-narrated.** ~$534M of principal retired in a year on a $3.62B market cap is a live transfer of value from debt to equity, and the covenant is not even being tested.

## What could go wrong        (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

This is written about the position we *would* take if a gate opens (stage 1/3 sleeve at ≤$15.00 or on a clean Q3), not about the flat book.

**Failure narrative 1 — The value trap that stays a trap (monitorable).** We buy at $15.00 on a 15.8% recurring yield. Both are filing-computable.

**Failure narrative 2 — The cash-yield story breaks from the financing side (partly monitorable).** The ABL facility's stated August 2026 expiry was checked by neither bench, and the **8-K of 2026-07-20 was never opened** — that is our largest single documentary hole. If the ABL was renewed on materially worse terms, or if the 2035–2037 notes get taken out at a coupon above the retiring 7.5% paper, interest expense eats the FCF yield and the dividend cover goes from 2.9x toward 2x. Note red's original version of this tripwire ("an 8-K refinancing at >8%") is **misspecified** — BBWI is *retiring* debt, not refinancing it, so the trigger has to be written on any new issuance or facility amendment, not on a refi that may never come. *Tripwire exists but is incomplete:* an 8-K arrives only after the fact, and the ABL terms may never generate an 8-K at all.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: the demand side breaks off-filing.** Every consumer-side sensor we own came back **UNCHECKABLE on both benches** — brand heat scores, review velocity and cohort-rating deltas, app/loyalty velocity, social-velocity polls. We have **zero brand instrumentation armed** on a discretionary fragrance retailer, and the company has **stopped disclosing a comparable-sales metric**, so even the filings no longer contain the measurand. A fragrance-category preference shift, an ingredient-safety or regulatory scare, a mall-traffic step-change, or a competitor's viral launch could move the business a full quarter before any document we read reports it — and the first report would be the print itself, at which point the price has already moved. **Unknown-unknown class: off-filing demand-side regime break in a discretionary consumer brand — an unmonitored preference shock with a discontinued measurand.** We cannot tripwire this today; we can only size for it, which is why the sleeve is 1/3 and why the second and third thirds are gated on prints rather than on price.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our size vs the tape:* not the binding constraint. A $3.6B NYSE mid-cap with roughly 1x-ADV short interest absorbs a stage-1 sleeve without footprint — our estimated participation is low single-digit percent of a day's volume. - *Predicted flow — the important one:* the buyback is guided to **zero**. Historically that program was a large, price-insensitive, everyday bid under this stock. It is gone. Whatever support the tape had from the issuer's own hand is withdrawn precisely while the fundamentals soften. Any downside move now happens without that cushion.
- *Reflexive co-investor risk:* because guided **GAAP EPS ($3.13–3.33) exceeds adjusted**, mechanical value screens see 5.5x and may accumulate for a reason that evaporates in FY27 when the $88M does not repeat. We would be riding alongside holders whose thesis is an artifact. When FY27 GAAP normalizes toward the adjusted line, those holders unwind into the same door we would use.
- *Doctrine interactions:* red applied "consensus-only kills on a name at or below fair value → minimum starter." Blue overturned the fair-value input, which pulled the doctrine's trigger out from under it. If blue's recurring-FCF correction is itself too harsh — for instance if a second tariff-refund tranche lands and part of the $80M is effectively recurring policy-driven cash — then red's doctrine was right, the adjudication is wrong, and **we lose money by not owning a 13%+ yielder at the bottom of its range**. That is a symmetric risk and we state it as one. Two of our own new patterns (*one_off_cash_inside_fcf_guide*, *pre_resolved_conjunctive_tripwire*) also risk over-firing: applied too eagerly they turn every cheap guided issuer into a permanent WATCH, which is exactly the failure mode we just convicted the original brief of.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the store channel's demand broke faster than any filing-based tripwire could report it, while we sized to a cash yield that was a quarter non-recurring and had no buyback left underneath it.
