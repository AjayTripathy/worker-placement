# PITCH — LVS — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-11 — ADVANCE AT REDUCED SIZE: a gated starter of ~0.4% of the book (half our normal position) at $44.50 or better, because the bear's biggest number was arithmetically wrong and the surviving objections are about size and timing, not about whether the business is broken.**

---

## What we believe

Las Vegas Sands runs casino-resorts in two places: Macao (through its 74.8%-owned listed subsidiary) and Singapore (Marina Bay Sands, wholly owned). The stock is $44.02 — 0.9% above its 52-week low of $43.60 and 37% below its 52-week high of $69.69. It has made a new 52-week low roughly every month for a year.

Four things we think are true:

1. **The stock's recent fall was driven by a story that turns out to be mostly wrong.** The market read Q2 as "Singapore is collapsing" — Marina Bay Sands earnings looked down 14% after adjusting for luck at the tables. That number is an arithmetic error. Casino results swing on "hold" (the share of money wagered that the house keeps), and analysts strip it out to see the underlying business. Whoever built the 14% figure *added* a favorable luck adjustment where they should have subtracted it, and used the wrong prior-year figure too. Corrected, Marina Bay Sands was down about 1.4% to 5.2%, not 14.4%. J.P. Morgan independently estimated "down about 5%." Both of our own benches initially inherited the error before one of them caught it.

2. **Macao's June–July air pocket was largely a transient event, and August confirmed it.** August Macau gaming revenue printed MOP21.9bn — down 1.2% against a very strong August 2025 (which was itself up 12.2%), but **up 8.1% month-over-month** off the trough. On a two-year stack that is +10.9%, roughly 5.3% a year straight through the weak patch. Year-to-date the market is +3.7%.

3. **On corrected numbers the stock is cheap, not extreme.** Correcting the valuation for the 25.2% of the Macao subsidiary we don't own (which the original write-up left out of the enterprise value entirely), we get roughly 9.6–10.2× enterprise value to normalized annual EBITDA of about $4.4B. Call it ten times cash earnings for the dominant operator in two protected, licence-limited markets.

4. **The downside is bounded by things you can point at.** Two long-dated concessions, a buyback authorization just *raised* to $6.0B through 2029, a $1.20 dividend, and separate financing structures for each geography so trouble in one does not automatically cascade into the other.

We are buying a half-size starter — not a full position — because two real problems survive.

## What the market believes

The market believes Macao is a structurally decelerating market and that Sands is buying its share rather than earning it.

Concretely: Morgan Stanley cut its 2026 Macau revenue forecast to MOP260.6bn (+5.3%) and cut sector EBITDA growth to about +1%, calling the reinvestment pressure "now structural." Argus cut the stock to Hold. Stifel took its target from $74 to $60; Mizuho from $67 to $61. Q2 was a clean double miss — EPS $0.59 against roughly $0.76 expected, revenue $3.15B against $3.31B. The company itself bought back $787M of stock in Q2 at an average of $52.37 and is down about 16% on that purchase in a single quarter.

The market is not confused about this name. It has read the filings, cut the numbers, and sold anyway. This is a well-covered stock trading at a well-informed low. That fact caps how much of an edge we can honestly claim.

## Why we might have edge

**The Singapore correction is the whole edge, and it is checkable.** The "Marina Bay Sands is down 14%" figure is a sign error on a hold adjustment, verifiable from the company's own disclosure of what earnings "would have been" at normal hold ($652M), and independently corroborated by J.P. Morgan's separate "down about 5%" estimate. If the market has priced Singapore as a broken asset when it was flat-to-slightly-down on a transient luck swing, that is a real mispricing with a named mechanism.

**Second, the enterprise value most people quote is too low, and correcting it cuts both ways.** The original bull write-up said 8.8× because it counted 100% of the Macao subsidiary's earnings but 0% of the 25.2% minority stake in the price. Correcting it gives ~10×. That is honestly worse for us — but it means anyone still working off 8.8× is wrong in the direction of over-optimism, and anyone assuming "casino at ten times is a value trap" is anchoring on the same bad number from the other side.

**Third, we resolved the August print in-court on the day it landed.** Our official-series layer pulled DICJ August gaming revenue during the adjudication rather than after — the first live use of that market-data layer inside a court. The court did not have to speculate about a data point that already existed.

The edge here is *arithmetic*, not information. We do not know anything the market cannot know. We checked a number the market appears to have taken on trust.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, a lot of it probably is.

**Our own forecast is more bearish than the street's, which is the wrong direction for a long.** Seaport publicly predicted August *and* September would return to growth. August did not. To hold their Q3 numbers, the street now needs September up 6% (J.P. Morgan) to 11% (Seaport). Our own estimate is flat to +3%. When you are more pessimistic than consensus and still buying, you are betting the price has fallen further than your own worse numbers justify. That is a thinner claim than "we see something they don't."

**Year-to-date is running below an already-cut forecast.** Macau is +3.7% through August against Morgan Stanley's full-year +5.3%. To hit that, September through December has to run +8.5% against strong prior-year comparisons. It probably won't, which makes another round of estimate cuts the base case, not the bear case.

**The stock's decline is a slow grind, not a shock.** Twelve months of monthly new lows is not a dislocation you can arbitrage; it is a market steadily marking down its view. Our own doctrine says a slow beta-bleed de-rating is not the same thing as a mispricing, and this looks more like the former.

**The share-gain story is weaker than advertised.** Macao revenue adjusted for hold grew about 7.6% against a market up roughly 1.8% — but Macao EBITDA on the same adjusted basis *fell* 9.8%, with margin down 520 basis points. On +$140M of revenue, incremental EBITDA was −$56M. Sands is paying more in promotions and reinvestment than the incremental revenue brings in. Share you buy at a negative margin is not a moat; it is a subsidy. And Citi's table survey shows premium-mass wagering — the highest-margin segment — down 38% year over year to a post-pandemic low, which the World Cup cannot explain.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The margin erosion is permanent and 10× turns out to be expensive.** The reinvestment war in Macao does not end; competitors match every promotion; hold-adjusted EBITDA keeps declining while revenue grows. Ten times a shrinking number is not cheap. *Tripwire: Q3 (Oct 21) Macao hold-adjusted EBITDA margin below 30%, or promotional/reinvestment spend up again year over year.* Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — The Adelson trust distribution becomes a supply overhang.** Four independently-sellable trusts of roughly $2.0B each were created on 2026-06-16 (13D/A). Any one of them can sell without the others. That is potentially ~$8B of stock against a company whose own buyback is already underwater at $52.37. *Tripwire: any Form 4 sale by a successor trust — this is a hard exit, not a trim.* Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 3 — Beijing or the Macao SAR administratively tightens, and there is no warning.** This is the one with **no monitorable tripwire.** Enforcement of cross-border payment rules, credit to premium-mass players, visa issuance cadence, or informal guidance to the operators can change with no public announcement, no filing, no series to watch. It shows up *only* in the monthly gaming revenue number, after the fact, by which time the stock has already moved. The unknown-unknown class is **administrative-discretion regime shift with no published leading indicator** — a sovereign policy channel that is a first-order driver of the P&L and structurally un-instrumentable by us. We have no sensor for it and cannot build one. It is the single largest uncompensated risk in the position, and the reason the position is half size rather than full.

**The reflexive and structural leg.** Our own size is not the risk here. A 0.4% starter is a five-figure order in a stock that trades in the hundreds of millions of dollars a day; we are far below 1% of daily volume and cannot move the price or front-run our own flow. (Exact average daily volume was not pulled this session — flagged below.) The structural risks are elsewhere:

- **We would be buying alongside the company's own buyback, which is the marginal bid.** If Sands slows repurchases to fund the committed capital spending below, a support we did not underwrite disappears. We are implicitly long the buyback without having priced it.
- **The predicted flow runs against us.** Our own arithmetic says estimate cuts are the base case for September through December. Our doctrine is not to bid into flow we ourselves predict — and here we are, buying ahead of cuts we forecast. The gated, tranched structure is the concession to that, not a solution to it.
- **Committed capital, largely non-earning.** About $3.78B of mandatory Macao non-gaming spending remains through 2032 (roughly 92% non-gaming, ~US$723M of a US$4.5B commitment spent through 2024), concurrent with the $8B Marina Bay Sands expansion whose cost rose $1B and whose opening slipped to January 2031. That is roughly $11.8B of commitments against ~$4.4B of annual EBITDA, a $1.20 dividend and a $6B buyback. Each geography has its own financing, which is why this sizes the position rather than killing it — but it constrains every other use of cash.
- **Doctrine interaction:** this would be a taxable-account starter in a name with an active dividend and a multi-quarter thesis clock. The tranche-2 date (Oct 21) and the exit tripwire (any trust sale) can both fire inside the short-term holding window, so any forced exit is a short-term loss with a wash-sale consideration against nothing else we hold. That is acceptable at 0.4%; it would not be at full size.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that the reinvestment war in Macao proves structural rather than cyclical — Sands keeps buying revenue at a negative incremental margin, the highest-margin premium-mass segment stays at its post-pandemic low, estimates get cut two more times into year-end exactly as our own arithmetic predicts, and "ten times a declining number" re-rates to eight times a smaller one, while the Singapore correction we are right about proves too small to matter.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Gate 1 status: CLEARED** on the adjudication's stated terms (August Macau revenue MOP21.9bn ≥ ~MOP21bn, +8.1% sequential, two-year stack +10.9%). The starter is live.

**Starter — buy now.** 0.4% of the book, limit **$44.50 or better** (last $44.02, 52-week low $43.60). This is half our normal position size. Rationale: the largest bear leg is refuted, the tape is at the low, and the surviving objections size the position rather than kill it.

**Add — tranche 2, gated, not automatic.** Take the position to no more than a full normal weight (~0.8% of book) **only if all three hold at the Q3 report on 2026-10-21**:
1. Macao rolling hold back at or above 3.0%;
2. Marina Bay Sands hold-adjusted earnings flat or better year over year;
3. Macao hold-adjusted EBITDA margin back above 30% with promotional/reinvestment spend flat or down.

If any one fails, stay at the starter. The tranche-2 sizing is a deck inference from "≤0.5× sleeve for the starter"; the three conditions are the courts' own.

**Interim gate — 2026-10-01, September Macau revenue.** Needs a **two-year stack of +10% or better** (comparison-neutral, not raw year-over-year — the prior-year months are distorted). Below that: no adds at any price, hold the starter. This gate was proposed by the bear bench and ratified by the bull bench, which is why we weight it.

**Hard ceiling.** No adds above **$50**, regardless of what the data does. Above $50 the correction we are being paid for is largely in the price.

**Hard exit.** **Any** Form 4 sale by a successor Adelson trust — full exit, not a trim. This is a supply event we cannot size or time, and we do not want to discover its magnitude while holding.

**Full-size reopen.** Below **$36** (roughly 8× the corrected enterprise value including the minority interest) the price pays for zero recovery. That is where the half-size discipline is released — it is a *lower* entry, not a stop.

**Catalyst dates:** 2026-10-01 (September Macau revenue), 2026-10-21 (Q3 earnings — vendor-estimated, not confirmed by the company), 2026-11-02 and monthly thereafter (October–December Macau revenue, which decide whether the full-year estimate cuts land), 2027-02-06 (end of the trust-sale filing watch window).
