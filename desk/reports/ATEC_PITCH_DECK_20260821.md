## What we believe

Alphatec sells spinal implants and surgical instruments to spine surgeons. It has been growing fast (surgical revenue +17% year over year, procedure volume +20%) and has spent the last several years losing money to buy that growth. The argument for owning it is that the losing-money phase is ending: in the June quarter, adjusted profit went from $23.5M to $36.0M year over year, and management raised the full-year adjusted profit target from $134M to $140M. The stock, meanwhile, is down 60% from its 52-week high of $23.29 and trades at 2.3x revenue.

Our position, after running the argument through an adversarial review, is narrower than that: **the profit improvement is real and is not an accounting artifact, but the business still converts almost none of that profit into actual cash, and the price is not obviously cheap on its own merits — only relative to two peers.** That combination justifies a small starter position at a slightly lower price, not a conviction buy.

Specifically:

- The improvement in profitability is genuine. Stripping out stock compensation entirely, the margin still expanded 409 basis points, versus 415 basis points on the headline number. The improvement did not come from paying people in stock.
- But cash is thin to the point of being a rounding error. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is **$2.3M on roughly $850M of revenue** — 0.3%. Full-year 2025 was $2.8M. Management guides to "at least $20M" for 2026, which requires the second half to do far more than the first half did.
- Leverage is about 4x gross, but there is no near-term solvency question: roughly $55M of cash after repaying the 2026 convertible notes on July 30, plus about $85M undrawn on a $125M revolver.

## What the market believes

The market has taken the stock down 60% and left it at 2.3x revenue while the company grows 15%. That is not a market that thinks the business is broken — it is a market that thinks the business does not generate cash and dilutes its owners while it grows. Both of those are, in fact, true. Shares outstanding went from 144.15M in February 2025 to 154.45M in July 2026, about 4.8% a year.

The market also appears to believe management's guidance, because management's guidance *is* the consensus. Nobody on our side of this produced an estimate that differs from the company's own.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: not much, and we should say so.

The one thing our review produced that the tape may not have fully absorbed is that **the stock-compensation criticism — the standard bear argument on this name — does not explain the profit improvement.** Stock comp dollars have been roughly flat for four years ($81M, $73M, $74M, ~$81M) while revenue compounded, so the *intensity* halved from 16.9% of revenue to about 9.2%. A bear who quotes the absolute dollar figure describes a rising cost. The actual trend is a falling one. If the market is pricing the flat-dollar version, there is a small gap there.

The second thing is a relative-value observation: at 2.26x revenue, ATEC is cheaper than Globus Medical (3.47x, growing 6.9%, net cash, $756M of free cash flow) and cheaper than SI-Bone (3.67x, growing 15.6%, negative EBITDA). ATEC grows faster than one and makes money unlike the other. At SI-Bone's multiple, ATEC is $17.30. At a plain 3.0x, it is $13.50.

That is a thin edge and it is relative, not absolute. We should not dress it up.

## Why it might be priced in

Several reasons, and they are strong:

1. **We agree with the street.** Neither side of the review produced a number that differs from the company's guidance. There is no identified mechanism by which we know something others don't. A position taken here is a position that the consensus is roughly right and the price is wrong — which is a weaker claim than knowing the consensus is wrong.
2. **The stock is not at its lows.** It is 36% *above* the 52-week low of $6.82. This is a partially recovered decline, not a fresh dislocation. Whoever was going to catch the falling knife has already caught some of it.
3. **The valuation is only cheap against peers.** On its own cash economics — $2.3M of trailing free cash flow — the enterprise value of roughly $2.0B is not defensible by any absolute measure. The bull case depends on cash conversion arriving, which has been promised before.
4. **Ownership crowding.** This is a well-covered small-cap medtech name with an active retail and institutional following. It is not undiscovered.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these are load-bearing:

1. **The October 29 earnings date.** Vendor-derived, not company-confirmed.
2. **Actual cash interest expense.** We know the reported GAAP figure is contaminated by the May refinancing and we know the company claims a $6M annual saving. We did not read the refinancing 8-K, so the true post-refinancing cash interest is somewhere in a $18–44M range. That is a wide band on a leveraged company.
3. **The EOS revenue shortfall.** This is the bull side's own new bear finding and the sharpest of the three second-tranche tests. It was derived by subtracting rounded figures in a press release ($214M total − $196M surgical ≈ $18M). It should be verified against the 10-Q segment disclosure before it is used as a trigger.
4. **No quality or recall data was pulled.** No openFDA, no adverse-event counts. Unchecked, and material.
5. **No facility-level verification.** No OSHA records, no thermal/utilization data on the Carlsbad facility. Low priority here — revenue is confirmed from filings, not inferred — but it is a gap.
6. **Peer multiples come from a secondary aggregator**, not from peer filings. The relative-value argument, which is the only affirmative valuation case we have, rests on this.
7. **The market's actual number.** Neither bench produced the sell-side's load-bearing estimate or price target. We claim the price is wrong without having written down what the price implies.
8. **The revenue-per-case explanation.** The metric is computable from filings; management's explanation for why it is falling ("product mix, not price") is available only on the earnings call and is not independently verified.
9. **Our own data pack has a confirmed bug** — the revenue row is stale for every company filing under post-2018 accounting tags. Fixed separately; flagged here because it means pack revenue figures should not be trusted in any deck until it is.
