# PITCH — PRIM — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO POSITION. Do not buy and do not short Primoris at ~$83.30; watch it into the Q3 report (~2026-11-02, date unconfirmed), where management's maintained full-year guidance either survives or takes a third cut.**

## What we believe

Primoris Services (infrastructure and energy construction, CIK 1361538) is taking real damage right now, and management's maintained full-year guidance requires a second half strong enough to contradict management's own statements.

The first half, from the company's own filings: Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $11.4M, down 92.6% from a year ago (H1 total $71.9M); the Energy segment's Q2 gross profit was actually negative (−$2.7M, versus a 10.8% margin a year earlier); first-half operating cash flow was −$131.3M; and the balance sheet swung from $62.8M of net cash at year-end to $582.9M of net debt at June 30. Management nonetheless kept the full-year EBITDA guide at $275–325M — a guide already cut twice from an original $480–500M.

Three independent pieces of arithmetic, all built from officially filed documents, show the same strain:

1. **EBITDA:** hitting the maintained guide requires $203–253M in the second half — 2.8 to 3.5 times what the first half produced.
2. **Net income:** the company guides full-year net income of $71–101M, but the first half was a $6.7M *loss*, so the second half must deliver $78–108M of profit after a losing quarter.
3. **Energy margins:** the full-year Energy gross-margin target is 6–8%, but the first half ran at 3.6% — so at flat revenue the second half must run roughly 8.4–12.4%, at or above the "historical 10–12%" level management itself says only returns in 2027. That is an internal inconsistency inside a single company press release.

The identified swing factor cutting both ways: $244.0M of contract changes not yet approved by customers, of which $205.9M has *already been booked as revenue*. Customer approval is the mechanism that would deliver the cash and the margins; failure to get approval is the mechanism for further reversals (the first half already absorbed $87.6M of downward estimate revisions, $71.1M of it in Q2 alone). The company is also facing securities litigation over exactly this — its estimation controls.

We do not know which way this resolves. That is why the proposed verdict is no position in either direction until the Q3 report answers the margin and cash-flow questions.

## What the market believes

The stock is at $83.30, down 59.5% from its 52-week high of $205.50 — so the market has clearly punished the two guidance cuts. But it is also up ~28% from its 52-week low of $65, and up roughly 11% from its ~$74.76 low of the past three months. The market is not treating this as a company in distress: it is giving management credit for the maintained guidance, taking comfort from stated covenant compliance and manageable leverage (about 1.9x net debt to guided EBITDA at the midpoint), and treating the second-half recovery as plausible — construction is seasonally back-half-weighted, and last year's second half really did produce $325.8M of operating cash flow.

## Why we might have edge

- The strain is **arithmetic, not opinion**, and it lives inside the company's own filed and furnished documents — the press release's full-year targets simply don't reconcile with the 10-Q's first-half actuals without a second half management itself describes as a 2027 outcome.
- The market's ~11% recovery since the June cut suggests investors are extending trust to a guide whose internal math is strained — and whose credibility already failed twice this year.
- The **$205.9M of revenue booked on unapproved change orders** is a specific, dated, checkable overhang most holders are probably not tracking line-by-line, and it sits on top of active litigation about estimation controls.
- Our adversarial process caught what a casual bear would get wrong (see the table below) — the surviving case is the one that withstood a deliberate demolition attempt.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated symmetrically, because the concessions are real:

- Down 59.5% is a lot of punishment already; the easy money on the bear side has been made.
- Leverage, on corrected numbers, is **not** alarming: ~1.9x against the guide midpoint, covenant-compliant per the 10-Q. The original bear brief's scary balance-sheet figures ($1,715M receivables, $1,061M net debt) were simply wrong.
- The alleged "unbilled receivables blowout" isn't there: contract assets are *flat* ($937.7M vs $936.9M), and retainage is already being collected ($311.2M → $291.3M).
- A huge second half has precedent — H2 2025 produced roughly $269.5M of free cash flow — so "the guide implies a record half" is a weaker claim than it sounds.
- Change-order approval is genuinely possible; if the $244M gets approved, the cash arrives, the margins reconstruct, and the maintained guide is vindicated. The stock would not wait for us.
