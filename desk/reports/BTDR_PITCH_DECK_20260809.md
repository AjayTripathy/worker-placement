## What we believe

Bitdeer (BTDR, $10.88, down 61% from its 52-week high) owns real assets: a bitcoin-mining and data-center business that just signed a $4.7 billion, 16-year lease for its Tydal, Norway campus with an AI-computing tenant. The asset story is not fake, and even our own prosecution bench conceded the price is not absurd.

But we believe the stock is un-ownable **right now**, for three reasons acting together:

1. **The cash burn is real, not an accounting illusion.** The bull case claimed the huge first-quarter cash outflow (−$346.9M from operations) was mostly voluntary — prepaying for chip wafers to build inventory. The balance sheet says otherwise: the prepayment balance actually *fell* by about $336M while inventory rose $361M — those two roughly cancel. After both benches fought over the number, the genuine, structural cash burn is bounded at roughly **$100–150M per quarter**, from a business that ran a *negative* 20.7% gross margin.
2. **The company is already selling stock to fund itself.** Its own filing says Q1 financing came from "convertible senior note issuance in February, borrowings **and ATM program**" — an at-the-market program is ongoing share sales. The claim that this is financeable "without touching the equity" was already false when it was made.
3. **Tomorrow's earnings report is the most likely venue for more dilution.** With about $261M of unrestricted cash against that burn, and three convertible bonds outstanding (~$1.015B, one of which converts at $9.93 — *below* today's price), the base-case headline at the print is a financing announcement, not a clean quarter.

One more finding that cuts against the bull: **the bitcoin treasury is essentially gone** — 31 BTC held at Q1-end versus 1,156 a year earlier. About $210M of the "digital assets" line is *receivables* (possibly pledged or lent coin), not liquid cushion.

## What the market believes

At $10.88 the market has already cut the stock 61% from its high but holds it 57% above its low — a middle-ground price that says: "the Tydal lease is probably real and worth something, the burn is scary, and we'll find out more Monday." Institutional holders filed two 13G ownership stakes in late July, so this is not an abandoned name. The market is pricing neither collapse nor the bull's $1.7–2.1B Tydal valuation — it is waiting for the same print we are.

## Why we might have edge

- **We resolved the decisive question before the print.** The bull thesis framed Q2 earnings as the event that would reveal whether the burn was "discretionary inventory build" or "true burn." Our prosecution bench showed the Q1 balance sheet *already answers this* — the prepayment-release/inventory-build wash means the escape hatch was arithmetically dead before the print. That is knowable now, and most holders likely haven't done the decomposition.
- **We read the lease's fine print.** The $4.7B Tydal lease's $1.3B credit backstop is **milestone-gated, not posted**: the letters of credit are "subject to customary conditions," and Bitdeer itself holds a termination right if the tenant (Volta — a company that came out of stealth *for this deal*, with no credit rating) misses backstop milestones. The tenant also has a no-fee walk-away right at year 10 of a 16-year lease. Headline value $4.7B; risk-adjusted value in the unposted-backstop state is $0.8–1.0B by our benches' math.
- **We caught the treasury drawdown.** The 31-BTC figure and the $74M unexplained build in digital-asset receivables are details that change the liquidity picture and that we found only by pulling the primary filing.

## Why it might be priced in

Honest concessions, stated plainly:

- A 61% drawdown is a lot of bad news already in the price. The market may have substantially priced the dilution risk we're describing.
- Our own defense bench showed the scariest version of our burn number was wrong (see the table below) — the corrected $100–150M/quarter burn, against demonstrated capital-markets access ($568M raised in Q1 alone), gives 2–3 quarters of runway, not the "less than one quarter" the prosecution first claimed.
- The dilution math is less bad than it sounds: if the converts convert, ~$1B of debt is extinguished at the same time, so on an enterprise-value basis conversion is roughly neutral, not a 31% haircut.
- If the letters of credit get posted, the lease genuinely becomes bank-grade credit, and a $1.7–2.1B valuation for Tydal becomes defensible. We are one filing away from the bull being right.

This is why the court's rejection is about **timing**, not a claim the company is worthless.
