## What we believe

monday.com is a 22%-growing software business with record operating profitability, roughly $1B of net cash, and a share price 62–67% below its high. At $83.33 the enterprise (market value less net cash) is about **$2.7B against ~$1.47–1.49B of 2026 revenue — under 1.9x sales**, a multiple normally attached to businesses that have stopped growing. This one has not: Q2 revenue was **$364.6M, +22%**, the seventh consecutive beat of its own guidance, and net dollar retention **rose to 109%** ([Q2 2026 6-K, accession 0001178913-26-003971](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1845338/000117891326003971/)).

The re-rating case rests on three things the market is discounting as if they were failures:

1. **The large-customer business is accelerating while the small-customer funnel shrinks.** Customers paying over $100,000/yr: **2,019, +37%**. Over $500,000: **114, +68%**. Their share of recurring revenue went 26%→30% and 5%→7% in a year. The weakness is confined to self-serve signups, which management has deliberately stopped spending into.
2. **The company bought a fifth of itself at prices near here.** $870M repurchased, **10,486,207 shares ≈ 20% of shares outstanding**, blended **$82.97** — essentially today's price. Weighted-average share count is already down ~5.9% year over year and the *exit* count is roughly 12% below the Q1 average, so about 13% of per-share accretion has been paid for but has not yet shown up in any reported per-share number.
3. **AI is monetizing faster than our own file believed.** The Q2 release states ARR from AI products **doubled from Q1 and was 17% of net new ARR** — against a kill trigger our file had set at 3% (a transcription error; the real prior figure was ~10%).

## What the market believes

That seat-based work-management software is the single most AI-exposed category in software, and that monday is now proving it: it cut ~20% of its workforce in July, its self-serve funnel is broken by the decline in Google search traffic, and it is mid-way through changing how it charges (seats → seats-plus-credits), which is how software companies reset revenue downward while calling it a strategy. The options market prices **95.9% annualized volatility** — this is not a stock anyone thinks is safe. The bear reading of the same Q2 print: cash flow went **down** (adjusted free cash flow $52.3M vs $64.1M a year ago), the guided second half is the weakest monday has ever set, and the buyback that absorbed 4–6% of daily volume for eight months is **fully spent**.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the regulator correspondence, the proxy, and the Israeli company-law mechanics, not the transcript.** The clearest example: the buyback. Everyone can see it is exhausted. Blue found that Israeli law imposes a **30-day creditor-objection period** before repurchases may begin — which is why a September 2025 board approval produced no buying until November. So a new authorization is a *signal*, not a bid, for a month. Almost nobody trading this stock knows that.
- **We separated "the funnel is broken" from "acquisition is broken."** Red's original kill said acquisition is broken; the primary data say the small self-serve tail is flat (+0.3%) while the sales-led business compounds. Cohort revenue per customer in the >$100k band is up ~8% — the exact opposite of the HubSpot analogue we were pattern-matching to.
- **We caught our own file being wrong in a way that changes the sign.** The AI-monetization kill trigger was calibrated to a mis-transcribed earnings call. Corrected, AI monetization was 3x better than our record believed *before* Q2, and Q2 then doubled it again.
- **The value transfer from the completed buyback is banked and arrives mechanically over the next three prints.** It is arithmetic, not a forecast.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: much of it may be.

- **The cash flow divergence is real, unexplained, and got worse.** Operating cash flow and free cash flow both fell ~17–18% year over year in a quarter when revenue grew 22% and non-GAAP operating income hit a record. Red named the cause of this divergence as its #1 open question before the print. The print gave a second data point and no answer. Full-year free cash flow is guided to a **19–20% margin — below 2025's 26.2%, an absolute decline** on 20% more revenue.
- **"Record non-GAAP operating income" is fully offset by stock compensation.** Non-GAAP operating income $61.1M; GAAP operating result **−$1.5M**. The difference — roughly **$62.6M** of add-backs, the largest in the company's history — is what shareholders paid employees in stock. On revenue and cash flow this stock is cheap; on cash flow *after* paying people, it is roughly 30x. Both statements are true and the gap between them is the entire debate.
- **The second half is the weakest ever guided.** And if the full-year guide was merely maintained on 2026-08-10 (I could not verify this — the pack's excerpt is truncated before the outlook), the implied second half is now **+16.2%** growth and a 2H/1H ratio of ~1.056, worse than the 1.081 that red already called the weakest in company history.
- **The bid is gone.** No new authorization appears on EDGAR through 2026-08-07, including the 2026-08-06 AGM filing ([6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1845338/000117891326003915/zk2635889.htm)). Whether the Q2 release announced one is unverified.
- **Deep-value software at 1.8x sales is a crowded trade in 2026.** Everything above is available to anyone who reads the release.
