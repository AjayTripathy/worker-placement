## What we believe

Circle is, today, a leveraged bet on one number: the interest earned on the reserves backing USDC. In the June quarter, reserve income was $668M of $701M in total revenue — 95.3% ([Circle Q2 2026 release](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-reports-second-quarter-2026-results); 10-Q filed 2026-08-05, EDGAR accession 0001876042-26-000248, CIK 1876042).

Two of the three common bear arguments do not survive contact with the filings:

1. **"Coinbase's cut will eat the economics."** It didn't this quarter. Distribution costs were $412M, up 1%, against reserve income up 5% — the payout ratio fell from roughly 64.2% to 61.7%.
2. **"Falling rates will gut the business."** The rates market isn't pricing falling rates. As of August 4 the futures curve put a 61.9% probability on a 25bp *hike* at the September 16 meeting, with the path near 4% through mid-2027.

The third bear argument survives, and it is the one that matters: **the amount of USDC in circulation is shrinking.** Supply was $73.3B on June 30 and $72.0–72.5B in early August — down about $1.3B in five weeks, in a calm rate environment, before any hike. Our own thesis set the falsifier at "below $70B, held three weeks." At the court price we were being asked to buy 3% above our own kill-line.

So: the company is better than the loudest bear case, and still not ownable at the price we were offered. We believe there is a real business here that we want to own at a price that pays us for the supply risk — and $66.67 (let alone today's $74.59) is not that price.

What the market is *not* obviously pricing is that the rate leg is currently tilted the company's way — a hike mechanically raises reserve income roughly 7% on the entire float (25bp on a ~348bp base).
