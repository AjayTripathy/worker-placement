What the market believes vs. what we believe

What the market believes

Either a cheap staffing name — the screen reads it at ~7.9× EV/EBIT on an assumed net-cash balance sheet — or a post-COVID travel-nurse-rate peak about to mean-revert the way the acute-care pure-plays did after 2022.

What we believe

Both framings are wrong on the filings. It is net debt, not net cash (true ~8.6–8.8× EV/EBIT), and the healthcare book is sticky school nursing and education teletherapy (grew +15% to a record), not decaying acute-care travel nursing. The FY25 earnings re-inflection is engineering-led on a grid / energy-transition tailwind — a cleanly-disclosed ~18%-ROIC compounder-lite at a fair-ish multiple.

The decisive finding — the cap-structure correction

The screen's "net cash at 7.9×" is refuted by the balance sheet — it carries net debt, and the true multiple is ~8.6–8.8×. The audited XBRL and the filings show cash of only $2.9M against a drawn revolver of $24.7M at FY25 year-end (net debt ~$21.8M), rising to cash $2.6M vs revolver $28.8M by the freshest quarter (net debt ~$26.2M) as the revolver funds the buyback. Recomputed at the live $27.55 on FY25 operating income of $25.1M, EV/EBIT is ~8.6–8.8×, not 7.9×. The cap structure is otherwise clean: no preferred, no convertibles, no warrants, no pension — only ~$5M of operating leases underneath. This is the genuine diligence contribution: a fair, not deep, multiple, honestly labelled — and it flips the perceived risk from a melting nurse peak to a far more monitorable one (below).

The operating-income re-inflection path is real and off-peak: CY22 $28.8M (the COVID peak, three years back) → FY23 $23.7M → FY24 $22.3M → FY25 $25.1M. The COVID travel-nurse peak that crushed the acute-care pure-plays was 2022; RCMT's own peak is three years behind it, and earnings have re-inflected up on the back of engineering, not nursing.

The honest catches — both disclosed, both sized-for

Customer concentration ~34%, and a soft freshest quarter. Two customers exceeded 10% of FY25 revenue — 20.8% and 13.6% (= 34.4%), both in the healthcare book — disclosed in the 10-K. That is the single biggest business risk, and because it is disclosed it is a sizing input, not a hidden liability. And the freshest quarter (Q1-FY26) shows total operating income −9.8% YoY — but the softness is entirely engineering (revenue −18.7%, which management attributes to "the timing of milestones associated with its fixed-price contracts"), while healthcare grew +11.1% and its operating income rose. That is the inverse of the melting-peak trap: the lumpy part is the project business, not the nurse book. It is one give-back quarter after a +24.9% engineering year — monitorable, not yet a break.

Where we pushed hardest — and the bear failed on the data

Is the debt-funded buyback turning into balance-sheet stress? No — the opposite. Net-debt / EBIT ran FY23 1.03× → FY24 1.35× (the peak) → FY25 0.87×, falling as EBIT rose, and the buyback has decelerated ($25.8M → $7.8M → $7.4M) while FY25 free cash flow (~$17.4M) covered it more than twice over. Leverage sits comfortably below the ~1.5× line we would worry at. Is there a hidden obligation behind the "net debt" story? None found — no preferred, converts, warrants or pension; only ~$5M of leases. And the buyback is genuine share-shrink, not option-offset: shares outstanding fell from 13.0M (2019) to 7.02M (−46%), with the diluted weighted count falling to 7.64M in FY25. The marketed takeaway does not diverge from the audited numbers.

Tripwires

  • The add gate / the mechanism falsifier: two more down engineering-segment quarters would convert "lumpy fixed-price timing" into a genuine demand break and undercut the durable-earnings case that is the thesis. A bounce-back quarter confirms timing, not demand — that is where to add.
  • The biggest business risk: loss or material step-down of a top-2 customer (~34% combined). Disclosed, so a size input — but a loss re-rates the multiple. Watch the annual customer disclosure.
  • Capital-return discipline: the buyback is revolver-funded, so watch net-debt / EBIT stays below ~1.5× (currently ~0.9–1.0×). Aggressive return outpacing free cash flow would be the balance-sheet worry.
  • Healthcare margin: compression beyond the mild ~90bps bill-rate normalization would mean the "sticky vs travel-nurse" distinction is weaker than modelled.
