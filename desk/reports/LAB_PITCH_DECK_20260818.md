## What we believe

Standard BioTools (LAB, $0.662, a new 52-week low as of 2026-08-18) is no longer a life-science tools company. On 2026-06-08 it agreed to merge with Treeline Biosciences, a private cancer-drug developer. LAB is selling or has sold its operating businesses; what its shareholders will own after closing is roughly 15.5% of a combined company holding more than $900 million of cash and four early-stage cancer drug programs.

The agreement values LAB at "its net cash delivered at closing plus $10 million," estimated at $460 million. Against a realistic fully diluted share count of about 411.7 million, that is about $1.12 per share of agreed value, and about $1.09 per share of pure net cash. The stock trades at $0.662. That is roughly a 39% discount to what the contract says LAB is worth.

The reason the discount is interesting rather than merely cosmetic is what sits on the other side of it. Treeline has raised about $1.2 billion of private capital. At today's price the market is marking Treeline's entire drug portfolio at roughly $854 million — about 0.71 times the money investors have put in — for a company whose lead program has already reported human data: TLN-121, a BCL6 degrader, showed an 84% overall response rate (16 of 19 patients) and a 32% complete-response rate with no dose-limiting toxicities, disclosed in a filing made with the SEC on 2026-06-08 ([425 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1162194/000114036126028877/ny20077604x2_425.htm)).

The payoff shape, using the corrected share count:

| What Treeline's drugs turn out to be worth | LAB per share | vs. $0.662 |
|---|---|---|
| $0 — all four programs fail | $0.340 | −49% |
| $854M — what the market implies today | $0.662 | 0% |
| $1.5B | $0.906 | +37% |
| $2.06B — the sponsors' own mark | $1.117 | +69% |
| $3.5B — if BCL6 becomes a franchise | $1.661 | +151% |
| Deal breaks, shell holds its cash | $1.093 | +65% |

We believe this is a modest, gated long — a starter position, not a conviction bet — because the downside requires four independent drug programs to all fail *and* the deal to close, while several plausible middle outcomes pay well.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a broken small-cap tools stock that has become an untradeable stub, and it is selling it on that basis rather than pricing the merger arithmetic.

Concretely, the market is saying: the cash is not yours and never will be; you cannot force a liquidation; you cannot demand appraisal; whatever you receive will be shares in a private-turned-public drug developer, not money. It is also saying that four Phase 1 oncology programs with one small dataset are worth less than the money raised to build them — which, for early-stage biotech, is a normal and often correct judgment.

There is also a mechanical seller. Index funds and tools-sector funds hold a company that no longer belongs in their mandate. Institutional screens shed sub-$1 stocks. A reverse split is coming (ratio still blank in the filings), which is itself an event that forces some holders out. The stock made a new 52-week low today, down 3.29% on 1.58 million shares. Nothing about the tape says the selling is finished.

Two analysts carry a $1.55 target. We do not treat that as support. Those targets look like leftovers from valuing a tools business that stopped existing on 2026-06-08.

## Why we might have edge

**1. The denominator, done honestly.** Both of our own benches got the share count wrong in opposite directions, and the answer sits between them. Our first pass used basic shares and produced a flattering 44% discount. Our skeptic used every antidilutive security at full weight (456.6 million) and produced 34%. But 33.1 million of those are stock options with a weighted-average strike of $3.68 — 5.6 times the current price. Those options are worthless and dilute nobody. The economically real count is about 411.7 million, and the discount is 39%. Getting this right is not glamorous, but the entire trade is a per-share number.

**2. The collar cuts both ways, and one payment already landed.** The merger agreement adjusts LAB's value down if net cash falls below $449 million *and up if it exceeds $451 million*. In July, LAB received $30.04 million from an Illumina royalty buyout, and the 2026-07-28 restructuring routed that money into Parent Net Cash instead of into the contingent value right. That converted an uncertain, share-settled contingent payment into certain value inside the exchange ratio. A reader who quotes only the downward half of the collar — as our own skeptic did — sees a one-way ratchet that is not there.

**3. Data exists, and it is on file.** The strongest single claim in the bear case was "four programs, zero disclosed data." That is false, and it is falsified by a document filed with the SEC on the day the deal was announced. TLN-121's 84%/32% response numbers are public. TLN-499 was described as preclinical; it has been dosing patients since 2026-07-25 (NCT07713732). All four programs are registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. A bear case built on an evidentiary vacuum does not survive contact with the registry.

**4. The break branch is mispriced as a punishment.** If shareholders vote the deal down, they own a shell with roughly $450 million of cash against 392.6 million shares — about $1.09 to $1.15 per share — minus about $0.013 per share of expense reimbursement. That is 65% above today's price. It is not a good outcome, but it is not the disaster the price implies. (Caveat below.)

## Why it might be priced in

We should be honest that most of what we know, the market also knows, and has known for ten weeks.

- **The clinical data is public.** The 84% response rate was filed on 2026-06-08. This is not private information we dug up; it is information the market has seen and chosen to mark at 0.71 times money-in. Our "edge" on the data is that our own bear read failed to open the document — that is a correction of an internal error, not an insight about the world.
- **The $10 million is real and it is a ceiling.** An arm's-length counterparty, negotiating with full information, priced LAB's entire legacy business — instruments, consumables, the royalty stream, the listing, the brand — at $10 million, about two cents a share. Any thesis involving hidden operating value is dead. Both benches agree.
- **Money raised is not value.** "0.71 times capital raised" sounds like a discount only if private rounds are fair marks. Often they are not. Late private rounds carry structure, preferences and momentum pricing; public markets routinely and correctly re-price them lower. The sponsors' $2.5 billion mark has not been shown to us as an arm's-length third-party round within the last twelve months. If it is a sponsor mark, our "+69% at the sponsors' own number" is a bet on their optimism.
- **One dataset, 19 patients, blended.** The 84% is across doses, at a company-chosen cutoff, with no duration-of-response disclosed. Early oncology response rates in small blended cohorts have a long history of shrinking. And the field is not empty: Bristol-Myers Squibb has a competing BCL6 degrader, BMS-986458, in Phase 1/2 and recruiting — a fact neither of our benches' original briefs contained.
- **The cash is genuinely unreachable.** Shareholders have no appraisal rights. There is no redemption, no tender, no announced liquidation. Every branch of the outcome tree ends with owning stock, not receiving money. Our "+65% break branch" quietly assumes a board that just chose this merger would then hand the cash back. There is no mechanism compelling that.
- **The flow is unfinished.** Both benches agree on this. New 52-week low today, forced sellers still working, and a reverse split with an undisclosed ratio still ahead. Buying into a decline you yourself predict is how a good thesis becomes a bad entry.

**Trim 1 — sell half at $1.05–1.12.** $1.117 is the agreed deal value per share at the sponsors' own portfolio mark. Being paid the sponsors' full optimism is not a reason to keep holding.
