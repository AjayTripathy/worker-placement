Healthcare quality: the distributor and the lab

July 23, 2026 · the healthcare leg of the rotation-sleeve build · envelopes 113–116 staged, awaiting review · sized vs the $1,000,000 base

The portfolio's measured weakness is too little of the market's most profitable, durable businesses. Healthcare is where that quality is currently on sale — but only selectively, and the selection is the thesis. Out of the liquid quality-healthcare universe, two names survived the two-pass courts: McKesson, bought now as a fairly-priced toll-road that history shows gets bid in a crisis, and Quest Diagnostics, a genuinely excellent business we refuse to pay today's price for — so a resting bid 10% lower does the waiting. The refusals — two patent-cliff pharma giants and a distributor that doesn't flinch-proof — define the edges of the trade.

The test that picked them

The mandate wasn't "cheap healthcare" — it was quality that behaves like quality when it matters. That's checkable: in the 2022 bear market, with the index down ~25%, McKesson rose 51.7% and Quest rose 15.4%. Both are toll-roads — they clip volume-based fees on drugs moving and tests running, regardless of which drugmaker or insurer wins — and both diversify the book's existing policy exposure (our payor position carries pharmacy-benefit-manager legislative risk; these two answer to different agencies through different mechanisms). Each then faced a two-pass court: every load-bearing claim verified against the actual filings, then an independent attempt to kill the thesis.

McKesson (MCK) Buy now — the quality anchor

Staged: 8 @ $785 · 5 @ $751 · 6 @ $720 (GTC ladder, ~$14.4k ≈ 1.4% at full fill) · live ~$814 · largest US pharmaceutical distributor

The case

America's drug supply chain runs through three companies, and McKesson is the largest — an investment-grade toll-road earning a thin, reliable ~1% on core distribution with a genuinely valuable software-and-services arm (prescription technology, ~18% operating margins) growing on top. It generates ~$5.4B of free cash flow and returns ~95% of it, compounding per-share earnings 13–16% a year with a ~3% annual buyback. After de-rating from a frothy $999 February peak to ~$814, it trades at ~18.4× forward earnings against its own ~24× average — the cheapest of the big three. Our probability-weighted fair value is ~$824: the price is fair, not cheap. This is owned as quality carry with the crisis-bid attached, not as a mispricing — and it's labeled that way in the book.

What the court verified — and corrected

The opioid liability — the reason this stock traded at 9× a few years ago — was verified from the settlement note in the actual 10-K: $5.7B accrued, paid out at ~$0.5B a year through 2038, against $5.4B of annual free cash flow. Capped, amortizing, absorbable. The court also corrected its own first draft: an initial "net cash" characterization was wrong — McKesson carries ~$4.0B of net debt (~0.6× earnings, trivial for the rating, but the record should say so). Scenarios: bear $666 (25%) / base $818 (45%) / bull $966 (30%).

The real risk isn't opioids — it's CVS. Roughly 24% of consolidated revenue flows through one customer whose distribution contract expires in June 2027. That's a bigger swing to enterprise value than the litigation tail, and it's inside our holding horizon. Tripwire: the renewal (expected between late 2026 and mid-2027) — comparable terms removes the biggest overhang and earns the add; losing the specialty book or a punitive re-price is the exit signal. Secondary: an adverse verdict in the remanded opioid re-trials forcing a material new accrual.

Quest Diagnostics (DGX) Wait — expressed as a resting bid

Staged: 60 @ $189 GTC (~$11.3k ≈ 1.1% if it fills) · live ~$210, an all-time high · the second-largest US clinical lab

Excellent business, unacceptable price

The court's verdict splits cleanly in two. The business passed every quality test: organic test volumes growing +3.4% — the best in three years — with favorable mix on top, 33% gross margins, ~28% returns on tangible capital, and a valuation that's at least internally honest (the earnings yield, book multiple, and returns all reconcile). Its regulatory exposure — Medicare's lab fee schedule — was verified current: the scheduled cut for 2026 is frozen at zero by statute, resuming January 2027 capped at 15% a year. And it's the cleanest diversifier from our payor position's policy risk — different agency, different mechanism entirely.

Then the price: ~19.6× forward earnings is 27% above Quest's own historical norm and ~4 multiple-turns richer than LabCorp — an inversion of their long-run parity — for a business whose structural growth is ~3–4%. Probability-weighted fair value is ~$204 against a $210 tape. There is no margin of safety; there is only momentum. So the position is structured as patience with teeth: a resting bid at $189 (~17.6× forward, fair value minus a ~7% cushion) that fills only if the pullback comes, escalating toward a full starter at $180–183. Scenarios: bear $177 (30%) / base $204 (45%) / bull $236 (25%).

The risk we're refusing: if today's flattered ~9% growth normalizes back to the 3–4% base rate, the multiple de-rates toward LabCorp parity — roughly $170–180, a −15–19% drawdown from here. That's valuation risk, not business risk, and it's precisely what the $189 entry sidesteps. Tripwire (imminent): the Q2 report, due within days — organic volume below ~5% for two straight quarters and the bid drops toward $175; volume holding ≥7% means the premium is being earned, and any pullback under $200 becomes an active buy.

How the healthcare leg now fits together

Three positions, three different economics, three different policy exposures: Cigna (held, ~1%, adding after its July 30 report) earns insurance and pharmacy-benefit margins and carries PBM legislative risk; McKesson earns distribution and software tolls and carries customer-concentration risk; Quest, when it fills, earns testing fees and carries Medicare fee-schedule risk. No two share a failure mode. The aesthetics position deliberately doesn't count toward this axis — its court said so — and hospitals stay a remnant after the sector's guidance cuts. At full build the healthcare leg runs ~$35–40k across the three names, every entry below the market that prevailed when the court sat.

Honest limits

McKesson is carry, not edge — our fair value sits ~1% from the price, and the position's return comes from the earnings algorithm and the crisis bid, not from a mispricing closing. Quest at spot has negative edge, which is why only the $189 bid exists. Unverifiable and flagged: McKesson's GLP-1 revenue contribution (undisclosed), the residual opioid tail beyond the accrual (the company declines to estimate; immaterial at ~$0.5B/yr against $5.4B of cash flow), and Quest's precise Medicare-fee-schedule revenue share (~7–9%, triangulated). None of these move the verdicts; all of them are on the record.
