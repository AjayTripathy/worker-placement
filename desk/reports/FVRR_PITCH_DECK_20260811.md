## What we believe

Fiverr, the freelance-services marketplace, closed at $8.75 on August 11, 2026 — 0.1% above its 52-week low and 69% below its 52-week high. At that price the whole company is worth about $318M, and it holds about $308.5M in corporate cash and securities with no debt. Strip the cash out and the market is valuing the actual business — one that generated $85.6M of free cash flow over the last twelve months and is guided to $52–62M of adjusted EBITDA this year — at roughly **$12M**.

We believe that is too cheap even for a shrinking business. The revenue decline is real (Q3 is guided down 18–26%, active buyers fell 21.9% year over year), but the business still produces cash every single quarter, dilution from the convertible is minimal, and the cash pile is *growing*, not melting. The realistic bad outcome is dead money — cash trapped inside a company whose management prefers acquisitions to buybacks — not permanent loss. We are buying a verified floor and paying a waiting cost, with a free option on management ever returning the cash.

Both benches of our internal adversarial review — the one arguing to kill the idea and the one defending it — agreed on the arithmetic. They disagreed only on how much to pay for the wait.

## What the market believes

The market's position is coherent and mostly supported by the evidence:

- **This is a melting ice cube run by people who won't hand you the water.** Management halted its buyback exactly as the stock hit its cheapest-ever valuation ($22.5M → $10M → $8M → **$0** per quarter), and has named opportunistic M&A — after already admitting a prior acquisition (AutoDS) underperformed — as the priority, with "at least six quarters" of business transformation ahead.
- **The decline is accelerating**, not stabilizing: Q2 operating cash flow fell 45% year over year, and the Q3 revenue guide is worse than Q2's actual.
- **The "resilient spending-per-buyer" story is an illusion.** When the low-spend customers leave, average spend per remaining customer rises automatically. Spend per buyer +15.6% times buyers −21.9% still equals total cohort spend **down ~9.7%**.
- Coverage is thin but present: ~10 analysts, consensus Hold, average price target $10.75, and short interest around 10%. This is not an undiscovered stock; it's a stock the market has looked at and sentenced.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The single scariest question was checked and closed in our favor.** The obvious kill was that the $308.5M might really be customer escrow money dressed up as corporate cash. The red (kill-side) bench pulled the balance sheet itself: cash $151.2M + deposits $70.0M + securities $87.3M = $308.5M exactly, while user funds ($156.4M) and the matching user-account liability ($146.6M) sit on **separate, matched lines outside that total**. The kill failed under direct verification.
2. **The floor compounds rather than melting.** The blue (defense) bench's strongest new finding: free cash flow is positive every quarter (+$13.6M in Q2 even at −45% year over year), so the cash backing the stock is rising at roughly $55M/year at the current run rate. The bear case is time-value drag, not impairment — and that's a much less severe risk than the market's price implies.
3. **The revealed-preference tell cuts both ways.** Yes, the buyback is at zero — but the CFO's stated priorities on the call explicitly include "generate value for shareholders **including further repurchases**." The channel is idle, not shut. Any repurchase-resumption filing is a clean, dated, checkable trigger, and at this valuation even a modest buyback retires shares at close to net-cash value.
4. **The recent 13G/A is mild evidence on our side**: Ameriprise/Columbia raised a *passive* stake from 8.5% to 10.0% (filed 2026-08-07) — institutional money accumulating, not fleeing ([SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001762301/000119312526340090/ck0000000000-ex99_ii.pdf)).
5. **The asymmetry is unusual.** Downside from here requires either the cash being misclassified (checked, failed), value-destroying M&A at scale, or years of dead money. Upside requires only one of: buyback restart, decline deceleration, an activist (13D), or a strategic buyer noticing a cash-covered marketplace with positive cash flow.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires stating this side with equal force, because the kill-side bench made most of these points stick:

- **A ~$12M enterprise value is not an oversight; it's a verdict.** Ten analysts cover this. The market is pricing exactly the thing we can't disprove: that minority shareholders never see the cash. Management's own behavior — buyback to zero at the lows, M&A talk — is *evidence for the market's side*, not ours.
- **The waiting cost is real.** Stock compensation runs $38.8M/year — about 12% of the market cap handed to employees annually. The growing cash pile and the dilution roughly offset each other per share; the floor holds but doesn't rise much for *us*.
- **The cash-flow numerator is falling fast.** The trailing $85.6M of free cash flow flatters; the run rate is ~$55M and declining with the guide. The "0.1x free cash flow" framing decays every quarter.
- **The one growth claim in the thesis is now formally struck.** The session pulled the 6-K exhibit from EDGAR directly (see below): the vetted document states the claim as a *count* — "Clients completing $1,000+ projects grew 13% y/y on a trailing twelve month basis" — with no dollar basis anywhere. The dollar-sounding wording exists only in the spoken transcript.
- **We could not run two of our standard checks** (seasonality analysis and our attention/positioning read) this session, so "not crowded, not a value-trap pattern" is asserted, not measured.
