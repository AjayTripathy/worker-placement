# PITCH — SCM — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — both benches land on no position; this deck carries the reopen conditions, not an entry.

---

## What we believe

Stellus Capital Investment Corp (SCM) is a small, externally-managed lender to private mid-sized companies. It borrows money, lends it out at higher rates, and pays the spread to shareholders monthly. The stock trades at $8.64. The company says its assets are worth $12.80 per share. That gap — about $119 million — is what drew us in.

**What we now believe, after running both benches: the gap is not a mistake, and we do not want to own this here.**

The honest arithmetic: the company earns $0.26 per share per quarter, or $1.04 a year, on a book worth $12.80. That is a return on its own capital of **8.1%**. Investors currently demand about **11.6%** to hold it. A business that earns 8.1% on capital when buyers want 11.6% is *supposed* to trade below book value — specifically at about 0.70x book. It trades at 0.676x. The discount is not an unexplored opportunity. It is the market doing correct division.

More importantly, both benches converged on something neither started with: **SCM is, in substance, a large unhedged bet that short-term interest rates stay high.** About 97% of its $968 million loan book floats with short-term rates. Only $222 million of its $602 million of debt floats — the rest is fixed-rate SBA debentures and 2030 notes. That leaves roughly **$717 million of net floating exposure**. Every 1 percentage point that short-term rates fall takes about $7.2 million a year off income — **$0.25 per share, a full quarter of the entire $1.00 annual dividend** — with no credit problem required.

That is the position. Not a credit trade. Not a book-value trade. A rate trade wearing a private-credit costume.

## What the market believes

The market believes exactly one thing about SCM, and it believes it consistently: **the dividend is the security.**

The evidence is unusually clean. The stock fell from $13.45 to $8.65, a drop of 35.7%. The dividend was cut from $1.60 to $1.00 a year, a cut of 37.5%. The yield at the old high was 11.90%. The yield today is 11.56%. The market did not re-rate the credit, doubt the marks, or price a recovery. It divided the new dividend by roughly the same required yield and moved on.

So the market's view is: *this is an income instrument yielding 11.6%, and I will pay whatever price makes it yield 11.6%.* Nothing in the price expresses a view on the loan book, the manager, or book value. The stock has already recovered 26.5% off its $6.83 low, so a third of the fall is retraced — this is not a fresh dislocation either.

## Why we might have edge

Three places where we genuinely saw something the price does not obviously contain:

1. **The rate math is not in the story anyone tells about this stock.** SCM is discussed as a credit name. The $717 million net floating position is derivable only by pulling the debt composition out of the quarterly release and netting it against the asset side. Our own red bench got this wrong by a factor of two before blue corrected it. If a bench that was actively hunting for it understated it 2x, it is plausible the marginal retail income buyer has not computed it at all. That is a real informational asymmetry — it just points *down*, not up.

2. **The buyback tells you what the manager actually thinks.** The company has a $20 million buyback authorization and is trading at 0.68x book. Buying your own stock at a 32% discount to stated book is roughly a 40% return on capital — the single best investment available to them. In the second quarter they spent **$2.45 million**. Their average repurchase price, $8.92, is *above* today's price. A manager who believed its own marks would be spending far more. It isn't — and the management fee is charged on gross assets, so shrinking the balance sheet cuts the manager's pay. That conflict is structural and observable.

3. **The book is shrinking.** Repayments of $49 million against new fundings of $18 million. Total investment income fell 15.1% while the portfolio fell only 4.2% — realized yield on the book dropped from 10.4% to 9.2%, a 120 basis point decline. The headline "weighted average yield" the company reports fell only 8.5% to 8.4%, because that metric excludes non-accruals and equity. It measures the loans that survived. That is a 12x understatement of the actual income decay, and it is the number most people read.

## Why it might be priced in

The blunt answer: **at 0.70x book on an 8.1% return against an 11.6% cost of capital, everything visible is priced.** The math closes to within three cents of the tape. Our red bench initially argued "fair value equals dividend divided by required yield equals $8.62, so the tape is fair" — but that is circular, since the required yield was itself computed from the price. It returns the tape for any input. The blue bench replaced it with the non-circular version above, which reaches the same conclusion honestly.

Specific things we thought were edge and were not:
- **The book-value discount.** It has a complete explanation that requires no credit skepticism. It closes if and only if the return on capital rises. It is a residual, not a mispricing.
- **"The dividend is covered."** Comparing $0.26 of earnings to a $0.25 dividend is comparing a reported past quarter to a future rate. What SCM actually declared in that quarter was **$0.34** — it under-earned its own distribution by 23%. The cut fixed coverage going forward; it has not yet been earned in any reported period.
- **The non-accrual "improvement."** Non-performing loans fell from 9.2% to 8.5% measured at original cost, but *rose* from 5.2% to 5.4% measured at current value. One borrower exited. The remaining ones are marked higher as a group. The mix improved; the marks did not.

## What could go wrong

Written as though we had taken the position at today's price. This is the pre-mortem, and it is the strongest section of the deck.

**Failure 1 — Rate cuts, and the reflexive selling that follows them.** *This is the named kill.* One percentage point of easing removes $0.25 of the $1.00 coupon. At an unchanged 11.6% required yield, that alone puts the stock at **$6.49 — a 25% loss with the loan book entirely intact.** Then it gets worse in a second-order way: the only buyer of this stock is a retail income holder who owns it *for* the 11.6%. When the coupon falls, that holder does not re-underwrite the credit — they sell and buy the next 11.6%. The mechanism that would hurt us is the same mechanism that removes our bid. **Tripwire: monitorable** — Fed meeting dates and the forward short-rate curve. We have not armed it, and we have not confirmed the 2026 Fed calendar from a primary source.

**Failure 2 — The marks are optimistic and we find out on a 45-day lag.** These are private loans with no market price; values are management estimates. Non-performing loans are 8.5% of the book at cost and carried at roughly 62 cents on the dollar. If the remaining problem credits deteriorate, book value falls and — because earnings fall with it — the fair price falls faster than book does. **Tripwire: partially monitorable** — the quarterly schedule of investments, but it arrives six weeks after quarter-end, which is exactly long enough for the stock to have already moved.

**Failure 3 — No tripwire exists. The book is a list of companies we cannot see.** SCM's borrowers are private LLCs disclosed only as line items in a schedule. There is no earnings call, no product, no app, no hiring page, no customs record we can reliably bind to them. Our red bench proposed running our borrower-level detectors against entity names pulled from the filing's data tags (`TwoXLlcMember`, `AdcellerantLlcMember`, `ADSGroupOpcoLLCMember`, `AdvancedBarrierExtrusionsLLCMember`, `AmiiAcquisitionLlcMember`) — and our blue bench correctly refused, because nothing in the document binds those names to the *non-performing* loans. Running the tools anyway would have manufactured a false signal from a formatting artifact.

So the third failure narrative is: **one or two borrowers we cannot name deteriorate rapidly, or the manager takes an action at the fund level that is disclosed only when it executes.** Candidates in that second category: a shareholder authorization to issue new stock below book value (which at 0.68x book would be directly dilutive to us, and which sits unread in the April proxy); an unfavorable resolution of the pending regulatory application; a change to the advisory agreement; a sale of the manager itself. **Unknown-unknown class: *no native disclosure plane for the driver entity.*** The thing that moves the price has no continuous public surface at all — the first observation is the outcome. Every sensor we own reads public documents about public entities; none of them can see inside a private loan or ahead of a related-party decision. We cannot monitor what has no observable channel, and we should not pretend a quarterly filing is a monitor.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own predicted flow, our own rules:**
- **Size vs. liquidity: UNVERIFIED, and that is a real gap.** Our broker tools were permission-denied during the court, so we have no independent daily volume or short-interest reading. Market value is about $248 million. A 0.5% starter is small in absolute terms, but on a name this size a single print could plausibly exceed 1% of a day's volume, which our own publishing rule forbids. Any entry must be laddered over multiple days, and we should not size it until volume is measured.
- **We would be buying into flow we ourselves predict.** Our stated failure mechanism *is* retail income selling on a rate cut. Buying the dip caused by that selling means bidding against a wave we forecast. Our own rule against this exists precisely for this shape. The only clean entry is one that pre-pays for the cut — which is what the $6.49 reopen level is.
- **The tax treatment is genuinely bad for our book.** These distributions are ordinary income, not qualified dividends. At our roughly 37% blended rate, the $1.00 coupon nets about **$0.63**, so the 11.6% headline yield is about **7.3% after tax** in a taxable account. That is not obviously better than alternatives with lower rate sensitivity and better tax character. This alone is a serious argument against the name even if every other objection were answered.
- **We cannot call this "fairly-paid risk."** Our rule for owning a risk we understand requires the fairness of the payment to be *verified*. Here two inputs to fairness are unverified: the exact floating-rate share of the assets, and the forward rate path. Unverified fairness is not fair pay.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___** that short-term interest rates fell, the dividend fell roughly one-for-one with them, and the retail income buyers who are the only holders of this stock sold it to buy the next 11.6% yield — none of which required a single loan to go bad.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry. There is no immediate buy. What follows are the reopen bands.**

Current position: none held, no orders working.

**Reopen Band A — price-only, requires no new information.**
- **Level: $6.49 or below**, with the monthly distribution still at $0.0833 and non-performing loans not above 5.4% at current value.
- **Why that number:** $6.49 is the fair value that falls out of the bench's own math *after* a full 1 percentage point of rate cuts ($0.75 coupon ÷ 11.6%). Buying at or below it means the market has already paid us for the named kill before it happens, instead of us paying for it in advance.
- **Size if triggered: 0.5% of book**, laddered over at least three sessions and never more than 1% of measured daily volume — which requires the broker connection to be restored first.
- Note the 52-week low is $6.83, so this band sits below anything reached in the past year. It is meant to be a hard band, not a likely one.

**Reopen Band B — information-gated, allows entry up to about $8.65.**
All three must be true, none of them are today:
1. The pending regulatory application is **granted** *and* the SBA debentures are actually **drawn** and on the balance sheet at an all-in cost **below 6.0%** — capacity is not the same as drawn money.
2. The Q4 dividend is declared **on or about October 15, 2026 holding $0.0833 per month**, *and* the third-quarter investment income comes in at **$22.3 million or better** — i.e., revenue stops falling, not just a coverage ratio being met.
3. Either a rate hedge sits alongside the position, or we write down in the file that we are knowingly accepting **−$0.25 per share per 1% of easing** unhedged.
- A fourth item would materially strengthen the case though it is not required: **buyback execution of $8 million or more in the third quarter**, which would put the manager's money behind its own marks and refute the conflict finding.
- **Size if all triggered: 0.5% starter**, second 0.5% only after one full additional quarter with the above intact.

**Exits and trims, if ever owned:**
- **Trim at $10.81.** That is the everything-works fair value: the $1.00 coupon plus $0.07 from the SBA leverage and $0.18 from redeploying the $52.3M of non-performing loans, all fully executed, at the same 11.6% required yield. It is the ceiling of the bull case, not a stop on the way up.
- **Exit on any of:** the monthly rate falling below $0.0833; non-performing loans exceeding 7% at current value; 2 percentage points of cumulative short-rate decline without a hedge in place; or a shareholder authorization to issue stock below book value.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **~October 15, 2026** — Q4 dividend declaration. *This is the date that matters.* It is the only near-term event that resolves the one leg holding the price. Pattern-derived from the July 16 declaration and the identical prior-year sequence; not company-confirmed.
- **Late October 2026** — Q3 preliminary results release (the July 29 analogue). Unconfirmed.
- **November 11 or 16, 2026** — full Q3 report. The two sources conflict and neither is a company announcement. About eleven weeks out.
- **Fed meeting dates** — the actual driver, and we have not pulled the 2026 calendar from a primary source. This must be done before anything is armed.

**A note on probability weighting, stated honestly:** with three outcomes at $10.81, $8.65, and $6.49, almost any reasonable set of weights produces a number within a dime of today's price. That is not a finding — it means the answer is entirely determined by the probability of rate cuts, which we did not verify. We are deliberately not publishing a probability-weighted fair value as a gate, because it would be a guess dressed as arithmetic.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these could change the verdict:

1. **The forward path of short-term rates.** Never pulled. This is the input the entire kill depends on. The sensitivity is filing-derived and solid; the probability is not estimated at all.
2. **The exact floating-rate share of the assets.** The ~97% figure is plausible and consistent with the disclosures but was not independently re-read from the schedule of investments. The $717 million net floating figure moves with it.
3. **The pending regulatory application (40-APP/A, 2026-08-14).** Not read by either bench. It controls $250 million of the cheapest borrowing available to the company.
4. **The April proxy's below-book issuance authorization.** Not fetched. Direct dilution risk at today's price.
5. **The advisory agreement's actual incentive-fee hurdle.** Blue inferred roughly 2% per quarter from the fact that $0.26 of earnings sits near $12.80 × 2% = $0.256. Plausible, not read from the contract.
6. **Live price, daily volume, short interest.** Broker tools were permission-denied throughout the court. Everything here uses the evidence pack's $8.64 and the benches' $8.65.
7. **The next earnings date.** Two vendor sources give November 11 and November 16; neither is a company announcement.
8. **The identity of the non-performing borrowers.** Not resolvable from what we obtained, and correctly not guessed at. This is the reason Failure 3 above has no tripwire.
9. **Whether the loan marks are conservative or optimistic.** The check that would settle it — the performing book's weighted-average mark against cost, and the share of performing loans marked below 90 cents — requires the full schedule of investments, which returned an access error on direct retrieval.
