# PITCH — CORZ — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No purchase. Stay flat at $17.48; the growth everyone is underwriting earns a return at or below the cost of the debt required to build it, so re-open only on the four dated triggers below.

---

## What we believe

Core Scientific is no longer a bitcoin miner. It is a landlord that rents electrified, cooled buildings to AI companies on long contracts. That business is real, it is contracted, and it is growing. Our disagreement is not with whether the buildings get built. It is with what the buildings earn.

Three things we believe, in order of how much weight they carry:

**1. The company effectively has one customer.** The June 30, 2026 quarterly report says 83% of *total* revenue came from a single customer in the colocation segment (CoreWeave), versus 13% a year earlier. But total revenue includes a shrinking bitcoin self-mining stub. Restate it against the business that actually continues: 83% × $164.2M total revenue = $136.3M, against colocation segment revenue of $136.7M. That is **99.7% of the continuing business from one tenant.** And because the mining stub is being wound down, the *disclosed* 83% will keep climbing toward 100% even if nothing operationally goes wrong. Source: [10-Q, period ended 6/30/2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839341/000183934126000014/core-20260630.htm).

**2. The famous AMD contract is a thin-spread deal, not a windfall.** Management guides roughly $11–12 million of capital spending per megawatt. The AMD agreement is about 530 MW, so roughly **$6 billion of construction**. The contract is more than $14 billion of base revenue over 15 years with 2.5% annual escalators, which works out to about **$781 million in the first full year** — a 13.0% revenue return on the money spent. After operating costs, at a 50–70% margin, the *profit* return on that construction is **6.5%–9.1%**. The company's own senior secured notes carry a **7.75% coupon**. You are borrowing at 7.75% to buy a 6.5–9.1% asset.

**3. Therefore the growth barely adds equity value, even if the financing appears by magic.** We ran that test explicitly — assume the $6 billion shows up tomorrow at 7% project-level debt:

| Operating margin | Annual profit | Interest at 7% | Left for shareholders | Value at 15× | Added to today's ~$7.5B equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | $391M | $420M | −$29M | — | **−0.4%** |
| 60% | $469M | $420M | $49M | $0.7B | **+10%** |
| 70% | $547M | $420M | $127M | $1.9B | **+25%** |
| 100% (impossible) | $781M | $420M | $361M | $5.4B | +72% |

You need roughly an **86% operating margin** for this build to be worth +50% to the stock. Nobody runs an 86% margin data center. Financing was never the binding constraint on the upside — the spread was.

**4. And you pay a growth price for it.** Corrected fully diluted share count is **466.1 million** (321.3M shares outstanding, 96.4M warrants at $6.81, 6.6M warrants at $0.01, 41.8M shares from convertible notes that are deep in-the-money at $11.00, 27.8M from the $22.49 converts). Enterprise value is roughly **$10.05 billion** excluding $781.7M of restricted cash, or **$9.27 billion** including it. Against a stabilized, fully-leased profit estimate of about $413 million, that is **~24× earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization** — for a single-tenant, bond-like return.

Net: a levered landlord with one tenant, priced as an artificial-intelligence growth equity. Pass.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is early. Seventeen analysts cover it: **13 Strong Buy, 3 Buy, 1 Hold**. Average price target **$37.13** (range $28–$55), which is +112% from $17.48. Revenue consensus is **$671.6M for 2026** and **$1.15B for 2027**.

That price target implies about **$17.3 billion of equity plus $2.55 billion of net debt = ~$19.8 billion enterprise value, or 17.2× 2027 revenue.**

Here is the specific mechanism of the disagreement, and it is not "they are too optimistic on revenue." We rebuilt the street's 2027 revenue line ourselves — 590 MW leased × roughly $1.45M per MW per year ≈ $855M, plus a partial year of AMD, plus the mining stub, gets you to $1.0–1.1 billion. **The street's revenue forecast is reconstructable and reasonable. We do not disagree with it.** What the street is doing is capitalizing contracted revenue at a revenue multiple **without charging for the $6 billion of project debt required to earn that revenue.** That is the gap.

## Why we might have edge

- **The concentration restatement.** The disclosed number is 83% of total. The number that matters is ~100% of the continuing segment, and the disclosed number rises mechanically as mining winds down — which will read like deterioration when it is arithmetic. We have not seen that restatement anywhere in published research.
- **The spread test.** Both of our own benches argued for pages about whether the $6 billion could be *raised* before anyone asked what the $6 billion *earns*. Once you ask, the answer is decisive and it is available entirely from management's own guidance plus the contract terms. This is the load-bearing finding and we believe it is genuinely underweighted.
- **The corrected capital structure.** The widely-circulated share count is wrong. Our own initial brief had it wrong too (see below). Two convertible notes totaling $1.085 billion — 69.6 million shares — are routinely omitted, and one of them is deep in-the-money.

## Why it might be priced in

We are obliged to argue against ourselves here, and it is a real argument.

- **The 83% is printed on the face of a quarterly report covered by 17 analysts.** We cannot claim as proprietary a number that is one sentence in a public filing. We can only claim the restatement is proprietary, and that is an inference we cannot prove — we cannot cite the absence of someone else having done it.
- **The rejected CoreWeave takeover** (shareholders voted down a $9 billion all-stock bid on [October 30, 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/core-scientific-shareholders-reject-9-billion-coreweave-offer-deal-terminated.html)) is arguably the single most-covered fact about this company in the past year. Our red bench initially tagged it as a novel finding. That was wrong.
- **The stock has already fallen 43% from its 52-week high**, and it fell in near-lockstep with CoreWeave (−53%). That is the sector re-rating, not a name-specific mispricing waiting to be found. On the tape it is mid-range, not distressed: $17.48 against a $13.135–$30.46 band, **+33% off the low**. There is no dislocation to buy.
- **We have no revenue-forecast edge in either direction.** Our number sits inside consensus. Our entire disagreement is about the multiple applied to it — an opinion, not a fact. Opinions do not get graded.
- **The company has repeatedly bought power interconnections for cash rather than waiting in the utility queue.** That execution pattern could compress the $11–12M/MW cost below guidance and rescue the spread. If capex comes in at $8M/MW, most of this deck is wrong.

## What could go wrong

The pre-mortem below is written for the position we would actually take — nothing today, and a starter only if the re-open triggers fire. The three failure narratives are the three ways we end up poorer.

**Failure 1 — We buy the funding headline instead of the spread (monitorable).** A project bond prints at the AMD sites. Our own re-open trigger fires. We buy at $22–26 into a re-rating, and then the capex per megawatt drifts from $11M to $14M on transformer and turbine lead times, so the return on cost falls back below the coupon we just celebrated. We were right about the economics and bought anyway because a trigger we wrote told us to. *Tripwire exists: the bond pricing 8-K, and the capex-per-MW figure on the next two earnings calls.*

**Failure 2 — The one tenant weakens (partly monitorable).** CoreWeave is essentially the entire continuing revenue line. It is itself a capital-hungry, negative-free-cash-flow company down 53%. A funding stall, a renegotiation, or a slowed take-down does not dent CORZ's revenue — it removes it. And the tenant is a **rejected acquirer**: a counterparty that tried to buy the landlord and was turned down has demonstrably shown it would rather own than rent, which makes renewal and expansion terms adverse rather than neutral. *Tripwire is partial: we can watch CoreWeave's own filings and bond spreads, but we cannot see the lease-level take-down schedule, which is the thing that actually matters.*

**Failure 3 — The megawatts are not what we were told (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).** Everything above is downstream of one number: how much power is actually contracted, energized and billable. We have no independent way to check it. Three separate holes stack:

- The marketed "1.1 gigawatts" reconciles to 590 MW leased and **395 MW actually billing** in the filing. That gap is a legitimate basis difference (signed-including-undelivered versus in-service at the balance sheet date), not concealment — but it means the headline number is not a verified operating fact.
- The **$444 million Polaris DS acquisition** that closed August 13, 2026 was disclosed under Items 7.01 and 8.01 of an [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001839341/000183934126000018/core-20260813.htm) — **not** under Item 2.01 with audited target financial statements. The acquired "~440 MW already in service under existing utility agreements" therefore has **no audited support anywhere**. $444 million of cash went out the door against numbers no outside investor can inspect.
- The sites are in Reeves County and Hunt County Texas, Muskogee County Oklahoma, Auburn Alabama, and Dalton Georgia. **We have no data connector covering any of them,** and none covering the ERCOT large-load or SPP generator-interconnection queues, which are where a real megawatt claim would be independently confirmable.

**Unknown-unknown class: the un-instrumented physical-asset claim.** This is the family of risks where the single most load-bearing operating metric in the entire thesis exists only on a plane we cannot observe, and where the acquired assets carrying that metric were bought without audited financials. We would not learn we were wrong from a filing, a price, or a sensor. We would learn it from the company, after the fact, in a disclosure written by the party with the most to lose from writing it. There is no version of this deck that closes that hole; only a new connector build would.

**The reflexive and structural leg.** Three things here, and one of them is a live gap:

- **Our size versus daily volume: unknown.** Broker market-data access was denied in the session that produced this work, so we have **no live tick, no short interest, and no 90-day average daily volume.** Any sizing statement below is therefore unbacked on the liquidity side. That is a gap, not a footnote.
- **We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict.** There are 96.4 million warrants at $6.81 — **30% of shares outstanding**, deep in-the-money, held largely by post-bankruptcy claim holders with no long-horizon mandate — plus 41.8 million in-the-money convertible shares, none of it under any lock-up. Every rally is a mechanically-exercisable supply event. Buying strength here means buying into our own forecast of selling. Our house rule against bidding into predicted flow points at exactly this. (Honest caveat: we called this a "permanent mechanical seller" and we have **no** holder-composition or exercise-flow evidence for that. A July 28 filing of unregistered share sales suggests exercises may already be flowing through and partly retiring the overhang.)
- **Doctrine interactions.** (a) Our default rule that a clean court with no named objection owes at least a token position does **not** apply here — the objection is named, dated and arithmetic: contracted growth at a return on cost inside the cost of the debt that funds it. (b) The rule that we do not kill a name merely because we agree with the consensus also does not save it — we checked, and our own forecast is inside consensus, so there is no forecast divergence to defend. (c) Because CORZ trades in lockstep with CoreWeave, any position here is a second helping of the same AI-infrastructure exposure the book already carries elsewhere; it would size as a sector bet even if we intended it as a name bet. (d) GAAP earnings on this issuer are meaningless — the second-quarter net loss of $1,155.3 million includes $1,045.5 million of non-cash warrant revaluation caused by **the stock going up**. Any earnings-versus-consensus trigger we write against this name would be noise.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought the financing headline instead of the spread — a project bond printed at a coupon that looked like vindication, we entered on our own trigger at a much higher price, and the underlying business was still a single-tenant landlord earning a return on cost inside its cost of capital, with megawatt claims we never had any independent way to verify.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry.** No position is held and no orders exist. Both benches independently concluded flat, each at 8/10 conviction. These are the re-open bands instead.

**Re-open trigger 1 — cheap project debt (the one that actually inverts the thesis).**
Condition: project-level bonds price at the AMD sites at a coupon **at or inside ~5.50%**.
Why this level: the entire objection is a 6.5–9.1% return on cost against a 7.75% cost of debt. At 5.5%, the spread turns positive and the build creates value instead of consuming it. Note the correction here — our red bench proposed "any 2027 funding event" as the trigger. That is wrong. An expensive financing confirms the bear case; only a **cheap** one refutes it.
Action if it fires: re-court immediately, then a **0.25% starter** on the first close after the pricing 8-K, with a deliberate refusal to chase — no purchase more than 8% above the pre-announcement close, because that is exactly the "bought the headline" failure above.

**Re-open trigger 2 — construction cost comes in cheap.**
Condition: capital cost guided **below ~$9.0M per megawatt** on an earnings call or in a filing.
Why: at $9M/MW the AMD build is ~$4.8B rather than $6B, and the return on cost rises to roughly 8.7–12.2%, above the 7.75% coupon. This is the live possibility — the company has bought interconnects for cash rather than queuing, which is precisely the behavior that compresses cost per megawatt.

**Re-open trigger 3 — the concentration actually breaks.**
Condition: a quarterly report showing top-customer share of **total** revenue below ~65% **with a second named tenant actually billing** — not signed, billing. Alternatively, billing megawatts crossing **~700** with colocation gross margin holding at or above 43%, which would put annualized profit above the ~$270M interest run-rate.
Note: this cannot happen inside the next three reports. AMD's first delivery is in the first half of 2027. Until then the disclosed concentration goes **up**, not down.

**Re-open trigger 4 — price.**
Condition: **below $11.00.**
Why this exact level: $11.00 is the conversion price of the 2029 convertible notes. Below it, 41.8 million shares fall out of the diluted count and $460M reverts to being debt rather than equity; enterprise value against 2027 revenue drops to roughly **6×**, which is a different security. That is a **−37% move from $17.48** and would almost certainly arrive as sector-wide stress rather than a company-specific event — so it is a re-court trigger, not an automatic buy.
Secondary watch level: **$13.135**, the 52-week low. A break there is a cohort-stress signal and should pull the court forward regardless of whether $11.00 prints.

**Sizing if any trigger fires:** 0.25% of book maximum on the first tranche, and this is explicitly **unbacked on liquidity** — we have no average daily volume figure, so the standard "never more than 1% of daily volume" discipline cannot currently be applied. Restore market data before any order is written.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **2026-10-23** — third-quarter results. **UNCONFIRMED.** This date is aggregator-derived. The company does issue a scheduling press release (it did for Q1), and no such release exists as of August 24, 2026. Roughly 40 trading days out — not close enough to require a print-specific plan.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these carry real weight:

1. **The operating margin on the AMD contract.** The 50–70% bracket is our estimate. Management has not disclosed it. The entire prize table swings on this number, and at 50% the deal adds *negative* equity value.
2. **The 7% project-bond coupon** used in the prize table is our estimate, not a printed price. This is the largest single source of doubt in the bear case, and it is why conviction is 8/10 and not 10/10.
3. **The Polaris DS acquisition economics.** $444 million spent on August 13, 2026 for roughly 440 MW claimed to be "already in service" under existing utility agreements — disclosed with **no audited target financial statements**. Nobody outside the company can check it.
4. **Whether the warrant overhang is being retired.** A July 28, 2026 filing of unregistered share sales suggests exercises are flowing. We did not open it. This directly affects whether the "permanent supply shelf" characterization is fair; as written, it is plausible but unevidenced.
5. **Whether anyone else has done the segment restatement or the spread test.** We believe not. We cannot prove absence, so we are not claiming it as established.
6. **All live market microstructure** — current tick, short interest, average daily volume, borrow. Broker data access was denied. No order should be sized without it.
7. **The environmental exposure at the gas-generation sites.** Behind-the-meter gas generation is contemplated at Pecos; a satellite emissions check is genuinely applicable to a multi-hundred-megawatt power consumer making environmental claims. It was not run. This is a coverage gap, not a clean result.
8. **The October 23 print date**, which no company release confirms.
