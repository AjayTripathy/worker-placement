# PITCH — AGNT — PROPOSED VERDICT: REJECT — NO POSITION (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION; both benches concur, conviction 8/10 each)

## What we believe

The idea came in as: eXp World Holdings (AGNT) is down 66% from its high on a false "AI will replace real-estate agents" scare, and a +129% adjusted-EBITDA quarter proves the business is fine while the market isn't looking.

After the adversarial review, we believe half of that. The AI scare genuinely is not the story — agent-based brokerage is not dying (industry transactions and per-agent productivity are growing, per the defense bench's figures). But the stock is not mispriced either. It is a fairly priced share of a **deteriorating** franchise: stock compensation to agents runs about $140M a year against roughly $45M of free cash flow, the share count grew 7.1% in twelve months while buybacks stopped, organic agent growth is near zero, agent satisfaction is falling (NPS 77→69), the commission take rate is compressing, and the company actually winning the share war — The Real Brokerage, growing agents 26% a year — is in the process of buying RE/MAX. At $4.13, the benches' math says per-share value creation is approximately **zero**. That is dead money on a worsening trend, and both benches recommend no position.

## What the market believes

Roughly what we concluded — which is precisely the problem for the trade. The Q2 report on August 4 was an EPS miss, not an ignored beat ([company release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/04/3338844/0/en/AGNT-Inc-Reports-Q2-2026-Results.html)); the small post-report bounce (close $4.22) fully retraced to $4.13 within days. The name has live analyst coverage that reacted to the quarter — DA Davidson cut its target to $4.75 on August 6, essentially at the current price. The 66% decline is fully explained by the share war, the dilution, and the widening GAAP losses; no AI narrative is needed. The market's read and the court's read converged.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly, mostly we don't — the review found the market's pricing rational. The residual items the market may underweight:

- **The AI-fear rebuttal held up.** If the stock ever sells off again specifically on an "agents are obsolete" narrative, we have data-backed grounds to treat that as a false scare.
- **RE/MAX integration churn cuts both ways.** ~140,000 RE/MAX agents entering an acquisition integration is recruiting opportunity for eXp — a possible source of upside surprise at the Q3 report.
- **Stock compensation is trending down** ($209M → $140M trailing-twelve-months), and the math turns mechanically favorable at lower prices: the dilution drain shrinks in dollar terms as the price falls, so below roughly $4.10 the annual share issuance costs less than the ~$45M of free cash flow.

## Why it might be priced in

- This is a covered, actively followed stock with live consensus — not an overlooked corner of the market. Analysts saw the quarter and re-marked it to the tape.
- The post-report pop fully retraced, meaning the market digested the "record adjusted EBITDA" headline and correctly weighed it against the EPS miss and the stock-comp add-back.
- The loudest published bear case now targets $4.75 — *above* the current price. There is no visible gap between our corrected view of fair value and where the stock trades.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry**, so these are the reopen bands, built from the benches' own math:

- **No entry near the current price at any point.** Fair value per the corrected math is roughly $4.10–$4.75 (per-share value creation ≈ zero at $4.13; the most bearish published analyst target is $4.75). There is nothing to buy in that band.
- **Price reopen: below ~$3.30–$3.50** — a decisive break of the $3.685 52-week low. At those prices the annual dilution drain (~11M new shares × price ≈ $36–39M) falls clearly below the ~$45M of estimated free cash flow, so per-share economics turn positive on the bear bench's own numbers. A break there triggers a **new court run, not an automatic buy** — the deteriorating-trend objection must still be answered.
- **Fundamental reopen — any one of these, checked at the Q3 report (~Nov 5, date unconfirmed):** organic agent additions above 1,000/quarter; stock compensation guided below $100M/year; the buyback restarted; or visible evidence that RE/MAX integration churn is sending agents to eXp.
- **Near-term calendar:** Aug 14 — Real Brokerage shareholder vote on the RE/MAX deal; H2'26 — deal close. Either event materially shifting the agent-migration picture is grounds to re-look early.
- **Sizing if ever reopened:** start small (starter-size at most). Both benches landed 8/10 against; any future long begins as a probe, not a full position.
