# PITCH — LRN — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: REJECT — no position in Stride (LRN) before the late-October earnings report. The stock is genuinely cheap, but every bound we tried to put on the fraud/accounting tail failed verification, and the decisive variable (fall enrollment) is currently invisible to us. Re-open only if the three verification gates below clear.**

Both review teams reached the same answer independently: the skeptic recommended rejection (conviction 7/10) and the auditing bench sustained it after checking every finding (conviction 8/10), correcting errors in both directions along the way.

## What we believe

Stride runs online public schools (K12) and career-learning programs for school districts. The stock has been cut in half from its 52-week high of $171 to $83.36, largely on a fraud scandal: a New Mexico school district — Gallup-McKinley County Schools, one of Stride's own paying customers — filed a complaint in September 2025 alleging Stride inflated enrollment counts ("ghost students") to collect per-pupil state funding. Shareholder class actions followed.

On the numbers alone, the business looks like a bargain. Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) showed GAAP operating income of $450.8M and net income of $338.2M against a fully-diluted market cap of about $4.08B — roughly 12× earnings, closer to 6× operating profit after adjusting for the cash position, with no meaningful debt. Last year's one-time impairment did not repeat. That cheapness is real and both review teams confirmed it from the company's own press release ([FY26 results](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/04/3338841/0/en/Stride-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2026-financial-results.html)).

But we do not believe the stock is buyable today, because cheapness only carries the day if the worst-case scenarios can be bounded — and we could not bound them. Specifically:

- **The accusing party is a customer, not just shareholders.** A customer suing you for fraud over the exact metric that drives your revenue (enrollment) is a different and worse animal than a securities suit. Worse, we found the district had already **terminated its contract effective June 30, 2025** — before it sued.
- **The company has a documented history of internal-controls failure.** Its prior auditor (BDO) issued an *adverse* opinion on internal controls in 2023 — failures specifically in IT general controls (user access, program changes, data processing), which is exactly the surface area an enrollment-count fraud allegation touches. The material-weakness era runs right up to the start of the alleged class period. Primary source: the [auditor-change 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1157408/000110465924091274/tm2422054d1_8k.htm).
- **The new CEO (five days on the job at the report date) issued no forward guidance at all** — no revenue, profit, or capex outlook for fiscal 2027 — heading into the very enrollment season that decides the year.
- **Not one insider has bought stock in the open market** since the collapse, including the new CEO. All four post-report insider filings were grants and tax-withholding only.
- **The brand-new 10-K still contains "material weakness" language somewhere we haven't been able to read.** Three separate attempts to read the controls section (Item 9A) and the legal-proceedings section (Item 3) failed for technical fetch reasons.

## What the market believes

The market has priced roughly a halving of the equity: down 51% from the high, but also up 37% off the 52-week low of $60.61 — so this is a partially recovered wreck, not a fresh panic. The implied view is something like: "the fraud allegations are serious enough to destroy the growth premium, but the company earns too much real money to trade lower." At 12× trailing GAAP earnings for a company that was recently a growth darling, the market is treating the litigation as survivable and the earnings as mostly durable, while withholding any credit for growth until enrollment and legal clarity arrive.

## Why we might have edge

- **We identified the customer-plaintiff distinction.** Most coverage frames this as a shareholder-suit story. We traced the originating complaint to a paying district partner and — via local reporting the national press missed — found the [contract termination](https://www.cibolacitizen.com/news/gallup-mckinley-county-schools-terminates-contract-stridek12-citing-severe-academic-and-legal) preceded the complaint. That sequencing (customer quietly terminates, *then* sues) is a higher-signal fraud pattern than litigation alone, and we are cataloging it as a reusable detector.
- **We read the primary documents where secondary coverage got them wrong** — decoding all four insider filings, pulling the adverse internal-controls opinion from the actual 8-K, and re-verifying the earnings figures from the company's own release rather than transcripts.
- **We know exactly what would change the answer**, which is itself an edge: a clean controls opinion in the 10-K, a genuine open-market insider buy, or evidence that teacher hiring (a leading indicator of enrollment) is holding up year-over-year. Nobody clears those gates by accident; we can watch for them deliberately.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, much of the bear case may already be in the price. The stock has already fallen by half; the fraud allegations, the CEO change, and the guidance withdrawal are all public. The contract termination we "found" is realized history — that district's revenue loss is already inside the fiscal 2026 results we called cheap, not a pending shock. If October brings a clean controls opinion, stable enrollment, and reinstated guidance, today's $83 will have been the entry, and our caution will have cost us the discount. We accept that risk explicitly: the review found the situation *unverifiable*, not *doomed*, and unverifiable at 12× earnings is a closer call than the verdict's confidence might suggest.
