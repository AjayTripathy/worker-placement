## What we believe

Victoria's Secret sells underwear, sleepwear, and beauty products through about 1,300 stores and a large online business. It imports nearly all of it, mostly from Southeast Asia, so tariffs land directly on its cost of goods.

On September 3rd the stock fell about 12.4% and now sits at $73.49, down 28% from its 52-week high of $102.39 — though still up 194% from its $25 low.

We believe the drop was driven by a misreading of a tariff change. The company's own quarterly filing says a 10% across-the-board tariff (Section 122) **started February 24, 2026 and expired July 24, 2026** — the same day a narrower 10–12.5% tariff (Section 301) began. That is a swap, not a new cost stacked on top of an old one. The quarter that just reported carried the full 10% for its entire span and still produced $124M of adjusted operating income and a 312-basis-point gross-margin improvement. ([10-Q, filed 2026-09-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1856437/000185643726000021/vsco-20260801.htm))

We also believe this company has already proven it can absorb tariffs. The annual report states that tariffs, after mitigation, cost about **$85 million** of operating income in fiscal 2025 — and the company still grew operating income from $373M to $403M that year. ([FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1856437/000185643726000004/vsco-20260131.htm))

**But we do not believe this is a buy at $73.49.** When we put probabilities and prices on the outcomes (below), the average outcome is *lower* than today's price. The thesis is real; the entry price is not there yet.

## What the market believes

The market believes the July tariff is a new, permanent cost. The 12.4% drop erased roughly $770M of enterprise value. Capitalized at this stock's own 11.5x operating-income multiple, that implies the market is pricing about **$67M of permanent annual operating-income damage** — against a company that quantified only $19M of damage for a six-month stub period.

The market also has almost nothing to disagree with us about on the near term. Analysts' third-quarter estimate ($0.05 per share) is exactly the company's own guidance, and their full-year estimate ($4.71) is slightly *above* the top of the company's own range ($4.45–$4.70). On every period anyone has published a number for, we and the street agree.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The substitution fact.** The expiry date of the old tariff and the start date of the new one are the same day, and that fact is stated in plain text inside the 10-Q. It is not in any headline. If the true cost step is the *difference* between two regimes rather than the full new rate, the permanent damage is a fraction of what the tape priced.
2. **A documented mitigation record.** The company has told us what tariffs cost it before ($85M, net of mitigation) and grew through it. Most tariff-shock selloffs happen in names that have never given you that number.
3. **The fourth quarter is not the stretch it looks like.** Backing into it: $80M (Q1) + $124M (Q2) + $15M (Q3 guide midpoint) = $219M against a $575M full-year midpoint, so Q4 must deliver $356M. That is 62% of the year — but last year's Q4 was 78% of that year. Concentration is *falling*, not rising. And the implied incremental margin (34%) matches what the first half actually delivered (33%).

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: it substantially is, and this is the strongest argument against us.

- **We have no number that differs from the street on any period we can check.** Our edge is entirely about fiscal 2027, and neither bench produced a street fiscal-2027 estimate to differ *from*. We are asserting divergence we have not measured.
- **The growth we're pointing at is flattered.** Fiscal 2025's $403M included $85M of tariff drag. Add it back and the baseline is ~$488M, so the $575M guide is +18% growth, not +43%. The "explosive growth at 11.5x" framing is inflated by roughly 25 percentage points.
- **The cash-flow strength is partly a refund, not operations.** Cash went from $188M to $522M, but the quarter included a $135.8M tariff refund, and the filing describes the $148M total refund as ">95% of total IEEPA tariffs paid." That is a one-time recovery being read as earnings power.
- **The stock is up 194% from its low.** This is not an undiscovered situation; it is a crowded, well-covered $6B consumer name that already ran.
- **It was not a beat-and-raise on the top line.** Revenue was $1.61B against ~$1.62B expected. Small, but the marketing of this thesis rests on the earnings line while sales missed.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The tariff base is broad and fiscal 2027 has a real hole. (Has a tripwire.)**
The substitution argument only holds if Section 301's covered origins are no wider than the expired global 10%. If Section 301 sweeps in origins the old regime effectively priced differently, or the mitigation runway is exhausted, fiscal 2027 operating income lands at $325–425M instead of ~$600M. On the company's own earnings math that is roughly $2.50–$3.30 per share, and at 12x the stock is $30–40. **The 12.4% drop prices almost none of that.** The downside here is not symmetric with the upside — it is roughly three times larger.
*Tripwire:* the tariff footnote in the Q3 filing (early December) or the annual report (~March 2027) showing 301 scope broader than the expired 122 base; any company-quantified annual tariff figure above $110M.

**Failure narrative 2 — Demand rolls over and nobody warned us. (NO monitorable tripwire.)**
This is the one we cannot watch. Our only independent demand instrument, the consumer-heat detector, read **DECAYING, 13/100** over May–September — a window that overlaps a quarter which printed +9% comparable sales and +20% international. The detector disagreed with audited results on its own observation window, so we cannot use it: it is not a bear signal, it is a broken gauge. Our backup channel hit a bot wall, the app-review pull never ran, and the social-velocity poll never ran. So if the Victoria's Secret brand's cultural momentum inflects during the August–October quarter, **we will find out on December 3rd along with everyone else, at whatever price that print sets.**
*Unknown-unknown class: **sensor-invalidated latent demand** — we hold an instrument that has been empirically shown to be miscalibrated on this issuer, and we have no validated substitute. The danger is not that the gauge reads wrong; it is that we might have believed it.*

**Failure narrative 3 — The reflexive and structural leg: we are the buyer for a seller we can no longer see.**
The largest strategic holder, BBRC International, sold 1,364,570 shares between June 26 and July 28 at $88.50–$90.69 — five weeks before the print — and stopped at exactly **9.8%** ownership. That is one tick below the 10% threshold that requires insider transaction reports. **From here, BBRC can sell its remaining 7,810,631 shares (about 10% of the company) and we will not see a single filing until it crosses a 1% step on a Schedule 13D amendment.** Buying here means volunteering as the natural counterparty to an informed seller whose flow is now invisible.

Our own size is not the reflexive problem. At a 0.50–0.75% weight on this book, the position is a rounding error against this name's daily volume — impact is nil, and the "don't bid into your own predicted flow" concern does not bind. The doctrine that *does* bind is the tax calendar: 2026 is a loss-harvesting year, which (a) rules out any option expiry past December 31 for the losing branch, and (b) requires a wash-sale check against the Parametric separately-managed sleeve before any purchase — a check that has not been run.

Note also that the company's own repurchase program bought at a $45.27 year-to-date average with $150M of authorization sitting idle at $73.49. The company was a $45 bidder; the strategic holder was an $89 seller. Both bracket us, and neither is buying here.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the Section 301 tariff base turns out to be broader than the expired global 10% it replaced, so the "substitution not addition" insight is simply false — and we discover it in a filing while absorbing the last of an anchor holder's invisible 7.8-million-share exit.**
