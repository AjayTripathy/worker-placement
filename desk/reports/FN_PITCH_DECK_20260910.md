# PITCH — FN — VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Fabrinet (FN, CIK 1408710): classification survives, the position is small and *not yet live* — no entry at the current tape of $405.13; first buy only at $390 or lower, target size half a normal starter (1.0% of book), staged in three pieces.

---

## What we believe

Fabrinet builds optical modules and assemblies for other people's brands out of Thailand. FY26 (ended 2026-06-26) was a very good operating year that the stock hated: revenue about $4.64B (+36%), Q4 revenue $1,315.8M (+44.6% year over year), GAAP net income $473.0M, and a Q1-FY27 guide of $1.375–1.425B revenue with non-GAAP EPS of $4.10–4.25 (8-K Ex-99.1, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001408710/000140871026000026/fn-2026811xex991q426.htm; 10-K https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001408710/000140871026000028/fn-20260626.htm).

The stock is down 45.9% from its 52-week high and traded at $557 after hours on 2026-08-17 before falling to $405.13. Nothing in the filings looks like fraud, restatement risk, or a broken balance sheet: net cash is $875.1M (about $24/share), the auditor is Big-4, the company has been listed ~20 years, and hiring is *accelerating* into the quarter that closes 2026-09-25 (25 job postings in September vs 6–7/month before). So our belief is narrow and honest: **this is a real business having a real de-rating, not a blow-up.** At a low enough price that de-rating is worth owning in small size.

We also believe the two things the bear case got right, and we are not arguing them away:
- Cash conversion is thin. FY26 operating cash flow was $256.7M (down 22%) while pretax income rose 56%; capex was $252.5M, so free cash flow for the whole year was about **+$4.2M** on $473M of net income (0.54 of net income turned into operating cash).
- The most famous customer got smaller in dollars. NVIDIA went from 27.6% of $3.42B (~$944M) to 16% of $4.64B (~$742M) — about **−21% in dollars** while total revenue grew 36%. Growth came from Cisco (20%), Nokia (11%) and Amazon (11%).

## What the market believes

Two beliefs are fighting.

The **sell-side** believes it's a buy: 7 analysts, consensus "Buy," average target $513.43 with a range of $234–700 (https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fn/forecast/). Even the post-print cut (B. Riley to $598 from $635, Neutral) sits far above the tape.

The **actual buyers and sellers** believe something else. They sold a beat 13.5% lower on the Q3 print, sold another beat 19.4% lower on the Q4 print (gap from a $598.58 close to a $513.70 open), and have kept selling another ~16% in the three weeks since. That is the market saying: *we will not pay 29–31x GAAP earnings for growth that is financed by the company's own receivables and inventory, at a 12%-gross-margin assembler whose biggest AI name is shrinking.* At $405.13 the stock is 31.0x trailing FY26 GAAP EPS ($13.05), 29.2x the annualized GAAP guide ($13.86), and 24.3x the annualized non-GAAP guide — the "24x" everyone quotes is the friendliest of the three, and it excludes about $32M/year of stock comp.

Where price and targets disagree by 27%, the price is the position that costs money to hold. We side with price on timing and with the targets only on direction.

## Why we might have edge

Modest, and we will state it plainly rather than dress it up:

1. **Two decompositions neither the coverage nor the targets carry.** Full-year cash conversion of 0.54 (coverage focused on the −$37M *quarterly* free cash flow), and the −21% dollar decline at NVIDIA that is hidden by percentage-of-revenue disclosure plus a bigger denominator. The second is our best original fact in this whole court.
2. **A tax step that is already booked, not looming.** Effective tax rate went 6.4% → 14.8% ($82.1M vs $22.7M). That $59.4M step alone explains about **83%** of the $71.7M drop in operating cash flow. Anyone treating FY27 as "tax risk ahead" is double-counting a change that is already in the run-rate and already inside the Q1 guide (GAAP mid $3.465 vs non-GAAP mid $4.175).
3. **Working-capital intensity actually improved.** Days-sales-outstanding fell from 75.9 to 70.5 days. Receivables grew 34% against 45% Q4 revenue growth. The scary $1.02B receivable is a growth artifact more than a collections problem — which is the opposite of the story the tape is telling.
4. **Disclosed capacity.** Management says the Thailand and California expansions support long-term revenue capacity of $12.5–14B. That reframes capex at 5.4% of sales as bought capacity rather than maintenance leakage.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it probably is, and this is the strongest argument against the trade:

- The full-year $4M free cash flow number **was** in the earnings-call coverage. Our "novel" kill is really a consensus kill with better arithmetic.
- The direction of the Q1 margin step was widely noted. Our contribution is only the size (−49bp of non-GAAP net margin, guide mid vs Q4 actual) — and even that partly re-counts the tax step.
- The street's own next-quarter estimate ($3.93 EPS / $1.31B revenue, https://www.chartmill.com/stock/quote/FN/analyst-ratings) sits *below* the company's guide. Either the aggregation is stale (likely, and unverified) or the street already discounts management. Either way we would be buying with the company's number, not a number the market hasn't seen.
- Our EV work below lands at about **$390** against a $405.13 tape. That is not edge. That is roughly fair-to-slightly-rich, which is exactly why this deck proposes no immediate buy.

## What could go wrong (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

**Failure narrative 1 — Silent design displacement at the marquee account. (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)**
NVIDIA's dollars fell 21% while the story got louder. If that is displacement — insourcing, a second source, or a platform transition that skips Fabrinet — the decision was made 2–4 quarters before it will ever appear in a filing, and it will appear as a percentage that rounds, not as an 8-K. There is no sensor for this. Customs data returned **zero consignees**; the customer_id connector's posterior was None; the company names no programs. We cannot build a tripwire for a private roadmap decision at a counterparty who owes us no disclosure.
*Unknown-unknown class named:* **counterparty-private allocation decisions** — facts that exist, are decision-relevant, are held by a third party under NDA, and become public only after the price has moved. Adjacent class: **rounded-disclosure blindness** (a 16% line can hide a $46M swing either way; our own estimate is only robust in sign, −19% to −24%).

**Failure narrative 2 — The accrual de-rate repeats, and 20x turns out to be the ceiling, not the floor.**
Our own knowledge-graph candidate (`accrual_financed_growth_derate`) says a low-margin pass-through manufacturer whose growth is financed off its own balance sheet de-rates on consecutive prints even when it beats on EPS. FN has now done that twice. If the November 10-Q shows receivables and inventory building again with operating cash flow still under 0.7 of net income, the same mechanism fires a third time and the bear fair-value band of $330–390 becomes the new ceiling. *Tripwire exists:* 10-Q cash-flow statement, DSO series, and the price gates below.

**Failure narrative 3 — We buy the level and the level was a rail artifact.**
Our entry math leans on "16% off the 52-week low." One bench used $408.375 and 16.6% off the low; the other used $404.73 and 15.6%; the pack says $405.13 and 15.7%. And a secondary source (Macrotrends) shows a 52-week low of **$272.49** instead of the pack's $350.10. If the wider window is the true one, then "near the low" is wrong by 22% and every band in this deck is anchored to a bad number. *Tripwire exists:* pull exchange bars from IBKR before the first limit order goes live. This is cheap to fix and has not been fixed, which is embarrassing rather than clever.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
Our own size is not the problem. Estimated average daily volume is 0.9–1.6M shares (~$365–650M/day; estimate, not measured — see unverified #7). A full 1.0%-of-book position on a $50M book is ~1,200 shares, roughly 0.1% of a day — we are invisible and we should say so instead of pretending to worry about our footprint. The structural risk is *other people's* flow: sell-side targets are 27% above the tape, which means the pending flow is **target cuts**, not upgrades, and each cut lands as a headline into a tape already 46% off its high. Momentum and trend books have been forced sellers through two prints; passive/index flow around the name is unmeasured in this pack (unverified #7). Doctrine interactions we are choosing to live with: (a) **divergence-not-coverage** overturned the bear "this is consensus" kill, but the overturn rests on the *assumption* that the $3.93 estimate is stale — if it is actually a live post-guide number, the kill comes back and our edge goes to zero; (b) our buy zone of $350–390 sits exactly inside the zone where our own de-rate detector is still firing — we would be buying into our own sensor's fire zone, which is only defensible in small size and in tranches; (c) print proximity is ~36 trading days, outside the 5-day window, so no print-decisive reconstruction is required — but that also means the resolving event is far enough away for two more months of drift.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that NVIDIA's 21% dollar decline was displacement rather than program timing — a fact held privately at the customer, invisible in percentage-of-revenue disclosure, and untrackable by any sensor we own — compounded by a third cash-conversion de-rate that takes the multiple below the $330–390 fair-value band we used to justify the entry.

**Read:** our own scenario table only justifies paying about **20% implied vol** for upside calls, while the realized-vol proxy from the tape is **46–61%** (Parkinson estimator on the 52-week range; 46% using the pack's $350.10 low, 61% using the disputed $272.49 low). Any real chain will be *multiples* of what our table says the upside is worth. **Therefore: do not express this in long calls.** The reason is not that vol is expensive in the abstract — it is that our table's mean sits on top of spot, so we are not paying for edge, we are paying for dispersion we already said we can't call.

The same arithmetic routes gated adds to the **premium-selling gate**: σ_table scaled to a mid-December expiry (~0.27yr) is ~17% of spot, while a 45–60% chain IV implies 23–31%. Selling a cash-secured put at the $375–390 strike gets us paid to wait at exactly the level Tranche 1 wants to own. **Conditions before any such order:** (i) real chain-level midpoint IV and a live bid at that strike — never the underlying's aggregate IV feed, which is the mistake that showed 71 vs 101 on TSSI; (ii) put strike ≤ $390 so assignment lands inside the fair-value band; (iii) size the cash-secured put as part of the 1.0% cap, not on top of it.

**Honest counter to our own recommendation:** σ_table of 32% is well below the 46–61% realized proxy, which means our five scenarios are probably **under-dispersed**. If true vol is higher than our table, then selling puts under-prices the assignment tail in the very branch we fear most (displacement, $180). That argues for the smallest expression that works — plain stock on limit orders — and for treating the cash-secured put as an alternative to Tranche 1, never as leverage on top of it.

**TAX overlay.** In a harvest year the loss branch must land before 2026-12-31. A January or February 2027 expiry defers the loss into next year and is therefore **excluded** — the edge here (a few points of premium) does not numerically dominate the deferral cost. So: if premium is sold at all, it is a **December-2026 expiry or earlier**. Any 12-month structure priced above is illustrative arithmetic for the vol comparison only and is **not** approved during a harvest year. Plain stock has no expiry problem and can be harvested at any point before year-end, which is a second, independent reason the instrument output is **stock on limit**.

**Instrument recommendation (the output of the arithmetic above): STOCK, on resting limit orders at $390 / $350. No long calls (our table pays ~20% IV against 46–61% realized).
