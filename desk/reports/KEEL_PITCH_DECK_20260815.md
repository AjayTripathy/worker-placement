# PITCH — KEEL — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION): DO NOT BUY — stay flat; the stock at $3.31 already prices more megawatts than the company's own SEC filings disclose; re-open only on the specific filing triggers and price band below.

## What we believe

Keel Infrastructure Corp. (formerly a bitcoin miner; it acquired Stronghold Digital Mining in March 2025) is trying to convert power sites into AI/high-performance-computing datacenters. The stock is at $3.31, down 55% from its 52-week high of $7.37, but still nearly triple its 52-week low of $1.18.

Our conclusion after a two-sided internal review: **the equity today is essentially a lottery ticket on utility interconnection *applications*, not on assets the company actually controls.** The company's press release markets an "850 MW" near-term story and a "2.2 gigawatt development pipeline." Its own 10-Q — the legally vetted document — discloses only about **251 MW** of site capacity (Panther Creek ~60 MW, Scrubgrass ~63 MW, Sharon 110 MW planned, Washington State 18 MW), plus a further **96 MW at Sherbrooke that transfers only if conditions are met** (disclosed in the 8-K, not the 10-Q). The gap between what is marketed and what is filed is the central finding, not a footnote.

We also believe the balance sheet is weaker than the bull story admits: **$1.046 billion of convertible notes** ($588M at 1.375% due January 2031, converting around $6.86; $458M at 1.25% due January 2032, converting around $7.41) against $769M of cash including restricted amounts. Both conversion prices are more than double today's stock price, so this is real debt, not quasi-equity. The company burned $117.6M of operating cash in the first half of 2026; its cash funds roughly 70–90 MW of datacenter construction — a fraction of even the vetted site list.

Working entirely from the company's filed numbers, if every disclosed megawatt were fully financed, built, and leased at the **top** of the plausible rent range ($170 per kilowatt-month), the shares would be worth about **$4.84** — only +46% from here, before any execution risk. At a mid-range rent ($140), the vetted assets support roughly **$2.14–$3.04** per share, i.e., at or below today's price. Our internal bar for this kind of speculative name (+50% upside on the vetted record) does not clear at any reasonable assumption.

## What the market believes

At $3.31 the market is valuing Keel at roughly $2.0–2.2 billion of enterprise value on **zero datacenter revenue**. That valuation only makes sense if you credit a large part of the marketed 850 MW / 2.2 GW pipeline — capacity that exists today mostly as applications in utility interconnection queues, not as signed agreements or filed per-site disclosures. In other words, the market is already paying for a substantial probability that the applications convert into real, leased datacenters.

There are genuine bullish signals the market may be reading: the CEO (Gagnon) made an open-market purchase of 58,888 shares at ~$3.33 on August 13 (small in dollars, ~$196k, but +4.6% of his direct stake); there is a real wave of construction-related job postings near the Pennsylvania sites; the company says three priority sites are "nearing full permitting with multiple prospective tenants negotiating for each one"; and a new 5%+ holder filed a 13G on July 29. None of these change the arithmetic above, but they explain why the stock holds a multi-billion valuation pre-revenue.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the primary documents; much of the market is trading the press release.** The 10-Q per-site megawatt disclosure (the ~251 MW total) directly contradicts the marketed aggregate, and we verified this from the SEC filing itself: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1812477/000181247726000023/keel-20260630.htm — both of our independent review teams read it verbatim.
- **We did the valuation from the filed record, both directions.** Our upside table assumes perfect financing, perfect construction cost ($9M/MW), full lease-up, and a generous capitalization rate — and it *still* fails our bar. That is a strong result: the failure isn't assumption-sensitive at the top of the range.
- **We caught the debt understatement.** The original pitch cited "$458M of converts." That was half the stack; the true figure is $1.046B across two note series. This is exactly the kind of error the market's casual bulls are likely repeating.
- **We identified an earlier trigger than the obvious one.** Everyone waits for a signed anchor lease. The company's own language says permitting completes *first* — a permit-completion 8-K is the earlier, cheaper signal, and we can watch for it.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the symmetric case:

- **The bear case may itself be consensus.** The stock is already down 55% from its high; a 2x-out-of-the-money convert stack and pre-revenue status are visible to anyone. Keel carries multi-analyst coverage (count unverified — a gap on our side), so this is not an undiscovered name where our reading is unique.
- **The 10-Q's silence on pipeline MW may be conservatism, not concealment.** Vetted filings often disclose only current assets; development pipelines legitimately live in investor materials. Our review explicitly tested this ("is the divergence apples-to-oranges?") and judged the venue choice itself informative — but a signed lease or approved interconnection would collapse this distinction overnight, in the bulls' favor.
- **The genuinely adverse cluster is real:** CEO buying, hiring waves, "nearing full permitting," and a new 5% holder all arrived in the last six weeks. If permits complete and one 100+ MW lease signs at $140+/kW-month, our own math says the value can clear our bar — this is why we set tight triggers rather than discarding the name.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry today.** Both teams agree: flat, no starter position. The re-open conditions, taken from the benches' own math:

**Re-open on price (dislocation):** below **~$2.15**, the stock would trade at or under the fully-funded, fully-leased value of the *unconditional* filed assets (251 MW at $140/kW-month rent = $2.14/share) — meaning the entire application pipeline comes free. At that level, rebuild the valuation table and consider a starter; do not buy mechanically, because the convert stack ($1.046B) and cash burn can make cheap cheaper.

**Re-open on filings (any one of these, then re-run the valuation table before sizing):**
1. **Permit-completion 8-K** on any of the three priority sites — the earliest clock, per the company's own "nearing full permitting" language.
2. **Signed anchor lease of ≥100 MW with an investment-grade tenant at ≥$140/kW-month** — the benches' math says the value can then clear +50%: at $140 rent the record needs ~590 MW leased, at $170 it needs ~360 MW, so one large lease plus visible pipeline conversion is the shape that works.
3. **Any 10-Q/10-K that moves pipeline megawatts into per-site disclosed capacity** (e.g., Panther Creek 350 MW or Scrubgrass expansion appearing in the vetted document rather than the press release).

**Explicitly not an entry trigger:** buying *after* a lease announcement at whatever price it gaps to — the first-pass team's warning stands that a 60–100 MW lease resolves into a price that already assumed it. The lease trigger is a signal to re-run the math at the then-current price, not to chase.

**Sizing if re-opened:** speculative-bucket sizing only (the convert stack, burn rate, and share creep cap this as a small position regardless of trigger quality).

**Catalyst dates:** next earnings ~**2026-11-09** (derived from a data vendor, **not confirmed** by any company announcement); the decisive events — permit and lease 8-Ks — are unscheduled.
