## What we believe

Biohaven sold most of the economics of its lead epilepsy drug (azetukalner / BHV-7000) to SK Biopharmaceuticals for a $400M non-refundable upfront, kept a big royalty tail, and then — five days before the deal's closing paperwork cleared — the FDA put a partial clinical hold on new enrollment because of a metabolite seen in rodents. The stock fell about $2.20/share (~$332M). The filed 8-K says the hold stops new enrollment only; dosing continues for the >600 patients already randomized, and the fully-enrolled 303 trial still reads out in 2H26.

What we actually believe, stated narrowly:

1. **The original in-house thesis ("the damage is absent because the $350M is contractually safe") is dead.** Biohaven kept tiered royalties in the mid-teens to low-twenties on U.S. sales, mid-single-digits ex-U.S., plus up to $150M in milestones. That retained strip is the majority of the drug's value to Biohaven, and a clinical hold damages it directly. Both benches agree. So do we.
2. **But the opposite claim — "the tape is fully rational" — is also unproven.** The red bench's showcase kill was that a 15-point cut in probability-of-success explains the whole drawdown. On its own table that arithmetic is wrong: the strip moves about $0.0515/share per point of probability at a $1.0B U.S. peak, so 15 points is **$0.77/share**, not $2.30. Reproducing the full $2.30 needs a ~45-point collapse. So probability-repricing explains roughly $0.8–$1.5 of a $2.20 move — a third to two-thirds. The rest is unexplained.
3. **The binding question is one neither bench answered.** Add up what we can value: pro-forma cash after the upfront, plus the retained royalty strip. That is $3.45–$5.51/share on a net-of-debt basis, $5.17–$7.23/share on a gross-cash basis. The stock is $12.88. Everything above roughly $7 is the rest of the pipeline — BHV-1300, BHV-1400, taldefgrobep, BHV-8100, the antibody-drug conjugates — which **no one in this process priced**. To make this a +50% trade you need that pipeline to be worth $12–14/share, or about $1.9B, against roughly $77M of direct spend on it per half-year.
4. **Our honest scenario math says the stock is worth about $10.30 against a $12.88 tape.** That is a ~20% overvaluation, not a short (the fail case is only $2.50 and the squeeze case is $19), and not a buy.

Conclusion: **interesting, cheap-ish only at much lower prices, and gated.** We wait.

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we do about the drug and more than we do about everything else.

- Sell-side is split wide: RBC pulled its $19 target to Sector Perform, H.C. Wainwright is at $10, Raymond James cut $50 → $30. Bank of America went to Underperform with an $11 target on **2026-06-29 — before the hold** — on the competitive question versus Xenon's rival Kv7 drug. So today's $12.88 sits right on top of a bear target that predates the bad news.
- The tape is **down 30.5% from the 52-week high of $18.53 but still up 72% from the $7.49 low**. This is give-back from a double, not a fresh dislocation.
- Short interest is about 24.9M shares, roughly 16.5% of shares outstanding. The market is positioned for more bad news, which cuts both ways: a clean metabolite package into that positioning is a squeeze setup.
- Press coverage (BioSpace, BioPharma Dive, Investing.com within 24 hours) converged on one read: the second pivotal trial probably also needs to read out positively for approval, so the hold matters more than "enrollment only" sounds. Some reporting also notes the Street read SK Biopharma's willingness to sign — after being shown the metabolite data — as a sign SK was comfortable with the issue.

Net: the market prices a damaged but funded company whose remaining pipeline is worth something material. We can't verify that last part.

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the debt work.** The $250M Oberland note carries a covenant: if the FY2026 audit (due 2027-03-01) comes with going-concern language, that is a delivery-covenant breach, and holders can put the notes back at 120%. Shareholders' equity was $12.2M at 6/30/26. That makes the $350M not upside but the thing that keeps the auditor quiet. Most coverage treats the cash as a windfall.
- **We found the dead financing option.** The additional $150M Oberland tranche was conditioned on FDA approval of troriluzole; troriluzole got a complete response letter in November 2025. That tranche is gone. Both the original brief and the red bench missed it.
- **We separated "upfront" from "at risk."** The $50M second payment is part of the same "non-creditable and non-refundable upfront fee of $400 million" sentence — it belongs in cash (+$0.33/share), not in the risked royalty strip. Anyone who haircuts it is double-counting the damage.
- **We fixed our own burn number twice.** The brief said $77M/quarter off a balance change. The red bench said $117.5M/quarter off a half-year average. Differencing the Q1 filing gives Q2 operating cash burn of **$85.1M** with only $3.8M of financing in the quarter — the ATM money ($178.9M) landed almost entirely in Q1. Runway is ~3.2 quarters standalone and ~6.9 gross / ~3.3 net-of-debt pro-forma. Both prior numbers were wrong; the direction of the error mattered for sizing.
- **We know exactly what we don't know** and we've written it down as a drain queue rather than hiding it in prose.

## Why it might be priced in

Plainly: most of it probably is.

- The hold is public, the partial-hold mechanics are in the filed 8-K, and three outlets carried the "the other trial has to work too" point within a day. There is no informational asymmetry on the clinical fact.
- The royalty strip is disclosed with its ranges. Anyone can build the same grid we built.
- The $11 bear target existed before the hold, and the stock is at $12.88. The bear case has already been published and partially paid for.
- The 16.5% short interest means the skeptical view is not just known, it's crowded — which usually means the easy money on that side is gone.
- Our one genuinely differentiated finding (the dead $150M tranche + the going-concern tripwire) is a **sizing** input, not a direction input. It tells us to be small; it does not tell us the stock is wrong.
- And our "edge" on valuation is really an admission: we can't value the majority of what the market is paying for. That is not edge. That is a hole.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy the relief and the relief is the top. (Tripwire: YES)**
The metabolite package comes back rodent-specific, the hold lifts, HSR clears, $350M lands, and 16.5% short interest covers into it. The stock gaps from ~$11 to ~$16. Our own gates — "enter only after the events go green" — fire us into that gap. Then RISE-3 topline misses, or shows a thin effect against a rival Kv7 compound, and the strip goes to near-zero: cash plus residual pipeline, ~$6.50. We'd have bought the best news at the worst price and eaten a 60% drawdown. *Monitorable via: 8-K hold-lift, 8-K closing, RISE-3 topline.* **This is the single most likely way we lose.**

**Failure narrative 2 — The metabolite is a chemotype problem, not a compound problem. (Tripwire: PARTIAL)**
If the offending metabolite is genotoxic and structural to the Kv7 scaffold, this stops being a delay and becomes a program kill. The pre-closing exit then runs not through the safety-termination clause (which is post-closing — the original brief litigated the wrong paragraph) but through closing conditions and the bring-down: the deal is "subject to obtaining required antitrust clearances and the satisfaction or waiver of other customary closing conditions," and the material-adverse-effect carve-outs are macro/industry/law/war/projections — **asset-specific FDA action is not carved out**. Against that, the 8-K states all clinical and nonclinical data including metabolite work were shown to SK before signing, which is the classic known-at-signing exclusion. We can see a termination 8-K when it happens; we cannot see it coming, and the Outside Date is redacted. If the deal dies, the $350M never arrives, runway is 3.2 quarters, and the going-concern covenant trips the Oberland put at 120%. Downside ~$2.50.

**Failure narrative 3 — The roof was never there. (Tripwire: NONE) — unknown-unknown class: UNPRICED RESIDUAL (model-omission risk)**
The $5.70–$9.40/share the tape assigns to the non-Kv7 pipeline is simply wrong, and we find out the way these things are always found out: slowly, through quiet deprioritizations, a program dropped from a corporate deck, an enrollment page that stops updating, a partner that doesn't come. **There is no filing, no price level, no 8-K, no sensor that prints "the pipeline is worth $2, not $9."** This is the failure mode we cannot arm a tripwire against, because the quantity is not disclosed, not measured, and not adjudicated anywhere. Its class is the unpriced residual — the part of the market cap that exists only because no one subtracted it. A second, related unknown-unknown class sits alongside it: **private-counterparty intent** — what SK Biopharma actually concluded internally about the metabolite is knowable to exactly one party, and it is not us.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
Our own footprint is not the problem. A quarter-unit is 0.25% of book; even at the small end of a plausible 2–4M share average daily volume (~$25–50M notional), we are well under 1% of a day. We cannot move this tape and we should not pretend otherwise. The reflexivity that *does* bite us is doctrinal, not mechanical:

- **Our gating doctrine is procyclical here.** Requiring the hold-lift 8-K and the closing 8-K before entry means we structurally buy after the squeeze has already fired. Against 16.5% short interest, the gate is not free — it is a known, repeated cost, and it is exactly failure narrative 1.
- **Predicted flow runs against us on the good news and with us on the bad.** Short covering supplies the pop we'd be buying into; on the bad branch, index and passive holders don't sell fast, so the fail case grinds rather than gaps, giving us time we don't want to use.
- **Doctrine interaction:** our "no-entry above fair value" rule and our "gate on catalysts" rule point in opposite directions at the same moment. The moment the gates go green is the moment the price is furthest above the funded value. We resolve this explicitly below by making the event gates permit entry only *under a price ceiling*, never at any price.
- **Crowding interacts with the covenant.** If the deal slips past the 2027-03-01 audit deadline, a going-concern paragraph plus 16.5% short interest plus an at-the-market program is a reflexive down-spiral: the equity issuance that fixes the covenant is priced off a tape the covenant broke.
