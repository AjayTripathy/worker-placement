# PITCH — CWCO — VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION — do not buy here: no position at $29.75; reopen only at ≤$25 or on a named trigger below.**

---

## What we believe

Consolidated Water is a good, boring business trading at a price that already assumes it is a good, boring business. It runs seawater-desalination plants and a regulated retail water utility on Grand Cayman, plus water plants in the Bahamas, a US engineering/manufacturing arm (Aerex, Fort Pierce FL), and a services book. It carries $132.6M of cash against no meaningful debt — about $8.11 per share of the $29.75 price — and it pays a dividend.

What we believe, specifically and narrowly:

1. **The nine-year Cayman license overhang is genuinely resolved.** On 2026-06-24 the company signed a 25-year exclusive retail license, effective 2026-08-01, with tiered tariffs and an annual adjustment each July 1. That is a real reduction in the range of outcomes for the single most important asset.
2. **The price we would pay for that certainty is a permanently lower rate.** The company's own press release illustrated roughly a 6.5% revenue reduction (~$2.1M) on recast historical volumes. So the overhang was removed and the earnings power was cut at the same time — roughly offsetting.
3. **The business is currently earning below its own run-rate, and part of that is temporary.** Aerex (manufacturing) revenue fell 63% in the first half — that segment is project-lumpy and has been much larger before.
4. **Therefore: this is a fair-to-slightly-rich utility, not a dislocation.** We want to own it, but at $25 or lower, not at $29.75.

**The original thesis — that a −24.6% drawdown represented the market ignoring good news — is dead.** Both benches killed it, and we are reporting that plainly rather than dressing it up.

## What the market believes

The market believes almost exactly what we believe, which is the core problem.

There is **one** covering analyst. That analyst models FY26 revenue of about $127.5M (roughly flat) and statutory EPS falling ~11% to $0.91 ([Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/utilities/nasdaq-cwco/consolidated-water)). Our own independent build came to $0.85–$0.98. We reproduced the street's number without meaning to.

The tape says the same thing three times over:

The market has read the license, read the rate cut, and looked through the Aerex trough. The stock's durable verdict on both events is *mildly negative*. That is a priced security, not an ignored one.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly stated, the edge candidates are thin and none of them is load-bearing today:

- **Coverage.** One analyst on a $476M-cap name means the price is not the product of a well-fed consensus machine. Thin coverage does not create value, but it does mean a genuine surprise would take longer to price. This is the only structural argument for watching the name at all.
- **Balance sheet.** $132.6M cash = 28% of market cap, and the minority interest against it is only $5.36M (1.1% of cap) — we attacked the cash-attribution and the attack failed, so the full $8.11/share of net cash stands. Screens that use gross EV or ignore net cash will show this name as more expensive than it is.
- **Two unfunded call options we could size correctly if they ever fund.** The $204M Kalaeloa (Hawaii) project has a limited notice-to-proceed authorizing **only $6M** of long-lead equipment (2.9%), with construction "expected later in 2026 pending permits." And Aerex's trough is a trough, not a run-rate. If either converts to real, disclosed, dollar-quantified backlog, we would be early — because at that moment the sole analyst's flat model becomes wrong.

**Where we do NOT have edge:** we have no number that differs from the published number and no mechanism that would make one differ. That is the honest state of the file.

## Why it might be priced in

It is priced in, and here is the arithmetic that says so.

- Market cap $476.4M on 16.01M diluted shares; cash $132.6M; minority interest $5.36M → **enterprise value ≈ $346.5M**.
- Operating income, first half 2026: **$7.18M vs $9.89M** a year earlier, **−27.4%**. FY25 was $18.36M.
- First half has historically been ~54% of the full year, so doubling the half overstates it. Post-rate-reset run-rate operating income ≈ **$13.3M** (that is red's $14.4M corrected downward by the blue bench, against our own bull case).
- **$346.5M / $13.3M ≈ 26x EV/EBIT** for operating income that is shrinking.
- Peers: Middlesex Water 23.7x, Artesian 14.5x. CWCO's trailing figure is 22.1x. So 26x forward is at or above the top of the peer band, not below it.
- At a defensible 18–22x plus net cash, fair value is **$22–25**. Applying the desk's own documented tendency to set fair value ~13% too low lifts that to **$25–28**. Even fully corrected in our favor, $29.75 is **6–19% above fair**.

And the bull legs don't hold:
- **The "normalize Aerex back to prior-year revenue" leg is not allowed.** The original brief correctly refused to credit a lumpy construction segment's peak as an annuity — then did exactly that for manufacturing. You cannot apply the rule to one segment and not the other. (Our red bench then made the mirror error by treating the *trough* half as the run-rate. The truth is a multi-year average, which is a range, not a point.)
- **The retail annuity is shrinking, not flat.** The brief cited Q2 retail revenue +0.3% as proof the tourism-linked base was intact. The same release shows **first-half retail revenue $17.2M, −4.5% year over year.** The quarter was selected; the half was not checked.
- **Interest income was double-counted.** The brief assumed ~$5.2M/yr on the cash pile. Actual first-half investment income was $1.29M, i.e. ~$2.6M/yr. Half.

## What could go wrong

This section assumes the position we would actually take — a starter bought in the $24–25 reopen band — and asks how it loses money.

**Failure narrative 1 — "We bought a value trap at 20x." (monitorable)**
We buy at $25 because 20x post-reset operating income plus net cash is covered. Then the retail base keeps eroding: first-half retail is already −4.5%, and if that is *structural* (fewer connections, permanent Cayman tourism mix shift) rather than *weather*, the multiple doesn't compress — the E does. Operating income drifts from $13.3M to $11M, and $25 becomes 24x again. We are then holding a shrinking utility with a dividend, waiting.
*Tripwire:* Q3 (Nov 9) retail **gallons sold** — the volume line, not the revenue line. If gallons are negative year over year with the August 1 tariffs live, the weather story is falsified and we exit.

**Failure narrative 2 — "The two call options never fund." (monitorable)**
Kalaeloa stalls in permitting past 2027 and the $6M long-lead authorization is all that ever gets spent; Aerex's book stays at trough because the municipal capex cycle it serves is deferred. We paid for optionality that expired unexercised. This is the highest-probability quiet loss: nothing dramatic happens, the stock sits at $25–30 for two years, and we've funded a 2% dividend with dead capital.
*Tripwire:* full notice-to-proceed with **CWCO's own disclosed dollar share and margin** in a filing, and Aerex booked backlog ≥$25M with named municipal counterparties in the 10-Q. Absence of both by the Q1 2027 print = thesis is not developing.

**Failure narrative 3 — "Something happened on the island and we had no way to see it coming." (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE)**
Essentially the entire earnings base sits on one small island under one government under one newly-signed license. The loss modes here include: a hurricane or plant failure at Blue Hills/Windsor/West Bay; a Cayman political shift toward public ownership or re-tendering at the first license review; the undisclosed annual rate-adjustment formula being applied adversely on a July 1; a Bahamas government counterparty payment problem. We would learn about any of these from an 8-K **after** the price moved.

**We cannot instrument this.** Our satellite tooling was tried and came back **UNCHECKABLE**: every Grand Cayman facility geocodes to a single island centroid and Fort Pierce to a town center, so plant-thermal and buildout detectors have nothing to look at without hand-drawn parcel polygons we do not have. There is no permit feed for Cayman, no app, no customs series, no litigation docket.

**Unknown-unknown class, named: single-jurisdiction sovereign-and-physical concentration risk on a non-instrumented island.** This is the class where we have no sensor, no proxy series, and no leading indicator — only the filing that arrives after the fact. It is not a tail we can hedge or watch; it is a tail we simply carry, and the size of the position is the only control we have over it.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own predicted flow, our own doctrine:**

- **Liquidity is 2.4x worse than the brief claimed.** The brief said 116–151k shares/day. Vendor average is **63,201**. At our 1%-of-volume execution rail, that is roughly **632 shares/day ≈ $16k/day at a $25 price**. A $50k starter is **~3 sessions** of patient limit work, not one. An exit in stress is the same math running the other way, into a thinner book. This is a name where our own footprint is a first-order cost, not a rounding error.
- **We would be bidding into a decline we ourselves forecast.** Our reopen band is ≤$25 — 16% below spot. The only paths to $25 are a bad Q3, a broad small-cap drawdown, or the retail erosion continuing. In every one of those, the reason the price reached our limit is a reason not to buy it. Our own rule against bidding into our own predicted flow applies directly here, and the discipline is: *the band must be reached by a mechanism we can name and dismiss*, not simply touched.
- **Two of our own doctrines pull opposite ways, and we are resolving it explicitly.** Our "clean court, minimum starter" default says a court that produces no named kill should still take a starter rather than sit flat. This court produced **four independent, novel kills** — that default does not apply. Against that, our conservative-fair-value correction lifts fair value to $25–28, which means at $25 we would be buying at the *bottom* of a corrected fair range, not below it. So even the reopen band is "fair," not "cheap." Sizing must reflect that: this is a starter, permanently, unless a funded catalyst appears.
- **The original reason this name was in the queue is gone.** CWCO was enqueued as real-asset ballast — something that holds up when other things don't. It fell ~24% *into* a real-asset melt-up, has a beta of 0.52 and a −10.9% 52-week return. It is an idiosyncratic small cap, not ballast. If we own it, we own it as a single-name utility bet and must not count it against any diversification role.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we paid a fair-to-full multiple for a business whose regulated retail annuity was quietly shrinking — the −4.5% half-year we almost didn't check — while both of the growth legs we told ourselves were free options (Aerex normalization, Kalaeloa) stayed unfunded, so the earnings never arrived to grow into the price, and our position was too illiquid to leave quickly once that became obvious.

## What remains unverified

- **All prices, volumes, and the 52-week range** are vendor-sourced. Broker verification was denied to all three benches. This is the base layer of the valuation and it is not independently confirmed.
- **The rate-reduction magnitude.** The 6.5% / $2.1M / $1.9M / $0.6M figures appear in the June press wire only. The filed license and its exhibit contain the tariff tiers, the 25-year exclusivity, the Aug 1 commencement, and the fact of annual July 1 adjustments — but no reduction percentage, no pro forma, and no rate-of-return cap. **A static recast of old volumes at new rates is not a forward damage estimate.**
- **The forward rate-adjustment path.** The formula that governs each July 1 adjustment is not disclosed. We do not know whether it tracks inflation, fuel, capital, or something else, and therefore we cannot model years 2–25 of a 25-year license. This is the largest unverified item in the file.
- **Whether the retail decline is weather or structure.** The company reports revenue dollars in the release; the volume line (gallons sold) is what settles it. Unread until Nov 9.
- **Aerex forward book.** Trough half is a fact; whether it is a trough or a new level is unverified. Neither bench could establish a multi-year mean with the data pulled, so the correct treatment is a range, and we do not have the range.
- **Kalaeloa economics.** $6M authorized against a $204M headline, permits pending, and **CWCO's own dollar share and margin are not disclosed anywhere.** We do not know what this project is worth to this company even if it proceeds.
- **Physical asset condition and capex activity.** Satellite verification of plant operation and the West Bay "future operations campus" buildout is unavailable — every site collapses to a centroid. Parcel polygons would be required.
- **Bahamas segment counterparty health.** Not examined by either bench this session.
- **Two detector entries** were skipped by one bench and cleared by the other; a spurious consumer-products tag on this issuer fired four irrelevant checks. The issuer's feature set needs cleaning before the next court.

**Conviction that "no position at $29.75" is the right call: 8/10** (both benches, independently). Conviction on the *business* being sound: high. Conviction on the *original mispricing thesis*: it was wrong, and we are saying so.
