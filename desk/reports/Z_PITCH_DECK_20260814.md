# PITCH — Z — PROPOSED VERDICT: **PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Buy a reduced starter (~¼ of a full position, ~0.75% of the book) near $35.92 ahead of the Aug 24 FTC trial, with pre-planned adds at $33.23 / $31.65 / ~$29.50 and a hard keep-or-kill review at the Oct 29 earnings report.

## What we believe

Zillow at $35.92 is a sound business trading 62% below its 52-week high of $93.88, marked down by two fears — that AI assistants will replace real-estate search portals, and that the housing market stays frozen. The second fear is real and management just confirmed it. The first fear is, on the evidence, overstated: Zillow's own user numbers dipped only 2%, the one third-party measurement in evidence (Comscore, cited with numbers in the [Q1 press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/zillow-group-reports-first-quarter-2026-financial-results-302764351.html)) shows unique visitors **up 12% year over year to 127 million**, and revenue grew 18% in both Q1 and Q2. A company being disintermediated does not grow revenue 18% on a growing third-party-measured audience.

The company earns that growth from segments outside the frozen housing transaction itself (rentals, mortgages, ads), runs roughly breakeven on a GAAP basis (Q2 net loss of $4M including $10M of trial legal costs), holds $682M in cash, and is retiring stock — at the slower Q2 pace of $200M/quarter, still about 10% of the $8.08B market cap per year.

The risk that keeps this a quarter-sized position: an FTC antitrust trial over the Zillow–Redfin rentals agreement starts **August 24** (bench trial, about two weeks, both sides' summary-judgment motions denied, no settlement as of the court's review). Rentals is the fastest-growing segment — roughly 42% of Q2's year-over-year revenue growth — so the trial threatens the growth engine, not the balance sheet. The ~$100M already paid to Redfin is spent money either way; the realistic bad outcome is slower future Rentals growth, not a solvency event.

## What the market believes

The market has already read the Q2 report and reached a settled verdict of roughly "no change." The stock fell ~8% after hours on Aug 5 — not on AI fear, but because management cut its industry outlook to a low-to-mid single-digit **decline** in purchase mortgage originations for the rest of 2026, guided full-year revenue to $2.92–2.96B versus a $2.98B street consensus, and announced 500 layoffs ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4626644-zillow-stock-sinks-despite-q2-earnings-beat-on-layoffs-soft-revenue-guidance)). That selloff then fully recovered within six sessions: today's $35.92 sits about 0.5% below the pre-report close of ~$36.10.

So the market's current view is: growth is decelerating (Q3 guided to ~11% from 18%), the housing macro is getting worse per management's own words, and the FTC trial is a coin-flip nobody can price — analyst targets run from $37 (BofA) to $62 (JPM). The market is **not** currently paying anything for the trial risk resolving well, and is **not** currently discounting the stock for it either.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The AI-disintermediation story — the original reason for the 62% drawdown — is contradicted by third-party data.** Comscore visitors +12%; revenue +18% two quarters running. The bear case that created the low prices has quietly stopped being true, and the recovery from the post-earnings selloff suggests holders are no longer selling on it.
2. **The trial risk is bounded and asymmetric at this size.** Losing the FTC case slows one segment's growth; it doesn't impair the balance sheet, the buyback, or the core residential business. A win or settlement removes a two-year overhang on the fastest-growing segment. At ¼ size with adds reserved for a trial-headline selloff, we get paid for volatility we've pre-planned into.
3. **The selloff-and-recovery pattern is now demonstrated, not hypothetical.** The stock actually printed $31.65–$33.23 six sessions ago and recovered. Our add levels are prices the stock has recently traded at under stress, not hopeful round numbers.
4. **The buyback is a real floor-builder**: ~10% of shares per year at the current pace, funded from operations, against a share count that management is visibly shrinking (13.5M shares retired in Q1, 5.6M in Q2).

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the other side, and our own red-team review made it well:

1. **There is no discount left from the earnings report.** The stock round-tripped. Anyone buying today pays the pre-report price for a company that has since guided its own industry down. The "unnoticed refutation" our original thesis claimed does not exist — the market noticed, sold, and re-bought.
2. **Growth is flat-to-decelerating, not accelerating.** Q1 +18%, Q2 +18%, Q3 guided ~11%. Our original thesis said "accelerating"; that was wrong.
3. **The growth mix is concentrated in the two riskiest places.** Rentals (~42% of incremental revenue) is the segment on trial and its growth already slowed from 42% to 31% quarter over quarter — before any court remedy. Mortgages (~30% of incremental revenue) is now guided against a declining origination industry. Core residential grew only 7%.
4. **On a stricter valuation the stock is fair, not cheap**: ~9.9x EV/EBITDA becomes ~16.6x if you expense stock compensation — full price for ~11% growth. (This number was never independently recomputed; see below.)
5. **The trial outcome is genuinely unpriceable.** Neither bench could estimate odds. A starter bought today is partly a bet on an unknowable binary.
