## What we believe

Geostr (5282.T) makes precast concrete segments — the pre-made concrete rings that line shield-driven tunnels — built to order against Japanese public infrastructure work. It is a ~¥13.2bn market-cap company on TSE Standard with no analyst price targets and, per one industry database, a Nippon Steel group subsidiary.

Four things we believe after both benches were done:

1. **The screen price is a lie in the bull direction.** The "7x earnings" you see comes from company-guided net income of ~¥1,904mn, which contains a ~¥1.3bn gain on selling cross-held shares. Strip it at a 30.6% tax rate and underlying earnings are ~¥1.0–1.04bn, EPS ~¥32–33, so the real multiple is **~12.7–13.0x, not 7x**, on a **~4.0% return on equity**. The company confirms this itself: the 2026-08-06 tanshin says in plain Japanese that the ~¥1.3bn sale gain is *not* being put into the dividend funding base (https://www.geostr.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/f3d23611358d5bd95bd0a48153bdccec.pdf). A ¥10 dividend at ~31% payout back-solves to ~¥32 of clean EPS — the dividend arithmetic is the clean-earnings disclosure the P&L withholds.
2. **But the red bench's "it's already at fair value" is wrong.** Red built fair value (¥396–452) by capitalizing that ~3.9% ROE forever. That ROE is a company-declared trough — the firm guided H1 ordinary income to **zero** and the full year to ¥1,500mn (−28.7%) because large-segment orders are in a gap period, and the same filing says demand for large segment products recovers **from FY2028**. On mid-cycle earnings (ordinary ~¥1.73bn → ROE ~4.7%) fair value is ~¥505; on last year's clean actual (ordinary ¥2,104mn → ROE ~5.6%) it is ~¥614. So the honest band is **¥505–615 vs ¥419, i.e. +20% to +47%**.
3. **The cheapness is real but small.** +20–47% at mid-cycle, on an asset with a two-year wait and no coverage, is not a table that clears our +50% bar at the midpoint. That is the whole verdict.
4. **The balance sheet, not the income statement, is where the option lives.** Equity ratio ~67.9%, debt roughly flat, ¥4.0bn of receivables released, and cross-held shares still on the book after a ¥1,574mn reduction — meaning the "windfall" is likely a **multi-year disposal program**, not a single event, and it is explicitly earmarked "on the premise of" buybacks and capex.

## What the market believes

The market believes two contradictory things at once, and that's the interesting part.

- **The screen layer** (aggregators) believes this is a 0.52x-book, 7x-earnings, improving-profitability company. The Yahoo Japan page literally carries a machine-written summary saying sales keep rising year-on-year and profitability is somewhat strong **on the same page** as a −36.8% sales print with an operating loss. That is windfall-inflated data being narrated as a trend.
- **The people actually trading it** believe less. The stock is ¥419–425, ~17% below the ¥508 February high, and there are 1.21m shares of retail margin longs (≈3.8% of shares out, ≈16.5 days of average volume) with essentially zero short interest — a crowd that is long and stuck, not a coiled spring.
- **The street, where it exists:** IFIS consensus had FY3/26 ordinary at ¥1,650mn against a ¥2,104mn actual (+27.5% beat), and the FY3/27 company guide of ¥1,500mn came in **11.8% below** consensus. So the market's number is ~¥1.70bn and the company deliberately guided under it. The market is not modeling a permanent trough; it is modeling a gap year with a soft recovery, which is close to where we are.

The gap between us and the market is therefore *narrow*, not wide — and that is the case against the trade.

## Why we might have edge

- **Nobody publishes a clean EPS for this name.** With no broker targets, the only widely-visible earnings number is the guided one that contains the disposal gain. Anyone who does the ex-gain arithmetic knows something the screens don't — but note that cuts *against* the stock, so the edge here is mostly in **not** buying a value trap.
- **The recovery date is in the filing and almost nobody reads it.** The issuer dates large-segment demand recovery to FY2028+. That means the November H1 print is nearly information-free and the **FY3/28 guidance in May 2027** is the real repricing event. Both benches initially set tripwires on the wrong date; we now have the right one.
- **The disposal program is probably recurring.** Cross-held shares remain on the balance sheet. If Japan's ongoing corporate-governance pressure keeps forcing unwinds, there are more gains coming, and the company has already said in writing they're earmarked "on the premise of" buybacks. A real buyback resolution on a ¥13.2bn cap with 67.9% equity ratio is a genuinely large lever.
- **A parent tail exists.** If Nippon Steel really is a majority holder, the group has an incentive at some point to buy in a small listed subsidiary; historical Japanese take-private prices cluster around 0.7–0.9x book, which here is **¥565–727**.

## Why it might be priced in

- **0.52x book is the normal price for a 4% ROE.** At a 7.5% cost of equity, warranted price-to-book is ~0.52x → ~¥420. That is literally the tape. Red's error was capitalizing the trough forever; but if the trough *is* the through-cycle reality, red is simply right and the stock is exactly where it should be.
- **The bad news was pre-announced.** The Q1 operating loss of ¥146mn was not a surprise: the company guided interim ordinary income to zero back in May. A pre-guided loss doesn't create a dislocation, and the stock has already traded down ~17% from February without breaking.
- **The buyback is not a buyback.** The filing says "on the premise of" acquiring treasury shares **or** business expansion **or** capital expenditure. There is no board resolution, no yen amount, no share cap. Simultaneously the cash was ring-fenced *out* of the dividend base. Read coldly, shareholders were told the money is not theirs yet.
- **There is nothing to squeeze.** Zero short base, 16.5 days of leveraged retail length overhead. Any rally into ¥450–470 meets sellers who have been underwater since February.
- **The cheapness is 20–47%, not 100%.** Even the bull path needs ROE ~6.2% to reach +50% (¥630), which this company has not printed without selling shareholdings.

## What could go wrong (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

**Failure narrative #1 — the gap year isn't a gap, it's the new level (monitorable).**
Large-segment tunnel awards don't come back in FY2028; the −36.8% quarter turns out to be share loss or price competition dressed up as order timing. Mid-cycle ordinary of ¥1.73bn never appears, ROE stays ~4%, and fair value really is ¥400–450. We bought at ¥380–419 and dead-money for three years while the dividend pays us 2.4%.
*Tripwire:* FY3/28 guide (May 2027) ordinary below ¥1.5bn, or Japanese public-procurement award registries (MLIT / NEXCO / JRTT / prefectural bid results) showing segment-category award volumes flat-to-up while Geostr's revenue falls. **This one is checkable and we will check it.**

**Failure narrative #2 — the parent is the risk, not the prize (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).**
We have *no* verified ownership figure. One industry database calls it a Nippon Steel subsidiary; we could not fetch the filing that states the stake. If the parent relationship is real, the parent controls what happens to ¥1.3bn of cash, the treasury-share "premise," transfer pricing on group work, and the timing and price of any buy-in. A parent can also simply decide the listed sub should retain cash for a group capex program, or take it out at 0.6x book instead of 0.9x. **There is no sensor for a parent's intent.** Ownership tables update annually; board discussions never appear until the press release, which is typically issued after a trading halt at a price we did not negotiate.
*Unknown-unknown class: **principal-agent / controlling-shareholder discretion risk** — a second decision-maker whose objective function is not shareholder value at this entity, whose actions are unobservable until they are irreversible.* We cannot instrument this; we can only size for it. (Related, equally uninstrumented sub-class: **disclosure-regime opacity** — the items we need most, backlog and net cash detail, are simply not published quarterly in this market.)

**Failure narrative #3 — we get the science of the earnings right and the arithmetic of the exit wrong (reflexive / structural leg).**
Average daily volume is ~73,300 shares ≈ **¥31mn a day**. That is the binding constraint on everything.
- A 0.5%-of-book starter sized at ¥15mn is already **~0.5 days of ADV**; our stated maximum of ¥46mn is **1.5 days**, and getting out of that in a falling tape at a disciplined ≤15% of daily volume takes **~10 sessions**. In a −10% week we will not be the first seller.
- Above us sit **1.21m shares of retail margin longs — 16.5 days of ADV**, with zero short interest. Predicted flow on any good news: margin holders who have been trapped since ¥508 sell into strength. Our buying pressure meets their supply; our selling pressure meets nothing. The order book is asymmetric against us in both directions.
- **Doctrine interactions:** three of our house rules collide here. (a) The *windfall-masked-flat-guide* detector says sell/avoid; (b) the *trough-year warranted-PBR artifact* mechanism says red's kill was an input error and the name is cheap; (c) the *guided-zero interim bar* detector says the November print cannot resolve either one. Net effect: our own framework says "cheap, but you cannot learn anything for eight months." A position taken on (b) while (c) is true is a position with no feedback loop — we will be tempted to add on noise, and the illiquidity means every add is permanent. That is exactly how a 0.5% watch position becomes a 2% mistake.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a real but small discount (+20–47%) in an illiquid, uncovered, parent-controlled name where the only information event is eight months away — and then either the FY2028 order recovery the company promised didn't arrive, or it did and the 16 days of trapped retail length above us absorbed the entire re-rating before we could exit a position we couldn't sell in less than ten sessions.**
