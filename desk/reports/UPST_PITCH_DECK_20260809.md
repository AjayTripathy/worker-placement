# PITCH — UPST — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line)
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: BUY a starter position in two halves — first half now near $31, second half only if the September 3 borrower-health print improves (≤1.45) or an independent source confirms the company's funding-cost claims. Kill on two straight deteriorating borrower-health prints or August loan volume below $45M/day.**

## What we believe
Upstart is an AI lending platform whose stock is down ~60% from its 52-week high ($76.95 → $31.09) even though the most recent quarter and the independent evidence we could find do not show the damage that fall implies. Specifically:

- **The credit the company originates is holding up by third-party measure.** KBRA — an independent rating agency — reviewed 13 Upstart securitization trusts in February 2026 and issued 7 upgrades, 14 affirmations, and only 1 downgrade ([KBRA surveillance report](https://www.kbra.com/publications/fdHrfYwQ/kbra-affirms-upgrades-and-downgrades-ratings-from-13-upstart-securitization-trust-upstart-pass-through-trust-and-upstart-structured-pass-through-trust-transactions)). That one downgrade (a class facing majority principal loss) shows the bad-vintage tail is real, but the net picture from the only truly independent credit channel is positive.
- **Funding demand exists.** KBRA confirms the company priced its 51st loan-sale deal in 2026 ($569.4M). The machine that turns loans into cash is running.
- **The feared balance-sheet tail is smaller and later than the bears claim.** The company's convertible notes have no maturity before October 2029, and their conversion prices ($45.66, $82.50, $91.99) are 47–196% above today's stock price — so neither a forced refinancing nor conversion-driven dilution is a near-term event (confirmed in the Q2 [10-Q filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001647639/000164763926000063/upst-20260630.htm)).
- **Heavy short interest (27% of shares, ~33% of the float) is fuel, not just a warning.** On a name where the independent credit evidence is net-positive, a crowded short is a mechanical tailwind if the story improves.

Honesty requires saying what we do **not** believe anymore: two of our original bull pillars ("tightest funding spreads in three years" and "+660 basis point loan vintages") turned out to be sourced only from management's own statements — no independent venue publishes those numbers. The bull case survives on the KBRA channel and the balance-sheet math, not on those claims. This is a fairly-paid-risk position with a possible edge on top, not a clean mispricing.

## What the market believes
The market believes Upstart's borrowers are weakening and its self-reported numbers can't be trusted. The company's own macro index (its published gauge of borrower stress, [upstart.com/umi](https://www.upstart.com/umi)) sits at 1.50 and ticked up 0.7% over the last month; management blamed a revenue-line miss on that same rising stress. The stock carries $2.0B of convertible debt against $456M cash — net debt roughly $1.57B — so enterprise value is ~$4.6B against a $3.03B market cap, meaning the equity is a leveraged slice of the story. And roughly a third of the float is sold short by people actively pressing that view.

Part of the disagreement has already closed: the stock bounced +13.3% in the five sessions after the August 4 earnings ($27.44 on July 31 → $31.09 on August 7). Whatever "the quarter wasn't a disaster" was worth, several days of buying have collected some of it.

## Why we might have edge
1. **The independent credit channel is under-read.** The KBRA 13-trust surveillance (7 upgrades / 14 affirms / 1 downgrade) is public but obscure; the red bench — actively hunting for kills — hadn't seen it. If professional skeptics inside our own process missed it, the marginal seller may have too.
2. **The bear's balance-sheet arithmetic double-counts.** Bears stack the $2.0B of senior debt *on top of* the loans-to-equity ratio to claim amplified damage. But shareholders' equity already nets out that debt: a markdown on the $1.06B loan book hits the $797M equity at exactly 1.33×, not more. The scary version of the leverage story is a math error.
3. **The refinancing/dilution tail is dated and out of the money.** Nothing matures before October 2029; all conversion prices are far above the stock. The bears' own falsifier — "check the maturities and strikes" — resolves against them, and they never ran it.
4. **The short base is a spring.** 27% of shares short on a company that is GAAP-profitable and still placing loan-sale deals creates asymmetric upside on any confirmation.

## Why it might be priced in
- **We're buying after a +13% bounce, not into the panic.** The dislocation entry was $27.44 five sessions ago. Both benches concede this is timing-degraded — it caps size, and it's why the first tranche is only half.
- **The two headline bull facts are the company grading its own homework.** Until Finsight, Bloomberg, or a rating agency publishes actual execution spreads, "tightest in three years" is marketing, and the market may be right to discount it.
- **The borrower-stress gauge is genuinely worsening**, per the company's own index — and the latest reading can be revised ±5%, so even a modestly better September print may prove nothing.
- **A third of the float short means the bear case is well-researched and actively maintained**, not lazy consensus. Battlegrounds are priced tighter than they look.
