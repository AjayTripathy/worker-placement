## What we believe

Liftoff is a mobile advertising marketplace — it buys and sells app install and in-app ad inventory for other companies. It went public on 2026-06-03 at $23 a share, backed by Blackstone (49.5%) and General Atlantic (15%). It is real, cash-generative, and growing: roughly $875M of revenue guided for 2026, about $514M of adjusted profit, $305M of cash against $1.44B of term debt.

We believe one thing about it with confidence, and it comes straight from the company's own audited filings and its own guidance: **growth is slowing, quarter after quarter, and the slowdown is not fully explained by anything the company has disclosed.** Year-over-year revenue growth steps down 37% → 35% → 22% → 19% across the four quarters of 2026, where the last two are the company's own guidance. Roughly $13M a quarter of that deceleration has no disclosed one-time cause.

We also believe the second thing more strongly than the first: **there is a very large, dated supply of stock coming.** 147.5M restricted shares become sellable on 2026-11-30 — 6.7 times the 21.85M shares that currently trade. That lands two to three weeks *after* the next earnings report, expected around 2026-11-11.

And we believe the thing that actually decides the trade: **we do not have a differentiated view.** Not bullish, not bearish. Our own bearish analyst's revenue estimate for the next quarter sits *above* the published consensus. When your bear case is more optimistic than the street's, you do not have a short, and you certainly do not have a long. So: no position. Not "never" — **defer**, with a specific date and specific triggers to come back.

The one directional statement we will make is about *when*: a flood of supply from forced-ish sellers is where a buyer gets paid, not where a buyer gets run over. **December is the entry, not the exit.**

## What the market believes

The stock is priced as a business that is decelerating and that has a supply problem — and it is priced roughly correctly for both.

- Thirteen analysts cover it. All thirteen rate it Buy, with an average target of $35.46. Every one of them worked on the IPO or its syndicate. This is not independent opinion; it is the underwriters' book, and we discount it accordingly.
- The number that actually carries information is the consensus revenue estimate for Q3: **$214.8M**. The company guided $217–222M. So the street had *already* cut its estimate below the company's own recent trend, and the guidance came in above it. The market is not asleep on the slowdown; it has been modeling it.
- The tape says the same. The stock is down about 39% from its high and just made a new all-time low four days after the last earnings report, on no news. Only 12.9% of the company's shares actually trade. This is a broken sponsor-backed IPO still finding its clearing price, not a stable business that got mispriced by a panic.

Put plainly: the market believes growth is halving and that the sponsors will eventually sell. We checked, and we believe the same things. That's the problem.

## Why we might have edge

Three honest candidates, none of which survived contact with the evidence at full strength:

1. **We did the seasonality work that nobody in the sell-side book appears to have done.** Our bearish analyst tested the flat quarterly guidance against the company's own history of same-quarter growth rather than against consensus. That is the right test, and it is rarely run. It produced a real finding — the guided quarter is below the company's own recent sequential trend by roughly $15M — but a much smaller one than first claimed (see below).

2. **We bounded the benign explanation with arithmetic instead of accepting or rejecting it.** The bull story is that Q2 "borrowed" revenue from Q3, so the flat quarter is a calendar artifact. If that's true, the borrowed amount has a ceiling: the company's own full-year guidance implies Q4 revenue of $230.4M, and Q3 plus the borrowed amount must be less than that. That caps the borrowing at **$10.9M**. The trend gap is about $15M. So the benign story can explain at most two-thirds of the gap, and **at least ~$13M per quarter is unexplained**. That is a tighter, more defensible version of the bear case than either the street or our own first draft had.

3. **We found that a widely-cited dilution overhang runs backwards at this price.** The management option pool — 21.6M options, 12.7% of shares outstanding, with no expense yet recognized — only vests if the sponsors sell at a price achieving a specified return. At $18.36, 20% below the $23 IPO price, that condition fails. So the dilution is a bull-case-only cost, and management is actively *disincentivized* from supporting a cheap secondary offering. That cuts against the supply-cliff fear, not for it. Most people carrying this name as "dilution overhang + lockup cliff" are double-counting one event and getting its direction wrong.

**Why none of this is an edge today:** all three are refinements of a view the market already holds. Better-sourced agreement is not a position. The genuine edge, if there is one, is *timing* — we know the date the supply arrives and we know that the people who own it are structurally unwilling to sell below $23. That is an edge you exercise in December, not in August.

## Why it might be priced in

- **It is priced in, on the evidence.** Consensus $214.8M vs. our bear's $217M-or-under. Our pessimism is less pessimistic than theirs.
- The lockup date is public and in the prospectus. Everyone with a calendar has it.
- The 39% drawdown already happened. We are not looking at a business about to be re-rated; we are looking at one that was re-rated, correctly, in front of us.
- The valuation is not screaming either way: about 8.6x enterprise value to adjusted profit at $19.53 (roughly 8.2x at $18.36, our arithmetic). That is cheap for a 30%-grower and fair-to-full for a 19%-grower. Which one it is, is exactly the open question. There is no valuation asymmetry to lean on.
- The one thing that is *not* priced is the possibility of a sandbag: a sponsor-controlled company's first-ever public guidance is the single most likely place in the market to find a deliberately low bar. Both benches named this as their main residual doubt. It is a reason to watch, not a reason to own — you don't pay for a sandbag you can't verify.

## What remains unverified

- **The earnings date.** ~2026-11-11 comes from a data vendor. No company release names it. Every dated argument in this deck inherits that uncertainty.
- **The lockup mechanics.** We computed 2026-11-30 by counting 180 days from the prospectus date. We have not read the prospectus lockup section itself. Whether the days are calendar or trading days, whether an early price-based release exists, and whether the release is a cliff or staged are all **unread**.
- **The management option strike price.** Not disclosed in what we read, so we could not compute a diluted share count properly. Stated, not assumed.
- **The size of the World Cup effect.** The company named the FIFA World Cup (June 11 – July 19, 2026) as a driver of the June quarter, which means roughly 19 days of it fall into the September quarter and none fell into the year-ago September quarter. The company never quantified it. This makes the flat guidance look worse, but we cannot say by how much. Plausible, not confirmed.
- **Next year's consensus.** We never obtained a 2027 revenue consensus. Both benches tried and failed. This is the number that would tell us whether the street models 19% growth as a floor or a waypoint — which is the entire open question. It is a real gap, not a rounding one.
- **The live price.** Broker quote access was denied. Every valuation figure here is computed off the 8/17 close of $19.53; the pack's latest is $18.36. Nothing was recomputed at the lower price except where noted as our arithmetic.
- **Pre-IPO distributions.** Disclosed distributions of $14.6M (2025) and $10.3M (2024) to employee equity holders were read from a prospectus *summary*, not from the prospectus body. They appear to be incentive-plan related rather than a debt-funded sponsor dividend, and the $1.85B of term debt dates to the 2021 combination — outside the window that would concern us. Directionally clean, not primary-verified.
- **Whether the first public guidance is a sandbag.** Unknowable before the print. It is the single largest residual doubt on both sides of this court, and it is the reason the verdict is *defer* rather than *reject*.
