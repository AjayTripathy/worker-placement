# PITCH — TNC — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY. No position, no order, no price gate armed. Watch-and-reopen only, on Q3 evidence — both benches reached REJECT/WATCH independently, and the bull case survives only as an unbridged margin-restoration story at roughly today's price.**

---

## What we believe

Tennant makes industrial and commercial floor-cleaning machines — scrubbers, sweepers, the equipment that cleans warehouse and airport floors. It is a 155-year-old Minnesota company, NYSE-listed, with a 54-year record of paying and raising its dividend. It is not a fraud risk and it is not a solvency risk. It is a good business having a bad two years.

What we actually believe, stated plainly:

1. **The company's earnings power really did fall, and a large part of it is self-inflicted.** A botched ERP (accounting/order-management software) implementation broke the order-to-cash cycle. Gross margin fell 260 basis points to 39.5%. First-half adjusted EBITDA was $64.4M against $92.0M a year earlier — down 30%.
2. **The margin structure that the bull case wants back demonstrably existed 18 months ago.** Full-year 2024 adjusted EBITDA was $208.8M on a 16.2% margin. 2025 was $167.4M / 13.9%. The trailing-twelve-month figure today is $139.8M. So there is a real, sourced $69M gap between what this company earned in 2024 and what it earns now. That is the entire bull case.
3. **Management has not shown us how it closes.** The full-year guide requires second-half adjusted EBITDA of $90.6M–$105.6M, which is +41% to +64% over the first half, with no disclosed bridge and no full-year cash-flow guidance at all. On the revenue line it requires roughly +8–9% organic growth in the second half against −1.2% in the first half, from a business whose unit volumes fell 4.8%.
4. **And they have stopped telling us how big the problem is.** The ERP damage was sized in dollars in Q4 2025 ($22M of EBITDA) and again in Q1 2026 ($23M of sales, $17M of gross margin). In Q2 2026 — the third quarter of the same problem — there is no dollar figure. The cause is now bundled into "residual ERP inefficiencies, supply constraints, and elevated freight and tariff costs." Tariffs and European price pressure do not heal when the software gets fixed.

**Bottom line: we believe the recovery is possible and we do not believe we are being paid to underwrite it at $66.38.**

So the market is paying 8.7x a number the company has promised but not bridged, which is the same thing as 10.1x the money the business actually makes today. That is not a distressed price for an industrial. The stock is 27.6% off its 52-week high of $91.66 but only 10.2% above its 52-week low of $60.22 — the tape has already stopped panicking and settled into "we believe the guide, roughly."

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: **thinner than the screen suggested, and mostly of the arithmetic kind rather than the informational kind.**

- **Nobody appears to have computed the stub.** Both of our own benches initially valued this off the guide midpoint. The available sell-side (Sidoti, Roth, plus a Zacks quant rating) is thin for a $1.13B company. The specific finding — that the full-year guide requires a +41% half-over-half EBITDA step-up *and* a ~+8–9% second-half organic revenue swing simultaneously, with no bridge published — is simple arithmetic on a public 8-K that we have not seen anyone else print. That is an edge in the sense that being early to a disappointment is an edge. It is a reason not to own it; it is not a reason to be long.
- **Coverage is genuinely light.** Our blue bench argued this is a "discovering," not a crowded, name. That means if the recovery *does* land, there is room for the multiple to be re-rated by people who aren't watching yet. That is the only bull-side edge we can honestly claim, and it is contingent, not present.
- **The disclosure-degradation pattern is a real, generalizable signal.** A company that quantifies a self-inflicted problem for two quarters and then stops quantifying it while the margin is still falling is telling you something. We have written this up as a reusable detector. It is not, on its own, a trade.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is. Symmetrically:

- **You clear almost nothing even if you are right about everything.** Grant the bull case entirely — the ERP heals, the second half delivers the 41% step-up, the guide midpoint prints. At a normal 9x industrial multiple on $162.5M, the stock is **$69.24, or +4.3%** from here. At 8x it is **$59.71, or −10.1%**. That is the whole payoff distribution for "management was telling the truth."
- **The 27.6% drawdown is not a discount to today's earnings.** It is a discount to 2024's earnings, which no longer exist. At 9x the trailing $139.8M the stock is worth **$57.26**; at 8x, **$49.06**. Buying at $66.38 is already paying up for a recovery that has not started.
- **The dividend and buyback are constrained.** The credit agreement restricts dividends and repurchases above 2.50x leverage. Actual leverage on trailing adjusted EBITDA is **2.02x** — 0.48 turns of headroom. Two more quarters shaped like Q2 put the 54-year dividend streak inside the restricted zone. That is a floor-remover, not a floor.
- **The adjusted number is doing real work.** First-half adjusted EBITDA of $64.4M compares to roughly $48.8M unadjusted — about 24% of the headline is add-backs. The 8.7x is on the flattered line.
- **Cash conversion has inverted.** Operating cash flow went from +$22.1M to **−$26.2M**, a $48.3M swing, and the majority of that is earnings, not timing: net income fell from $33.3M to $7.8M. Cash earnings (net income plus D&A) fell $24.3M of the $48.3M. Collections do not fix that half.
- **Below the EBITDA line leaks.** Gross interest rose from $6.2M to $9.6M and the tax rate from 25.2% to 30.5%. EBITDA recovery does not reach earnings per share one-for-one.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no proposed entry. No buy price, no starter, no order staged. What follows are reopen conditions.**

The reason there is no price gate is deliberate and worth stating: we do not have a verified fair value for this company. The reported profit number carries 24% add-backs, the revenue line is inflated by currency, and management has stopped quantifying the one problem it had been quantifying. A price gate set today would arm against numbers we have already shown to be distorted — it would buy a stock because it fell, not because it got cheap.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these are load-bearing:

1. **The live price.** Broker access was permission-denied this session. Every valuation number in this deck rests on a pack tape of $66.38 that was not independently re-quoted, and the two benches quoted $66.76 and $66.52 against it. Re-quote before any action.
2. **The Q3 date.** 2026-11-02 is vendor-derived. No company release names it.
3. **Insider pledges and recent insider activity.** The proxy was never pulled; the 2026-08-13 Form 4s and the 2026-08-14 13G are unread. This is a genuine, closable gap that two benches left open.
4. **What is inside the 24% of add-backs.** We know first-half adjusted EBITDA is $64.4M against roughly $48.8M unadjusted. We have not verified the composition, and every multiple in this deck is computed on the adjusted line.
5. **How much of the damage is really ERP.** Because Q2 gave no quantification, any reconstruction of "volume excluding ERP disruption" is plausible reasoning, not evidence. No conclusion in this deck rests on it, and none should.
6. **Whether an H2 bridge exists at all.** The absence of a bridge and the absence of full-year cash-flow guidance are facts. Whether that reflects a plan management chose not to publish, or the absence of a plan, is unknown.
7. **The seasonality baseline for cash flow.** The $42.9M figure for H2 2025 operating cash flow comes from one bench and was not independently re-derived. It matters, because it sets how demanding the "$40M OCF" reopen condition really is.
8. **OSHA establishment records** for the Holland and Louisville plants — never queried. Low expected value here, but stated rather than skipped.
9. **The thermal detector's underlying data quality.** Absolute excess temperatures are small and the baseline is only a few years. Even setting aside the failed two-site agreement, it should not carry weight in either direction.

**One last honest note on where the value of this court actually sits.** The verdict — don't buy it — was reachable from a single arithmetic step: the full-year guide implies a 41% half-over-half step-up that nobody has bridged. The genuinely useful output is the four corrections above where our own machinery was wrong: the screen ranked this name on a currency-inflated growth score, the brief carried a phantom inventory build from a period-binding error, red over-read a satellite detector whose two sites disagreed and understated leverage in the bull's favor, and blue's headline bull target was 4.5% too high. Both benches also proposed a tripwire against a debt-funded buyback that had already stopped. That is five machinery defects on one name, and they are worth more than the verdict.
