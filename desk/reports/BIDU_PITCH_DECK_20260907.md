# PITCH — BIDU — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — REJECT-for-now — re-court Q3 (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Baidu is a cheap-looking stub wrapped around a large, partly illiquid, partly trapped pile of cash — and the cheapness is mostly an accounting timing artifact that is about to reverse.

The core facts we are underwriting:

- **The cash is real but smaller than the headline.** The company shows RMB283.1B "total cash and investments," gross. Strip out ~RMB45B of illiquid "adjusted long-term investments" and ~RMB113B of debt and you get roughly **RMB125B of net liquid assets ≈ $17.4–18.4B** depending on the FX rate you use — call it **~55–59% of the ~$31.4B market cap**.
- **The operating business behind that cash is being valued at roughly $13–14B**, against ~$18.7B of trailing revenue and ~RMB10B/yr of non-GAAP net income — about **9–10x ex-cash earnings**. On the surface, that is not expensive.
- **But the cash is being spent fast.** Free cash flow excluding iQIYI was **negative RMB8,274M in Q2-26** (operating cash flow 3,096 less capex 11,370), disclosed directly in the company's own non-GAAP reconciliation ([Q2-26 release, ir.baidu.com](https://ir.baidu.com/static-files/e57ce78e-e47a-4c0c-8797-3cb11c8c9b0a)). That is roughly **−RMB33B/yr**, and it has been negative for four straight quarters.
- **The bill for that spending has not hit the income statement yet.** Full-year FY25 depreciation of fixed assets/computer parts was **RMB7,746M** ([FY25 6-K exhibit, sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526110843/d34060dex991.pdf)). Capex is now running **RMB11,370M in a single quarter**. That is roughly a 5x step in the asset base, and it converts into depreciation over the next four to six quarters, into a gross margin that has already fallen to 40.9% from a historical ~50%. So today's 9–10x becomes tomorrow's 20x+ unless the GPU-cloud revenue scales faster than the depreciation tail.
- **The company has also promised money back** — a $5B repurchase through 2028 and a first-ever dividend policy with first payment expected in 2026 ([6-K 2026-02-04, sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526038477/d40681dex991.htm)) — while burning cash. Burn plus buyback is roughly **RMB44B/yr against ~RMB125B of net liquid**: about a **three-year clock** on the optionality, before any dividend.

So: we believe this is a real balance sheet attached to a business making a large, unhedged, undisclosed-backlog bet, at a price that pays us for *some* of that risk but not enough. The right posture is a **priced, gated shopping order**, not a position.

## What the market believes

The market believes two things at once, which is why the stock sits 39.8% below its 52-week high and only 11% off its low.

1. **The bull half:** AI cloud is inflecting (GPU-cloud growth cited at +283%), the balance sheet is a floor, the Stock Connect inclusion effective 2026-09-07 ([6-K exhibit, sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001329099/000119312526382873/d947999dex991.htm)) plus the pending dual-primary HK conversion brings a new marginal buyer, and street targets in the ~$180 area imply a large re-rate.
2. **The bear half:** search advertising has now printed four consecutive high-teens down quarters (Q2-26 online marketing RMB13.1B, −19% y/y), and the free cash flow collapse was covered by essentially everyone who wrote up the print.

Where consensus is *not* looking: the **timing gap between the capex and its depreciation**. Street models book the revenue from the buildout but appear to carry EPS flat-to-up (Q3-26 around $1.21/ADS on one vendor's page) without the D&A tail landing in a 40.9%-margin mix. That is the mechanical gap. It is a known-date gap, not a secret.

## Why we might have edge

- **A subtraction nobody published.** The issuer retired the net-cash-net-of-debt measure it used to disclose in 2025 (defined as cash + short-term investments + long-term time deposits/HTM **less** total loans, convertible notes and notes payable — [Q2-25 release](https://ir.baidu.com/news-releases/news-release-details/baidu-announces-second-quarter-2025-results)) and now publishes only the gross figure, which additionally *adds* illiquid long-term investments. Our own red bench initially called this "uncheckable" and docked itself two conviction points. It is checkable: the debt inputs sit in the same 6-K. Doing the subtraction ourselves is a small but genuine informational edge over anyone quoting the gross headline.
- **The D&A cliff is arithmetic, dated, and primary-sourced.** FY25 annual depreciation vs quarterly capex is a public number pair. The gap between it and flat-to-up consensus EPS is the trade — if there is one.
- **Nobody can check the backlog, and that asymmetry is the finding.** There is no RPO, no contracted-revenue table, no AI-Cloud customer concentration disclosure anywhere in the 6-K/20-F set. Growth is quarter-print-only. Anyone underwriting "durable" cloud growth is underwriting a number that does not exist in the filings. We are not.
- **Cheap optionality if the price comes to us.** At ~$76–81 the stub is 5–6x ex-cash earnings on the broad net-liquid basis. That is a price at which we are paid to be wrong about the depreciation tail.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- The FCF collapse was **consensus** — every print write-up carried it. Our red bench tagged it CONSENSUS itself.
- The ad decline is four quarters old and the stock is 40% off its high; a name 11% off its low is not a fresh dislocation, it is a name where the bear case has already been expressed.
- The balance-sheet floor is not a secret either; the gross number is in the release, and most buyers do not need our subtraction to know the company is cash-rich.
- The only genuinely non-consensus legs — the metric retirement and the depreciation tail — are (a) hygiene rather than valuation, and (b) a lag effect that the market can re-price in a single afternoon at the print. Being early on a mechanically-dated re-rate is not the same as being right, and it is not worth a carry.
- Stock Connect inclusion is, on the normal base rate, **demand-additive**, not a drain. Our venue-migration worry is a hypothesis with zero data behind it right now.

**Net: our edge is a subtraction and a lag, not an insight about the business. That is worth a limit order, not a market order.**

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — The value trap converts (most likely)
We buy at $80 because the stub is 5x ex-cash earnings; over the next four to six quarters the depreciation from the RMB11B/qtr capex lands, non-GAAP earnings halve, and the stub that was 5x is 12x on the new number, at which point it is no longer cheap and there is no catalyst to hold for. Meanwhile the cash floor we underwrote shrinks by RMB44B/yr from burn plus buyback. We lose 25–35% and the thesis never breaks in a single identifiable moment — it just decays.
*Monitorable tripwires: quarterly D&A line vs prior quarter; gross margin below 38%; capex ex-iQIYI staying above RMB9B; net-liquid estimate falling below 45% of market cap.* **Armed: none.**

### Failure narrative 2 — Controller-and-sovereign discretion (NO monitorable tripwire)
A dual-class controller with 10-vote Class B shares over a Cayman holdco with VIE-held mainland operations decides something we cannot see coming and cannot pre-empt: a large related-party asset injection, a capex mandate accepted for policy reasons, a domestic-chip procurement directive, a re-escalation of US listing/audit politics, or a tightening of the mainland cash-transfer restrictions the 20-F already flags qualitatively. Any one of these can re-rate the balance-sheet floor to a fraction of face value overnight. **There is no filing, no dataset, and no price series that leads this.** The disclosure appears simultaneously with, or after, the tape moves.
**Unknown-unknown class named: *outside-the-disclosure-perimeter discretion* — decisions taken by a controlling shareholder or a sovereign, first observable at the print or on the tape, with no monitorable precursor.** A related sub-class: *definitional discretion* — the issuer controls whether the metric our own gate depends on (restored net cash) is ever published again, and in what form. There is no sensor for "a number the company chose not to print."
**Because no tripwire exists, this risk must be paid for in size, not in monitoring: it is the reason the sleeve is capped at a quarter and unlevered.**

### Failure narrative 3 — The reflexive / structural leg
- **Our own size vs ADV:** this is the *benign* half. BIDU ADS turnover is deep (estimated ~7–10M ADS/day, roughly $0.7–1.0B notional — **estimate, unverified, see open item 10**). A quarter-sleeve at any plausible book size is well under 2% of one day's volume. We cannot move this stock and we will not be trapped by our own clip. **We are the price taker here, and that is the correct assumption to hold until the ADV data says otherwise.**
- **The structural half is the ADV assumption itself.** Stock Connect inclusion became effective 2026-09-07 and dual-primary conversion is expected within 2026. If marginal price discovery and index demand migrate to the Hong Kong line (9888.HK), the ADS becomes the residual venue: thinner, wider, and prone to a persistent discount to the HK parity price. Our exit liquidity assumption — the one thing that makes the "quarter-sleeve, unlevered" sizing safe — is exactly the thing the Connect event puts in question. Our blue bench reduced this to PLAUSIBLE precisely because **no ADV or parity data was pulled by either bench.** We are sizing on an unmeasured liquidity assumption.
- **Doctrine interactions, stated plainly:**
 (i) Our **net-cash-floor doctrine** and our **metric-retirement detector** point opposite directions on the same fact. The floor doctrine says "probably clears 50%, therefore support"; the retirement detector says "the issuer's choice to stop publishing it *is* the datum." If we let the floor doctrine win, we have just underwritten a number the issuer can redefine at will. This deck deliberately lets the detector win and demands the *restored line*, not our own reconstruction, as the gate.
 (ii) Our **reopen-on-restored-disclosure gate is gameable.** An issuer that wants the re-rate can restore a net-cash line on a flattering definition. The gate therefore requires the *2025 definition verbatim*, ex-iQIYI, not any net-cash line.
 (iii) Our **runway_calculator** fired soft (≈3-year clock, no going-concern) while our **buy-the-floor** instinct reads the same balance sheet as support. Same numerator, opposite conclusions; we resolve it by pricing the floor at a haircut, not at face.
 (iv) The **prize-table mandate** and the blue bench's "N/A — not a pre-revenue developer" ruling collided. The deck overrules the bench: there are time-gated private unknowns, so the table is built below.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we bought a stub that only *looked* cheap — the depreciation tail from a roughly 5x asset-base step landed in a 40.9%-gross-margin mix and turned 9x ex-cash earnings into 20x+ without the stock ever giving us a single clean moment to get out, while the balance-sheet floor we underwrote was simultaneously drained by ~RMB44B/yr of capex and buyback and haircut by mainland cash-transfer restrictions we never quantified.
