Say the awkward part first. We bought this as a de-risked growth story near its lows, the thesis worked faster than modeled — and then our own fair-value anchor went stale in a single print. The re-rate court that fixed it split: the challenge bench said the market had already priced everything; the review bench caught the challenge bench using the wrong peer growth rates. The corrected answer lifted fair value ~9%, not to peer parity — because a governance discount this specific doesn't disappear on a good quarter.

Pane 1 · What we believe

The solvency question died; the growth is real; the discount is earned but finite

Caris IPO'd in June 2025 with going-concern doubt in its own S-1. That question is dead: four consecutive operating inflections, and the August 5 quarter printed $263.7M revenue (+45%), gross margin 68% (+500bps), adjusted EBITDA $55.7M, positive free cash flow, and a guidance raise to $1.03–1.04B — the second raise this year, adding ~$25M to the second half beyond banking the beat. The near-term catalyst stack (Assure blood-biopsy ramp at 10,700 cases, Detect launch) is funded. What remains between the price and the bull case is not the business — it's the register: a control-family pledge, an unremediated accounting weakness, and a fresh-IPO multiple debate.

Pane 2 · What the market believes

Half the street's growth deserves half the street's multiple — minus the hair

At ~5.9× forward sales, Caris trades at roughly a third of Natera's and Guardant's multiples. The bear's version: the +45% is optical — reimbursement and mix, not volume (clinical cases grew +18%) — so durable growth is ~20%-class and the discount is the correct price for a controlled company (founder family 44.6%) with pledged stock and an accounting department its own former auditor walked away from. On this view the market has already run the court and priced its verdict.

Pane 3 · Why we might have edge

The bear's comp math was wrong — by enough to matter

The review bench re-pulled the peers' actual guidance: Natera guides +31% ex-true-ups (not the +37% the bear used) and Guardant +36–38% — and the bear compared Caris's volume growth to the peers' revenue growth, an asymmetric test. Apples-to-apples, Caris guides 27.5% against peers' 31–38%: a ratio of ~0.8, not 0.5. Growth-adjusting the peer band gives ~13×; applying the full 25–35% governance discount still lands 8.5–10× — comfortably above the ~5.9× it trades at. The discount is deserved; its current depth is not.

Two more facts the bear missed: the ASP "lap" argument understates Assure — blood-biopsy mix grows into the number rather than lapping out of it; and the founder family bought $990k of stock in the open market near the May lows, which does not look like a family preparing a pledge cascade.

Pane 4 · Why it might be priced in

The tape already moved, and the hair is real

The stock is +40% off the pre-print level in three sessions and sits at a 26-week high — chasing a re-rate into day-3 momentum is how good theses get bad cost bases. And the discount stack is not cosmetic: 25M founder-family shares (8.8% of the company) are pledged against personal debt — a forced-sale channel that opens at higher prices; the material weakness (insufficient qualified accounting resources for complex transactions, per the fresh 10-Q) is still unremediated, the auditor change of 2024 was company-initiated (a tender EY lost to Deloitte — the prior record's “EY declined re-election” was wrong and is corrected here); and consensus sits at $26.67 — barely above the court's own base. The re-rate case is for $25.5, not $31, until the register cleans up.
