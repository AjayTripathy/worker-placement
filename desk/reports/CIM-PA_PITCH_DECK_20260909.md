## What we believe

Chimera Investment is a mortgage REIT. It borrows, owns residential loans and mortgage bonds, and pays out the spread. It has four preferred share classes stacked side by side — A, B, C, D — all with the same seniority, all callable by the company at any time, all $25 face value. Series A (CIM-PA) is the only one with a fixed payment: $0.50 a quarter, $2.00 a year, forever. At $20.89 that's a 9.57% cash yield.

What we actually believe, after both benches:

1. **The income is probably real for now.** The company declared Series A's $0.50 on 5-Aug-26, payable 30-Sep-26, and all four preferred classes together cost about $21.2M a quarter against a common-share payout of about $37M that management can cut first. That's a 1.75x cushion of cuttable money sitting underneath us, and roughly 2.8x on earnings if the reported $39M of "earnings available for distribution" is after preferred payments.
2. **But we would be buying the wrong ticket in the right theatre.** The same 5-Aug-26 filing resets the other three: B at 9.778%, D at 9.366%, C at 8.730% of face — versus A's fixed 8.000%. A pays the least of the four, and it trades at the *smallest* discount to face. Same company, same rank, same call terms, less cash.
3. **The one honest argument for A is time, not today.** A's payment never falls. The others float with short-term rates and have already been shrinking — B's payment was 11.38% two years ago and is 9.778% now. The gap between A and B has closed from 338bp to 178bp. If rates keep falling, A's fixed coupon eventually wins. That is a real mechanism, and it is why we say "wrong price," not "wrong idea."
4. **The trigger that put this on the screen was fake.** The price dipped through $21.00 because the stock went ex-dividend on 1-Sep-26 and mechanically dropped ~$0.50. That happens four times a year. Nothing dislocated.

**Disposition: REDIRECT, not reject.** No position in CIM-PA at $20.89.

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we believe, and it has believed it in print for months. Retail preferred-income coverage already ranks B and C as the better buys and has called A expensive — a mid-Aug-26 retail note put CIM-A at 9.27% versus CIM-B at 10.24% and used the word "overpriced." A 13-Jul-26 Seeking Alpha screen had C ~11.0%, B 10.98%, D 10.71%.

The market also believes — correctly, in our view — that a fixed 8% coupon deserves to trade closer to face than a floating coupon that is visibly shrinking. That is not sloppiness; it's a rational price for a payment that doesn't decay.

Where the market may be lazy: nobody is pricing the *credit* of this stack. Book value per share is $17.75 and fell 3.2% in the quarter — about 12% a year if that pace holds. Total equity of roughly $1.47bn sits over about $930M of preferred face. Nobody in the retail preferred press is running that clock, and that clock is the only thing that actually threatens the coupon.

## Why we might have edge

- **We priced the crossover, they didn't.** Using the reset formula in the company's own 8-K, Series C's floating payment only drops below A's fixed 8% if 3-month term SOFR falls under about 3.00% (2.995% with the 26.161bp tenor adjustment); B's only below about 1.95%. Implied SOFR today is ~3.725%. So "A wins on fixed" is not a today trade — it's a 2027-28 trade that requires meaningful further cuts. Most retail write-ups compare today's coupons and stop.
- **We anchored value to the company's own borrowing cost, not a sector yield table.** Chimera's registered senior notes carry 9.000% (2029), 9.250% (2029), 8.875% (2030), and those notes trade at or slightly above face — so the company's real marginal cost is ~8.8-9.0%. CIM-PA at 9.57% is only ~72bp more for going from senior debt to a perpetual with no maturity. That is a thin payment for a large step down in the stack.
- **That same fact kills the "it might get called" story.** No company redeems 8% money to replace it with 9% money. Series A is Chimera's cheapest capital and therefore the *last* thing it calls. Any deck that books call upside as return is booking a fantasy. (Corollary the red bench missed: B and D at 9.4-9.8% are the ones plausibly called, which adds pull-to-face on top of their bigger coupon.)
- **The screen artifact is generalizable.** Any $25-face preferred with a quarterly coupon above ~2% of price will trip a price band four times a year on the ex-dividend drop alone. We now suppress those.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: most of it is.

- The "A is rich, B is better" call is already retail consensus. We are not first.
- The fixed-versus-floating decay argument is also published — it's the standard defence of fixed preferreds in a cutting cycle.
- The only genuinely uncrowded piece is the book-value erosion clock, and it's uncrowded because it's slow and hard to trade. It won't reprice on a headline.
- The 72bp step over the company's own senior notes may not be a mispricing at all. REIT preferred dividends generally qualify for the §199A deduction; interest on baby bonds does not. For the top-bracket individual who sets both of these tapes, that's worth roughly 100bp after tax — which would explain most of the "too-thin" spread. We have not verified this for CIM specifically (see unverified #6), and it is the single biggest reason our relative-value read could be wrong in the boring direction.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy the decay story and rates don't cooperate (monitorable).** We eventually buy A on the argument that floaters are converging. The Fed stops cutting at 3.5-4.0%. C never crosses below A, B never comes close, and we sit for two years earning ~100bp less than the identical sibling while the opportunity cost compounds. *Tripwire: 3-month term SOFR forward curve at each reset date; if the 12-month-forward SOFR stays above 3.25%, the convergence thesis is dead and we swap to B/C.*

**Failure narrative 2 — Book value keeps bleeding and the preferred re-rates (monitorable).** BVPS $17.75, −3.2% in the quarter. At that pace, equity cover over preferred face erodes ~12%/yr. Nothing breaks for a long time — and then a common-dividend cut lands, the whole preferred complex widens 150-250bp, and A goes to $18 or below. *Tripwire: quarterly BVPS change; two consecutive quarters worse than −2% is our exit, not our entry.*

**Failure narrative 3 — Collateral credit turns inside the securitizations, and we do not see it (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).** Chimera's risk lives in non-agency residential loan pools and its non-QM originator (HomeXpress/Palisades). The number that matters is the 60+ day delinquency roll inside those pools, and **we have no connector for it.** Both benches flagged this and neither could fill it. Loan-pool deterioration shows up in remittance data we don't subscribe to, then in book value a quarter later, then in the preferred price a day after that. By the time our BVPS tripwire fires, the move has happened. **Unknown-unknown class: opaque-collateral credit migration at a consolidated VIE — the loss is inside securitization trusts we do not receive loan-level tapes for, so the failure is invisible until it is disclosed.** A second, related blind class: the un-fetched business-combination note for the acquired originator — an unaudited-absorption question (three acquired offices, purchase accounting unseen) that could carry goodwill or rep-and-warranty exposure we've never priced.

**Reflexive / structural leg.** Series A is the smallest and thinnest of the four: 5.8M shares, about $121M of float. We have no same-day volume rail, so we estimate average daily volume at roughly 15-30k shares (~$300-600k) from the standard base rate for a $25-face REIT preferred of this size. A $10k starter is ~479 shares, roughly 2-3% of a 20k-share day — no impact on the way in, and one to two days on the way out in a calm tape. In a stress tape, preferred bid depth on the small series evaporates first, and the spread we cross could exceed a full quarter's coupon. That asymmetry is the real position-sizing constraint, not the credit.

Predicted flow is the other reflexive hazard: retail preferred screens run the same "discount to face" filter we did, they will trip on the same ex-dividend drops on ~1-Dec-26, ~1-Mar-27, and every quarter after, and the thin series moves most on that flow. We would be buying into mechanical noise and mistaking it for entry skill.

Doctrine interactions worth naming: (a) our "price above fair value ⇒ full force" rule would have vetoed this at the red bench's $17.6-18.4 fair value, but that fair value rested on an unsourced 200-350bp assumption and was overturned — so the doctrine nearly fired on a bad number; (b) our "verdict must rest on CONFIRMED, not PLAUSIBLE" rule is what actually saved us, twice, in both directions.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we got paid 9.6% to own the weakest-paying line in a stack whose collateral was quietly deteriorating inside securitizations we cannot see into, and the sibling series we should have owned paid us ~100bp more to take exactly the same hit.

**Options / expression math: NOT APPLICABLE, and stated rather than skipped.** CIM-PA is a preferred share class with **no listed options chain.** There is no chain-level midpoint IV, no candidate strikes to price against C_ours = Σ pᵢ × max(Sᵢ − K, 0), no IV × √T to set against σ_table, no IV percentile and no VRP to compute. This is the one case where the arithmetic cannot run, and we are not going to substitute the underlying's aggregate feed for a chain that doesn't exist — that is exactly the TSSI error (aggregate feed showed 71 IV, chain-level midpoint was 101; they diverge and the decision flips).

**Instrument recommendation, as the output of the above:** with no chain, the expression set collapses to **stock or nothing.** Cheap-IV-routes-to-calls and rich-IV-routes-to-premium-selling are both unavailable. **Output: NONE at $20.89.** If the reopen level is hit and gates turn green, the only expression is common-stock-style outright purchase of the preferred, starter size. Using CIM *common* options as a proxy is explicitly rejected — the common is a different instrument with different risk (equity residual, not fixed claim), and the correlation is unstable in exactly the scenario-4 state we care about.

**TAX overlay.** No position is held, so there is nothing to harvest and no wash-sale exposure. Two forward notes: (a) if 2026 turns out to be a harvest year, the loss branch of any CIM-PA lot bought after roughly mid-December would not be realizable until 2027 — an entry inside the last two weeks of December defers the loss past 31-Dec and is excluded unless the sibling-parity edge is numerically dominant, which at a ~100bp carry gap it is not; (b) if we ever do own A and want to swap to B or C at a loss, different series of the same issuer are **not** substantially identical, so the swap harvests the loss cleanly while keeping the exposure — that swap is the preferred way to correct this position if we ever get it wrong.
