# PITCH — CG — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — WATCH — pre-event print (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Carlyle's stock has fallen 37% from its 52-week high (pack tape: $43.105 vs $68.4603 high, $39.60 low — so we are only 8.9% above the low). The market is treating Carlyle as if a private-credit accident is already inside its earnings. We believe two things:

1. **The fee business has not been measured since the damage.** The last real fee print is the Q2'26 10-Q filed 2026-08-10 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001527166/000152716626000045/cg-20260630.htm), which predates the September dislocation. In that print the credit fee line was *accelerating*, not decaying: Global Credit fee-related earnings ran $138mm vs $111mm a year earlier (+24%), and the implied fee rate on credit assets rose from ~21.9bps to ~26.2bps. So the thing the bear story is about had, at last look, gotten better.
2. **The price embeds target failure.** At $43.105 against management's 2028 deal-earnings-per-share target of $6.00, we are paying 7.18x a three-year-out number. That is a discount for the target missing, not a price that assumes it is met.

What we do **not** believe is that we have proven the damage is absent. The honest class for this name, adopted by both benches, is **DAMAGE-UNMEASURED (pre-event print)**. We are buying a small amount of a cheap manager ahead of the first venue that can tell us anything, and we are paying for the right to learn.

## What the market believes

The market believes Carlyle is a leveraged bet on private credit at exactly the wrong moment, and it has evidence:

- Carlyle's own listed lending vehicle, CGBD, printed net asset value per share of $15.61 at 6/30/26 — down from $15.89 and $16.26 before it, three declines in a row (−4.0%), with two new non-accruals and net investment income covering the (already cut) dividend at exactly 1.00x (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001544206/000154420626000055/csl-20260630.htm).
- Headline assets rose faster than *fee-paying* assets in Q2'26 (AUM +4% to $485bn vs fee-earning AUM +3% to $334bn; 68.9% fee-bearing and falling), and a record $28bn of "pending" fee-earning AUM is explicitly not billing yet.
- The marginal seller is still working: we are 8.9% off the low, not 30% off it.

And critically: **this long is not a new idea.** A street note on 2026-05-31 argued the same thing we are arguing — limited private-credit exposure, ~11x, fair value in the mid-$60s, buy — and the stock has since lost about a third. We are not early. We are the same trade, later and cheaper.

## Why we might have edge

- **Timing, not information.** Our edge is that the first post-dislocation fee print lands 2026-10-29 and nobody — including us — has the numbers. Buying a 7.2x multiple *before* the measurement, in small size, is a different trade from buying an 11x multiple in May.
- **One real analytic correction.** The bear "fee base is decaying" story is mostly an accounting artifact. Fee-earning AUM is struck on committed/invested **cost**; total AUM carries **fair value**. When the portfolio appreciates, the fee-bearing *ratio* mechanically falls even though nothing has decayed. Corporate Private Equity AUM −1% is late-life fee step-down, which is what a harvesting fund looks like. Our Blue bench caught this and it overturned Red's headline attack.
- **The insider cluster is benign.** Eight Form 4s from eight different filers on the same day (2026-08-28), eighteen days after an S-8 comp-plan registration, is a scheduled vest — not distribution, and not a forced seller.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The consensus objection stands as a sizing instrument.** Neither bench could produce a single consensus estimate for the metric our thesis rests on (Q3 Global Credit FRE ≥ $138mm). We therefore cannot show our number is above the street's — we only know the street liked the stock at $60+ and the stock is $43.
- **The cheapness may be correctly cheap.** 7.2x a 2028 target is only a discount if the target survives. The target is management's, it is denominated in *deal earnings* (which contain marks), and it has never been tested in this tape.
- **The buyback is not doing what bulls say.** Diluted shares went from 361.3mm (6/30/23) to 367.0mm (6/30/25) — **up 1.58%** across two years of continuous repurchase, against roughly $390–440mm/yr of stock compensation. Buying back stock that comp re-issues is a wash, and the per-share target is the one metric that flatters from it.
- **"Targets have not been withdrawn" is nearly worthless.** A company can withdraw guidance any day via an 8-K; Carlyle simply hasn't used a venue. Non-withdrawal through five weeks of dislocation is weak-positive at best.

## What could go wrong  (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

**Failure narrative #1 — the damage lands in the mark lines, not the fee line (monitorable, but only quarterly).**
At an alternative manager, a market drawdown hits **net accrued performance revenue** (unrealized carry) and **equity-method credit marks** — including the ~$86.4bn Insurance Solutions / Fortitude sleeve — long before it hits management fees. Both of those land in *deal earnings*, which is the exact metric the $6.00 2028 target is written in. Fee-related earnings can print a record straight into the damage. Neither bench had these numbers; neither priced them. Tripwire: Q3 10-Q accrued-carry and insurance notes, 2026-10-29. This is the one we think is most likely.

**Failure narrative #2 — credit cycle, not credit scare (monitorable).**
CGBD's NAV falls again, non-accruals go above 1.2%, dividend coverage breaks below 1.00x, and the read-through stops being "a 0.33%-of-fee-base indicator" and starts being "the sponsor's underwriting." Tripwires: CGBD's next 10-Q/NAV release and Carlyle's own credit segment disclosure.

**Failure narrative #3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: private-affiliate mark opacity.**
Fortitude is a private insurance affiliate. It has no tape, no continuous filings, and no venue that prints between Carlyle's quarters. If its investment portfolio or reinsurance economics deteriorate, we find out when Carlyle chooses to tell us, in an equity-method line, in arrears — and there is no sensor we can build that fires earlier. **Unknown-unknown class: "private-affiliate mark opacity (no continuous reporting venue)."** A second class rides alongside it: **"consolidated-fund/VIE accounting opacity"** — the pack's cash-flow rows are Q1-only stubs flipping sign (−$352mm, +$71mm, −$113mm, −$372mm) because consolidated-fund flows dominate them, so we cannot audit cash conversion at all. Neither bench may use that series, and we do not.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size vs ADV:* not in the pack. Estimated Carlyle ADV ~3–4mm shares (~$130–170mm/day) from large-cap alt-manager base rates. A 0.50%-of-book starter is a rounding error against that — **our flow has no market impact and no reflexivity.** The structural risk runs the other way: we are the small participant in a crowded, drawdown-heavy name where the marginal seller sets the price.
- *Predicted flow:* redemption-driven selling in alt-manager baskets, index/ETF de-risking, and tax-loss selling into December on a name 37% off its high. That flow is indifferent to our fee-rate argument and can push through $39.60 without any new information.
- *Doctrine interactions:* our own pattern library fought itself here. `feaum_activation_wedge` **fired and was wrong** — it mistook mark appreciation for fee decay, and needs an appreciation/step-down guard or it will misfire on every rising-mark PE book. `carry_and_insurance_mark_channel_vs_fre_decoy` is the doctrine that actually describes this name, and it was only raised on rebuttal, after the first bench had already spent its strongest kill elsewhere. `multi_venue_disclosure_consistency` fired correctly (the original brief preferred a transcript gloss over the 10-Q's arithmetic). If we are wrong, it will likely be because we litigated the fee line while the risk sat in the mark line — a doctrine-selection error, not a data error.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that the September dislocation shows up in the mark-driven deal-earnings inputs — net accrued carry and the Fortitude/Insurance Solutions equity-method line — which no bench could measure before the print, and the 2028 $6.00 per-share target gets qualified or withdrawn on 2026-10-29.

**Expression math (run on the table itself).** No chain was retrievable, so these are *gates on real quotes*, not orders. All must be re-priced off **chain-level midpoint IV and actual bid/ask for the named strike** — never the underlying's aggregate IV feed; the two diverge badly (the TSSI lesson: 71 vs 101).

- C_ours(K=45, Dec-18-2026) = Σ p·max(S−45,0) = 0.25×9 + 0.35×1 = **$2.60**. The IV that reproduces $2.60 on a 48-day $45 call at S=43.105 is **≈53%**. A 40-vol chain prints that call near **$1.80**.

- P_ours(K=40) = Σ p·max(40−S,0) = 0.25×4 + 0.15×12 = **$2.80** → table-implied **≈71%**; a 40-vol chain prints ≈$1.09.

**Output of that arithmetic:**
- **Starter: STOCK.** EV is flat; there is no drift to lever, and options would pay time-decay for a zero-drift view.
- **Gated add: CALLS permitted** — Dec-18-2026 $45s (or $50s), *only if* chain midpoint IV prints **below 45%**, giving us a ≥15% cushion to our table's 49–53%. If chain IV ≥ 50%, the market has already bought our dispersion and the add reverts to stock.
- **PREMIUM SELLING: REFUSED, on our own numbers.** A cash-secured $40 put is the obvious "get paid to wait" trade and our table prices it at $2.80 — about 71 vol. Any realistic chain (≈40 vol, ≈$1.09) would have us selling a tail we think is more than twice as fat as quoted. We do not sell that put. This is the premium-selling gate failing, not being skipped.

Estimated RV 42–48% (alt-manager dislocation base rate) against estimated IV 38–46% → **VRP ≈ 0 to slightly negative**, i.e. options are probably *not* rich here, consistent with the calls-not-puts routing above. Arm the vol sensor before acting.

A lognormal chain will systematically misprice that shape — which is exactly where a long-wings, short-nothing expression earns its keep, and exactly why a short put is the wrong side.
