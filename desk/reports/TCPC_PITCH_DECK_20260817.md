# PITCH — TCPC — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-17 — KILL** — flat, no position, and the binding reason is that we cannot trade the size, not that the marks are wrong.

---

## What we believe

BlackRock TCP Capital (TCPC, $4.06, ~$340M market cap) is a business development company — a listed fund that lends to mid-sized private companies. On August 4, 2026 it sold 78 of its 134 loans into a new vehicle bought 95% by Pantheon for ~$152M gross, and used the proceeds to pay down debt. The filed pro-forma balance sheet at 6/30/26 shows investments of $778.6M, debt of $318.9M and net assets of $494.9M — about $5.90 of book value per share against a $4.06 stock ([EX-99.1, 8-K filed 2026-08-06](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370755/000119312526336845/tcpc-ex99_1.htm)).

We came in believing the stock was cheap because the loans were marked *conservatively* — that management was writing things down harder than peers, so book value was, if anything, understated. Several supporting facts held up under attack and were never contradicted: TCPC carries its non-performing loans at roughly 19 cents on the dollar where FSK carries similar exposure near 48 cents and MFIC near 55 cents; its incentive fee hurdle has not been met since 2013, so the manager currently accrues no performance fee; its payment-in-kind income (interest paid in more debt rather than cash — usually a sign of stress being deferred) fell 48% while peers' rose; and it carries the disputed Kellermeyer loan at 7.2 cents while FSK carries the same credit at 98.4 cents and still accrues interest on it.

**What we now believe, after the court:** none of that is enough to own the stock. The remaining, defensible case is a 17–31% discount to a genuinely de-levered balance sheet, a dividend covered roughly 80–92%, and a ceiling of about +45%. That is a good outcome, not an exceptional one — and the stock trades $1.34M a day. Under our own rule of never being more than 1% of daily volume in a name we publish on, that is about $13,000 a day of participation. A position we cannot build and cannot exit is not a position, however right the analysis. **Flat.**

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a shrinking, externally-managed credit fund with a dividend that is going to be cut again, and prices it accordingly. Sell-side consensus is **Sell** with a $4.33 target — sixteen analysts cover the BDC space and this is not an orphan security. The stock is 44% below its 52-week high but already 31% off its low, having risen roughly 10% on the deal announcement, so the market has already re-rated *once* on the news we were reacting to.

The marginal holder is a yield buyer: a 16.7% headline yield on a $0.17 quarterly dividend is one of the most-scanned screens in retail income investing. Book value per share has fallen from $9.18 to $6.58 over five quarters. The market's view, plainly stated, is: the discount is compensation for a book that keeps shrinking and a manager who keeps cutting.

## Why we might have edge

Three things a screen does not see:

1. **The balance sheet is genuinely fixed, and the fix is very recent.** Before the sale, asset coverage was 160.6% against a 150% regulatory floor — about 6.6% of further markdown before the company would have breached. After the sale it is 255%. That is a different company, and the pro-forma statement showing it was filed only on August 6.
2. **The buyback silence was a legal constraint, not disinterest.** TCPC repurchased only $2.87M of a $50M authorization in the first half. Both benches initially read that as a board unwilling to back its own discount. It is better explained mechanically: buying back stock shrinks net assets, which is the numerator of the coverage ratio, and with only 6.6% of room the board could not spend meaningfully. That constraint lifted on August 4. Q3 repurchase pace is therefore the first buyback number that carries any information at all.
3. **The mark-integrity evidence.** If the 19-cent non-accrual carrying, the zero incentive fee since 2013, and the 48% PIK decline mean what they appear to mean, this manager recognises losses earlier than the cohort — which would make its remaining book more trustworthy than peers', not less.

## Why it might be priced in

- **Consensus is Sell at $4.33, above the tape.** Nobody is missing this; the Street simply thinks it is worth roughly what it costs.
- **The discount is normal for what this is.** A sub-scale, externally-managed BDC in run-off-adjacent posture trading at 0.69x reported book is not an anomaly. Adjust book down even modestly for what a liquidation actually costs and the discount lands at 17%, which is unremarkable.
- **The dividend is under-earned.** Rebuilding earnings off the filed pro-forma asset base at the realized yield gives about $0.155/share against a $0.17 dividend — roughly 91%. It is a trim, not a break, but it is a trim, and the shareholder base is there for the payout.
- **The markdown may not be finished.** Book value has fallen for five straight quarters. Buying into a decline that is still in progress means bidding against the seller who is still selling.
- **There is no date on anything.** The company's own language on the strategic-alternatives process is "no specific timetable."

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry at any price today. Size zero.** This is not a valuation call — at $4.06 the stock is plausibly 17–31% below a defensible book value. It is a capacity call: $1.34M of daily volume permits roughly $13,000/day of participation at our own trading limits, against a catalyst with no date. We would spend months building a position we could not exit.

Reopen gates, reproduced exactly as adjudicated:

> - Q3 10-Q ONLY, and BOTH: (i) retained-book depreciation < 3% AND (ii) Q3 repurchases >= $10M now that coverage permits them.
> - SOURCES-AND-USES RECONCILIATION (8-K/A or the Q3 10-Q): debt -$591.6M vs $152M gross proceeds and a $141M cash decline do not tie. No clearing price is stateable until it does — this is the finding that blocks BOTH the bull and the bear case, and it is now the PRIMARY reason for FLAT.
> - A DATED catalyst. 'No specific timetable' on the bankers' process is not one, and the markdown may not be finished (NAV 9.18->6.58 over five quarters) — value-ladder flow gate: do not bid into a markdown still in progress.
> - The mark-integrity legs (19.4c recognition, zero incentive fee since 2013, PIK -48%) are RETAINED AS COHORT SCREENING KNOWLEDGE, not a TCPC entry case.

In plain terms: we look again only when the next quarterly report shows the loans they kept have *not* been written down much, when the company has actually bought back a meaningful amount of its own stock now that it legally can, when the $591.6M/$152M gap is explained, and when someone puts a date on the strategic process. All four, not any one. And even then, the size constraint has to be re-argued separately — a reopened thesis does not automatically become an ownable position.

## What remains unverified

- **What Pantheon actually paid.** Unresolvable from filed documents. The −$57.1M line mixes loss and transaction expenses by the filing's own footnote, and the sources and uses do not reconcile. Our original thesis — that the marks are wrong in our favour — is neither confirmed nor refuted. We should say that plainly rather than claim a win or concede a loss.
- **How the 56 retained loans are marked.** Disclosed non-accruals (7.4% at cost, 1.6% at fair value) are consolidated *pre-transaction* only. The retained-book split is not disclosed anywhere. Only the Q3 Schedule of Investments closes this.
- **Whether the write-downs are finished.** Five consecutive quarters of decline, and nothing in the filings says the cycle has turned.
- **The retained portfolio's concentration risk.** 56 loans at 2.8% of net assets each means a single default costs roughly three times what it did in the 134-name book. We have no names to test.
- **Run-rate earnings.** The company filed a pro-forma balance sheet showing a 40% reduction in earning assets and a pro-forma income statement with every line adjusted by **$0**. No filed document states what this company now earns. Every coverage number in this deck — ours, red's, blue's — is a reconstruction.
- **Whether the mark-integrity evidence means what we thought.** The individual facts (19c non-accrual carrying, no incentive fee since 2013, PIK down 48%, Kellermeyer at 7.2c vs FSK's 98.4c) were never contradicted. But the framework we used to rank them against peers was invalidated the next day. Treat these as screening inputs to be re-derived, not as a conclusion.
- **Whether this is ever ownable at our size.** Even if every reopen gate clears, $1.34M of daily volume does not change. That is the constraint that actually decided this, and no filing will fix it.
