# PITCH — BGSI — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line; label clearly "PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION")
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION:** Buy a small starter position (one quarter of intended size, ~0.5% of the book) near $102 ahead of the August 12 earnings report, with all further buying strictly conditional on that report confirming leverage ≤3.0x and continued sales growth — the red bench would stay flat until after the report, and adjudication must break that split.

## What we believe

Boyd Group Services (Gerber Collision & Glass and related brands; CIK 2091467) is a North American collision-repair consolidator whose stock has fallen 44% from its 52-week high of $183.10 to $102.34, and now sits 15% above its 52-week low. We believe most of that decline is mechanical rather than fundamental. Two things happened during the drawdown window: the company did a large debt-funded acquisition (JHCC, 258 sites) plus a share offering priced at $141 in November 2025, which raised net debt to about $2.0 billion, and the equity then amplified every move in the total business value. Converting the stock decline into a change in total enterprise value, the last -27% leg in the shares is only about a -18% move in what the whole business is valued at: from roughly 12.1x annualized EBITDA down to about 9.9x.

Meanwhile the fundamentals the market seems worried about have, per the company's own most recent report, stabilized: Boyd's Q1 2026 press release disclosed record quarterly sales of $996.7 million, adjusted EBITDA of $122.4 million (margin up ~200bps), same-store sales up 1.7% attributed to market-share gains, industry claim volumes down only 0–2% ("now back in-line with Boyd's long-term growth framework"), and pro forma leverage of 2.9x against a stated 2.7x target ([Boyd Q1 2026 press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/boyd-group-services-inc-reports-record-first-quarter-2026-sales-of-996-7-million-and-adjusted-ebitda-of-122-4-million-driven-by-continued-market-share-gains-through-same-store-sales-growth-and-completion-of-strategic-acquisiti-302770420.html)). If the August 12 report confirms that leverage path and that sales trajectory, ~10x EBITDA for a share-gaining consolidator is cheap.

## What the market believes

The market appears to believe some combination of: (1) the pool of repairable car crashes is structurally shrinking — the CCC Crash Course 2026 industry report showed repairable claims down 9.7% in 2025 and a record 23.1% of claims resolved as total losses (cars scrapped instead of repaired), meaning fewer jobs for body shops; (2) Boyd chose the worst moment to lever up ~$1.3 billion to buy more body shops exposed to that shrinking pool; and (3) its headline growth is illusory, because "same-store sales" is measured in dollars, and when repair prices are inflating faster than +1.7%, positive dollar growth is mathematically compatible with fewer actual repairs. On that view, a de-rating from 12x to 10x EBITDA for a levered consolidator of a declining market is not a mispricing — it is the correct price.

## Why we might have edge

The blue bench's decisive finding is that the market's (and our own red bench's) loudest bear datum is **stale**. The CCC industry data covers calendar 2025; Boyd's own Q1 2026 report — published five weeks before the stock's June decline — disclosed that industry volumes had already normalized to just 0–2% down. A sell-off justified by "-10%/year shrinking pool" when the operative disclosed run-rate is 0–2% is a stale-base error, and we caught our own bench making it.

Second, a timeline point: the 2025 volume-decline data was public *before* Boyd's November 2025 share offering priced at $141. Investors who knew that data paid $141. Using the same data to explain the fall from $141 to $102 requires a story about why tolerance for it changed — nobody, including our red bench, supplied one.

Third, the two questions the bears rest on both get answered on August 12 at a knowable date: the leverage reconciliation (disclosed 2.9x pro forma vs the 2.7x target) and whether sales growth continued with volume commentary intact. That gives us a cheap, well-defined test rather than an open-ended argument.

## Why it might be priced in

Honest concessions, and there are several. The original thesis claimed the decline had "no disclosed cause" — both benches refuted that; the total-loss and claims-volume data is published, structural, and a legitimate reason to de-rate this business ([CCC Crash Course 2026](https://www.cccis.com/reports/crash-course-2026)). Record 23.1% total-loss frequency is a real long-run headwind even if 2026 volumes have stabilized, and "0–2% down" is still not growth. The dollar-based same-store-sales metric genuinely cannot prove repair volumes are rising, so the share-gain story rests partly on management's characterization. The move from 12x to ~10x EBITDA may simply be the market correctly repricing a now-more-levered company in a flat-to-shrinking end market — that is a valuation disagreement, not a dislocation. The hoped-for hail-season tailwind is unverified and may even cut against us (hail damage skews toward total losses, which Boyd doesn't repair). And we could not check short interest or crowding (our live brokerage data feed was denied in this run), so we cannot rule out that sophisticated money is already positioned on one side of the August 12 report.
