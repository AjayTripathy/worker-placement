# PITCH — TYRA — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — efficacy read failed (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Tyra is a pre-revenue drug company whose oral FGFR3 pill (dabogratinib) worked well enough on September 9 to keep the program alive and badly enough to break the stock. The company said the drug is safe enough to take every day and picked 60 mg as the dose it will carry into a registrational adjuvant study ([8-K, 2026-09-09](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001863127&type=8-K)).

What we actually believe, in order:

1. **The efficacy read is worse than the bulls say and worse than the original thesis said.** The number the sell side pre-committed to — pooled three-month complete response across the 50 mg and 60 mg cohorts — computes to **12/26 = 46%** against a **≥70%** bar. The company headlined 64% instead, which was a 60 mg-only, best-response-ever figure on 14 patients.
2. **The company is not funded for the plan it just announced.** Cash of $353.9M against a real run-rate of about **$182M/year** is roughly **1.9 years**, and that is *before* a registrational Phase 3, a new 70 mg cohort, and a fifth pediatric dose level. A share sale is close to certain before the next dated catalyst.
3. **The stock is not cheap enough to pay us for either problem.** At $21.73 the whole company is $1,294M, of which $354M is cash, so the market is paying about **$940M for the pipeline**. Our own three-program value table puts base case at **$25.59**. That is +18% with a guaranteed financing event standing in front of it.
4. **Therefore: no position now, and a specific price at which this becomes interesting.** Everything valuable here is in the reopen bands, not in a buy.

## What the market believes

The market believes the arithmetic, not the press release. The stock fell from a $26.73 pre-print close to $21.73 — about **−18.7%** — with an intraday low near $18.76, which is a lot closer to a 46% pooled response than to a 64% headline. So the tape did the pooling itself on day one.

The covering sell side believes something more generous: BofA cut its target to **$39** and kept a Buy. Piper Sandler has modeled peak sales above $1B on this asset alone. So the street's position is roughly "the dose is now known, the safety is clean enough for chronic dosing, and the real prize is the achondroplasia program nobody is paying for." That is not a crazy view — it is the same view our own bull case takes — but it requires paying full price for a program that just missed the bar it was measured against.

The market also does not appear to be pricing the financing. There is a live at-the-market share-sale program with roughly **$250M available** — about 19% of the whole company — and the stock is 47% below its 52-week high. That is an unusually attractive moment for the issuer and an unusually bad one for a new buyer.

## Why we might have edge

- **We priced three programs, not one.** The bear case that got argued in court valued only bladder cancer. The company's own framing is three uses of one molecule: low-grade upper-tract cancer, intermediate-risk bladder cancer, and achondroplasia. The achondroplasia comparator, BioMarin's Voxzogo, ran roughly $900–935M of FY25 revenue, up 26%. Once you credit that leg, base case moves from "below the tape" to "18% above it," which flips the trade from *permanent reject* to *wrong price, come back later*.
- **We did the burn math on the current quarter, not a five-quarter-old one.** The evidence pack's cash-flow data stops at Q1 2025. Using it gives ~$102M/year and a comfortable 3.5-year runway. The actual Q2 2026 filing gives ~$182M/year and ~1.9 years ([10-Q, period ended 2026-06-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001863127/000119312526332995/tyra-20260630.htm)). Nearly the whole bull-vs-bear argument in this name is decided by which of those two numbers you use.
- **We found the subset arithmetic nobody published.** Disclosed overall response was 11/14, with 8/8 in patients who had a single target lesion. That leaves the multiple-lesion patients at **3/6 = 50%**. No outside source we found carried that derivation. It matters because it turns the "8/8!" talking point into a selection artifact.
- **We noticed the dose contradiction.** The company says 60 mg is the dose for the registrational plan. The company also disclosed that response was 86% (12/14) above a drug-exposure threshold and 58% (7/12) below it. Both cannot be comfortable at once: the exposure data is the argument for 70 mg, which is an argument that 60 mg is not the optimized dose.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- The 46% pooled figure was **derivable the same day** — the 50 mg three-month response (33%) and the 60 mg figure (57%) were both public. The "hidden bad number" framing is wrong. Trade press carried the sell-side bar and the cohort splits within hours. A 38%-peak-to-trough intraday move is what a market looks like when it has already done the division.
- The financing risk is not a secret either. The shelf takedown and the share-sale program are on file; anyone can read them.
- The competitive read is public: UroGen's ZUSDURI is an **approved** drug in recurrent low-grade intermediate-risk bladder cancer, and its successor UGN-103 printed 77.8% three-month response with FDA alignment on filing. Everyone covering this name knows Tyra's pill has to beat an approved product.
- The only genuinely non-public things left are (a) whatever the FDA has or hasn't agreed to about the adjuvant endpoint, and (b) the accumulating pediatric safety and efficacy data. Those are the *only* places an edge can live, and neither is drainable by us today.

Net: our differentiation is one valuation frame (three programs) and one number (the real burn). That is thin. It is enough to justify watching; it is not enough to justify paying $21.73.

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — We buy the dip and the company sells us the stock (MONITORABLE)
We enter on a reopen band, and within weeks the issuer prints shares into our bid using the ~$250M available program. Even at today's price a full draw adds ~11.5M shares (59.6M → ~71.1M). Our own base case survives it — $24.97 post-draw versus $25.59 pre-draw — but the *path* is a lower price, a wider spread, and the humiliation of being the exit liquidity for the company's own treasury.
**Tripwire:** any new 424B5, prospectus supplement, 8-K Item 1.01/3.02, or a share count on the next 10-Q cover above ~62M. All monitorable, all free.

### Failure narrative 2 — The adjuvant plan is not the plan the FDA agreed to (PARTIALLY MONITORABLE)
"Registrational adjuvant strategy" is the company's phrase, not a regulator's. Adjuvant means after the tumor is removed, so there is no lesion left to shrink — the endpoint becomes recurrence-free survival, which needs more patients, more years, and more money than an ablation study. If the trial that gets agreed to is twice the size and 18 months longer than modeled, every probability-of-success number in our table is too high and the funding gap widens.
**Tripwire:** weak. Meeting outcomes are disclosed at the company's discretion. We can watch clinicaltrials.gov for a registered protocol with an N and an endpoint, but the absence of a posting tells us nothing.

### Failure narrative 3 — The value quietly leaves the achondroplasia leg (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE)
Most of the upside in our own table — $700–900M of peak sales at 25–40% probability — sits in achondroplasia, where the readout is a pediatric chronic-dosing study whose interim data lives entirely inside the sponsor and its safety monitoring committee until a date they choose. A growth-plate or long-bone signal, a slow enrollment problem, a competitor's unannounced oral program, or a shift in what regulators will accept as a surrogate for height velocity — none of these generate a filing, a price gate, or a registry change before the fact. We would learn about it in the same press release as everyone else, and by then the price is already there.
**Unknown-unknown class, named: PRIVATE-STATE CLINICAL ACCUMULATION** — value-relevant information that legally, routinely, and indefinitely exists off-tape inside a single private counterparty, with no disclosure trigger and no third-party observable proxy. Sibling class: **REGULATORY-DOCTRINE DRIFT** (what the agency will accept as an endpoint changes through advisory-committee precedent and personnel, not through anything we can subscribe to). Both are un-sensored by construction. We do not get a tripwire; we only get to size for it.

### The reflexive / structural leg
- **Our size versus liquidity: unverified, and that is a real gap.** We do not have average daily volume or short interest for this name — both benches reported this as a coverage gap, neither could run it. Estimating from comparable $1–1.5B clinical-stage biotech that just had a 38% intraday event: roughly **1.5–3.0M shares/day** normal, several multiples of that for a week or two post-print. At the sizes contemplated here (0.50–1.25% of book), we are almost certainly a small fraction of one day's volume — but "almost certainly" is doing load-bearing work in that sentence, and the exit in a post-financing, post-disappointment tape is worse than the entry.
- **Predicted flow is against us near-term.** Three 13G/13G-A filings inside six weeks (July 30, August 14 ×2) say institutions are repositioning. A Form 144 on June 26 says registered insider selling. A live share-sale program says the issuer itself is a seller. That is three classes of supply and no identified marginal buyer other than the covering sell side's clients.
- **Reflexivity we create ourselves:** buying a name whose issuer has an open at-the-market program *at a price the issuer likes* converts our capital into the company's runway and caps the very re-rating we are underwriting. Our bid subsidizes the dilution that lowers our per-share value. This is not a tail risk; it is the mechanism.
- **Doctrine interactions:** our "cheap implied volatility at high uncertainty routes gated adds into calls" rule collides with the tax rule here — the decisive fork (the pediatric sentinel readout) sits after December 31, so any option that expresses it defers a loss out of the current year. The two doctrines cannot both be satisfied; the tax rule wins unless the volatility edge is arithmetically dominant, and we cannot show that it is because we do not have a chain. Second collision: our "no entry above our own base case" rule was the entire basis of the reject recommendation — and it was built on a one-asset table that turned out to be wrong. A rule applied to a bad table produces a confident bad answer.

**Filling the blank honestly: we will most likely lose money because we paid for the achondroplasia leg at Voxzogo scale — a number no bench in this file could verify — and then funded the issuer's own share sale on the way down.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both courts say no entry. Recommendation: NO POSITION, NO ORDERS. Reopen bands below.** The dead zone is deliberate: between roughly $16 and $24 we would be buying an unfinanced company at a price its own treasury likes, ahead of a share sale, on data that missed its bar. There is no size at which that is a good trade.

**Reopen R1 — price alone (no new information required)**
- Trigger: **≤ $15.50**. Basis: our bear case is **$14.00/share** and base is **$25.59**, so $15.50 buys ~1.65× to base with ~10% of downside to bear. It also sits ~29% below today's tape, which is roughly where a full share sale plus continued disappointment would put it.
- Starter size: **0.50% of book**.
- Add 1: **≤ $12.50** → +0.50%. Add 2: **≤ $11.70** (52-week low) → +0.25%. **Hard cap 1.25% of book.** Cash alone is $5.94/share, which is the floor this ladder respects.
- Kill rule on the starter: if a share sale prices *below* our average entry, we do not average down further; we cut back to 0.25% and re-court.

**Reopen R2 — financing cleared (event, not price)**
- Trigger: an 8-K or completed offering leaving post-money cash **≥ $550M**, i.e. more than two years of the real $182M/yr burn plus the Phase 3 increment.
- Then entry permitted up to **$24.00** for **0.75% of book** — because the certain event in front of us has happened and our post-draw base case is **$24.97**. Never above 0.95× the recomputed base.

**Reopen R3 — evidence improves (any one of these re-derives the table upward)**
- Pooled three-month complete response **≥70% on ≥26 patients** at a company-dated later cut; or
- multiple-lesion subset response **≥60% with denominators disclosed**; or
- a **written health-authority agreement** that adjuvant recurrence-free survival is the endpoint, with a stated patient number and cost; or
- a 70 mg cohort that holds the clean safety profile while lifting response toward the exposure-response ceiling.
- Then: recompute the table, entry up to **0.90× new base**, size **1.00% of book**.

**Trims/exits:** none — there is nothing to trim. For any future starter: exit in full on (a) a change of the lead indication away from the adjuvant strategy, (b) a Grade 4/5 event or a class toxicity emerging in chronic dosing, (c) cash below one year of burn with no financing announced.
