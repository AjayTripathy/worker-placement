# PITCH — JBI — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position today at $5.11; arm a $4.60 limit for a 0.25% starter, stood down through the 10/29 print, on a novel kill: the broken channel is commercial metal-building doors, not self-storage, and nothing external measures it.

---

## What we believe

Janus makes and installs the doors, hallway systems and locks that go into self-storage facilities, plus sheet doors that go into commercial pre-engineered metal buildings. The stock is $5.11, down 53% from its 52-week high of $10.80 and up 20% from its low of $4.26. There are ~136.4M shares out, so the equity is worth ~$697M. Cash is $127.0M against $550.8M of gross debt, so net debt is ~$424M and enterprise value is ~$1,121M. On the roughly $150M of adjusted EBITDA we are willing to underwrite, that is **7.5x** — and about 2.8x net leverage, at the top of the company's own 2–3x target.

Three things we think are true and not widely stated:

**1. The self-storage business is not what broke.** The 10-Q discloses revenue by channel. Back out the Kiwi II acquisition (closed 2026-01-08, contributed $37.3M in the first half and $19.2M in Q2, and it sells self-storage steel buildings so it lands in new construction) and the picture inverts: new-construction self-storage was **−0.1%** organically in Q2, the restore/rebuild/replace channel was **+6.6%**, and "commercial & other" was **−21.2%**. Across the first half, commercial accounts for **−$19.0M of a −$19.7M total organic decline — about 96% of it.** Management names the culprit on the call: sheet doors into pre-engineered metal buildings, "the biggest drag." Everyone — including both of our own benches on first pass — was pricing a self-storage cycle story.

**2. The cost base is not variable.** Q2 gross profit was $80.3M (34.4%) against $93.2M (40.9%). Even crediting the acquisition with a 5% gross margin — generous, since it lost $3.8M on $19.2M of revenue — organic gross profit fell about $15M on about $14M of lost organic revenue. That is a **93–101% decremental margin**. Costs are not coming out as fast as revenue is going away, which is a structural fact for any downturn of realistic length, not a temporary absorption issue.

**3. Capital allocation went the wrong way into the trough.** Cash fell $194.4M → $127.0M, debt was flat, and $97.2M went to Kiwi II. Net debt rose roughly $65M this year. Meanwhile the buyback ran $15.7M (2.86M shares) in Q1 and then collapsed to **$1.9M (367,096 shares)** in Q2 as the stock fell to ~$5 — while the CFO called the price "very attractive" on the call. $63.1M of authorization sits untouched at the low.

Put together: the multiple is genuinely low, the healthy 72% of the business is holding, and the number underneath the multiple is still falling in a channel nobody is tracking. That is a real opportunity at the right price. $5.11 is not the right price.

## What the market believes

Coverage is thin but real — 5 to 8 analysts. Going into the 8/11 print, consensus for FY26 was **$957.3M of revenue and $0.36 of EPS**. Management then guided to **$925–945M including $80–90M of acquisition revenue** — i.e. the pre-print consensus was *above* the new guide. UBS moved to neutral and cut its target from $6.00 to $5.60, which is 9% above today's tape.

So the market's position is roughly: "the cut is done, the storage cycle is bottoming, the multiple is low, wait for the turn." The stock has already bounced 20% off its low on that reading. The Street is marking the midpoint of the guide; we are underwriting the low end. Nobody in the published work we can see has decomposed the channel data.

This is a *measured* name, not a crowded one — which means low coverage is a timing input, not a reason to believe we see something others cannot.

## Why we might have edge

The edge is one table in one filing, read against a press release nobody links to it.

The 10-Q's revenue disaggregation splits new construction, restore/rebuild/replace, and commercial & other. The Kiwi II acquisition press release tells you the acquired business sells **self-storage** steel buildings, so its $37.3M has to be subtracted from *new construction*, not spread across channels. Do that subtraction — which requires holding two documents at once — and the stated cause of the guide-down dissolves. The storage franchise is flat. The break is a smaller, different end market with a different driver.

The second edge is negative and just as useful: **the external data everyone cites validates the wrong thing.** The storage-starts series (down ~21% from peak) measures the channel that is currently fine. The channel that is actually collapsing — non-residential pre-engineered metal buildings — has no anchor in anyone's brief, including ours. Under our own rule that a mispricing is only real when checked against an independent measure of the *same* event, this thesis currently has no anchor at all. That is the finding, not a footnote.

Third, and smaller: reported revenue is *growing*. FY25 was $884.2M; the first half was $456.2M vs $438.6M, +4.0%; the guide midpoint of $935M is +5.7%. All of that growth is acquired, while adjusted EBITDA falls ~18%. A company buying $37.3M of near-zero-margin construction revenue for $97.2M cash while its core margin compresses is making a capital-allocation statement, and the reported top line hides it. That masking ends mechanically on 2027-01-08 when the acquisition laps.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: a lot of it might be.

- **The stock is already down 53% and the guide was already cut nine days ago.** "Cheap after a cut" is the most common false edge there is. We are not the first people to notice 7.5x.
- **UBS's $5.60 target is only 9% above the tape** — the sell side is close to our own posture, which means our disagreement with the Street is about $925M vs $957M of revenue, not about the direction.
- **The commercial channel is 28% of the business and shrinking.** If it goes to zero it costs maybe $60M of revenue at low margin. The market may simply be netting that against a flat 72% and arriving at the same 7.5x we did.
- **Our EBITDA number may be too low.** We underwrite $150M, the guide's low end, and both benches did the same. Our own experience is that conservative fair-value work runs roughly 13% light because two or three cautious assumptions compound. If the honest number is $165–170M, then today's $5.11 is already 6.6–6.8x and our $4.60 band never fills — we would miss a cheap, cash-generative, cycle-levered operator to save 10%.
- **A named self-help bridge exists that we did not test.** Management describes factory consolidations, optimizations, and resource realignment producing "a sequentially stronger back half." Our red bench asserted "no mechanism — market improvement assumed"; that is half wrong. No *market* improvement is assumed, but a cost bridge is, and neither bench sized it.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the commercial channel keeps going and takes the guide with it (tripwire exists).** Commercial & other went from −2.5% in Q1 to −21.2% in Q2. If Q3 prints −25% or worse, the low end of the guide fails too, management cuts again on 10/29, and $4.60 becomes the *first* stop rather than the floor. We would have filled a limit order into a gap and be sitting on 7.0x of a number that is now $130M, i.e. 8.1x. **Monitorable:** the Q3 10-Q channel table; secondarily, non-residential metal-building starts and shipments, which we do not currently pull.

**Failure narrative 2 — the storage leg breaks after all (tripwire exists, and it is management's own words).** The company's full-year guide says North American organic self-storage is down high single digits. The first half was −0.6%. If that guide is literal rather than stale, the second half of storage has to deteriorate sharply — which makes the total guide *harder* than even the bear case argued, and removes the one genuinely good datum in this thesis. **Monitorable:** same table, and management's framing on the Q3 call. This fork is unresolved and is the single largest open question in the deck.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: the balance sheet and the next deal.** Net debt rose $65M into a downcycle, leverage is at the top of the stated band, the buyback was throttled to almost nothing, and **no bench read the credit agreement.** We have no covenant terms, no step-down schedule, no revolver headroom netted against outstandings. We also have no customer concentration data — the 10-Q read returned no >10% customer disclosure, and the detector that would triangulate this via shipping records was never run, even though the +6.6% restore/rebuild channel plausibly rests on one or two large storage REIT accounts. If there is a springing covenant test, or a lost anchor customer, or another Kiwi-sized acquisition announced on a Tuesday morning, the equity reprices for a reason that has no precursor we could have watched.

The unknown-unknown class here is **facts that exist only inside documents we have not read and events that are announced rather than developed**: credit agreements, customer contracts, letters of intent. For this class the first observable is the 8-K itself. You cannot build a sensor for it; you can only size for it, and refuse to be large before you have read the debt documents.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- *Our own size versus liquidity:* 0.25% of book is a trivial notional for a $700M-cap NYSE name, so our footprint is almost certainly not the risk. But we should be plain that this is an assertion, not a measurement — the IBKR contract-search tool was **permission-denied to all three benches**, so we have a price with no average daily volume behind it. We are sizing against an assumption.
- *We would be bidding into flow we ourselves forecast.* This is the sharpest structural problem in the deck and neither bench raised it. Our thesis says a second guide-down on 10/29 is live. Our band is $4.60. A resting good-till-cancelled order at $4.60 through that print will fill **on the gap created by the exact event we are predicting** — we become the liquidity provider to our own forecast, and a resting bid through an earnings date is an unintentional short volatility position. Two of our standing rules bite here: never bid into your own predicted flow, and no resting order through a catalyst. The band must come down before 10/28's close and be re-placed only after we have read the channel table.
- *Doctrine cutting both ways.* Our rule that a clean court with only a consensus objection ("they guided down") owes a minimum starter does **not** rescue a buy here, because two genuinely novel objections are named — the channel misattribution and the >90% decremental. FLAT is earned rather than defaulted to. But our conservative-fair-value rule cuts the other way and says our $150M is probably light, which argues the $4.60 band is too greedy. And there is no integrity flag on this name, so we are not capped below fair value — the only thing standing between us and ownership is price.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a cheap multiple on an EBITDA number that was still falling — filling at $4.60 on the 10/29 gap, into the second guide-down we ourselves forecast, and discovering that 7.0x on $150M was really 9x on $115M.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry at $5.11. Flat today.** The reason is not "it went down and might go down more" — it is that the channel doing the damage has no independent measure, and we are being asked to buy 20% off the low into a print we expect to be bad.

**Primary band — $4.60 limit, 0.25% of book.**
$4.60 puts enterprise value at ~$1,051M, or **7.0x on the $150M EBITDA we actually underwrite** — i.e. it pays today's multiple for our number rather than the Street's. It is 10% below spot and 8% above the 52-week low. This is a starter, not a position.

**Order handling — the band must not rest through the print.** Place it now, cancel it at the close on **2026-10-28**, and re-place only after reading the Q3 channel table. A resting order at $4.60 through 10/29 fills on the gap from the second guide-down we ourselves predict; that is selling volatility by accident. This constraint is our addition, not the benches'.

**Upgrade to a full starter (0.5–1.0% of book):** only on Q3 evidence that commercial & other is declining **less than 10% year over year**, with the acquisition contribution disclosed separately. That is the single condition that converts this from a cheap-looking cyclical into an ownable one. Sizing range is the standard first tranche for a fairly-priced name, our extension of the benches' "full starter."

**Second rung — $4.00 (≈6.4x), conditional.** Only if the Q3 table shows commercial stabilizing. If commercial is *worse* and the stock is below the 52-week low, do not average down: at that point the denominator is in question, not the multiple, and the correct action is to re-court the name from scratch.

**Trim/exit logic (for the position we do not yet own):** absent an EBITDA upgrade, ~$6.50 is roughly 8.7x on $150M and is where a starter bought at $4.60 should be reduced. Any exit will need a tax-lot check for holding period before it is placed.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **2026-10-29 — Q3 print.** This is derived from a data vendor and is **not confirmed by any company announcement.** It must be confirmed against a company press release before any order timing depends on it. ~46 trading days out. Last print, 2026-08-11, is confirmed on EDGAR.
- **Q3 10-Q, filed within days of the print.** The revenue-by-channel table is the decisive document, not the release headline.
- **2027-01-08 — the acquisition laps.** From this date reported revenue growth is no longer flattered by Kiwi II, and organic performance becomes visible in the headline. Dated and certain.
- **FY27 guidance, with the Q4 print (roughly late February 2027, unconfirmed).**

**Fair-value note:** no integrity or disclosure flag exists on this name, so we are not capped below fair value here — the constraint is purely price.
