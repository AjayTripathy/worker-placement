the NYSE-listed public company by ExxonMobil Holdings Corporation (Texas, CIK 0002115436), one share

1 · The four-idea frame

Market believes

Big Oil is a late-cycle trade after a huge run. The Iran-war price spike is a windfall, not a run-rate;

crude has already round-tripped; the melt-up is over. Take the dividend and expect nothing more.

We believe

Exactly that, and we bought it anyway — deliberately, as carry. The record's own words: *"XOM at ~$156

is fair, not cheap — and fair value IS the entry for a fortress-quality carry name."*

The claim is not that XOM is mispriced. It is that XOM is the **only name in the energy universe that

unambiguously adds quality** rather than cyclical leverage, and that the household is measurably

underweight quality. The supporting facts are structural, not cyclical: a corporate breakeven near $40

Brent and falling, a fortress balance sheet, a 43-year consecutive dividend-growth record, distributions

funded from free cash flow rather than new debt (which is precisely where Shell failed the same test), and

a buyback large enough to act as a drawdown shock-absorber. We own the oil-price beta as a by-product.

Q2 confirmed the balance-sheet and distribution legs emphatically, and it did so on a "miss." Free

cash flow was $17.2B in a single quarter against $23.6B of operating cash flow. Distributions were

$9.4B ($4.3B dividends + $5.1B buybacks — a ~$20B annual pace, exactly the authorised rate). The

third-quarter dividend was raised to $1.03 from $0.99. Net debt to capital fell to ~10.9% from the

13.1% in our record — the fortress got stronger while paying out $9.4B. Cumulative structural cost savings

reached $16.3B, which management states is more than all other international oil companies combined.

Production was the highest in more than two decades excluding the Middle East disruptions, with record

Permian volumes.

And the market's response was to mark it down 1.0% on the day — because consensus wanted more.

Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

1. Q2 is peak-cycle, not run-rate. Crude round-tripped violently: WTI spot ran from ~$69.74 on July 1

to a $93.08 peak on July 23 and back to ~$84.67 at month-end, after averaging $102.13 in May and

$84.81 in June. A $17.2B quarterly free-cash-flow figure earned through a war spike should not be

annualised. Any model anchored on "Brent above $100 and rising" is extrapolating the top of a

round-trip.

2. The stock has already been paid for this. It is up substantially year-to-date and sits ~11% below

a 52-week high of $175.27 that was itself set in the spike. Buying quality at fair value is defensible;

buying it near the top of a commodity round-trip is a different proposition.

3. The bear is a real number. The record's bear FV is $120, −22.8% from spot, and it does not

require anything to break — only for crude to mean-revert to the low end of the cycle.

4. Expected value from here is fractionally negative. Probability-weighting the record's own cone

(bear $120 at 0.30 / base $158 at 0.45 / bull $185 at 0.25) gives e_fv ≈ $153.35 against a

$155.44 spot. Before the dividend, the expected price return is about −1.3%. The ~2.8% yield is

what makes the total positive. That is precisely what "carry" means, and it is worth saying in numbers

rather than in adjectives.

5. Refining dragged. Contemporaneous reporting attributes the consensus miss partly to repair and

maintenance impacts in refining — the Energy Products adjusted result of $4,099M sits below the GAAP

$5,465M, and scheduled maintenance is named in the release.

Mechanism falsifier

Net-debt-to-capital rising above ~20% (the balance sheet levering to sustain distributions), **the

$20B buyback suspended on "market conditions", or corporate breakeven guided above ~$50 Brent**.

Any of the three means the carry thesis broke and the position trims to nothing more than a dividend.

breaching the receiving name's cap.** That is a structural point, not an XOM point, and it applies to

every same-sleeve pairing in the core table (XOM/SU, KNSL/WRB, LDOS/BAH).

Liquidity note

~$2.26B/day 90-day average dollar volume — the deepest name in the entire book. A $120k line is

~0.005% of one day. There is no size, spread or timing constraint of any kind. This is the name to use

when a large harvest has to be executed quickly.

**But two non-liquidity frictions bind hard here, and they are the most important execution facts in this

doc:**

1. The redomiciliation may have disturbed the tax lots — verify before harvesting. The 1-for-1 share

exchange in a redomiciliation of this type is ordinarily tax-free with **carryover basis and a tacked

holding period**, so the lots should survive intact. But brokers routinely re-book positions when the

CUSIP changes, and a re-booking can reset reported acquisition dates or collapse specific-ID lots into

an average-cost blob. This book's entire harvest engine depends on specific-ID lots. Before the

first XOM harvest, pull the IBKR tax-lot report and confirm that the pre-July-2026 acquisition dates

and bases survived the exchange. If they did not, fix it with the broker before selling — an

incorrect holding-period start can convert a long-term loss into a short-term one, or worse, misreport

basis entirely.

2. XOM is on the Parametric wash-sale coordination list — this is not optional. The core table names

XOM (with CI) as sitting inside the Parametric direct-indexing sleeve. Parametric runs its own

tax-lot management on 304 singles and will buy and sell XOM on its own schedule, with no knowledge of

what this book does. A Parametric purchase inside the 61-day window silently disallows the loss.

Coordinate every XOM lot change against the direct-indexing sleeve before executing, and consider

adding XOM to Parametric's restriction list for the duration of any harvest window — the same step the

EQT court directed for that name.
