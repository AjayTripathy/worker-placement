## What we believe

We believe BlackSky is a real, revenue-generating satellite-imagery company that is **not** in demand distress — and that this is exactly why there is no trade here today.

The honest core of the long case: management raised FY26 revenue guidance to $130–150M on the 2026-05-07 print and reaffirmed it on 2026-08-06, backlog is ~$345M, Q2 revenue grew ~50% to $33.3M, and the stock is 60.5% below its 52-week high. That is the shape of a name where the story is intact and the price is broken.

We believe that shape is a trap here, for one arithmetic reason both benches initially missed: **the cash is not net cash.** The $244.1M balance the bull case leans on sits against $217.15M of debt principal ($185.0M of 8.25% convertible notes due 2033 at a ~$36.78 conversion price, plus $32.15M of satellite launch vendor financing). Net cash is ~$27M — about 3% of the ~$853M market cap, not 29%. Enterprise value is ~$826M, which is **5.9× the FY26 guide midpoint** and **7.6× what the business has actually delivered in H1 annualized** ($108M). That is not a distressed multiple. It is a full one on a company that must produce a back-half revenue shape 32–67% steeper than it produced last year.

We also believe the second half is a genuine open question, not a settled one — and that the right posture toward an open question priced at 5.9× sales with a negative free-cash-flow engine is to wait for evidence rather than pay for the option.

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what the company guided. Street FY26 revenue sits at ~$132.7–141.8M against a $130–150M guide whose midpoint is $140M. Consensus price target is ~$27.6; Jefferies cut its target from $50 to $33 but kept a Hold **after** calling Q2 strong.

So the market's position is: the guide is credible, the back half is largely believed, and the stock is worth ~30–60% more than the tape. There is no "the street hates this" story available. Notably, the sell-side's *level* is bullish even where its *direction* has been negative — a $33 target on a $20.85 tape is +58%, which is why the "analysts are cutting" argument does not do the work the red bench wanted it to do.

What the market appears **not** to have fully repriced is the same thing our benches missed until late: that the cash cushion is financed, not earned, and that cash interest of ~$15.3M/year consumes essentially the entire guided Adjusted-EBITDA midpoint of ~$18M.

## Why we might have edge

Three candidate edges, stated at their real strength, not their maximum strength:

1. **The balance-sheet correction.** Both of our own benches computed EV/sales at 4.3× before one of them pulled the 10-Q debt note and found 5.9×. If two adversarial benches working the same name both anchored on the press release's gross-cash paragraph, it is at least plausible that parts of the street did too. This is a real, checkable, primary-document edge — but it is an edge that argues *against* the long, not for it.
2. **Drawdown decomposition.** Neither the Q1 miss nor the warrant delisting explains the move. The path was ~52.75 → ~40.5 before the Q1 print (−23%), then −20.4% on the print day (to ~32), then ~32 → 20.85 (−35%) **after** guidance was raised and reaffirmed. Roughly two-thirds of the decline is unattributed by both benches. If the unexplained two-thirds is ATM supply and convert-arb hedging rather than fundamental information, then a post-ATM, post-supply tape is a genuinely different setup — at a genuinely lower price.
3. **The classified/IC leg.** The plausible home of an H2 government ramp — an NRO AROS contract modification and unnamed international contracts — is invisible in USAspending and unnamed in the exhibit. If it is funded, the ramp is real and nobody can see it in public data. That is where an information edge would live. **We did not obtain it.** The Pentagon J-Book and appropriations detectors were never run by either bench, and both flagged this as the single highest-value unrun check.

## Why it might be priced in

Mostly, it is. The load-bearing number of the bull case — the reaffirmed FY26 guide — *is* the street's number. Consensus sits inside the guide range. A price target 32–58% above the tape means the street already owns the recovery narrative. There is no published gap to arbitrage.

The valuation math makes this concrete. To clear +50% ($31.28), enterprise value must reach ~$1.25B — **8.95× the FY26 guide midpoint**, or 6.3× a hypothetical FY27 $200M. For reference, 4× the guide midpoint implies $14.34/share; 5× implies $17.77; 6× implies $21.19, which is approximately today. **We are already at a 6× multiple.** The upside case therefore requires the ramp to land *and* the multiple to expand into a name with structurally negative free cash flow — roughly −$52M/year at guide midpoints (18 EBITDA − 15.3 interest − 55 capex).

And the marginal seller is the issuer. $150M was raised in Q2 from 3.6 million ATM shares at ~$41.67/share against a $20.85 tape. The next $150M costs ~7.19M shares — 17.6% dilution at today's price. Four distinct-CIK Form 144s (8/28, 9/9, 9/10 ×2) and Form 4s (8/31, 9/10) sit on top of that. Buying in front of an open ATM at half the last issue price is paying for a story while the author sells it.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — "We're right about the ramp and still lose money" (multiple compression).** Q3 prints $38–48M, FY26 lands at $140M, and the stock goes nowhere or down because 5.9× EV/sales was already the right multiple for a −$52M-FCF issuer with an open ATM. This is the most likely losing path if we enter on evidence. *Tripwire: monitorable* — price gates below $17.80 (5× guide) and $14.40 (4× guide) fire regardless of fundamental news.

**Failure narrative 2 — "Ramp slips and the ATM catches the fall."** H2 comes in at ~1.1× H1 (FY26 ≈ $114M), guidance is cut at the Q3 print, and the company taps the ATM into weakness for 7–9M shares, taking the count from ~40.9M to ~50M. Equity value compresses through two channels at once. *Tripwire: monitorable* — 424B5/prospectus supplements, the 10-Q cover share count, and the equity note.

**Failure narrative 3 — "The dark customer goes away, and we find out at the print." NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** A classified ISR task order is reprogrammed, de-scoped, or not renewed; or an on-orbit anomaly degrades Gen-3 capacity mid-quarter. There is no 8-K obligation that reliably fires on a task-order reprogramming, no appropriations line that resolves a classified account, no USAspending record, and no tape signature until the quarterly print — at which point the information and the drawdown arrive together. **Unknown-unknown class: DARK-COUNTERPARTY / UNOBSERVABLE-PROGRAM RISK.** Its cousin class is **ON-ORBIT ASSET IMPAIRMENT**, which is equally invisible between prints. We cannot build a sensor for either. We can only size for them, and the correct size for an unsensored tail on a single-name, single-constellation issuer is small.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** Our own size is *not* the reflexive risk here, and we should say so plainly rather than perform risk management: a starter of 1.0–2.5% of book is a small fraction of a day's volume in this name (average daily volume is not in the pack — see unverified item 6 — but the working estimate is ~3–5M shares/day, so a starter is well under 5% of ADV and is not the marginal price-setter). The reflexive risk is **someone else's** size:

- **The ATM is the marginal supply.** A $150M draw at today's price is ~7.19M shares ≈ roughly two days of estimated ADV, sold programmatically into the bid. The issuer's need to sell is *increasing* as the price falls (fixed dollar requirement, falling share price), which is the textbook destabilizing feedback loop. Our own buying makes their next tranche cheaper to execute.
- **Convert-arb hedging.** $185M of 8.25% notes struck at ~$36.78 against a $20.85 spot means arb holders are long the bond and short a small delta. That delta is small today, but it *grows* as the stock rallies toward the strike — meaning the convert book supplies incremental stock into exactly the rally we would be buying for. It is a soft ceiling near the $30s.
- **Affiliate supply.** Four separate Form 144 filers in two weeks plus Form 4s. These are notices of proposed sale, not completions, and we should not overstate them — but the direction is one-way.
- **Doctrine interaction.** Our own house doctrines collided here and we should name it: the *divergence-not-coverage* doctrine correctly overturned the red bench's "consensus = no trade" kill, but if we let that overturn carry us into a position, we would be long a name whose only genuine edge (the debt correction) points the other way. An overturned kill is not a reason to buy. It is a reason to stop using a bad argument.

The blank fills as: **paying a 5.9× multiple for a back-half ramp we cannot verify, while the issuer sells stock into us.**
