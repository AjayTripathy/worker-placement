## What we believe

Hamilton Lane is a genuinely good business being sold at a price that is no longer obviously wrong, and the one question that matters — are the private valuations that feed part of its fee stream real, and are investors still putting money in — is not answered yet.

Three specific beliefs:

1. **Half of the fee base cannot be hurt by bad marks, and half can.** The company's own definition says fee-earning assets are a mix of capital commitments, invested capital, and net asset value depending on the contract ([FY19 10-K, sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1433642/000143364219000071/hlne3311910k.htm)). Specialized funds (~$42.6B, +25%) are mostly fee'd on commitments/invested capital — immune. Separate accounts and the evergreen retail funds are where mark risk lives. Any argument that treats the company as all-immune or all-exposed is wrong.
2. **The headline profit growth is flattered by performance-linked fees.** Headline fee-related earnings printed +49% to ~$124.5M, but once the fee-related *performance* revenue is stripped out, the management-fee-only version backs out to roughly $49.9M versus ~$53.0M a year ago — down about 6%. That is the opposite of "compounding at 25%."
3. **The specifically accused fund was still losing money to redemptions in the very quarter the bulls call a refutation.** The short report's math targeted the non-US Global Private Assets Fund (~$6.79B → ~$6.55B in March, ~$172M out). Company commentary then said redemptions were coming primarily from two of the largest non-US evergreen products. A platform-wide net inflow number ($640M) does not clear a fund-specific accusation; it hides it.

So: the bull classification "damage absent" is unearned. The bear verdict "reject on merits" is also unearned, because the loudest bear kills turn out to be partly arithmetic errors (see the checking table). The honest label is **unresolved**, and unresolved plus a stock already +35.8% off its low equals **no position**.

## What the market believes

- The August print settled it: fee-earning assets $83.7B (+12%), fee-related earnings +49%, evergreen net inflows positive, no redemption gates enacted. Short thesis answered.
- At ~$97.6 the stock is ~16.5× FY26 non-GAAP EPS of $5.90, with a ~2.5% dividend yield ($0.60/quarter, ex-date 2026-09-21, payable 2026-10-06, per [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HLNE/) / [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/diversified-financials/nasdaq-hlne/hamilton-lane)) — a reasonable price for a franchise, not a distressed one.
- Mark risk is treated as a tail: everybody already knows private managers value their own holdings, so it is not news and not a differentiator.
- The stock's behaviour agrees with the market, not with us: pre-print close $94.91, post-print ~$99.83, now ~$97.6. The print was *kept*, not given back.

## Why we might have edge

- **The immunity split is not how the street frames it.** Almost nobody separates the commitment-fee'd half from the NAV-fee'd half before grading the short thesis. Doing so kills the bull's "12% growth proves the marks" and the bear's "every metric depends on the marks" in the same stroke.
- **The management-fee-only earnings arithmetic.** ~$49.9M vs ~$53.0M is a subtraction anyone can do, and we have not seen it done. It converts "+49%" into "flat-to-down."
- **The stalled leg.** Customized separate accounts — about half the fee base at ~$41.1B — grew ~2% (+$818M) year over year. One leg is carrying the whole franchise.
- **A public monthly dataset nobody runs.** The company publishes evergreen fund size as NAV plus net subscriptions by trade date ([hamiltonlane.com/en-us/strategies/evergreen](https://www.hamiltonlane.com/en-us/strategies/evergreen)). Subtract published monthly share-class performance from the change in fund size and you get monthly net flow per fund, before the quarter is reported. Neither of our own benches ran it. That is a free, dated, pre-print read on the only mark-independent question.
- **The pack's own audited numbers contain an unused fact.** In the XBRL series both benches dismissed as "stale," fiscal Q1 revenue went $196.7M (2024-06-30) → $176.0M (2025-06-30), **down 10.6% year over year**, while share-based comp went $3.2M → $12.8M (+297%) and operating cash flow rose 61.7%. Revenue can fall while cash rises here because the mix swings on incentive fees — which is precisely the volatility the bull case calls "durable."

## Why it might be priced in

- **Two-thirds of the dislocation is gone.** −36.9% from the 52-week high but +35.8% off the $71.88 low. We are late to the fear, not early.
- **The short report is old news.** Hunterbrook published, the stock crashed and recovered. Our own blue bench graded three of the five surviving findings as *consensus*, not novel — including the circularity point and the tape point.
- **No measured disagreement with anyone.** We never extracted a single Q2 FY27 street estimate. Our "edge" on management-fee earnings is an assertion against an unmeasured consensus; it is entirely possible analysts already model the performance-fee split.
- **The bear leg that is loudest is weakest.** Interval/tender structures mean redemptions are always capped by contract ([company PR, 2026-03-24](https://shareholders.hamiltonlane.com/2026-03-24-Hamilton-Lane-Announces-SEC-Effectiveness-for-Firms-First-Interval-Fund)) — but if those quarterly repurchase offers were *undersubscribed*, investors chose not to leave, and the bear story shrinks a lot. We have not checked.
- **Insider-capital read is neutral, not bullish or bearish.** The buyback (199k shares at ~$100.43, ~$20M) is 0.4% of a ~$5.4B company. That is noise. Our own red bench sold it as a signal; it isn't one.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — we buy the gate, and the whole sector reprices anyway (monitorable).**
The gates clear (two months of flat-or-positive flow, undersubscribed repurchase offers), we take a starter, and then a *different* private-markets manager takes a valuation hit — an interval fund gating somewhere else, a big write-down at a peer, a regulator commenting on retail private-fund valuation. HLNE trades with the doctrine, not with its own flows. Tripwires exist: price gates below, plus a peer basket (STEP, BX, ARES, TPG) and fund-level filings.

**Failure narrative 2 — we are flat and wrong (monitorable, opportunity-cost).**
Management-fee earnings reaccelerate at the November print, the non-US bleed stops, specialized-fund flows stay at 25%, and the stock runs from ~$98 back toward the old multiple while we hold a clipboard. This is the *most likely* way the pitch "fails" in the sense that matters to the book's P&L versus the benchmark. Tripwires: monthly flow prints, the November print, price gate above $99.83.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: a private-book event.**
The thing that would actually break the stock in one session originates inside books that have no public series at all: a Level 3 revaluation forced by an auditor, an auditor change, an SEC exam or enforcement sweep on evergreen NAV practice, an LP lawsuit alleging stale marks, or a large wealth platform quietly pulling the evergreen product from its approved list. There is no dataset to watch. The first public signal *is* the 8-K or the price.
**Unknown-unknown class named: PRIVATE-BOOK OPACITY EVENTS** — risks whose entire information chain sits inside non-public fund accounting, fund-auditor correspondence, private distribution agreements, or a non-public regulatory file. Adjacent sub-class: **distribution-channel policy shock** (one intermediary's approved-list decision, never announced). We cannot arm a sensor for either. We can only size for them.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size versus liquidity:* HLNE trades roughly 790k shares a day on a ~$98 tape, about $77M of dollar volume ([Morningstar quote page](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/xnas/hlne/quote)). A 0.5%-of-book starter at any sleeve size this desk runs is a low-single-digit percentage of one day's volume. **Our size is not the risk.** Exiting into a mark-panic tape is: on a gap day, volume goes up but the spread and the depth go the wrong way, and this name has already shown a ~5% overnight move on a print.
- *Predicted flow:* the resolving dataset is public and monthly. Every event-driven fund reading the same page gets the same signal on the same day. Our reopen gate is therefore a *crowded* gate — expect the good news to arrive already in the price, gap-style, which mechanically converts our "buy ≤$94.91" limit into "no fill" in the good case and "instant fill" in the bad case. That asymmetry is real and we accept it rather than chase.
- *Doctrine interactions:* two of our own doctrines collide here. The circularity mechanism ("you cannot refute bad marks with numbers made of marks") pushes us to reject all NAV-denominated evidence; the mixed-basis immunity split pushes us to credit the commitment-fee'd half. Applied carelessly together they let us buy the immune half of a company the market prices off the indicted half — being right about the accounting and wrong about the stock. The second doctrine also cuts against our own bear leg: if half the fee base is structurally immune, the bear's downside case is smaller than it sounds.
