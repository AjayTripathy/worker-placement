# PITCH — CTMX — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — take a 0.45% starter in three calendar tranches around $3.29, gated to stay a starter unless the competing Phase 3 drug's early data comes in weak; no resting orders.

---

## What we believe

CytomX is a South San Francisco drug developer with no approved product. Its revenue line looks like a collapse — $50.9M in one quarter of 2025 down to $18.7M — but that is an accounting roll-off, not a business failure. The company books partner upfront payments as revenue spread over the years it does the work, so revenue falls when an old contract finishes recognizing even if new partner cash is arriving. Both of our review benches checked this against the filings and both agreed: no debt, the cash is real, partner money is going up, not down. There is no hidden damage here.

What you are actually buying is one drug. CX-2051 is an antibody-drug conjugate for late-line colorectal cancer — patients who have already failed the standard options, where approved alternatives produce response rates in the low single digits. In the most recent disclosed data cut (data as of January 16, 2026; 93 patients enrolled, 56 evaluable), 20% responded at the 8.6 mg/kg dose and 32% at 10 mg/kg, with 88% of patients showing at least disease stabilization. That is a large multiple of what the approved comparators do. The company has said it will give an update "by the end of 2026."

At $3.29 the whole company is worth about $721M (219.1M shares including immediately-exercisable pre-funded warrants). Strip out the cash and short-term investments and the market is paying somewhere between $390M and $445M for the drug — our two benches disagree on exactly where in that band, for a reason explained below. Against that, our own valuation grid says that if the drug works and reaches a $1.5B worldwide peak-sales market, the stock is worth $7.86 at a 40% probability of success and $4.39 at 20%. The market is currently paying for roughly a 8–13% chance. We think that is too low, but not dramatically too low — and the gap is smaller than it first looked.

**This is a starter-size position, not a conviction position.** We do not have a differentiated view on whether the drug works. We have a view that the price implies a probability lower than the data supports.

## What the market believes

Two markets, and they disagree violently.

The sell side is unanimously bullish: 8 analysts, all Buy, average price target between $11.75 and $12.25, range $5 to $17. They raised 2026 revenue estimates from $22.2M to $33.6M. Nobody covering this stock is confused by the revenue line.

The tape says something else. The stock is at $3.29 against a 52-week high of $8.21 — down 60% from the high, though still up 73% from the $1.90 low. And 35.11M shares are sold short, 19.5% of the 179.8M-share float, about 14.6 days of average volume.

So the market's *marginal* holder is not saying "the drug won't work." The market is saying: the company will need to sell more stock before this resolves. That reading is supported by behavior. CytomX sold 76.9M shares at $1.30 in May 2025 (near the low) and 46.0M shares at $5.30 in March 2026 (into the spike) — 170.2M shares outstanding at year-end 2025 became 217.9M by June 30, 2026, a 28% increase in six months. The March deal priced about 35% below the 52-week high. Today's buyer is 38% below the last price at which management itself sold stock.

Both benches agreed that this — not the clinical data — is what explains the drawdown.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The revenue optical illusion is real and mechanical.** Anyone screening on revenue decline sees a -92% line and moves on. We verified in the 10-Q that it is contract accounting. That is a genuine screening artifact.

2. **The price is below our own funded-outcome grid at almost any credible probability.** Assuming a future ~$500M raise (about 302M shares) and a 3.5x peak-sales multiple:

| Probability of success ↓ / Worldwide peak sales → | $1.0B | $1.5B | $2.5B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | $3.23 | $4.39 | $6.71 |
| 40% | $5.55 | $7.86 | $12.50 |
| 60% | $7.86 | $11.34 | $17.90 |

Spot ($3.29) is essentially the 20%-probability / $1.0B-peak cell. You are paying for the most pessimistic plausible corner of the grid.

3. **The cash floor is genuine.** Net cash is $275.6M–$331M depending on accounting treatment — $1.26 to $1.51 per share against a $3.29 price. There is no debt. There is no going-concern language. The company is funded past the next readout.

4. **The short position is one-sided against a hard floor.** A 19.5% short float into a company that cannot go bankrupt before the catalyst is an upside accelerant if the data is good, not a symmetric standoff.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because these are serious:

1. **We agree with a unanimously bullish street.** Eight analysts, all Buy, targets 3.6x the tape. Our direction is the consensus direction. We are not seeing something nobody sees; we are taking the tape's side of a gap between the tape and the street, without a differentiated clinical view to arbitrate it. That is the weakest structural feature of this pitch.

2. **A Phase 3 competitor is already in the same line of treatment.** NCT07549412 — precemtabart tocentecan (M9140), EMD Serono, ± bevacizumab, Phase 3, recruiting, previously-treated metastatic colorectal cancer — uses the same class of payload as CX-2051 and is two development phases ahead in the identical patient population. Two more are enrolling (Hansoh HS-20093, Junshi JS212). The premise "approved comparators deliver low-single-digit response rates" describes the wrong comparator set for a 2029 launch. This attacks both the probability of success *and* the peak-sales number at the same time — and on the $1.0B column, a +50% return requires a 35–40% probability of success, not the 23% the more optimistic column implies.

3. **The upside is likely to be sold to us.** This management raises equity within weeks of good news, twice demonstrated. Guided runway is "at least 2H 2028" while observed cash burn ($43.6M in the first half of 2026, ~$87M/year) would fund the balance sheet to roughly 2030 — implying planned burn ramps materially for a registrational trial start in 1H 2027. A good readout probably produces an offering, not a re-rating you get to hold.

4. **It is one drug.** CX-801, the next asset, has no combination data until 1H 2027. The $390–445M we would be paying buys effectively a single binary.

5. **There is no survival data.** Overall survival is the registrational endpoint and no OS number is public. The response-rate data is encouraging; it is not the number the FDA approves on.

## Adjudication & update (2026-08-23)

**The size gate has since RESOLVED OPEN — on published data.** The post-ruling biology deep-dive
(desk/reports/CTMX_BIOLOGY_DEEPDIVE_20260822.md) found M9140's Phase 1 published in *Nature
Medicine* (Oct-2025): confirmed ORR **7.5%** (8.8% at active doses), mPFS 6.7mo — versus CX-2051's
confirmed **20%/32%** with mPFS 6.8–7.1mo in near-identical ~100%-prior-irinotecan MSS populations.
CX-2051 leads 2.3–3.6× on every like-for-like cut. Further findings folded into the record:
- **The mask works in humans:** no dose-limiting toxicities at 10 mg/kg systemic dosing on a target
  where unmasked IV catumaxomab caused immune-mediated liver injury at *microgram* doses. Of the
  four historical EpCAM failure modes, three are structurally retired; payload GI tox remains as a
  management question with a working prophylaxis protocol.
- **Kill trigger re-benchmarked:** the "G3 diarrhea >15%" trigger now reads against the
  *prophylaxis cohort* (10%, n=20) at the end-2026 update — the legacy pooled 23.8% predates
  mandated prophylaxis and would have false-fired.
- **FDA annex:** refractory-CRC single-arm accelerated approval exists twice (KRAZATI: ORR 34%,

  response is the statutory pillar CytomX has not yet disclosed**, and both precedents carried one.
  Regulatory reframe: M9140's n=1,020 *randomized* Phase 3 is the compulsory path for a 7.5%-ORR
