A SignalOS pitch deck · the return of a wallet that went rogue

Baccarat Rogue

The scent lingers after the money's left the room.

The trade in one paragraph

For the five weeks of the 2026 World Cup (June 11 – July 19)[1], the world's table-gambling wallet left the casino floor. Macau gross gaming revenue fell 12.1% year-over-year in June[2] and 8.4% in July[3]; Las Vegas Strip baccarat volume fell ~22% in June while ex-baccarat play grew[4]; Pennsylvania table games fell 21% while its slot revenue was roughly flat[5]. Slots held everywhere; tables emptied everywhere; and jurisdictions that count online betting showed the money arriving there instead[6]. The tournament ended July 19 and Macau's daily revenue run-rate inflected upward that same week and has climbed since[7]. The market, reading luck-distorted operator headlines, treated part of a dated diversion as structural decline[8]. The position: dated, defined-risk WYNN call spreads bought at the cheapest implied volatility of the year[9], sized so total failure costs ~40bps of book after tax, graded against a public-statistics calendar that culminates September 1.

The split identifies the mechanism. The slot-floor demographic stayed put in every jurisdiction; the table demographic — younger, bet-inclined, the sports-betting overlap — went missing in every jurisdiction at once. Macau's visitors kept arriving (+4.0%); their gambling spend went elsewhere. New Jersey, the one U.S. state whose regulator publishes retail and online in one release, shows the wallet conserved: floor down, total up 7.9%[6].

Exhibit 2 — where the wallet went, measured

"my own gambling was diverted to world cup."— the principal. n=1, but it was our n.

Exhibit 3 — the reversal, already on the tape

Timing discriminates cause. The enforcement campaign continued past the tournament; the tournament did not. Macau's recovery dates to the final whistle: Citi's channel check recorded daily revenue +9% week-over-week in the exact week the final was played[7], and J.P. Morgan's August checks show the daily rate climbing from ~MOP710M to ~MOP793M with VIP and mass volumes both +10–15% y/y[14]. July's full month still printed −8.4%[3] — the month averages over the inflection, which is why the market's monthly instruments lag the weekly fingerprint. Consensus for August sits at ~+1.3% y/y (CLSA)[15]; Jefferies frames August as the start of the recovery[16]; our frozen call is p = 0.72 that August beats that bar, graded September 1 against the DICJ release[17].

Exhibit 4 — what five adversarial courts did to this thesis

Every operator went through a red-bench/blue-bench court (prosecution and defense, no shared notes; artifacts on file)[18]. Two results shaped the position:

1. Every operator's growth headline this cycle was flattered by elevated table hold — and it was not luck. Wynn Palace printed mass-table hold of 29.7% vs 22.3% a year earlier[19]; Galaxy and Studio City printed above their own disclosed normal ranges[21]. Quarterly hold deviations, if they were card luck, would be independent across operators — the chance of three-plus printing elevated simultaneously is under ~1%[22]. What actually raised hold is the thesis seen from the other side of the fraction: (a) composition — the cohort that left for the World Cup is the low-hold clientele (large main-bet baccarat at ~1.1% house edge, few side bets), so its absence mechanically raised the blended win rate on the volume that remained; (b) structure — engineered game and room mix (side bets at 5–15% edges, premium-mass rooms) raises hold where it is deployed, permanently. Headline GGR is win = volume × hold, so the same diversion that emptied the tables dressed the survivors' win rates up as "growth" — the courts restated every claim in volume terms before believing it, and underlying premium volume ran weaker than every headline. That is why no cash equity cleared a conviction bar and the expression moved to dated options. Sands is the exception that proves the mechanism: drop +15% but EBITDA −24% on 1.35% VIP hold — the one operator whose luck genuinely ran bad while its volume recovered[20].

2. The model that says which is which: 607 property-quarters across 18 series from primary filings[22]. Fitted findings: there is no cohort-wide hold elevation (cohort z +0.10; six of twelve Macau series sit below their own trends) — the elevation is concentrated exactly where the mechanisms are. Wynn Palace sits +11σ above trend — card luck cannot do that; the identified driver is a new room with different clientele: the Chairman's Club premium-mass expansion, tripled to ~100,000 sq ft in February 2026[23], importing the highest-recycle players on the highest-edge mix into the mass math. Around it: slow structural hold drift at MGM (+1.36pp/yr — which quietly deleted its old 16–22% guidance range while running 25–27%) and Galaxy (+1.18pp/yr), corroborated by a decade of Nevada baccarat hold drifting +0.36pp/yr[4][22]. The Street's framing of the Q2 beat was "beats on Macau hold" — a low-quality-beat discount, normalized away in hold-adjusted models — yet consensus price targets are constructive (avg ~$134 vs a ~$105 tape, 19 analysts, targets raised post-print)[35]. The residual skeptics are the tape and, most precisely, the options market[9]. If the Palace elevation is furniture, the Q3 prints meet it as run-rate. One honesty note our own model forced: the hold split is not visible in any monthly print — monthly GGR is win = volume × hold, conflated — so the composition leg is confirmed at the Q3 filings (November), with weekly bank checks as a noisy interim proxy. September 1 grades the volume thesis; the furniture question resolves at the prints[17][22].

"how is it a catalyst if no one cares?"— the principal. The only question that matters.

First, the exposure is not a side bet: Wynn Palace + Wynn Macau were $1,004.5M of WYNN's $1,860M Q2 operating revenue — 54% — and $297.0M of $568.3M property EBITDAR — 52%[19]. A Macau revenue swing moves more than half the company's earnings power, and it reaches the equity through the parent's leverage. And the tape confirms the market prices it: on June 1, a good May DICJ print (+6.7%)[24] moved WYNN +5.25% in one session[25]. The market reacts violently to the monthly surprise and gradually to the weekly checks — the June de-rate happened ahead of the bad print via the same channel checks now running positive[7][14]. The edge is calendared, and it decays: you buy before the Mondays run out.

Exhibit 6b — the Singapore question: did the money leave for good?

The strongest version of the bear case is not Q4 softness — it is the 2021 precedent: when China abolished the junkets, the premium wallet did not die, it permanently relocated to Singapore and Manila, and Marina Bay Sands printed record years[29]. If this summer's diversion resolves the same way, the recovery happens somewhere our spreads don't reach. We checked both sides against the drain quarter itself:

The wallet relocated (bear)The wallet paused (our read)
2021 proves this cohort can leave for good when pushed, and the receiving infrastructure is still there — MBS broke ground on an $8B expansion in 2025[33].Singapore's own Q2 refutes the relocation for this episode: MBS net revenue was flat (−0.6%, $1,380M vs $1,388M) and EBITDA fell 10.3% in April–June — the exact drain window[20]. A wallet arriving in Singapore would print as a surge; none appears.
China's enforcement wave has no final whistle — if part of June's −12.1% was payment-rail chill rather than the tournament, that fraction does not auto-revert[12].The drain appeared simultaneously in Vegas, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Macau[4][5][6][2] — a synchronized global table-cohort event timed to a scheduled tournament, not a Macau-specific exit. Nothing about Singapore explains Pennsylvania.
2027 adds new supply (Wynn Al Marjan, the first Gulf casino[27]) that could siphon premium demand structurally.Macau arrivals ran +4.0% through the drain[10] — the bodies kept coming; only spend paused. And the money is already observably returning to Macau: +9% w/w at the final whistle, daily rates climbing since[7][14]. Relocation is a hypothesis; the return is on the tape.

Where we land, honestly: the 2021 mechanism was the destruction of a credit rail; 2026 was a scheduled event with an end date — different physics, and the MBS non-surge is the closest thing to a controlled experiment the quarter offers. But the bear's residual is real: June's −12.1% exceeds any historical World Cup drag (~3–5pp), so some fraction may be enforcement chill that doesn't revert — which is precisely what the September 1 gate exists to price, and what the kill rule below hands back if the print misses. We believe the return case because it is the one that can be falsified on a date.

Exhibit 6c — the seasonality audit: is August the wrong month to bet on?

Asked and checked, because August Macau is genuinely unpleasant — heat, humidity, peak typhoon season. The short answer: the gate is seasonality-proof by construction, and the audit marginally strengthens it. The September 1 read is August-2026 versus August-2025 — same school-holiday peak, same heat — so the seasonal shape nets out; and despite the weather, August is one of Macau's strongest months, because mainland summer school holidays drive family visitation[3]. The market's monthly consensus is built off daily run-rates, so the seasonal shape is well priced — our claimed edge was never "the market can't do seasonality"; it is the recovery slope within the month. How the verdict actually arrives — three installments, not one

A correction worth showing the work on, caught in review before the print: the deck originally promised three reads on September 1. Only one exists that day. The gambling number (DICJ GGR) publishes on the 1st; the tourism number (DSEC arrivals) lags it by ~3 weeks; and the hold split appears in no monthly print at all — monthly GGR is win = volume × hold, conflated, the exact error our own courts prosecute. So the grading arrives staged, and each installment answers a different question:

Kill rules: daily-rate checks fading mid-August = no adds, review the spreads. Sept-1 ≤ +1.3% = calendar excuse dead: exit remaining premium value, grade the call UNFAVORABLE, sleeve closes to new entries. MSC volume declining again on Aug 13 = that name is dead regardless of the macro.

The honest close. The checks are public. The names are off their lows. Our own courts refused every cash entry and cleared exactly one gated starter — the mechanism survived every court; every vehicle got gated, which is precisely why the expression is a dated debit. If the weekly leaks pre-price everything so smoothly that nothing moves by October 16, we lose the debit to a thesis that technically succeeded, and the sleeve writes that down too. But a wallet this size does not vanish — tables-not-slots does not lie across three regulators[4][5][6] — and Baccarat Rouge's whole trick is that absence announces itself. So does return. Quis custodiet? The calendar does.
