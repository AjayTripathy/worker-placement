# PITCH — ALVO — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Do not buy. Both benches ended at no-position, and the reason is not price: the profit number the whole story rests on is not backed by cash, and part of the one growing revenue line is a sale to a company that shares Alvotech's own large shareholder.

---

## What we believe

Alvotech makes biosimilars — copies of expensive biologic drugs. It is based in Luxembourg with its main plant in Reykjavík, Iceland. Trading at $4.27, down 54% from its 52-week high but up 45% from its low.

Three things we believe, in order of how much they matter:

**1. The headline profit is not cash.** For the first six months of 2026 the company reported "adjusted EBITDA" of **+$46.9M**. Over the same six months, cash actually generated by operations was **−$80.2M**. That is a $127M gap, and a $148M swing from the same period last year (+$68.3M). Every valuation multiple anyone quotes on this stock — ours included — divides by a number that did not turn into cash. Source: the company's own management discussion filed 19 Aug 2026 ([mdaq22026.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1898416/000162828026057988/mdaq22026.htm)).

**2. The one revenue line that still looks healthy is propped up by a related party.** Product revenue halved (−48%). Licensing revenue looked resilient at $105.7M, up 4.4%. But buried in the related-party note of the interim financial statements is $39.8M — 37.6% of that line — recognized in the second quarter under a new licensing agreement with "an entity under common control/influence." Alvogen Lux owns 28.2% of Alvotech. Strip that item out and licensing is −35%, and total revenue is −44%, not the −31% the press release implies. The current CEO came from Alvogen at the end of Q1 2026, and the 21 Aug 2026 announcement is another deal — with Lotus Pharmaceutical, Alvogen's acquirer ([6-K, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1898416/000117184326005697/0001171843-26-005697-index.htm)).

**3. Even if everything goes right, you don't get paid much.** Using corrected share count (361M) and the 30 June balance sheet, enterprise value is about **$2.78B** — roughly 13.9× the midpoint of management's own 2026 profit guidance. If the company hits the *high* end of guidance ($220M) and the market pays today's same multiple, the stock is worth about **$5.04** — up 18%. To make +50% you need the market to pay 16.2×, an expansion nobody has argued for. Meanwhile the downside leg is real: if the guide is missed and the multiple compresses to 11×, the math gives **$2.66**, down 38%.

So: the upside is thin, the downside is wide, and the number in the middle is unverified. That is a pass, and it would be a pass at a somewhat lower price too.

## What the market believes

The market believes management. On 19 Aug 2026 Alvotech reported half-year revenue of $211.9M (down from $306.1M) and a loss — and **the stock rose about 7%** to $4.27. Headline coverage read "Reports Q2 Loss, Beats Revenue Estimates." The market bought the print, because management reaffirmed full-year guidance of $650–700M revenue and $180–220M adjusted EBITDA, blamed the shortfall on temporary manufacturing and quality-system work following an FDA inspection, and said the plant returned to planned operating levels at the end of Q2.

Wall Street analysts are slightly more cautious than management but not much: consensus full-year revenue is $644.8M–$663.8M, which implies a second half of $433–452M. Management's guidance implies $438–488M. So the street sits at the very bottom of management's range — shading it about 5%, not rejecting it.

Nobody appears to be publicly focused on the cash-flow gap or the related-party revenue.

## Why we might have edge

Four things we found that are not in the coverage, all sourced from filings rather than press releases:

- **The cash-flow gap.** It is disclosed, but only in the management discussion filed alongside the earnings release — not in the press release, which leads with adjusted EBITDA. Analysts writing same-day off the PR would not have it.
- **The related-party licensing revenue.** Disclosed only in a note to the interim financial statements. The press release presents the licensing line as resilient with no indication that 37.6% of it came from a company connected to Alvotech's 28.2% shareholder.
- **Shareholders' equity is negative.** Total equity at 30 June 2026 is **−$191.5M** against $1.306B of borrowings and $1.588B of assets ([interim statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1898416/000162828026057988/alvo-20260630.htm)). Not a going-concern flag — management asserts twelve months of liquidity — but it means any further shortfall is funded by issuing stock or borrowing more from an existing creditor.
- **$110M of payables to related-party landlords.** Fasteignafélagið Eyjólfur ehf ($95.9M) and Flóki fasteignir ($13.7M) sit outside the reported borrowings figure, so the true obligation load is understated by anyone reading only the debt line. Including them cuts the "everything goes right" price from $5.04 to about $4.74.

Whether this is *edge* depends on whether these facts eventually move the price. They are all in public filings; we found them by reading the statements rather than the release, which is a repeatable process advantage, not a secret.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because parts of the bearish case are already consensus and one of our concerns has a legitimate innocent explanation:

- **The second-half growth question is not a discovery.** Our own initial write-up treated "they need 53–70% growth in H2" as a devastating find. Analysts model almost exactly that. Agreeing with the street is not an edge in either direction.
- **The business genuinely is back-half loaded.** In 2025, Q4 alone did $173M revenue and $69M adjusted EBITDA out of a full year of $593M/$137M — implying Q3 was roughly $114M/$14M. A guidance shape that looks absurd on a straight-line read is closer to normal for this company.
- **The cause of the revenue decline checks out as temporary.** We tested whether adalimumab (Humira biosimilar) was losing formulary placement — which would mean the revenue is gone, not delayed. The management discussion attributes it in primary text to post-inspection quality-system work, i.e. supply-side. That supports management's story.
- **The stock is already down 54% from its high** and the last equity raise cleared at $3.75. A lot of skepticism is in the price.
- **The cash-flow gap may be timing, not quality.** Working capital rebuild after a manufacturing pause plausibly consumes cash. We cannot yet distinguish "timing" from "the profit isn't real" — which is precisely why this is a pass rather than a short.

**Step 1 — the data gate must clear first (mandatory, no price substitutes for it).** Reopen the file only if one of these happens:

- The next results release includes a cash-flow statement showing **operating cash flow at or above reported adjusted EBITDA** for the second half, or at minimum a full-year operating cash flow that is not deeply negative; **or**
- The 2026 annual report (Form 20-F) related-party note quantifies the Alvogen/Lotus licensing revenue and shows the recurring, non-related-party business standing on its own.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, including the things that would embarrass us:

1. **Nobody in this court independently verified the price.** Our broker price tool refused the request for both benches. $4.27 comes from one data feed plus a secondary news read. Everything downstream — the multiple, the bands — inherits that.
2. **Whether the −$80.2M operating cash flow is timing or quality.** A working-capital rebuild after a manufacturing pause consumes cash legitimately. We have the number but not the decomposition, and that distinction is the whole thesis.
3. **Whether the FDA inspection has an enforcement or recall companion.** We could not query the database. Open.
4. **Whether the Reykjavík plant genuinely returned to planned output.** Management says so and the management discussion supports the cause story, but our independent instrument (satellite thermal) returned no usable signal at that latitude. Absence of corroboration, not contradiction.
5. **Whether the Q4-heavy seasonality survives 2026 specifically.** 2025's Q4 was 29% of the year's revenue and half its profit. Whether 2026's Q4 can repeat that depends on FDA approvals landing early enough in the quarter to ship — we have not stress-tested that timing.
6. **Whether the Lotus agreement announced 21 Aug is arm's-length.** Lotus acquired Alvogen; Alvogen owns 28.2% of Alvotech. The economics of that deal are not disclosed. This is the second such agreement in two quarters.
7. **Whether competing biosimilars are filed against the same targets.** The FDA does not publish pending biosimilar applications, and the Orange Book does not cover biologics. Not checkable with public data.
8. **The concentration risk in adalimumab (AVT02).** Real, acknowledged, and not quantified by either bench.
