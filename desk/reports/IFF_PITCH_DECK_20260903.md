# PITCH — IFF — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no order: the no-alcohol picks-and-shovels case dies on order-of-magnitude materiality (~0.03–0.13% of revenue), and the separate carve-out case at ~14.4× pro-forma was never tried, so it is neither bought nor killed here.

---

## What we believe

Three things, and only the first is a conviction.

**1. Removing ethanol from beer breaks three distinct things, and only one of them is a flavour-house sale.** Ethanol is not a passenger. It is a substrate for ester formation, a solvent that holds worty Strecker aldehydes out of the headspace, and a TRPV1 agonist that produces "warmth." Each break is repaired by a different party. The ester deficit is stoichiometric — no ethanol, no ethyl esters ([Muller et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1590/fst.32318)). The aldehyde problem switches on abruptly at ~0.5% ABV because that is where ethanol stops retaining them in liquid. The warmth deficit cannot be fixed by any process at all — only by adding a molecule.

**2. Because of that, the "theme" cannot lift a basket.** Each of the two production routes pays a *different* set of vendors. Physical removal (membrane/RO, vapour stripping, spinning cone) pays the equipment makers. The biological route — interrupted fermentation, cold contact, special yeast — needs **no additional plant equipment at all**, stated as its principal advantage in the peer-reviewed literature. So every hectolitre of NA beer routes to exactly one leg and starves the others. IFF's own DIAZYME NOLO is *marketed* as reducing dealcoholisation energy and throughput burden — i.e. as a reason to defer buying the equipment. A basket long IFF and long GEA is long both sides of the same substitution.

**3. IFF is not where the NA money is, and the amount of NA money is small.** NA beer is ~2% of ~1.85bn hL of global beer ≈ 37m hL. Purchased repair chemistry runs ~$0.30–1.50/hL. That is a global pool of **$11–55m**, split among at least six suppliers. IFF's share: **$2–10m against a $7.4–7.6bn revenue guide** — 0.03% to 0.13%. Meanwhile the only primary-verifiable patent aimed at the one unfixable break (warmth) belongs to **dsm-firmenich**, which was not in the basket, and the masking fix is patented by **Heineken** — the customer.

We believe the kill. We do not believe it says anything about whether IFF the equity is cheap.

## What the market believes

The market has no view on non-alcoholic beer at IFF whatsoever, and that is the point — IFF's own Q2 2026 earnings release does not contain the words alcohol, beer, or NOLO, and attributes Health & Biosciences growth to "Grain Processing, Food Biosciences & Animal Nutrition" ([8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-08-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000051253/000005125326000028/q22026991pressreleaseofiff.htm)).

What the market is actually trading is a **portfolio-reshaping story**: Pharma Solutions sold May 2025, Soy Crush/Concentrates/Lecithin closed March 2026, and Food Ingredients agreed to CVC for $3.832bn with close expected by end of Q2 2027 and IFF retaining ~10% of the vehicle ([8-K 2026-08-04, Item 7.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/51253/000005125326000032/)). At $82.83 that is a $21.1bn cap, ~$26.3bn EV, **16.8× the FY26 EBITDA guide and ~14.4× pro-forma the CVC proceeds**, 27.1× consensus EPS of $3.06. Street price target $91.21, about +10%. The consensus story is: close the deal, delever off 2.5×, and let guided EBITDA growth of cc +4–8% against sales of cc +2–4% do the work.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: on the thesis as filed, we don't — and the honest form of the edge is that we established that cheaply and are declining to pay for it.

Where we did do work the street has not:

- **We priced the pool from two independent directions and got the same answer.** Top-down (37m hL × $0.30–1.50/hL, six ways) and bottom-up off DIAZYME NOLO's *own* marketing — it is sold on cost savings, "up to 37% grist saving," which on ~$8–12/hL of malt is $3.0–4.4/hL saved, of which a supplier captures 20–30% = $0.6–1.3/hL. Two routes, same band. That is the strongest form the kill can take.
- **We read the patent landscape as an ownership map rather than a technology map.** The unengineerable break has a named owner and it is not IFF.
- **We identified an item neither the original thesis nor the first bench priced**: the retained ~10% equity in the CVC vehicle becomes an equity-method or Level-3 carrying value on a business IFF no longer controls — valuation opacity attached to a $3.832bn disposal.
- **The mechanism generalises.** "A thematic basket whose legs are technical substitutes for each other cannot be lifted by the theme" is filed as a reusable detector, not a one-off.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of the above isn't "priced in" — it's *irrelevant to price*, which is worse. A correct NA call moves ~0.1% of revenue. You cannot be paid for being right about it. The next four quarters of IFF equity are driven by deal close, leverage and mix, not by beer.

And on the parts that do matter to price, we are not differentiated:

- We produced the street's numbers and no desk fair value beside them. Ruling IFF "fairly priced" on that basis is counting analysts, not naming a mechanism — that finding was struck from the kill for exactly this reason.
- The "Nourish segment no longer exists" observation is the publicly disclosed January-2025 resegmentation, carried in every sell-side note. Consensus, not novel.
- The deceleration case is weaker than it first looked (see below), which makes the business look better and does nothing for us either way.

## What could go wrong

This is a FLAT verdict, so the losses available to us are the losses of being absent, and of buying the reopen band for the wrong reason.

**Failure 1 — we filed the right kill on the wrong question, and the name runs without us.** *Monitorable.* We killed "IFF as an NA-chemistry expression" and never tried "IFF as a post-carve-out compounder at ~14.4× with EBITDA guided to grow at twice sales." If the CVC deal closes on schedule and the multiple re-rates, IFF prints $95–105 and our reopen band at ~$62 never comes. Tripwire: price gate at $91.21 (street PT reached) forces the untried carve-out court, not a chase.

**Failure 2 — the reopen band fires because the denominator moved.** *Monitorable, and the trap is well-documented in our own history.* $62 is defined as 11× *pro-forma* EBITDA. The stock reaches $62 most easily by the EBITDA guide breaking or the CVC deal breaking — in which case the multiple we thought we were buying does not exist. This is the beta-bleed-vs-dislocation error in its purest form: a price gate that fires for the reason that invalidates it. Tripwire: no purchase at $62 without an 8-K-confirmed deal path and a reaffirmed guide.

**Failure 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE. The materiality kill rests on a number nobody publishes.** Our entire order-of-magnitude verdict is built on a per-hectolitre price for purchased NA repair chemistry that does not exist in any filing, any regulatory disclosure, or any vendor pricing sheet. If NA repair is not sold per-litre as commodity chemistry but as a **bundled multi-year systems contract** — flavour system + enzyme + technical service + line qualification, priced at $8–25/hL under exclusivity with a top-five brewer — then our TAM is wrong by precisely the order of magnitude we killed on. There is no instrument that would tell us. Ingredient shipments carry generic HS 3302 codes with no application field. Brewers do not disclose supplier identity. IFF publishes no end-market disaggregation, and we confirmed the 10-K does not contain that table. **Unknown-unknown class: private-contract pricing opacity in B2B ingredient supply** — the same class as supplier-identity non-disclosure, and rated permanently unverifiable in the science brief. We are betting on a base rate against an unobservable contract structure, and we would never learn we were wrong.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** Our own size is *not* the risk here, and it is worth stating why rather than assuming it. On an estimated $125–200m of daily dollar volume, a 1% book position is roughly 0.1–0.2% of ADV — three orders of magnitude below the 1%-of-ADV publishing ceiling. There is no market impact, no predicted-flow problem, and the value-ladder flow gate does not bind. The real structural interaction is elsewhere: **IFF is an S&P constituent and the Parametric direct-index sleeve is levered 159%**, so it very likely already holds IFF. Buying at a reopen band while that sleeve harvests creates wash-sale entanglement and silent duplication in a name we just rejected on the merits — and 2026 is a harvest year with a $1.855m reserve against a zero-basis gain. Second doctrine interaction: any option expression whose loss branch lands after Dec 31 defers the loss out of the harvest year, which alone disqualifies every 2027-dated structure unless the edge numerically dominates. It does not (arithmetic below).

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought the reopen band at ~$62 for the multiple rather than for the business, and the multiple was cheap because the CVC proceeds or the EBITDA denominator had already moved before we did.
