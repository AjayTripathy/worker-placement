03 / 10The wrong thesis — and the court's own concessions

The AI-margin pitch dies on the pricing model. The court killed two of its own bears too.

The generator sourced CRVL as an "AI adopter": a 3-year audited grind in the personnel-expense ratio with the stock −32%. The court's disconfirming pass dismantled the pitch — and, in the same pass, two of its own bear arguments. We keep all three corrections, because corrections are the credibility:

ClaimWhat the record showsStatus
"AI savings flow to CorVel's margin" Filing history: "%-of-savings" pricing language weakened from FY1997 and was dropped entirely by FY2012. Bill review is priced per bill — efficiency accrues to the vendor. Confirming datum: network solutions +15% on "higher revenue per bill" with expanding gross margin. Re-framed — real, but it is a vendor-economics point, not an AI story
Court's bear #1: "savings-share pass-through caps the margin" Conceded by the court itself — refuted by the same filing history above. The surviving fragment: case management (inside Patient Management) bills on time incurred, so automating it shrinks its own revenue. Withdrawn
Court's bear #2: "declining WC premiums shrink the TPA fee pool" Refuted by the court itself — it conflated premium rate with medical spend. Loss costs are decelerating (−9.2% → −6.1% → −5.0%), NCCI accident-year combined ratio is 102 (soft market ending), medical cost per claim +6–7%/yr, and California is an outright tailwind: pure premium −2.1% → +8.7% → +6.6%, OMFS physician conversion factor +5.8%. The dollar pool CorVel monetizes is growing. Withdrawn

What this slide buys the reader: the bear case that survives (next slide) is the one that outlived the court's own attempt to kill it.

05 / 10Disclosure behavior — two genuine tells

The metric vanished the year it turned, and the question got a non-answer

TellThe record
1. The deleted metric The FY25 10-K quantified the operating driver plainly: "Total new claims increased by 5%." The FY26 10-K — the year Patient Management went to +0.1% — contains no current-year claims-count figure at all (grep-verified against the full filing text). The one number that measures the franchise disappeared exactly when it stopped flattering.
2. The deflection Asked about the deceleration on the Q3 call, Executive Chair Combs attributed it to "short-term operational factors… including the normalization of our effective tax rate" — a tax rate cannot explain a revenue deceleration — and, pressed further: "we do not break up the various products."
3. The concession (fair to management) The risk itself is disclosed in their own words: "Although pricing pressure is likely over time, we expect cost efficiencies to more than offset any associated fee adjustments." That is the entire bear-and-bull of the margin story in one sentence — honestly stated, outcome unproven.

Grading note (house honesty framework): these are emphasis-and-omission tells — a review flag, not an exclusion. Every damaging number in this deck is computable from CorVel's own filings. But takeaway-vs-data divergence is exactly what the frozen calls on slide 10 are built to score: if the December-quarter break was noise, Wednesday's 10-Q will say so in the service-line note.

06 / 10The real bull — CERIS, not AI

The ownable thesis is payment integrity with one good year of evidence

  • Bookings +56% year-over-year — disclosed on the Q4 call, the single strongest forward datum in the file.
  • Four large payer programs launching CY2026, including two multiyear top-10 national payer partnerships; revenue explicitly back-half weighted.
  • CERIS shifting to prepay — realization moves from 6+ months to 1–2 months, pulling the bookings wave into the P&L faster than the historical pattern.
  • This is a dated, mechanical H2-FY27 catalyst — and it is a payment-integrity story (per-bill vendor economics, the slide-3 finding working for the holder), not an AI story.

Why half-size even on a clean gate: the CERIS evidence is one year old, the segment is the smaller of the two, and nobody outside the company has audited the bookings-to-revenue conversion. The court sized the starter at 0.75% — half the normal quality-starter weight — for exactly that reason.

Coverage vacuum, restated: with no consensus FY27 and no analyst engagement, a CERIS-driven beat has no estimate ladder to climb — re-rating requires the company to put the numbers where the market can see them. Patience is structural here, not optional.
