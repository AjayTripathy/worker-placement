# PITCH — TTD — PROPOSED VERDICT: NO ENTRY / STAY FLAT, with a defined event-gated reopen — **PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

## What we believe

The Trade Desk has real, *arriving* damage — but our original brief overstated it, and the court cut it back.

What is confirmed: in May 2026 management guided Q2 revenue to "at least $750M," and in August printed **$715.1M** ([Q2'26 press release](https://investors.thetradedesk.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/The-Trade-Desk-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). Breaking an explicit "at least" floor within one quarter of issuing it means the new Q3 floor ("at least $650M," ~$160M adjusted EBITDA) has no underwriting value. The company's full-year 40% adjusted-EBITDA-margin target is arithmetically dead — hitting it would require roughly a 64% margin in Q4. Incremental revenue declines are flowing through to profit at more than 100%.

What did *not* survive prosecution: the collapse story. Margin **improved** sequentially (30% in Q1 → 33.7% in Q2). Stock compensation is **falling**, not exploding — $109M per quarter, down 15% year over year, about $438M annualized. The diluted share count **shrank 5.2%** year over year (495.8M → 469.9M) because the buyback more than offsets stock comp. There is no debt, $1.49B in cash and short-term investments, and positive operating cash flow. Revenue still grew ~3% year over year in the miss quarter.

The decisive open question is *why* revenue slowed: macro (consumer-goods and auto advertisers are ~25% of spend, per management) or Amazon taking the business. The court ruled this **unresolved** — and we cannot own or short an unresolved question run by a management team whose guidance we can no longer use.

## What the market believes

At $13.80, the market prices structural loss. The stock is down ~76% from the evidence pack's 52-week high of $56.77 — and roughly 90% from its 2024 all-time high near $141.53 — and sits 7.6% above its 52-week low of $12.83. (The true 52-week high is disputed: one secondary source says $91.45, another corroborates $56.77; the court left it unresolved, but either way, a very large amount of damage is already in the price.) Enterprise value is about $5.0B, or roughly **13.5×** annualized adjusted EBITDA after subtracting stock compensation. The market is treating a company that still grew 3% and expanded margins sequentially as if it were melting — because it no longer believes anything management says, and because industry data (Guideline, via [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/amazon-google-and-meta-are-eating-the-ad-market-and-the-data-proves-it/)) shows Amazon's share of programmatic spend going from under 10% to nearly 20% in about fifteen months.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The bear case, as commonly told, contains a selection error.** The same Guideline dataset that shows Amazon's rise also shows The Trade Desk going from 22% to 31% of the measured spend over the same period, "growing at exactly the market rate" globally. Amazon appears to be eating *others*, not (yet, globally) The Trade Desk. Our own brief cited only the Amazon half; the court caught it.
2. **The numbers are better than the consensus narrative.** Shrinking share count, falling stock comp, no debt, sequential margin improvement — none of that fits a terminal-decline story, and all of it was misreported in our own first-pass brief (bearishly), which suggests the market may be making the same errors.
3. **There is a clean, near-term falsifier.** The court adopted a symmetric test: a Q3 revenue print of **$700M or above, by itself, refutes the structural-loss story** (guide is "at least $650M"). If the market is pricing structural loss and the refutation fires, the reopen gate below is live at very depressed prices.
