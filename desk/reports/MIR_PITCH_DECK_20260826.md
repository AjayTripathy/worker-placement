# PITCH — MIR — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position at $14.685; this is a price-gated wait, not a rejection: the business is decelerating on every clean measure, but the only defensible action is a small starter at $12.50 or below *after* the Q3 print, not a short and not a rejection of the name.

---

## What we believe

Mirion makes radiation-detection and measurement equipment — dosimeters, monitoring systems, instrumentation for nuclear power plants, defense, labs and hospitals. It is the kind of business the market has been paying up for since 2025 because nuclear power came back into fashion.

We believe three things, and only three, with confidence:

1. **The underlying business is slowing, not accelerating.** The company's own filed comparison — what the business would have earned if last year's acquisitions had been owned all along — shows growth going from +10.9% in Q1 to +3.7% in Q2. That comparison has no definitional wiggle room: same assets, both periods, in the 10-Q ([SEC 10-Q, period ended 2026-06-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001809987/000162828026050604/mir-20260630.htm)).
2. **The forward-looking number the bulls point to is also rolling over.** New orders excluding acquisitions went from $241M (+19%) in Q1 to $229M (+10%) in Q2 — down sequentially, and reported in the vetted press release, not just a slide ([8-K exhibit, Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001809987/000162828026050171/a2026-07x28exhibit991.htm)).
3. **The price already assumes this goes fine.** At $14.685 the whole company (equity plus net debt) is about $4.47B. On a realistic 3%-growth year (~$265M of adjusted EBITDA) that's roughly 17x. Our own fair-value band puts the stock at $12.50–$14.75. We are being asked to buy at the top of our band.

We also believe the full-year profit guidance is being read too kindly. It was described as "reaffirmed," but the per-share number was quietly lowered earlier in the year — from $0.50–$0.57 in February to $0.48–$0.55 in April, then held. Reaffirming a number you already cut is not the same as holding the line.

## What the market believes

The market believes Mirion is a picks-and-shovels way to own the nuclear build-out. Specifically:

- Full-year revenue lands at guidance (~$1.14B) and adjusted earnings come in at about $0.53 a share — *above* the middle of the company's own range.
- Second-half strength is normal for this business (it is, for revenue) and the acquisitions bought in 2025 fill any gap.
- Backlog and order growth in nuclear power — management has talked about backlog up ~17% and nuclear power orders up ~50% — mean demand is fine and the weak first half is a timing artifact.

There is nothing crazy about this view. The company does have real backlog covering roughly 81% of the year's revenue guidance, and the acquired businesses are genuinely growing faster than the legacy base. The disagreement is about the last two percentage points of growth and about what multiple you pay for them.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the filings and the market read the slides.** The two most bullish numbers — backlog growth and nuclear order growth — appear only in the earnings deck and the call. They are absent from the 8-K press release and absent from the 10-Q. A company that publishes orders-excluding-acquisitions ($229M, +10%) in the vetted document and puts backlog only on a slide has made a choice, and the choice is information.
- **We caught the guidance cut that press coverage did not.** Almost everyone diffed Q2 guidance against Q1 guidance and saw "reaffirmed." Diffed against the original February guide, per-share earnings were cut about 4% at both ends. This is now a reusable pattern for us: always diff a "reaffirmation" against the *original* full-year guide.
- **We used the acquisition-adjusted table the sell side skipped.** The +10.9% → +3.7% deceleration is in the 10-Q's pro-forma note, which is exactly the table that gets ignored because it's boring and unaudited-looking. It is also the only clean like-for-like read available.
- **Our number is 8% below the street's, with a stated mechanism** — not just a lower guess. The mechanism: second-half profit has to grow 27–39% year over year to hit guidance, while the acquisition tailwind is shrinking as last year's deals lap into the base.

## Why it might be priced in

Honest answer: **substantially, it is.** The stock is down 51% from its 52-week high of $30.20 and sits 3.9% above its 52-week low of $14.13. That is not a market that has missed the deceleration — it is a market that has already taken the punishment. The February gap down (about −12.5%) appears to be the market repricing exactly the guide-credibility problem our bearish case rests on.

What this means practically:
- Our bearish case is **mostly consensus**. After the court audit, only about four of the surviving findings are genuinely non-obvious; two are things the tape already knows.
- There is no short here. A name 4% off its low with a real nuclear-demand narrative and a shrinking float story is not a short at 17x.
- The remaining edge is narrow: it is *the size and timing of the miss*, and whether the multiple compresses further, not whether a miss happens.

## What could go wrong

Assume we act on the plan below — a 0.5% starter at $12.50 after a Q3 print that clears our gates. Here is how we lose.

**Failure narrative 1 — The value trap (most likely).** Q3 clears our gates (orders excluding acquisitions ≥ +10%, organic growth ≥ 6%), we buy at $12.50, and then nothing improves. Growth settles at 3%, the company misses the low end of its earnings range the way it did last year, and the multiple compresses from ~15x toward 12x as the nuclear enthusiasm cools. That is roughly $9.50–$10.50, a 20–25% loss on a starter. *Tripwire exists:* Q4 orders excluding acquisitions, and the FY27 guide.

**Failure narrative 2 — We are right and make nothing.** The order cycle genuinely inflects, the stock re-rates off the $14.13 low, and it never trades at $12.50. We were correct about the deceleration and correct that the price was full, and we own zero. Given how many quality names we have already passed on at "fair," this is a real and repeating cost, not a hypothetical one. *Tripwire exists:* the price gate itself is the tripwire — it just fires as a non-event.

**Failure narrative 3 — Something in the capital structure we never saw (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).** Both benches built the enterprise value bridge without confirming whether a **tax receivable agreement** exists. Mirion came public via SPAC with a dual-class structure; that structure very often carries a TRA, which is a cash claim on the company that sits ahead of shareholders and appears in *no* quarterly filing — only in the 10-K note neither bench pulled. Adjacent and equally invisible: the Paragon purchase-price allocation is explicitly **not finalized**, so the intangible amortization that creates the entire gap between "adjusted" and actual GAAP earnings is still moving; and Mirion is a serial acquirer, meaning a large debt-funded deal or an equity raise can land between filings with no observable precursor. **Unknown-unknown class: undisclosed claims on the equity plus discretionary capital-allocation actions — facts that live in documents we have not read, or in decisions not yet made.** There is no sensor that can be armed for a decision management has not taken yet. The only mitigation is (a) read the 10-K TRA note before any entry, and (b) size for the possibility that the enterprise value bridge is understated.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**
- *Our size vs. the market.* A 0.5% starter is roughly $25k on a $5M book against a $3.6B company. Market impact is nil. **Caveat: we never actually pulled MIR's average daily volume this session — that is a gap, and the "impact is nil" claim is an inference from market cap, not a measurement.**
- *Bidding into our own predicted flow.* This is the sharper risk. Our thesis says the Q3 print disappoints. A resting good-till-cancelled buy order at $12.50 would therefore fill precisely on the day we are proven right — and, because a disappointing print can gap well through $12.50, it fills at $12.50 when fair value has just moved to $11. A resting limit into a scheduled earnings date is selling volatility for nothing. **Any entry must be a discretionary click after the print, never a resting order through it.**
- *Doctrine interactions.* (a) Valuation is a price gate, never a rejection — which is why we are overruling the red bench's REJECT grade. (b) A named kill is required to sit flat rather than take a minimum starter; we have named kills (confirmed deceleration on three independent measures, price at the top of our own band), so flat is legitimate here. (c) Any starter opens a short-term tax clock in a taxable account with no offsetting harvest value; the position must be justified on return alone. (d) We have not checked whether MIR overlaps the Parametric sleeve's holdings and creates a wash-sale surface — unverified, and it must be checked before the first click.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we mistook one bounced orders number for a genuine demand inflection, bought a structurally decelerating business at the top of our own fair-value band, and then watched the multiple compress as the nuclear build-out narrative that supported it cooled.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry at $14.685 under any branch.** Both benches independently reached flat. What follows are the reopen conditions.

**Reopen gate (both conditions, at the Q3 print):**
- Orders excluding acquisitions ≥ **+10% year over year** (this is the vetted-venue metric, immune to acquisition-base distortion), **AND**
- Organic growth ≥ **6%** (widened by ~1 point to absorb the Certrec lapping effect measured above)

**If both clear:**
- **Starter: 0.5% of book, at $12.50 or lower.** Discretionary click after the print — **no resting order through the print** (see the pre-mortem's reflexive leg).
- $12.50 corresponds to roughly 14.8x a 3%-growth year (~$265M EBITDA) after subtracting $807.9M of net debt across 249.0M shares. The hard floor from the same math is **$11.65**.
- **Second tranche: additional 0.5% at $11.00 or lower**, only if the Q4 orders number excluding acquisitions is still ≥ +10%. Price alone does not add.
- **Maximum position without a fresh court: 1.0% of book.**

**The stricter alternative gate (one bench's higher bar), which no base-composition artifact can manufacture:**
- Q3 organic growth **≥ 9% with the Medical segment organically positive**, or Q3 adjusted EBITDA **≥ $70M** (putting the year on pace for ~$150M through Q3), at a price **≤ $12.00**. Clearing this justifies going straight to the full 1.0%.

**Structural break — no re-court until the FY27 guide (roughly February 2027):**
- Orders excluding acquisitions **negative**, or organic growth **≤ 4%**.

**Exits / trims:** none proposed — there is no position. If a starter is ever taken: a close below **$10.00** with orders excluding acquisitions negative is a full exit, tax-adjusted for a short-term loss.
