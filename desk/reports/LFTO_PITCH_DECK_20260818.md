# PITCH — LFTO — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position today, and no starter: both benches land on flat, and the honest reason is that we do not have a view the market doesn't already have — but this is a deferral with dated reopen conditions (Q3 print ~2026-11-11, share lockup 2026-11-30), not a permanent no.

---

## What we believe

Liftoff Mobile runs a mobile advertising marketplace — it buys and places ads inside phone apps for advertisers. Blackstone put Liftoff and Vungle together in 2021 and took the combined company public on June 3, 2026 at $23 a share. The stock is $19.53, which is 15% below the IPO price, 35% below its high, and 1% off an all-time low set on August 17 — four sessions after the first earnings report as a public company.

What we believe, stated plainly:

1. **The business is decelerating, and the company's own guidance says so.** Revenue growth year-over-year steps down 37% → 35% → 22% → 19% across this year's four quarters. The "27–28% full-year growth" headline is an average of two different regimes. The rate the company is actually exiting the year at is roughly 19%.
2. **The size of the deceleration is real but smaller than our first pass claimed.** Against the company's own recent quarterly trend (about +6.7% sequential growth), a flat third quarter is roughly $15M light — not the $24M our red bench first computed off bad data.
3. **The valuation is not obviously wrong in either direction.** About 8.6x enterprise value to this year's guided adjusted profit, and roughly a 6.6% free-cash-flow yield on the equity after the June debt paydown. That is cheap for a 27% grower and fair-to-full for a 19% grower. Which one it is, is exactly what the next two quarters decide.
4. **There is a large, dated supply event ahead.** Roughly 147.5M insider shares become sellable on about November 30 — about 6.7x the entire 21.85M share float. Blackstone holds 49.5%, General Atlantic 15% with board representation.
5. **Therefore: the December supply is the buying opportunity, not the thing to own through.** We see no reason to hold a likely-soft print and a supply cliff back to back when the same shares should be available afterward.

## What the market believes

The sell side is unanimously positive and unanimously conflicted: 13 analysts, 13 Buys, average target $35.46 — every one of them from a bank that underwrote the deal. There is essentially no independent coverage yet.

But the *numbers* the market carries are not bullish. Consensus for Q3 was $214.8M — already below the company's own recent sequential trend. The company guided $217–222M and "beat." So the Street had already marked down the growth; the price action (down 35% from the high, an all-time low four days after the print) says the marginal buyer has done the same and then some.

The market's working view is roughly: *this is a decelerating but cash-generative asset with a busted IPO chart, a tiny float, a leveraged balance sheet, and a very large seller arriving in December. Nobody has to own it before then.*

We agree with that. That is the problem with the idea.

## Why we might have edge

Three candidates, honestly rated:

- **The dilution nobody is modeling (real, but not yet operative).** The 10-Q discloses 21,576,558 management options — 12.7% of shares outstanding, about $150M of grant-date value — with **zero expense recognized**, because vesting requires the sponsors to sell at a specified return. Every enterprise-value calculation in circulation uses 169.3M shares and adds back stock compensation. If the stock ever works, those 21.6M shares come with it. That is a real, unmodeled haircut to any bull case above roughly $23. It is not a bear argument at $19.53 — see below.
- **The supply is worse than casually assumed.** 6.7x the float, with a 13D holder that has board rights. This is knowable and dated, and small-float IPO supply events are routinely underweighted.
- **A method we can reuse.** Test a guide against the company's *own* same-quarter history, not against consensus. A guide that clears the Street while landing below the company's own trend is deceleration wearing a beat's clothing. That method is sound. Our first application of it was contaminated (see below), which is itself the lesson.

**What we do not have: a differentiated number.** Our own red bench's Q3 estimate ($217M or below) sits *above* the $214.8M consensus. We do not have a 2027 consensus figure to disagree with — we could not obtain one. So for the period that matters right now, we are not saying anything the market isn't. That is the single most important line in this deck.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The stock is already down 35% and sitting at an all-time low.** A 20% de-rate on the print, then a fresh low on no news, is a market that has already absorbed the deceleration.
- **Consensus was already below trend before the guide.** Our mechanism is in the numbers.
- **The lockup is on every calendar.** A 180-day expiry from a June 3 prospectus is arithmetic, not insight.
- **The valuation already reflects a lower growth rate.** 8.6x is not a growth-company multiple. The market re-rated the asset; that is what a correctly functioning market does.
- **The one genuinely differentiated bear finding inverts at today's price.** The management options require the sponsors to sell at a *specified return*. At $19.53 — below the $23 IPO — a December sale does not achieve that. Nothing vests. There is no dilution in the base case, and management is actively *disincentivized* from supporting a cheap secondary. That cuts against our own supply-cliff argument.

- **The Q3 report date.** Feed-derived only. No company announcement confirms 2026-11-11.
- **The lockup terms.** The 180-day count is arithmetic; the actual language — early-release price triggers, staged releases, underwriter waiver provisions — is unread. A staged release instead of a single cliff would materially change the December setup.
- **The share count.** 169.33M (filing cover) vs 177.1M (implied by the price feed). Unresolved, and it moves the multiple from 8.6x to 8.9x.
- **The live price.** Broker quote denied on permission. Everything here is an August 17 close.
- **The bridge from $514M of guided adjusted profit to a GAAP net loss.** Adjusted profit is 59% of revenue while the company reported a GAAP net loss of $4.2M in the quarter. Interest (~$36M/quarter), stock compensation and one-time IPO charges account for much of it, and the small depreciation line is consistent with the 2021 combination's carryover accounting — but **neither bench built the full reconciliation.** A 59% adjusted margin at an advertising marketplace deserves one.
- **Two stock-compensation series that don't tie.** The income statement shows $34.834M for the first half; the cash-flow tag shows $26.634M. Blue attributes the difference to $5.1M capitalized into internal software plus $1.8M of cash distributions. That explains $6.9M of an $8.2M gap. Close, not closed.
- **The World Cup contribution.** Named by management as a Q2 driver, never sized. Our own $13M "unexplained deceleration" figure depends on an implicit ~$9M assumption for it that nobody wrote down.

- **2027 consensus.** Never obtained. We cannot say whether we disagree with the market about next year — which is the only period where a differentiated view would be worth anything.
- **Pre-IPO sponsor distributions.** Read from a prospectus summary, not the filed document.
- **The core operating metric.** The company's daily-active-revenue figure is company-defined and unaudited. There is no contracted backlog to check it against — this is spot-market ad buying, so no such disclosure exists.
