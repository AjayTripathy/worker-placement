## What we believe

Webull (BULL, $7.26) will likely report Q2 revenue of roughly **$187–194M on August 19**, above the ~$178M analyst consensus. This isn't a guess about momentum — both of our internal review teams independently rebuilt the revenue from Webull's own published monthly operating data (trading volumes, margin balances of $990M up 110% year-over-year, interest-earning assets) and both landed above consensus even after adversarial cuts. A new regulatory change effective June 4, 2026 — the SEC's approval of FINRA's loosened pattern-day-trading rules ([SEC order](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-105226.pdf)) — is a genuine tailwind to options activity, Webull's core engine.

The stock is also down 57.5% from its 52-week high of $17.08, which looks like a large dislocation on a screen.

## What the market believes

The market is not primarily trading this stock on revenue. On May 21, Webull reported Q1 revenue up 36% — and the stock went to new lows, because the report also showed a $21.7M GAAP loss, marketing spend up 115% to $49.4M, and stock compensation up 113%, with management refusing to guide and explicitly signaling intent to keep spending into the day-trading land-grab. The market's operating axis is **discretionary spending, share dilution, and supply** — 147.4M shares plus ~26.8M warrants are registered for resale ([424B3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1866364/000121390026048019/ea0287646-424b3_webull.htm)), including 20.75M shares from the China-domiciled founder. Options are pricing roughly a ±13% move into the report.

The market may also be rationally capping the multiple: funded accounts have been **flat at 5.1M for four months**. All the growth is existing customers trading harder at record margin levels — today's +45–50% comparisons become next year's impossible ones.

## Why we might have edge

- The revenue reconstruction is real and was verified twice, adversarially. Both teams tried to kill it and only shaved it (from ~$190M midpoint by $2–4M for a shrinking low-yield deposit base).
- We have a **free, dated look before the event**: July operating data has historically arrived around the 14th, five days before earnings. If it shows options volume holding above ~4.3M contracts/day *and* funded accounts growing again, the two biggest bear findings (reflexivity, spend-into-flat-accounts) weaken materially — and we get to buy at roughly the same price.
- We caught the market carrying a wrong earnings date (several aggregators show Aug 27; the company's own Aug 5 press release confirms **Aug 19**). Small, but it tells us attention on this name is sloppy.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: much of it may be. The "57% off the high" framing is **half-artifact** — our blue team dated the $17.08 high to August 2025, the decay tail of the low-float post-SPAC squeeze (the all-time high was $62.90 in April 2025). The stock is actually **61% off its low and only ~11% below its 26-week high of $8.15** — the tape has already re-rated substantially since the strong June data was published July 15. Worse, the $17.08 anchor drops out of the 52-week window around **August 18**, so the "big drawdown" statistic self-heals the day before earnings regardless of fundamentals. And the payoff variable that actually moves this stock — marketing spend — is unobservable until August 19. A beat on revenue with another $60M marketing quarter likely loses money.
