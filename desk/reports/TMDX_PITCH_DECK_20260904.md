# PITCH — TMDX — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-03 — REJECT (2/10). No position, no order, no size. The thesis died on the primary record; the stock is roughly fairly priced, which is a different and weaker reason to stay away.**

---

## What we believe

TransMedics sells a machine that keeps a donated organ alive and warm on a pump while it travels, instead of packing it in ice. It also flies the organ there on its own planes. The clinical case for the machine is real but narrow: in the one large randomized liver trial it cut early graft dysfunction (17.3% vs 30.5%) and bile-duct complications, and it did **not** improve one-year survival ([PROTECT, JAMA Surgery](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8733869/)). In hearts it proved *non-inferior*, not better ([NEJM 2023](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2212438)). There is no randomized evidence of a survival advantage for this device in any organ.

We believe the business is a genuine ~21% grower with $491M of cash and positive operating profit — and that at $88 the market has already worked out most of what is wrong with it. Specifically we believe four things the original pitch got backwards:

1. **Guidance was cut.** Full-year adjusted operating margin went from about 16% to 12.5–14%. The pitch said "guidance never cut."
2. **The profit decline is mostly deliberate spending, not decay.** Operating profit fell $12.8M year over year, but research spending alone rose $15.7M. Take that out and profit is *up* $2.9M on 21% more revenue.
3. **There is live legal trouble** — just not the kind the pitch searched for. A shareholder fraud case survived dismissal on 2026-07-21, and an outside party's petition asking the FDA to suspend approval of the device has been sitting undecided on the agency docket since January 2025 ([FDA-2025-P-0182](https://www.regulations.gov/docket/FDA-2025-P-0182)).
4. **The accounting controls are still broken.** The company says in present tense, in its own Q2-26 release, that it has an unfixed weakness in how it controls inventory — while installing a new ERP system and heading into legal discovery.

Net: a fair price for a decent business carrying an unusual stack of integrity and disclosure problems. Fair plus baggage is not an investment.

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we just wrote, and has for months. The stock is 43% below its 52-week high but has already bounced 47% off its low — this is not a fresh break, it is a stock that has been argued over and settled at a number. Analysts' published full-year estimate ($2.47 of earnings on $737.7M of revenue) sits at the *low* end of the company's own guidance range, and the lowest published price target is $85 — within a dollar or two of where it trades. Coverage of the second quarter led with "margins compress." Nobody is confused.

The one thing the market may not have fully priced: the company's earnings-per-share number is unstable for a mechanical reason. It has a $460M convertible bond due June 2028 that converts near $92. With the stock at $88, the share count used for earnings flips between about 35.5M and 40.6M depending on the day — the same profit prints either $2.53 or $2.21. A "beat" or "miss" here can be an artifact of which share count a broker used, not of how the business performed.

## Why we might have edge

Three places where we did work the sell-side visibly has not:

- **Seasonality.** In both prior years the third quarter's revenue fell versus the second: −8.6% in 2025, −4.9% in 2024. Two for two. The next report (expected 2026-10-29) is a third quarter, off a record $189.9M base. If the pattern holds, the headline will look ugly for a reason that has nothing to do with the thesis.
- **The guidance perimeter.** The company raised its revenue range while explicitly *excluding* revenue from the aviation business it just bought — and left the margin number out of that document entirely, disclosing the margin cut only on the call. So reported revenue will clear a bar that excludes the acquired revenue, while the metric the acquisition damages was never stated in the vetted document.
- **The spending has no paper trail.** Management said the margin cut is mostly investment in a kidney version of the device. There is no registered kidney trial on ClinicalTrials.gov and no public FDA application for one. Shareholders are absorbing a jump in research spending from 10.1% to 16.7% of revenue against a program that cannot be verified anywhere in the public record.

None of these is a reason to buy. Two of the three are reasons the next few months could be worse than consensus, and the third cuts both ways.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost all of it, honestly. The margin cut was covered in the press the day it happened. The shareholder lawsuit is on the Stanford securities-litigation database and was written up when the dismissal ruling came down. The valuation-allowance distortion in last year's fourth-quarter earnings was corrected by the company in a standalone release, so every year-over-year comparison in the street model is already normalized. The competitive threats — in-donor perfusion for hearts, cold oxygenated perfusion for livers, OrganOx now owned by Terumo — are discussed openly in the transplant literature.

The honest summary is that our "edge" reduces to a seasonal pattern, a guidance-definition trick, and an unverifiable R&D line. That is worth maybe a few dollars of expected value on the short side and nothing at all on the long side.

## What could go wrong

*(The three ways we lose money — including on the decision to stay flat.)*

**Failure 1 — We are wrong about "fair," and it re-rates up without us.** The bear case is entirely about the next two quarters. If the third-quarter report breaks the seasonal pattern, the aviation dilution lands smaller than guided, and the FDA denies the petition, this is a 21% grower with 60% gross margins re-rating off a washed-out base. Our own generous 2027 recovery case values it at $104. **Tripwire exists:** price above $105, or a Q3 adjusted operating margin at/above the top of the cut band with the aviation business consolidated.

**Failure 2 — The integrity stack compounds instead of resolving.** An unfixed inventory control weakness, a new ERP going in, and legal discovery starting are three things that individually are survivable and together are how restatements happen. If inventory gets restated while a fraud case is in discovery, $88 is not a floor and the fair-value band becomes irrelevant. **Tripwire exists:** the controls section of each 10-Q, and any 8-K.

**Failure 3 — no monitorable tripwire.** The surviving fraud claim goes to what the CEO said about whether hospitals were required to buy the logistics service in order to get the device. That service is 42% of quarterly revenue. Discovery is private: internal emails, sales-comp plans, and hospital contracts will be read by the plaintiffs long before we ever see them, and the first public signal will be either a settlement number or a dismissal — both arriving already priced. **There is no instrument on our desk that watches this.** *Unknown-unknown class: adversary-controlled information release — an opposing party accumulates decisive facts about the revenue model on a timetable we cannot observe and cannot subpoena.* The second untripwired item is the kidney program: no registry entry, no agency docket, so the largest single driver of the margin cut has no public observable at all until the company chooses to speak. *Class: unregistered internal program — capital allocation into an asset with no external verification surface.*

**The structural / reflexive leg.** Our size is not the problem here and we should not pretend otherwise: at a 0.5% starter against the ~$5M deployment plan that is roughly $25,000, a few hundred shares, immaterial against any plausible daily volume in this name. (We could not quote actual volume — market-data permissions were denied, see below.) The reflexive risk is doctrinal, not mechanical, and it runs in three ways:

- *We would be buying into flow we ourselves predict.* Our own seasonal work says the 2026-10-29 report will very likely show revenue down sequentially. Entering before it means paying today for a price we expect to be lower in eight weeks. That is the exact pattern the desk has ruled against.
- *Our standing default pushes toward a starter, and we are overriding it.* The house rule is that a clean court with no named kill still buys a minimum starter, because refusing to act is itself a decision that goes ungraded. We are not flat by drift — we are flat on named kills: the margin-guidance cut, the surviving fraud claim, and the unremediated control weakness. That override is recorded here so it can be graded later.
- *The integrity rule caps us below the market.* Our own framework says a name with an unfixed control weakness and a live fraud claim cannot be treated as fairly-paid risk and must be discounted to 0.85× fair value — that is $72–89 against a fair band of $85–105. The cap lands at or below the current price. Under our own rules there is no entry, which means the *only* way we lose here is by winning the argument and buying anyway.

**"If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___"** → filled: *that we override a correctly-reasoned rejection out of discomfort at owning nothing, buy a starter before the October report, and eat a predictable sequential revenue decline in a name where every other open question — controls, discovery, the FDA docket — resolves on somebody else's calendar and not ours.*

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS

**M-1 — The 3-year "60.7% vs 83.3%" number is the most likely bad kill in this file.** The Heart Surgery Forum analysis (C-9) is retrospective UNOS, OCS n=507 vs NRP n=265, and its **abstract discloses neither propensity matching nor confidence intervals nor pairwise p-values**. Selection is the obvious confounder: DPP/OCS is chosen for *long-distance, longer-out-of-body* recoveries (NRP's own literature reports significantly shorter distance and out-of-body time), so the OCS cohort is systematically the harder cohort. A propensity-matched analysis of the same question found **no 1-year difference** (C-10). Do not present these two as agreeing or average them; they are different designs answering the same question with opposite implications.

**M-2 — Non-inferiority is not superiority, and 6 months is not 3 years.** The DCD Heart trial's estimand was *6-month risk-adjusted survival, non-inferiority margin*. Reading it as evidence of long-term durability is a category error in both directions.

**M-3 — Chapman ≠ PROTECT.** Both are US liver NMP RCTs; the null one used **OrganOx metra**, the positive one used **OCS Liver**. They also differ in primary endpoint definition and donor risk (Chapman median DRI 1.6). Anyone using Chapman to refute PROTECT, or PROTECT to validate metra, is comparing devices, populations and endpoints simultaneously.

**M-4 — The two liver labels are not the same population, and TransMedics' is broader.** OCS Liver: DCD **≤55 years, ≤30 min WIT**. OrganOx metra: DCD **≤40 years, ≤20 min WIT**. Both cap macrosteatosis at ≤15%. A "both are FDA approved for DCD" comparison silently equates a wider indication with a narrower one.

**M-5 — "Approved" carries different evidentiary weight per device.** OCS Liver: PMA on a positive 300-patient RCT. VitaSmart: **De Novo** (a different pathway) on a 219-patient RCT. LungFX: **PMA despite missing its performance goal with numerically worse 12-month mortality**. Ranking these by regulatory status alone inverts the evidence.

**M-6 — MAUDE is a numerator without a denominator.** The failure reports in C-12 are real and specific, but MAUDE is passive-surveillance, is not a rate, and the only denominator on offer is the manufacturer's own "<0.2%" (C-11). Neither a bull nor a bear case can be built on MAUDE counts alone; the Scorpion petition's under-reporting allegation (T-5b) is precisely a claim that the numerator is understated, and it is unresolved at FDA.

**M-7 — The company's per-organ revenue is not product revenue.** Q2-26: liver ~$148M + heart ~$33M + lung ~$2M = **$183M**, against **transplant product revenue of $111M** and total revenue of $190M. The organ split therefore bundles logistics/service. Any "device ASP" or "per-case device revenue" derived from the organ split will be inflated by roughly the logistics load.

**M-8 — Two terminated TransMedics trials are benign; do not build a kill on them.** **NCT04194437 (OCS Liver DCD, n=9)** — registry whyStopped: *"Enrollment in Liver DCD was stopped as a result of the FDA approval for the OCS Liver System."* **NCT03343535 (EXPAND II Lung, n=46)** — *"Enrollment in EXPAND II was stopped June 24, 2019 as a result of the FDA approval for the EXPAND indication."* Both terminations are administrative consequences of approval, not safety or futility stops.

**M-9 — "Utilization rate 89%" is not an outcome.** It is the fraction of *perfused* DCD hearts that were transplanted (90/101). It says nothing about survival and cannot be compared to a survival percentage.

**M-10 — The ENHANCE Part B control arm is a sponsor device.** If the CHOPS IDE amendment is granted, the "static cold storage" comparator will be TransMedics' own investigational active-cooling device rather than conventional phase-change cold storage. Whichever way the trial reads out, the comparator is not the standard of care that clinicians actually use today, and a superiority result would be against CHOPS specifically.

**M-11 — DPP ≠ OCS in registry data.** UNOS/SRTR cannot reliably identify which perfusion device was used; the SRTR-based study explicitly notes the registry "poorly captures organ storage/transport practices" and that misclassification is possible. "DPP" is a proxy for OCS in the US, not a measurement of it.
