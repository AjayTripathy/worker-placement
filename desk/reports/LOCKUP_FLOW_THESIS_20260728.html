The Lockup-Flow Pair: Ethos (LIFE) & York Space (YSS)

REV. 2 — all staged orders WITHDRAWN by our own adversarial court, same day · the court record below is the point
The mechanism. Both companies IPO'd January 30, 2026. Their 180-day lockups expired this week (LIFE July 27, YSS July 28 — both a day earlier than the screen's estimate; the courts read the actual documents). In each case the newly sellable shares are roughly 3.7–5× the tradable float (rev. 2: restoring our own earlier caveat — LIFE's early-release tranche already vented in May at $22–24, and GV and the CFO had already sold). When that supply actually prints, it is price-insensitive — the sellers are venture funds and employees, not value judges — and it can briefly push the price well below any defensible value. The trade is buying that overshoot, small, at pre-committed levels. If no overshoot prints by Friday's close, the orders come down and the desk grades its own prediction either way (we pre-registered only 35–44% odds that a buyable flush materializes at all).

Epilogue: the court killed our own trade (rev. 2, same day)

We ran the same adversarial court on this document that we run on outside pitches. It scored the YSS staging 6/10 and the LIFE staging 7/10 against us, and every order above was withdrawn before submission. The confirmed findings, kept here because the process is the product:

1. We contradicted our own pre-registration. On July 13 we wrote "buy the CONFIRMED stabilizing flush — do NOT pre-position." The resting orders above were pre-positioning by our own definition.
2. The LIFE window was pointed at the wrong four days. Ethos reports earnings August 3; its likely sellers (board-linked venture funds) are blackout-locked until roughly August 5. A flush structurally could not print inside our window — an order filling this week could only fill on bad news, the exact scenario our own plan says to avoid.
3. Our kill-conditions were unenforceable. Nothing watched for the SEC filings (secondaries, insider sales) that our conditions referenced. We built the watcher the same day; its first pass caught Google Ventures selling ~$1.65M of LIFE on the first unlocked day — the supply mechanism, confirmed live.
4. A price anchor was another company's history. The "$9.45 52-week low" belongs to the previous owner of the recycled ticker.
5. The blue court created new doctrine. A name with a customer-corroborated integrity question (YSS) may never be owned as ordinary fairly-paid risk: fairness must be re-derived from externally verified data only, a fraud branch of at least 20% must be priced, and the maximum entry is 15% BELOW that branch-weighted value — which puts YSS's real buy level near $10.50–11, under our withdrawn ladder entirely.

Where this leaves the trades: LIFE re-arms after its August 3 report is read (the filing that resolves the receivable question decides the band); YSS is buyable only near $10.50–11 after a verified-only valuation pass, manually, small. Nothing may rest blind. If neither setup returns, the desk grades its pre-registered predictions and moves on.

Exits. These are flow trades, not investments: YSS targets the $19–20 reclaim; LIFE exits into the recovery toward fair value or on any kill fact; both positions are reviewed at the ~August earnings reports regardless. If the window closes with no fills, the desk logs the miss and grades whether the discipline was right.
