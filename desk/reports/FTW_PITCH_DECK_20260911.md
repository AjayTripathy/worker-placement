# PITCH — FTW — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

---

## What we believe

FTW is a harvest vehicle: it buys producing oil and gas wells that are already drilled, spends almost nothing drilling new ones, and pays out most of the cash as a dividend. The declared dividend is $1.35/yr, which at $10.61 is a **12.7% yield**, and it has been pre-declared through 1Q27.

The stock fell from **$12.40** (the company's own June 9 investor deck mark, 31.1M shares, $828M enterprise value) to **$10.61** — down ~14% in three months — while the business got *better* on the surface: a quarter with $54.0M revenue and $33.2M adjusted EBITDA, a refinancing that cut the securitized debt coupon from 8.22% to 6.38%, and an acquisition that added production.

We believe the main reason for that fall is **share supply, not deterioration**. Inside thirty days, roughly **5.38M Class A shares — about 19% of the Class A count — became freely sellable**: 1,962,240 shares registered for resale (S-1 7/30 → effective 8/12 → three 424B3s), plus **3,422,260 shares the private-equity sponsor handed directly to its own fund investors on 2026-09-09** and stopped owning ([Form 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002083125/000121390026098800/ownership.xml), same-day 13G/A). Hundreds of uncoordinated fund investors selling a block into a two-analyst stock looks exactly like a fundamental repricing and isn't one.

We also believe the bull case is **thin, not fake**. Best available cash math: capex was $0.6M for the quarter and free cash flow $15.7M (~$0.50/share), i.e. ~$62.8M/yr against ~$39.6M of common dividends and roughly $20M of preferred dividends ahead of us — about **1.05x covered, with no cushion**. That is a real dividend with zero margin for error, not a fabricated one.

## What the market believes

The market believes it is looking at a broken de-SPAC yielding 12.7% because the yield isn't real. Price sits **+9.3% off the 52-week low ($9.71)** and **−37.7% from the 52-week high ($17.04)**. The two analysts who cover it carry a **$16.50 target and "Strong Buy"** (stockanalysis.com/stocks/ftw) — a 55% gap to spot that nobody is arbitraging, which tells you how few real holders there are.

The market also (correctly) suspects the funding: the new $350M securitization uses an **"ARD structure implemented to lower annual amortization in the first 5 years,"** described in the company's own materials as increasing cash available for dividends ([Ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2083125/000121390026089284/ea030206301ex99-1.htm)). A yield paid out of deferred principal deserves a discount. The open question is whether the discount is now too big.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The supply event is documented and datable.** Both the resale registration (effective 8/12) and the sponsor's in-kind distribution (9/9, zero shares owned after, no longer a Section 16 insider) are in primary filings. Most people reading the chart are reading it as news.
2. **Two-analyst coverage.** There is no efficient-price machinery here. Attributing the drop to "the tape figured it out" is the classic orphan-stock error.
3. **A mandated hedge floor.** The securitization requires hedging **≥85% of projected production**; falling out of compliance triggers accelerated amortization (424B3, [link](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002083125/000121390026067494/ea0294168-424b3_presidio.htm)). So the "unhedged EBITDA" figure ($26.3M vs $33.2M) is a stress test, not the run rate — cuts against the bear framing *and* against the original bull brief's chosen metric.
4. **Production was flat, not falling**: 22,755 Boe/d vs 22,800 in the prior successor period, before the Canyon Creek addition.
5. **A cheap anchor with a date attached.** The one number that decides this — cash flow after debt principal — arrives with the next 10-Q, ~8 weeks out. We can wait and still own the re-rating.

## Why it might be priced in

1. **Coverage really is ~1.05x**, and 1.05x on a depleting asset base is not an investable cushion. The market may simply be pricing a dividend cut at fair odds.
2. **Growth is bought, not earned.** Canyon Creek (+3.5 MBoe/d, Arkoma) was funded with a **$55M warehouse draw plus 1,962,240 new shares** — the second deal in six months. Every deal adds dividend obligation and interest to hold volumes flat. That treadmill is visible to anyone who reads the press releases.
3. **The preferred is ahead of us and bigger than advertised.** $125M Series A plus 27,173 Series B converting into 2,717,300 Class A (+9.3% dilution to common). Backing out the reported $0.34 EPS implies preferred dividends of roughly **$20M/yr, above the $15.2M both of our benches had been using**.
4. **Depletion is the deferred bill.** ~$27M/quarter of DD&A against ~zero reinvestment means the reserve base is being consumed. "Minimal capex" is partly efficiency and partly liquidation; we cannot yet say which mix, because **nobody has pulled the disclosed PDP base decline rate**.
5. **The supply may not be finished.** LP-distributed blocks bleed for weeks-to-months, and this company has an active shelf habit (four 424B3s since June).

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative #1 — The dividend was the thesis, and the dividend gets cut (monitorable).**
Our own expected-value table below says the *price-only* return is +3.3% and the *total* return is +14.6%. Almost the entire edge is the coupon. If the board trims to ~$0.95 and the market re-prices at an 11–12% yield, the stock is ~$8.25; a hard cut takes it toward $5.75. **Tripwires exist:** the quarterly 8-K dividend declaration, any DSCR disclosure, an early-amortization notice, a new ATM or S-1.

**Failure narrative #2 — The float shock isn't over (monitorable).**
Another sponsor entity, LP tranche, or fresh resale registration lands on top of the 5.38M already freed, and $9.71 goes. **Tripwires:** Form 4 J-code dispositions, 13G/13D amendments, new S-1/424B3, volume-on-decline.

**Failure narrative #3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: the mechanics we can't see.**
The securitization indenture itself is not filed — only a prospectus summary is public. Cash-trap mechanics, hedge-counterparty terms, collateral release conditions, a possible Up-C **Tax Receivable Agreement** attached to the 1,676,830 Class B shares, and the purchase-accounting basis under Successor/Predecessor presentation all sit outside anything we can watch on a tape or a calendar. FTW is an **Emerging Growth Company (checked ☒ on its own 8-K cover)**, so it runs a reduced-disclosure regime with no auditor attestation on internal controls. If value leaks through a private contract or a restated basis, we learn about it the day it's announced, at the announced price.
**Unknown-unknown class named: off-tape contract mechanics under reduced-disclosure (EGC) — private-indenture and related-party economics.** There is no sensor for this class. The only defense is size.

**Reflexive / structural leg (our own footprint, predicted flow, doctrine collisions).**
- **Our size vs ADV:** the pack delivered **no volume data at all** — a real gap. Estimated ADV for a ~$310M-cap NYSE de-SPAC with two analysts: **150k–400k shares/day (~$1.6–4.2M)**, from comparable orphan small-cap E&P names. A ≤0.5% sleeve is ~0.1–1.0 days of that estimate, so we are not the marginal price, but we are also not liquid on the way out — a 1% sleeve would be, and that is why the cap is 0.5%.
- **Predicted flow:** we would be buying the *same side* of an ongoing distribution. LP-distributed blocks historically bleed 40–70% of the block over 6–10 weeks; center of that window is **mid-October to mid-November** — i.e. it overlaps the print. Buying before the bleed finishes means paying for our own patience twice.
- **Crowding:** a 12.7% headline yield recruits yield-only buyers who all exit on the same 8-K. That is a reflexive downside, not a diversifying one: the cut and the crowd unwind together.
- **Doctrine collision:** our "buy a documented supply dislocation" doctrine and our "never buy a financing-funded dividend" doctrine both fire here, in opposite directions. House rule when two doctrines fire on the same name: **no entry, gate it.** That is the ruling.
- **Venue collision:** every load-bearing number in this deck is **furnished, non-GAAP, or from a transcript** — the pack's XBRL block was delivered **empty**, and the pack's own primary document (the 8/17 8-K) is an investor-presentation furnishing that explicitly disclaims "filed" status. We are underwriting a cash-flow claim we have never seen in an audited cash-flow statement.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a 12.7% yield whose coverage turned out to be an artifact of five years of deferred debt principal, and the dividend was cut before the float expansion finished digesting.**
