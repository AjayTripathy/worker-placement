## What we believe

Descartes runs a good business we would like to own at the right price, and today's price is *near* but not *at* that level.

The specifics:

1. **The business is not breaking.** Last printed quarter (Q1 FY27, filed as a 6-K on 2026-06-03): revenue $193.6m, up 15%; net income $48.5m, up 34%; gross margin 78%; cash $377.0m; effectively debt-free (total debt ~$8.1m, mostly leases); a $350m credit line untouched; ~$21m of stock bought back in the quarter. Primary source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001050140/000092963826002122/exhibit99-2.htm
2. **The underlying growth rate went up, not down.** Growth excluding acquisitions and currency moved from about 8% in the prior quarter to "just over 9%." Deferred revenue (money customers have already committed) rose 14% year over year to $112m, roughly in line with revenue growth. There is no sign of a demand air pocket in the public numbers.
3. **The stock has genuinely fallen a lot.** At the desk's own tape of $73.49 it is about 32% below its 52-week closing high of $108.09 and about 17% above its 52-week low of $62.56.
4. **But we cannot yet say it is cheap, because nobody on this row ever wrote down a fair value.** At $73.49 the whole company is worth roughly $6.1B after netting out cash — about 7.5x next year's expected revenue and about 28x next year's expected earnings. For ~9% underlying growth that is a fair-to-full price, not a bargain. Both benches agreed the row has no stated fair value and no stated entry logic. That is why the verdict is "starter at best," not "buy."
5. **The thing we hold today is a mistake and should go.** The book is short one $70 put (premium taken in: $234.96) with unlimited-ish downside of $6,765 if the stock went to zero, sitting through an earnings report that the company itself scheduled for after the close on 2026-09-10. Our own scenario table (below) says that put is worth about $2.90 and we sold it for $2.35. We are short something at less than our own estimate of its value, into a known event, for $235. Close it.

## What the market believes

The market believes Descartes is a high-quality compounder whose growth rate has slipped from mid-teens to high-single-digits, and it has repriced the shares roughly a third lower to reflect that — while still paying a premium multiple for the quality.

Concretely, the published expectations for the quarter that prints on 2026-09-10 are about **$199.1m of revenue and $0.57 of adjusted earnings per share**, with about $807.8m of revenue and $2.62 of adjusted EPS for the full fiscal year. The name is widely covered; there is no information vacuum here.

The market also appears to believe the recent buying spree (Extensiv ~$120m on 2026-09-01, Tai ~$100m on 2026-08-24, plus an undisclosed Chile last-mile deal on 2026-07-06) is a signal that organic growth needs help. Three deals in about eight weeks and $220m+ of cash out the door is a fact; the interpretation is contested.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: **thin.** We should say so.

- **A small timing/plumbing edge.** Our broker feed had the stock at $73.49 late on 2026-09-09 while public aggregators were still showing $75.61 (2026-09-08) and $78.94 (2026-09-04). We were seeing a three-session, roughly 7% slide into the print before the secondary sources caught up. That is worth something for one day, not for a thesis.
- **A framing edge on the growth argument.** The gap between 15% total growth and 9% organic growth is *not* all bought growth — the company states the organic figure excludes currency, so part of that gap is FX, and the organic rate accelerated (8% → 9%+). One bench got this backwards. Reading it correctly is a modest edge over the lazy "growth is purchased" take.
- **A disclosure-substitute edge.** Descartes does not publish contracted backlog (RPO), which is normally how you'd catch a slowdown before it prints. Deferred revenue (+14%) is a usable stand-in. One bench declared this uncheckable; it isn't.
- **What is NOT an edge:** we have no proprietary forecast for the quarter, no channel work, no customer checks, no fair value. We cannot state a disagreement with the published $199.1m/$0.57. A trade with no stated disagreement is not a trade.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The event is public and dated.** The company published the 2026-09-10 after-close report date on 2026-08-04. Nobody is early.
- **Both acquisitions are announced with prices attached.** Extensiv at ~US$120m is in the company's own 2026-09-01 press release; Tai at ~$100m cash was announced 2026-08-24. There is no hidden cash story.
- **The de-rating has already happened.** A 32% drawdown from the high on a business still growing 15% *is* the market pricing the slowdown. Buying it at 7.5x sales and 28x forward earnings is buying a consensus-quality asset at a consensus-quality price.
- **The shelf is a non-event.** The 2026-07-15 F-10EF looks alarming next to $220m of deal spend, but it is a routine renewal: the prior shelf (filed 2024-07-15, 25-month term) was expiring, nothing was ever sold under it, and the new one runs 37 months. Primary: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001050140/000110465926083949/tm2618229d1_f10ef.htm
- **The one genuinely non-priced thing is our own position**, and that is an execution problem, not an edge.

## What could go wrong

**The pre-mortem (v1.7). Top three failure narratives.**

**Failure 1 — We get assigned on the put and end up long a falling stock we never authorized (HAS a tripwire).**
The print lands after the close on 2026-09-10. Our $70 put is only 4.7% away. A 6–8% down-move on a soft organic number puts us long 100 shares at $70 (a $7,000 obligation) at the same moment the resting $73.50 buy order (162 shares, ~$11.9k) and the $68.50 order (149 shares, ~$10.2k) are live. Worst case we wake up with roughly $18k–$29k of long exposure inside a sleeve capped at $5–10k, all bought above where the stock now trades.
*Tripwires:* price alert at 70.00 and 68.50; the 6-K filing itself; the open-orders check before the 9/10 close.

**Failure 2 — Organic growth is not 9%, it is 7%, and the acquisitions were the tell (HAS a tripwire).**
Three deals in eight weeks is what a company does when the core is decelerating. If the 2026-09-10 release shows organic services growth below ~8%, or the total looks fine only because Extensiv/Tai/Chile got stapled on, then 28x forward earnings compresses toward 20x and the stock has $10–14 of downside from here. The deferred-revenue check (+14%) argues against this, but deferred revenue also *includes* acquired books.
*Tripwires:* the organic-services-growth line in the 9/10 MD&A; deferred revenue below +10% year over year; any disclosure that acquisition contribution exceeded ~5 points.

**Failure 3 — The deals cost more than the press releases say, and we find out two years late (NO monitorable tripwire).**
The announced prices are $120m and $100m "cash on hand," with the Chile deal undisclosed. What is not in any release: earnouts, holdbacks, retention pools, seller notes, assumed hosting/infrastructure obligations, indemnity caps, or acquired-customer concentration inside Extensiv's 3PL base. Descartes is a foreign private issuer filing 6-Ks; interim disclosure is thin and per-deal economics are aggregated. There is no filing, no feed, no alert, and no price level that tells us this is happening. It surfaces as an unexplained margin sag or a goodwill charge several quarters out, after we have already re-underwritten the name on the announced numbers.
**Unknown-unknown class: PRIVATE CONTRACT TERMS / NON-FILED CONSIDERATION STRUCTURE.** (Sibling class we have been burned by before: acquired-entity revenue quality — the acquired book's churn is invisible until it is blended away.)
*Best available partial mitigation, not a tripwire:* re-read every future MD&A for changes in the acquisition-consideration and contingent-consideration language, and treat announced purchase prices as floors, never totals.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**

- **Our size is irrelevant to the tape; the tape is not the risk.** The resting orders are 162 and 149 shares. Against a name that trades on the order of several hundred thousand shares a day, that is well under one tenth of one percent of a day's volume. We cannot move this stock and we will not be trapped in it. *Estimate flagged — ADV is not in the pack (see unverified #6).*

Our own process, not the market, is about to put on a position the adjudication declined to approve — and it will fill on the way *down* into a scheduled event, which is the worst possible fill mechanism.
- **Doctrine interaction #2:** the sensor pack is dated for a print on 2026-09-02 while the company-confirmed date is 2026-09-10. A stale date on an armed sensor means the "print proximity" guard was off during the exact week it was needed.
- **Doctrine interaction #3:** the short put's own guardrail ("roll only if delta ≤0.10") cannot currently be satisfied. Backing out from the $2.35 premium at a $70 strike, the position sits around 0.32 delta at *any* expiry consistent with that premium. There is no compliant roll; there is only closing it.
- **Flow prediction:** none needed. Nobody is on the other side of us because of us.
