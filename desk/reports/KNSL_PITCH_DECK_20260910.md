## What we believe

Kinsale is a good insurance company at a price that already assumes it stays good. Two separate things are true and we are not allowed to blur them:

1. **The business is still working.** Net earned premium +8.9% to $417.6M, investment income +19.9%, operating earnings per share +15.9%, and a 24.4% operating return on equity for the first half. That is not decay.
2. **The stock is not cheap, and the story we were originally told was arithmetically wrong.** The "11.7x earnings" that started this idea came from annualizing one quarter of *accounting* earnings ($7.72) that included gains on Kinsale's own investment portfolio. On the company's own *operating* earnings ($5.54/qtr), the stock is **16.25x** at $360.11; on the street's 2026 number ($21.17) it is **17.0x**; on book value ($89.34/share) it is **4.03x**.

So our belief is narrow and unglamorous: **right business, wrong price.** The cheapness we thought we found does not exist, and the thing we said would kill the idea — softening prices in excess & surplus insurance — has *already happened and is already in a filing*. Gross written premium fell 5.0% to $527.6M, commercial property premium fell 32.7%, and management said on the record it will not chase volume that damages returns ([Q2-2026 earnings release, Ex-99.1 to the 8-K filed 2026-07-23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001669162/000166916226000039/earningsrelease2q2026.htm)).

The one genuinely new, useful fact: **the company itself bought its own stock at an average of $311.47 last quarter** ($100.0M for 321,055 shares). The best-informed buyer in the world was accumulating 13.5% below where we are being asked to add. We take that as our entry gate rather than inventing our own.

## What the market believes

The market believes almost exactly what the numbers say, which is why there is so little room here. Eighteen analysts cover it, the consensus stance is "hold," and price targets cluster roughly $361–403 — i.e. **around today's price**. Estimates have come down only 1.7% over three months while the stock is down 25.6% from its 52-week high. So the market has not marked down the *earnings*; it has marked down the *multiple* it will pay for a company whose top line has stopped growing. Post-print the shares actually rose — the soft market was not a surprise to anyone but us.

Translated: the market believes Kinsale keeps earning mid-teens growth in per-share operating profit, keeps a mid-20s return on equity, and no longer deserves a 25x growth multiple. Our own forecast sits on top of the street's. When your number equals the street's number, you are not being paid for insight.

## Why we might have edge

Honest answer: **thin, and mostly procedural rather than analytical.**

- **The revealed-price anchor.** Neither bench initially computed the company's own average buyback price. Dividing dollars by shares in the filing gives $311.47. Using the issuer's clearing price as an entry gate — rather than a chart level — is a real, repeatable discipline, and it happens to land near the price the bear case independently called "where the soft cycle is paid for" (~$300).
- **A mechanism both benches only half-saw.** Kinsale is keeping more risk itself as prices soften: gross premium −5.0% but *net* written premium only −1.4%, and the expense ratio rose to 21.7% from 20.7% because it gave up ceding commissions from reinsurers. This flatters the reported numbers today and quietly puts more loss volatility on Kinsale's own balance sheet at the exact moment loss estimates are least conservative. The market reads the expense-ratio rise as sloppiness; it is a deliberate risk choice. Knowing the difference tells us *which* bad quarter to expect.
- **The borrowed-margin clock.** The 75.5% combined ratio benefited from 4.5 points of favorable development on old (hard-market 2022–24) policies — a $19.4M release. Business written into today's falling prices becomes the "prior years" of 2028–30. That reversal is dateable in principle, and almost nobody models it.
- **Position size lets us be patient.** 37 shares (~$13.3K) is a rounding error. We can wait for $311 without cost.

That is the whole edge list. None of it is a reason to own the stock at $360.

## Why it might be priced in

Nearly all of it.

- The soft market is not a forecast; it is a **filed fact** from 2026-07-23. Anyone who read the release knows.
- "Use operating, not GAAP, earnings" is standard practice on every sell-side desk. Our multiple correction gave us the truth, not an edge — it corrected *our* error, not the market's.
- The valuation is a completed de-rating, not a mispricing: 17x forward on a mid-20s ROE compounder with a shrinking top line is a defensible, consensus-looking price.
- The buyback is disclosed publicly, so $311 is not a secret level.
- The interest-income tailwind we leaned on is trivially small: 19 basis points across ~$4.5B of invested assets is about **$8.6M pretax ≈ $0.30/share after tax ≈ 1.4%** of this year's earnings. One point of accident-year combined ratio on $1.67B of earned premium is **~$0.58/share — about twice as big** and moving the wrong way.

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — We buy the $311 gate and the cycle keeps going (monitorable)
Prices keep falling in casualty, not just property. Gross premium goes −5% → −10%; the company writes less, earned premium rolls over 2–3 quarters later, and the multiple compresses toward 12–13x on *falling* estimates. $311 stops being a floor and becomes a waypoint to $260 (≈2.9x book). **Tripwires:** consolidated gross premium still negative on 2026-10-22; ex-commercial-property growth below ~4%; price through $288 (the 52-week low).

### Failure narrative 2 — The borrowed margin reverses (monitorable, but only once a year)
Favorable development goes from +4.5 points to zero to *negative*. Because Kinsale simultaneously raised its retention, an adverse move is no longer partly absorbed by reinsurers — it lands fully on us. A single reserve-strengthening 8-K on a 4x-book stock is a 20–30% event. **Tripwires:** development contribution under 2 points is fine, above 5 points is a *warning* (borrowing more), a negative number is the event; accident-year combined ratio above 80%; any further retention step-up. Note the honest weakness: the real test — the loss development triangles — prints **once a year**, in the 10-K (est. 2027-02-25). We are blind for four quarters at a time.

### Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE
**Unknown-unknown class: latent liability / social-inflation regime shift in long-tail casualty.** Kinsale's book is casualty-dominant and long-tail. The thing that historically destroys specialty insurers is not a hurricane you can see on radar; it is a legal and jury environment that re-prices policies written five years ago — mass-tort theories, litigation funding, expanded coverage rulings, a novel exclusion read against the insurer. There is no dataset, no filing cadence, no connector, and no price level that warns us. We do not know the geographic or class mix of what is in force, because insurers do not disclose geocoded exposure. By the time it is visible it is in the reserves, and reserves only speak annually. **We cannot arm a sensor for this. We accept it as the price of owning any casualty underwriter, and it is the single best reason to own less than we think we should.** A related unarmed cousin: a mega-catastrophe hitting the retained (now larger) property net — the direction is knowable, the magnitude is not.

### The reflexive / structural leg — our own size, flow, and doctrine interactions
- **Our size is irrelevant to the tape and that cuts both ways.** 37 shares against a stock that trades tens of millions of dollars a day means we have zero market impact and zero exit risk — but it also means this position cannot pay for the attention it is consuming. (Honest gap: no average-daily-volume figure was actually pulled by either bench; we call it plausible, not measured.)
- **The dominant predicted flow is not ours, it is the company's.** Repurchases ran 321,055 shares / $100.0M in one quarter — roughly 1.4% of shares outstanding in a quarter, ~5% annualized, with authorization raised by $250M to $337M. That is a real bid *below* the tape. The reflexive trap: it supports the stock near $311 and thereby prevents us from ever getting a genuinely washed-out price, so we either pay up or never own it. If the company slows the buyback (because it is preserving capital for reserves), our floor and our thesis fail *together*.
- **Doctrine interaction.** Two of our rules point in opposite directions here: "buy where the issuer buys" says $311; "do not add into a confirmed negative fundamental trend" says do not buy at all while gross premium is negative. If we let the price gate fire mechanically without the fundamental gate, we will buy exactly the falling knife the buyback is slicing. **The gates are AND, never OR.**
- **Correlation we must not net out.** Our two legs — higher investment yield and premium growth — are not independent. Float grows only because premium grows. A soft market attacks the *base* of the investment income, not just the rate. We are not allowed to offset one against the other.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___** that we treated the company's own $311 buyback price as a floor and bought into a softening casualty cycle whose loss picks had not yet been tested, so the multiple and the reserves de-rated at the same time.
