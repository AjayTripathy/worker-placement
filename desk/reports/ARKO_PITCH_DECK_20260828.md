# PITCH — ARKO — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position, no order. Both benches land on *don't buy*, and they agree the original reason to be interested (a "negative stub" — being paid to take the c-stores) was an arithmetic mistake we made ourselves.

---

## What we believe

ARKO Corp. runs about 1,057 convenience stores and gas stations, mostly in the East, Southeast and Midwest, plus a wholesale fuel business, a fleet-fueling business, and a 73.6% economic / 93.3% voting stake in ARKO Petroleum Corp. ("APC"), which it partially listed in February 2026. ARKO trades at $4.41, roughly half its 52-week high of $8.755 and 21% above its 52-week low of $3.6479.

The idea that got this into court was this: ARKO's whole stock market value is $494.7M (112,185,721 shares × $4.41). Its 35,000,000 APC shares are worth $645.4M at APC's $18.44 price. Subtract, and you appear to be getting 1,057 stores for *minus* $150M — someone paying you to take them.

**We now believe that is false, and we found it false ourselves.** The subtraction leaves out the parent company's debt. Splitting the two 10-Q balance sheets:

| | ARKO consolidated | APC standalone | ARKO parent only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funded debt | $877.4M | $184.7M | ~$692.7M |
| Cash | $245.6M | $14.6M | ~$231.0M |
| **Net debt** | **$631.8M** | **$170.1M** | **~$461.7M** |

Fold that back in and the stores are not free. The retail leg is priced at **+$311M**, roughly **3.1×** its ~$100–105M of annual operating cash earnings — or, once you charge the $1,456.8M of store leases where they actually sit, about **6.0× rent-inclusive enterprise value**. Cheap-ish for a shrinking, lease-heavy operator. Not free, and not obviously mispriced.

What we believe after the court, in one paragraph: **ARKO is a fairly-to-modestly-cheaply priced, slowly shrinking retailer, wrapped around a $645M holding in a subsidiary it cannot easily sell, where the price split between the two halves is set by a contract the parent writes for itself.** That last clause is why we are flat, and it is the single most important sentence in this deck.

Primary source for all balance-sheet figures: [ARKO 10-Q, period ended 2026-06-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1823794/000119312526339096/arko-20260630.htm) (fetched through a proxy after sec.gov returned a 403).

## What the market believes

The sell side believes ARKO is worth $7–$11 per share against a $4.41 tape — 59% to 150% upside, with the *lowest* published target ($7, Raymond James) still 59% above the price. The load-bearing number in every one of those models is the company's own FY26 adjusted-earnings guide of $245–265M.

Short sellers disagree: 5.01M shares short, 8.83% of the tradeable float. Working backward from that percentage, the tradeable float is roughly 56.7M shares of the 112.2M outstanding — insiders and affiliates hold about half the company, and the freely traded stock is worth only about $250M.

So this is a **contested, actively argued stock**, not an overlooked one. That matters: when both a bullish sell side and a large short position are already engaged, a difference of opinion is not by itself an edge.

We reconciled the two views arithmetically, which nobody in the pack had done. Taking the APC stake at full face value and using our corrected parent debt, the street's targets imply:

| Price | Implied rent-inclusive multiple of the retail business |
|---|---|
| $3.65 (52-wk low) | ~5.7× |
| **$4.41 (today)** | **~6.0×** |
| $7.00 (lowest street target) | ~7.0× |
| $11.00 (highest street target) | ~8.5× |

The entire street case is therefore **one to two-and-a-half turns of multiple, plus zero discount on a stake that is 2.8× the size of the market it would have to be sold into.** That is a much smaller and much more fragile claim than "+59% to +150%" sounds. (Multiples derived by us from the benches' figures; not published anywhere.)

## Why we might have edge

Three things we did that we do not think are widely done:

1. **We split the parent and subsidiary balance sheets by hand.** Nobody publishes ARKO-ex-APC. Both benches had to pull two separate 10-Qs, one of them through a proxy after being blocked. The corrected picture — $461.7M of parent net debt — is not in any screen, any data vendor field, or any of the sell-side notes we can see.

2. **We found the transfer-price dial.** ARKO's fuel partnership is the exclusive fuel supplier to substantially all of its own retail sites at a **fixed markup of 5.0 cents per gallon through 2025-12-31, rising to 6.0 cents thereafter**, plus a new fixed fee on fleet sites **starting 2026-02-01** — eleven days before the APC listing closed on 2026-02-13. On our estimate of ARKO's retail gallons, one cent per gallon is on the order of **$15M a year**, or roughly 15% of the retail leg's earnings, moved *out of* the half being valued at 3.1× and *into* the half being marked at $18.44. This is disclosed in the related-party note; it is not, so far as we can tell, in anyone's sum-of-the-parts.

3. **We measured leverage on the actual balance sheet.** Management's "1.7×" and the widely quoted $413M of net debt use only the long-term debt line ($658.5M) minus cash. Add the $16.0M current portion and $202.9M of financing lease obligations disclosed on the same page and true net funded debt is **$631.8M — about 2.5×**, not 1.7×. Management has guided leverage to 3.0–3.5× after a pending private placement; on a consistent basis that is closer to **3.7–4.2×**. (Our derivation.)

The honest limit on all three: these sharpen the *price* of the two halves. None of them produces a gap between price and value large enough to act on, and item 2 argues *against* acting.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The stock already moved.** It is down 49.6% from its high but has retraced 21% off the low. Whatever the August dislocation was, it is half-healed. We are not early to it.
- **8.83% of the float is short.** Someone has done work here and reached a bearish conclusion. We should assume our bear case is partly their bear case.
- **The APC lock-up story is public.** APC listed 2026-02-13; a conventional 180-day lock-up expires around 2026-08-12, and ARKO's decline ran 08-07 to 08-13 into the $3.6479 low. The market appears to have already traded the overhang. *(We never read the actual lock-up clause — see unverified.)*
- **The cheap multiple may simply be correct.** Gallons are down 5.7%, same-store merchandise sales are down 1.7%, and the store count went from 1,079 to 1,057. A shrinking business with $1.46B of lease obligations arguably *should* trade at 6× rather than 7×. There is no rule that says the discount closes.
- **We produced no independent fair value.** Both benches noted this. If we simply adopt the company's own guide, our "edge" is the company's forecast, which is also everyone else's.

## What could go wrong

*(This is a pre-mortem for a position we do not hold. It is written as though we had bought a starter and lost money.)*

**Failure 1 — The mark was never real, and it came down to meet the stub.**
The $645.4M is 35.0M shares valued at APC's $18.44 quote, against a total tradeable APC float of 12,570,223 shares worth about $232M. Our block is **278% of the entire float**, in a share class ARKO's own 10-Q says "may only be owned by the Company and its affiliates." And $18.44 versus the $18.00 IPO price is +2.4% in six and a half months — the "market price" is still, functionally, the price at which the company sold the stock to itself. Any real monetization is a marketed secondary at a discount, and the discount destroys the number that made the trade. **Sensitivity: every 10% haircut to that mark is $64.5M, or $0.575 per ARKO share — 13% of today's price. A 25% control-block discount is $1.44 per share, a third of the stock.** *Monitorable:* partly — APC's quote is public and we can watch for an offering document.

**Failure 2 — The controller turns the dial again, and we find out a quarter later. (NO TRIPWIRE.)**
The split between "the stub" and "the marked stake" is set by an intercompany supply contract, at a fixed cents-per-gallon markup, between two entities where one owns 94% of the other's votes. It was raised 5.0 → 6.0 cents effective the start of this year and a new fleet fee was added the month of the listing. It can be raised again, or extended to new site categories, by a party with no external counterparty, no market test, no obligation to pre-announce, and no 8-K trigger. There is no price series, no filing type, and no data feed that fires when this happens. We would learn about it in a related-party footnote, up to four months in arrears, after the earnings had already moved.

**Unknown-unknown class named: *controller-set private prices*.** The economics of the position are governed by a contract that has no external anchor, no advance disclosure, and no observable proxy — the class of risks where the quantity you are underwriting is a *decision* rather than a *measurement*. This class defeats tripwires by construction, because a tripwire needs something published to trip on. It is the reason a sum-of-the-parts across two entities under common control is not a valuation method; it is a rearrangement of one.

**A second, smaller unknown-unknown of the same family: *no between-print observability of the operating asset*.** Gallons, traffic, and same-store sales are published only quarterly. Both benches independently identified the one available proxy — the App Store review series for ARKO's *fas REWARDS* loyalty app — and **neither pulled it**. So between 2026-08-07 and 2026-11-04 we are blind to the operating business by roughly 90 days, holding a stock whose whole cheapness case is a multiple on that business.

**Failure 3 — Cheap gets cheaper because the balance sheet gets worse.**
First-half free cash flow was **negative $2.7M** (operating cash $60.9M less capital spending $63.6M), and negative $9.4M after the dividend. The $219.4M of debt repaid this year was funded by the $210.4M of APC listing proceeds and by drawing cash from $305.0M to $245.6M — not by operations. First-half net income was $3.8M, three cents a share, and part of that belongs to APC's new minority holders (minority interest went from $0 at year-end to $68.7M). Meanwhile management is guiding leverage *up*, to 3.0–3.5× (our consistent basis: 3.7–4.2×), paying $205M cash plus $30M of stock — 7.8× — for $30M of earnings, while its own equity trades at roughly 4.4×. Buying assets above the multiple of your own stock, funded with debt, in a shrinking business, is how a value stock becomes a leveraged value trap. *Monitorable:* yes, at prints and on the placement 8-K.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- *Size versus liquidity:* the tradeable float is about 56.7M shares, ~$250M. A 0.5–1.0% starter on our book is small enough in absolute dollars that fill is not the constraint. **But ARKO's average daily volume is not in our evidence pack and we did not pull it** — so days-to-cover on the 5.01M short position, and therefore how gappy an exit is, is genuinely unknown. That is a required pre-entry check, not a detail.
- *We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict.* Our own Failure-1 analysis says the likely path to realizing the APC stake is a discounted secondary. Placing a bid now puts us directly in front of the supply event our own thesis forecasts. Buying ahead of flow you predict is the specific error we have a standing rule against.
- *Crowded short as a two-sided instrument:* 8.83% short is not a reason to reject — it is a reason to size small and expect violent two-way moves. If we are right, we get help. If we are wrong, we are wrong alongside a crowd exiting the same door.
- *Gearing:* enterprise value including leases is about $1,768M against $494.7M of equity. A **1% move in enterprise value is 3.6% of the share price.** This is a levered stub. Whatever we would size it at on conviction, gearing argues for less.
- *Doctrine interaction, stated as a risk to ourselves:* our standing default is that a clean court with no named kill earns a minimum starter. This court **does** have named kills, so FLAT is consistent. But the strongest kill (the transfer-price dial) is a *quality-of-evidence* objection, not a *price* objection. If we later talk ourselves into "6.0× is cheap enough," we will have reversed on identical facts with no new information — which is exactly how a discipline decays. Any future reopen must cite a **document we have not yet read**, not a changed mood.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the $645M APC stake — 2.8× its own float, still quoted at its own IPO price, in a share class ARKO may not be able to transfer — proves to be worth materially less than its mark, while the retail half we were "getting cheap" turns out to be cheap only because a related-party fuel markup the parent sets for itself had already been raised to move earnings out of it.**

*(The mirror risk, since we are flat rather than long: if the charter turns out to permit a tax-free split-off to ARKO shareholders, the mark becomes claimable without any float constraint and we will have skipped a 59–150% move over a document we could have read.)*

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry. No order. No bid.** Both benches reached no-position, with a named reason rather than a shrug: the split between the two halves of this company is set by a contract the parent writes for itself, so neither "+$311M" nor "−$150M" is an externally anchored fact; and the $645M stake rests on a charter clause **neither bench read**.

Because the block is a document question rather than a price question, the reopen conditions are documents first and prices second.

**Reopen triggers — documents (any ONE reopens the file; the first two would reopen it as a buy candidate):**

1. **Charter or registration-rights language showing Class B converts to Class A on transfer**, *plus* either a demand right exercised or a dated Form 10 / tax-free split-off filing. A distribution straight to ARKO shareholders bypasses the float problem entirely and makes the $645M claimable.
2. **An APC secondary that clears near $18.00 on ≥$150M** — direct proof the float can absorb size without breaking the mark.
3. **Segment disclosure of retail earnings at arm's-length fuel pricing** (or the removal/re-basing of the fixed cents-per-gallon markup). This is the only thing that makes a sum-of-the-parts meaningful here.
4. **Second-half operating cash flow ≥$120M with capex held ≤$60M** — refutes the negative-free-cash-flow finding.
5. **The private-placement 8-K**, disclosing which entity is the borrower (ARKO or APC) and whose stock the $30M consideration is. If it is APC stock, the acquisition dilutes the very stake being marked, and the case gets worse, not better.

**Reopen triggers — price (informational; a price alone does not reopen the file):**

- **$3.6479** — the 52-week low, set on the lock-up-expiry week. A clean break puts the retail leg at ~5.7× rent-inclusive value and would mean the market is pricing further deterioration, which is a reason to re-read, not to buy.
- **$1.64** — the price at which the original claim would actually be true: below this, the market cap really is less than the APC stake net of parent debt. Stated for calibration only, to show how far the original thesis was from the arithmetic.
- **APC below $15.00** — a 19% de-rate in the subsidiary is $1.07 per ARKO share of net asset value, or 24% of ARKO's price. Because ARKO's equity is ~3.6× geared to enterprise value, the subsidiary's quote is the fastest-moving input in the whole structure. This is the level at which the Failure-1 narrative is confirming itself.

**Sizing, if and only if a document trigger fires:** starter of **0.5%** of book, maximum **1.0%** even on a clean reopen — justified by the ~3.6× gearing to enterprise value, half the shares held by affiliates, and 8.83% of the float short. **Mandatory pre-entry check: average daily volume and days-to-cover, neither of which is in our evidence pack.** No add before a second, independent document trigger. Any position is in a taxable account, so a gain taken inside twelve months is taxed at the top ordinary rate — a starter here is a multi-quarter document bet, not a trade.

**Catalyst dates:**

- **2026-11-04** — Q3 results. **This date is derived from a data vendor and is NOT confirmed by any company announcement.** It is ~47 trading days out; nothing about this deck is print-driven, and no print-day reconstruction is owed. What it resolves: whether the FY guide of $245–265M holds when the raised fuel-margin guide (45.5–47.5 cents) sits *below* Q2's realized 48.7 cents; and whether Q2's −6.4% was noise or the trend.
- **2027-02-13** — twelve months from the APC listing. Our own analysis says the "still anchored to the IPO print" argument weakens past roughly a year; this is the date after which APC's quote starts to be evidence rather than an echo. *(Our derivation, not an announced event.)*
- **Undated** — closing of the private placement. A dated event to watch, not a gate; we do not know when it lands.

## What remains unverified

1. **The charter.** Whether Class B converts to Class A on transfer — the hinge of the entire $645M question. Not in the 10-Q, not surfaced in the S-1/A. Neither bench read it. Red graded it "confirmed prohibited" anyway; that grade was wrong.
2. **The lock-up clause.** The ~2026-08-12 expiry is inferred from the 180-day market convention, not read. The date coincides suspiciously well with the low, which makes it tempting and therefore worth verifying rather than assuming.
3. **Insider transaction directions.** The June–July Form 4 / 144 cluster. Codes unread. Unknown, not benign.
4. **APC's own financing leases.** Blue assigned all $202.9M of finance leases to the parent. The reasoning (they are ARKO store sale-leasebacks) is sound but unproven; if some sit at APC, the corrected retail enterprise value of $311M is too high.
5. **The private placement's issuer and consideration.** Whether ARKO or APC borrows, and whose stock the $30M is. If APC's, the deal dilutes the marked stake.
6. **Retail gallons at contract granularity.** Our "$15M per cent per gallon" is derived from a gallons estimate, not from disclosure. The magnitude of the transfer-price finding — the court's strongest kill — is therefore order-of-magnitude, not precise.
7. **Any independent fair value.** Both benches flagged this and neither fixed it. Every number in this deck traces to the company's own guide or its own filings. We have corrected the market's arithmetic; we have not yet formed our own view of what the retail business earns.
8. **Average daily volume and days-to-cover.** Not pulled. Required before any order.
9. **APC's carve-out financials.** APC's pre-listing statements are common-control carve-out statements, not separately audited standalone results for the full comparison period — so *every* parent-versus-subsidiary split in this deck, including ours, is a derivation from two balance sheets rather than a disclosure. State it that way whenever these numbers are quoted onward.
