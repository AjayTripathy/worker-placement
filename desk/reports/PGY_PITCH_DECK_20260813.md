# PITCH — PGY — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: No entry at $21.46. Pagaya is a real, cash-generating business trading at a fair price after doubling off its low — the court killed the *entry*, not the company. Re-engage on a pullback to $17.00–$18.50 or on a clean Q3 print (see bands).**

## What we believe

Pagaya Technologies runs an AI credit network: banks and lenders send it loan applications, Pagaya's models approve a slice the lenders would have declined, and the loans are funded mostly by outside investors through securitizations. Pagaya earns fees on the volume. The bull framing that brought this name to court was: fees are real cash (operating cash flow of $117.9M in the first half of 2026 against $70.0M of net income), the business is "capital-light," and at roughly 10.6x forward earnings the stock is cheap for a company growing volume 33%.

After both benches finished, here is what we actually believe: **the fees are real cash and the company is self-funding — cash on hand grew $16.1M in the half to $249.3M — but "capital-light" is false.** Regulation and market practice force Pagaya to buy a retained piece of its own securitizations; those purchases ran about $496M gross in the half (roughly $131M net of paydowns and sales, about 7% of loan volume), partly funded with $253M of secured borrowing. So this is a *hybrid*: a fee network bolted to a small, growing lender balance sheet. That makes ~10.6x headline earnings (closer to 12x after dilution) a **fair** price, not a cheap one. Fair-priced quality is ownable on our framework — but not 4% below a 26-week high, up ~106% off the 52-week low, with no confirmed catalyst for roughly three months.

## What the market believes

The stock is at $21.46, down 52% from its 52-week high of $44.99 but up ~106% from the low of $10.40, and sitting just below its 26-week high of $22.24. The market has already re-embraced the turnaround: the February 9, 2026 guidance cut (a −24% one-day crash) has been fully forgiven, the post-earnings gap from the July 30 print has held, and the current price implies the market broadly accepts the $155–180M net-income guide and the growth story. In short: the market believes what the blue bench believes, and it got there first.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the whole cash-flow statement, including the part each side wanted to stop at.** We now know precisely where the bear case is real (mandatory risk-retention purchases consume the operating cash at the company's chosen growth rate; the book is partly debt-funded) and where it is rhetoric ("negative free cash flow" — false; cash grew).
- **We have specific, checkable deterioration markers the market may be ignoring:** retained-loan losses grew 85% year over year ($43.3M → $80.3M in the half) while volume grew 33%, and the fee margin on volume (fee revenue less production costs) compressed from 4.8% to 4.2%. If those two lines keep bending the wrong way, the fair multiple contracts.
- **Funding-market proof is current and verifiable:** an $800M consumer-loan securitization launched January 2026 ([company press release](https://investor.pagaya.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pagaya-launches-2026-capital-markets-activity-800m-consumer-loan)) and $3.7B raised across six securitizations in Q2. We know exactly what evidence would falsify the funding-freeze bear case, and it is currently falsified.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: mostly, it is. The turnaround, the cash generation, and the open funding window are all visible in public filings and the tape reflects them — the stock doubled. Our "edge" items are watch-metrics, not variant perception: the loss growth and margin compression are disclosed in the company's own materials, and the sell side can read them too. The red bench's strongest surviving point is exactly this — at a fair multiple, with ~15–16%/yr share-count growth eating per-share economics, and the President selling a small block at $22.00 on the post-earnings pop, there is no mispricing to harvest *at this price*. Both benches landed at 7/10 conviction on their own side, which is the signature of a fairly priced stock, not a dislocated one.
