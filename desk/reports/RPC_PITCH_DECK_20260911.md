## What we believe

Ridgepost Capital (RPC, CIK 1841968) is a multi-affiliate asset manager that earns fees on committed capital. The original idea was: the stock is down a third from its high, but the fees can't fall the way the price implies, because they're charged on money LPs promised, not on the market value of the assets. That "damage-absent" idea is **dead** — and it died on the company's own filings, not on either bench's opinion.

What we actually believe now, after both courts:

1. **The mark-insensitivity mechanism is real and primary-sourced.** The 10-Q MD&A says fees are charged on committed capital and, in certain cases, net invested capital, and that committed-capital fees are not affected by market appreciation or depreciation ([10-Q MD&A text family](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841968/000095017024123725/px-20240930.htm)). Blue overturned Red's claim that this line was marketing-only — same venue, not a weaker one.
2. **But mark-insensitivity was never the question.** The issuer publishes the actual damage channel and it isn't marks. In the disclosed FPAUM roll-forward analog, NAV change was **−$117M** over nine months against **−$321M** of scheduled fee-base step-downs and **−$953M** of fee-period expirations on a ~$22.7B base ([px-20230930](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841968/000095017023063085/px-20230930.htm)). That is a ~7.5% annualized gross bleed of the fee base versus ~0.7% from marks — roughly **11x**. Headline FPAUM still grows because new capital is raised over the top of it.
3. **The company is levered, so equity multiples flatter it.** At the dispatched tape of $8.095: 110.23M A+B shares plus 11.19M exchangeable Stellus units = 121.4M, so equity ≈ **$983M**; debt went from $373.2M to **$490.8M** with ~$37M cash, so net debt ≈ **$454M**; enterprise value ≈ **$1.44B**, or **9.9x** a $145.6M fee-related-earnings run-rate (80.9 × 4 × 45%), **12.0x** after the ~$26M/yr of stock comp, net debt/FRE **3.1x** ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841968/000119312526340176/rpc-20260630.htm)). Not "7.2x."
4. **Therefore: this is a price question, not a thesis question.** 9.9x EV/FRE is below where listed alt managers usually trade, so the correction kills the cheapness *label* without proving the business is broken. We want it at Blue's number or better, with the roll-forward in hand.

Belief in one line: *the fee base leaks by contract, not by market, and we will not pay ~10x for a leak we haven't measured.*

## What the market believes

The market believes the de-rate is about fee-rate/mix compression and leverage, and it has already acted on it. The Q2 print was **in line** — $80.9M actual against ~$80.8M consensus (stockanalysis aggregation of the 8/5 release; secondary source) — and the stock fell ~19% anyway, from about $9.89 into the $8s. That is the market re-pricing the *forward* fee base, not reacting to a miss.

The market has also seen: a $139M revolver draw on 2026-06-18, only ~$34M of facility left, ~$37M cash, a $60M contingent earnout, and 11.77M newly issued deal units/shares at an $8.03 basis with exchange and registration rights attached — sellers who are flat-to-underwater at today's price.

Where the market appears **not** to be precise: the actual roll-forward line items. Nobody we can see is publishing the split between NAV drag, scheduled step-downs, and fee-period expirations. The narrative is "fee rate is compressing"; the mechanism is "contracted fee periods are ending." Those have different half-lives and different cures.

## Why we might have edge

- **A disclosed number nobody is quoting.** The step-down / expiry / NAV-change breakout in the Ex 99.2 roll-forward converts a vibe ("compression") into a rate we can trend quarter over quarter. It's in the filings and it's free. Both of our own benches argued proxies while the issuer published the answer — if we did that, others are doing it too.
- **A clean, pre-committed numeric gate.** Blue's gate is falsifiable before we risk money: step-downs + expirations under **2% of FPAUM annualized** and blended rate on **average** FPAUM at or above **100bp** ⇒ starter. Over **4%** ⇒ it's a short candidate, not a long. Most desks don't have a threshold; they have a feeling.
- **A structural seller we can name and time.** The deal cohort's basis is $8.03/unit (the 10-Q booked $94,515k on 11,770,245 units). We know roughly where their supply lives relative to our entry. That's a rare, dateable flow fact.
- **Arithmetic discipline that already paid.** Both benches made price and share-count errors that would have mis-sized us by 9–18% (below). Catching those *is* the edge on a levered small-cap.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The in-line print is the strongest argument against us.** We agree with the street on the one number the thesis turned on. There is no published gap to monetize today.
- **The mechanism is consensus, not novel.** Red tagged leverage and rate/mix as NOVEL; Blue's tag audit overturned that — the market de-rated ~19% on exactly those two mechanisms right after the print. Only the deal-unit overhang and the tangible-book/intangible tail are arguably not fully discounted, and even the overhang is visible in public filings.
- **9.9x may simply be the right multiple** for a manager carrying 3.1x net-debt/FRE, $885.9M of goodwill and intangibles against $517.3M of total equity (tangible book ≈ **−$369M**), $34M of undrawn capacity, and a fee base that contractually shrinks.
- **The roll-forward is public.** If it's readable by us on 2026-11-05, it's readable by everyone else the same morning. Our edge is in having pre-committed thresholds, not in exclusive data.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The leak is bigger than Blue's gate and the gate lets us in anyway.** Our only analog says the drag runs ~7.5% of FPAUM annualized gross (−$1,274M of step-downs + expirations on $22.7B over nine months). Blue's "clean" threshold is under 2%. If Q3 prints a flattering quarter because a large raise lands in the same period, we buy a starter into a base that's structurally shrinking, and the next two prints re-de-rate us. **Tripwire: armed and monitorable** — the Ex 99.2 roll-forward each quarter; two consecutive quarters above 3% annualized kills the long.

**Failure narrative 2 — The balance sheet, not the fee base, does the damage.** $34M availability plus $37M cash against a $60M earnout (2027/2029, marked at $6.6M using an 11.8–12% discount rate), ~$19M/yr of dividend, and a −$369M tangible book. An intangible impairment on the very contracts the fees sit on, a covenant renegotiation, or a dividend cut all re-rate the equity faster than any FRE math. **Tripwire: partial and lagging** — 8-K Item 2.06 (impairment), Item 1.01/2.04 (facility amendment), quarterly availability disclosure. All of these print *after* the decision was made in a private lender conversation.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE. Private LP and affiliate-consent risk.** A large LP declines to re-up in a successor fund; a key person leaves an affiliate and triggers a key-man or removal clause; an affiliate's economics get renegotiated. These are decided in private closings and Form ADV-adjacent documents we cannot see, they are invisible to price gates and event gates alike, and they surface only as an FPAUM line item one to two quarters later — by which point the fee base is already gone. There is **no filing, no connector, and no tape signature** that fires in advance. **Unknown-unknown class: "private counterparty-consent risk at unconsolidated affiliates"** — outcomes determined by named third parties under NDA, with no public pre-echo. Sub-class we also cannot instrument: **LP concentration.** Red flagged correctly that this would require Form ADV or fund LP schedules, not any connector we hold; we do not know whether the top five LPs are 15% or 50% of FPAUM.

**A second unknown-unknown class we created ourselves: desk data integrity.** Blue priced $8.80, Red priced $7.98, and the dispatched pack said $8.095. Three prices, one name, one day. That is a failure class ("stale/versioned tape propagating into derived share counts and multiples") with no tripwire because the error looks like data.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our size vs. ADV:* we do not have an ADV field in the pack (see unverified #3). Estimated from comps — small-cap alt managers turn 0.3–0.8% of float daily; on a ~$871M screen cap with roughly 60–70M of real float, that's **~400–900k shares, or ~$3–7M/day**. A starter of 0.5% of book is tolerable; the problem is the exit, not the entry. In the bad branch we would be selling into the same $8.03-basis cohort, and a 3–4 day liquidation is self-punishing. **Rule: cap any position at ≤0.5x one day's estimated ADV until ADV is measured.**
- *Predicted flow:* 11.77M units/shares (~9.7% of the diluted count) with a basis of $8.03 and exchange/registration rights pending. That is a supply shelf sitting *exactly* at our current price. Any re-rate through $8.03–$9.00 is likely met with cohort selling, which caps the good branch and makes the reopen bands *below* $8.03 the only ones with a clean tape. Two new 13Gs (2026-07-15, 2026-07-29) plus a 13G/A (2026-08-06) also say the holder base was churning right into the drawdown.
- *Doctrine interactions:* `lockup_expiration_calendar` **FIRED on both benches** (Red first; Blue reduced Red's "$125M → $94.5M markdown" framing to an artifact because the $125M was the *cash* leg, but the $8.03 at-water basis and the overhang stand). Red's proposed `equity_consideration_markdown_overhang` detector rests on that overturned framing and should **not** be promoted. Blue's `fee_base_expiry_roll_forward` detector is the one worth keeping; note it is *self-referential* — if we build sensors that fire on the same disclosure everyone reads at 6am, our "edge" degrades to speed. Also: Red consulted a detector atlas that was not the dispatched set (a validator-grade procedural defect), so coverage claims on both sides are weaker than they look.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the contracted fee base kept expiring and stepping down at 4%+ of FPAUM a year while $454M of net debt held the multiple down and the 11.77M at-water deal units sold every bounce — and we found out one print too late because the decisive facts were made in private LP rooms with no tripwire.
