# PITCH — FWRG — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

First Watch Restaurant Group (Nasdaq: FWRG), $12.05, no position held, no orders working. Both benches finished; neither bench found a reason to walk away entirely, and neither bench's headline number survived contact with the other. The two benches disagree on one thing only — whether we buy anything before the November 12 investor day — and that disagreement is what the adjudicator needs to settle.

---

## What we believe

First Watch runs 665 breakfast-and-lunch restaurants (586 of them company-owned, the rest franchised). It is growing revenue about 16% a year, adding roughly 55 restaurants a year, and it earns a healthy 18.8% profit margin at the restaurant level, which is expanding rather than shrinking. Its private-equity sponsor (Advent) has finished selling — the last ownership filing went from 14,689,784 shares to zero — so the multi-year supply of stock hitting the market from that seller is genuinely over.

The stock has fallen 37% from its 52-week high of $19.08. The reason it screens as expensive is that the company is spending heavily to build new restaurants, and the accounting depreciation from all those new buildings crushes reported earnings. Reported earnings put the stock at roughly 43x — and even that number is fake, because 85% of the last twelve months' reported profit came from a single one-time, non-cash tax item. Strip that out and reported earnings are near zero.

The underlying belief: the business generates real cash — about $128M a year of operating cash flow — and a meaningful chunk of the spending that makes it look unprofitable is *growth* spending the company could stop tomorrow. What you'd actually earn if you stopped growing is somewhere in the neighborhood of $39M–$59M a year against a ~$750M market value, or roughly 13x–19x. That is not a bargain. It is a fair-to-slightly-cheap price for a mid-teens grower whose reported numbers will look bad for two more years for mechanical reasons.

**That last paragraph is a demotion from where this idea started.** The original pitch claimed you were paying about 10x. You are not.

## What the market believes

The market is not confused about this company, and it is not ignoring it. Eleven analysts cover it, the consensus rating is Strong Buy, and the average price target is about $19.30 — 60% above today's price. **The street already publishes our thesis.** They already look through the depreciation; they already model the cash inflection.

And the stock fell 37% anyway.

So what the market actually believes is narrower than "this is a bad company." Three things are visible in the price:

1. **The cash inflection is a 2027 story with no trigger before then.** Depreciation is growing 24.6% a year while profits grow 13.5%, so reported earnings get *worse* through 2027. Anyone screening on reported profitability keeps rejecting this stock for two more years.
2. **The balance sheet is doing work it shouldn't have to.** Management said out loud on the earnings call that "maintaining these long-term unit growth targets has required us to access our credit facility." The revolver is 56% drawn ($70.0M of $125.0M, $52.5M left, costing 7.24%), against $145–150M of annual capital spending. Then they slowed the growth plan.
3. **11.75% of the shares are sold short** (7.14M shares), and institutional ownership at 106.9% means the stock is heavily lent out. A real contingent of professionals is positioned against it.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: **thin, and in the same direction as everyone else.** That is the weakest shape an edge can have. Here is what is left after both benches:

- **The reported-earnings distortion is worse than the screens capture.** Not just the depreciation drag — the last twelve months of reported profit is 85% a one-time $10.7M non-cash tax benefit from recognizing a tip-credit deferred tax asset. On a clean basis reported profit is about $7.1M. Any mechanical value or quality screen is rejecting this name on a number that has almost nothing to do with the business. That's a durable reason a fair-priced business stays fair-priced instead of re-rating.
- **The forced-seller overhang is genuinely, verifiably gone.** We checked this rather than assumed it. The sponsor distribution completed in 2025; there is no follow-on offering in the filing record and no lockup left to expire. A lot of "cheap post-IPO grower" stories are really just "sponsor still selling" stories. This one isn't, anymore.
- **A specific, dated, mispriced event.** Management's own August 4 investor deck lays out a long-term growth algorithm of 11–14% profit growth off this year's $134.5M guide — that's $149–153M for 2027. The street models $161.5M. The gap requires 70–90 basis points of margin expansion where management's own arithmetic implies 10–20. On November 12 in Boston, management presents its new long-term strategic plan. One of those two numbers is going to move. We know which side each of them is on, and we know the date.
- **Cost inputs just inverted.** Commodity costs are running −1.6%. An August-set long-term algorithm plausibly doesn't embed that. This is the single named mechanism under which the street's higher number turns out to be right.

## Why it might be priced in

This deserves more space than the edge section, because it is stronger.

- **Consensus is already long our thesis, louder than we are.** Strong Buy, $19.30 target, versus our own fair value of roughly $14.40 (8x management's own 2027 profit number). We are not disagreeing with the market — we are agreeing with the market's direction and disagreeing about *how much*. That is a rounding-error edge.
- **The stock has already bounced 21% off its low of $9.97.** Whatever maximum-pessimism entry existed, it was three dollars ago. This is not a falling knife we are catching; it is a partial recovery we are joining.
- **On the two conventional cheapness measures, the stock is priced consistently with a slowing grower, not a distressed one.** Enterprise value excluding leases is $1,020M against $134.5M of profit = 7.58x. Including the $780.9M of leases against lease-inclusive profit = 7.89x. Both conventions agree. The hostile bench tried to break the cheapness claim and could not — but 7.6x is a normal multiple for a company that just cut its growth plan for balance-sheet reasons, not an anomaly.
- **The November 12 catalyst more likely cuts against us than for us.** If the investor day ratifies management's 11–14% algorithm, the street's $161.5M comes down 5–8%, and the $19.30 target comes down with it. The catalyst we're waiting for is more likely a downward reconciliation than an upward one.

## What could go wrong

**The pre-mortem. Three narratives, ranked by likelihood × damage.**

### Failure 1 — Maintenance spending turns out to be far higher than we assumed, and the whole cash story deflates.
*Tripwire: YES.* The entire thesis rests on one number nobody discloses: how much First Watch has to spend each year just to keep its existing 586 restaurants running and looking current. The bull case assumed $48M. The hostile bench argued for $88M (this year's depreciation run-rate, on the theory that leasehold improvements depreciate over the lease term, which *is* the re-spend period). The defending bench broke the $88M number on internal consistency — if maintenance were $88M, the leftover for new restaurants would imply building each new store for $1.07–1.16M when both benches verified the actual build cost at $1.8M. The honest band is $50–65M. But nobody has *seen* the number. If the investor day discloses a re-image program at, say, $250k a restaurant on an 8-year cycle across 586 units, that's $18M — thesis intact. If it discloses a full remodel program at $600k on a 7-year cycle, that's $50M *on top of* routine upkeep and the thesis is dead.
**Monitorable via:** the November 12 investor day deck (filed as an 8-K exhibit), and the FY26 10-K's capital-spending discussion in February 2027.

### Failure 2 — The balance sheet, not the business, sets the timetable.
*Tripwire: PARTIAL, and this is a real gap.* The revolver is 56% drawn and costs 7.24%. Lease-adjusted leverage is around 4.6x. The Altman Z-score is 1.14, which is in the distress zone (this is a normal artifact for lease-heavy restaurant operators, but it is not nothing). The credit agreement has a "Total Rent Adjusted Net Leverage Ratio" covenant — **and the required level is not disclosed in the quarterly filing.** Both benches went looking for it. Neither found it; the defending bench's 10-K fetch truncated before the relevant section. So we know there is a covenant, we know roughly where the company sits, and we do not know where the line is. If a soft quarter plus continued capital spending pushes them toward it, the response is an equity raise or a further growth cut, either of which resets the stock lower regardless of whether the restaurants are doing fine.
**Monitorable via:** the covenant level once we find it (10-K exhibit or credit agreement), quarterly revolver balance, any 8-K amending the facility. **The gap:** until we locate the required ratio, we are monitoring a distance to a line we can't see.

### Failure 3 — Customer traffic quietly deteriorates and we have no way to see it. *(NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE)*
This is the failure we cannot instrument, and it deserves to be named plainly.

Traffic is already running −0.4%, with sales growth carried by price. The whole demand leg of this thesis — "the business is undamaged" — rests on management's own reporting of its own comparable sales, checked once a quarter, by them. Our independent sensors on this are all unavailable:

- Our restaurant-ratings tool needs a per-location address list for all 586 restaurants. We don't have one. It returned "needs an address" on both attempts.
- Our reservation-exhaust tool doesn't apply — First Watch is walk-in and waitlist, so there is no public booking surface to observe.
- No government statistical series covers breakfast-daypart restaurant traffic at the individual-company level. The pitch's "brunch sector growing 11.5%" claim could not be sourced to any primary series and carries **zero weight** in this deck.
- Our app-review tool did fire, weakly negative: review velocity 0.37/day, with the recent cohort rating 4.64 against a 4.93 lifetime average. But people who bother to write reviews skew angrier as a matter of base rate, and a −0.29-star gap on a 4.9 base is inside that bias. It cannot carry a verdict.
- Our building-permit tool returned zero First Watch permits in Austin for 2026 — which would be a striking result for a claimed growth market — except that tenant-improvement permits are routinely filed under the general contractor's or landlord's name, a known false-negative. Inconclusive.

**Unknown-unknown class: measurement-gap blindness — we have no independent sensor on the single variable that drives the outcome.** Every check we can run is either management-sourced or an unusable tool. If the breakfast daypart is structurally softening — a consumer trading down from a $16 restaurant breakfast, a shift in remote-work patterns changing weekday morning traffic — we will learn about it from the same quarterly release everyone else reads, at the same moment, with no lead. There is no tripwire to arm because there is nothing to watch.

### The reflexive and structural leg

**Our own size:** immaterial. A 1% starter is roughly $150k against a $750M company. We will not move this stock and we should not pretend our own flow is a risk. **However, average daily volume was not pulled by either bench — that check has not been done and must be run before any order goes in.** With 7.14M shares held short, liquidity is almost certainly adequate, but "almost certainly" is not a verified number.

**Predicted flow — this one is real.** Our proposed structure buys a small piece now and the larger piece after November 12. If the investor day is *good*, the 11.75% short base covers into it and we would be paying up for our own second tranche — bidding into flow we ourselves predicted. That is precisely the pattern our own flow discipline warns against. The mitigation is to accept that the second tranche has a price ceiling, not just a date, and to let it go unfilled if the pop is large.

**Doctrine interaction — the uncomfortable one.** Our clean-court rule says that when no bench produces a kill that threatens the *existence* of the thesis, we don't go flat; we take a minimum starter. Neither bench produced an existence-class kill here. So this position would come into being partly because **our own rules mechanically produce a starter when the kills are about size and timing rather than survival** — not because anyone's conviction is high. Both benches rated themselves 7/10, and both were rating the *kill* case. Nobody in this court argued hard *for* the position. That should be visible on the face of the deck, and it is.

**Second doctrine interaction:** we would be establishing a long position whose thesis is directionally identical to a Strong-Buy street consensus, at a fair-value estimate 25% *below* that street's target. Our differentiated view is "less bullish than the bulls." That is a legitimate stance, but it is not an edge, and it should not be sized as if it were.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the cost of keeping 586 existing restaurants current turns out to be $80M+ a year rather than $50–65M — a number nobody discloses and neither bench could measure — which erases the cash-earnings gap between us and the reported-earnings screens, leaving us long a fairly-priced, balance-sheet-constrained, mid-teens grower at 19x with consensus already crowded on the same side.**
