# PITCH — TREE — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**No position today. LendingTree's damage is real but industry-shaped, not company-specific; the surviving bull case ("margins just stay where they are") isn't paid well enough for its risks. Re-open only on the specific prices or facts below.**

## What we believe

LendingTree runs online marketplaces that sell consumer leads to insurance carriers, mortgage lenders, and consumer lenders. The stock is at $32.13, down 58.5% from its 52-week high of $77.35 and only 7.9% above its 52-week low — market cap roughly $451 million on 14.0 million shares (per the July 31 [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001434621&type=10-Q); the evidence pack's 13.65M share count was a year stale and was corrected in court).

What we believe after both benches finished:

1. **The profitability damage is real and confirmed.** Every segment's margin compressed in Q2 2026: Insurance from 27.1% to 23.9%, Home from 32.6% to 25.7%, Consumer from 51.1% to 45.8%. Roughly 4 percentage points of that is genuine deterioration, not just business-mix shift. Both benches reproduced this math independently from the earnings release.
2. **But the damage is not "worst in the industry" — that leg of our original pitch was simply wrong.** We claimed rival EverQuote grew its marketing profit dollars +15% while LendingTree managed +4%. EverQuote actually grew them [+25%](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/03/3337874/0/en/EverQuote-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results.html) — and LendingTree's own Insurance segment profit *also* grew +25%. Compared like-for-like, LendingTree is in line with its closest peer. Our +4% figure was the consolidated number, dragged down by the shrinking Home and Consumer segments.
3. **The honest residual concern:** on each *new* dollar of Insurance revenue, LendingTree kept only about 16 cents of marketing profit last quarter, versus EverQuote's steady ~29 cents. Rising Google ad costs are eating the incremental economics. Management guides Q3 Insurance margins "similar to Q2" — i.e., no recovery assumed.
4. **The company generates real cash.** Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is about $73.9 million — a 16.4% yield on the market cap. (Our original pitch said $80M / 17.7%; the court trimmed it.) The $400 million term loan doesn't mature until August 2030, so there is no near-term debt cliff — but it's priced at SOFR+4.50%, junk-level, and cash is $110.8 million against $386 million net debt.

## What the market believes

At ~$32, the market is pricing this as a damaged, levered, single-engine business: about 4.8x on the freshly-cut guidance, a 16%+ trailing free-cash-flow yield, and a stock that fell ~16% on the Q2 print. Insurance is now 67% of revenue, and the market appears to treat that segment's boom (revenue +42% year-over-year) as a temporary insurance-carrier advertising binge that will fade, while Home and Consumer keep shrinking. The debt pricing says lenders, too, charge for the tail risk. In short: the market already believes most of the bear case we walked in with.

## Why we might have edge

- **The reversion math is less scary than it looks.** The red (prosecution) bench argued that a carrier-spending downturn "kills 67% of revenue." The blue (defense) bench showed that conflates revenue with profit: if Insurance revenue fell all the way back to last year's level *and* margins recovered to ~30% as competition for leads eased, segment profit goes from ~$50M to ~$44M — roughly a 12% hit, not a collapse. A 16% free-cash-flow yield can absorb that.
- **A possible free option nobody is pricing:** LendingTree joined a [mass arbitration by advertisers against Google](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-13/google-faces-mass-arbitration-by-advertisers-seeking-billions) on July 17, citing $2.8 billion spent with Google over a decade. Any award would be largely shielded from tax by carryforwards, per the CFO. The market values this at zero or as a negative signal.
- **Sentiment mechanics:** the stock sits 8% off its low after a capitulation-style earnings drop. If Q3 merely matches management's "margins similar to Q2" guide — flat margins on growing dollars — the stock is cheap on its own guidance.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The core bull branch our pitch wanted — margin *recovery* — is dead by management's own words.** They guide flat, not better. What survives is only "things stay this bad but no worse," and a 16% cash yield is arguably fair payment for that, not a bargain.
- **Concentration and structural risk are unbounded.** Two-thirds of revenue is one segment riding a carrier advertising cycle that industry press suggests is late-stage (Progressive's ad budget roughly tripled 2023→2024 — though this is blog-grade evidence, not verified statutory data). Meanwhile the arbitration filing is a signal the company itself may view its Google channel economics as broken — and if Google ad costs keep rising into a guide that assumes they don't, the "flat carry" case fails too.
- **We couldn't check crowding.** Broker data access was denied to both benches this session, so short interest and analyst coverage are unknown. We may be late to a trade everyone has already seen.
- Both benches converged: this is a damaged business fairly marked down, not a mispricing. Court-worthiness was scored ~4–5 out of 10.
