## PITCH — QFIN — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Do not buy. Stay flat into the ~Aug 13 earnings report. The original buy case is rejected, but the stock is not proven overpriced either — four specific conditions, listed below, would reopen it.**

## What we believe

Qfin Holdings is a Chinese consumer-lending platform trading at $13.24, down 62% from its 52-week high (evidence pack, Aug 7 close). Both of our internal reviews — the bear-case team and the cross-examining team — independently concluded the same thing: **the pitch that brought this name to court is dead, but the case that the stock is now fairly priced was asserted, not demonstrated.**

The two legs of the original buy case both failed under checking. First, the claim that the company trades at roughly its cash value was wrong: the quoted cash figure of RMB 10.8 billion included RMB 3.94 billion of restricted cash that is pledged against the company's loan-guarantee obligations and is not available to shareholders. Usable cash plus short-term investments is RMB 6.55 billion — about 59% of the market value, not 97% ([Q1 2026 results](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/26/3301561/0/en/qfin-holdings-announces-first-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial-results.html)). Second, the argument that "the fee rate is holding up, so the market's fear is overdone" doesn't work: that 13% fee rate was earned under the old rules. Chinese regulators have ordered that new loans stop above a 24% all-in borrower rate immediately and have stated a goal of roughly 12% all-in by end-2027 ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-04-01/china-tightens-grip-on-consumer-lending-platforms-to-force-down-interest-rates-102430039.html)). Simple arithmetic — 12% charged to the borrower, minus ~6–7% funding cost, minus ~4% credit losses — leaves no room for a 13% platform fee. Today's healthy fee rate is evidence from a regime that is ending.

What survives is this: a shrinking lender trading at roughly 0.45× its book value of RMB 24.4 billion, still earning roughly RMB 3.5 billion a year under the old rules for about six more quarters while those rules phase out. Nobody — not the original pitch, not either review team — has actually valued that wind-down stream. Until someone does, "fairly priced" and "cheap" are both guesses.

## What the market believes

The market is pricing a business in managed decline: near-terminal earnings power once the 12% cap arrives, a loan book already shrinking (new loans down 26.8%), and no support under the stock — the $450 million 2025 buyback was fully spent at an average price of $28.20, no 2026 program has been announced, and the company chose to spend US$502 million retiring its convertible bonds rather than buying stock at $13. Bank of America downgraded in May explicitly citing the regulatory squeeze and the absence of buybacks, with a price target of $13.80 — essentially the current price. That downgrade is three months old; this is not a fresh panic the market hasn't absorbed. The stock is 17% *above* its 52-week low, so it is not sitting at a fresh point of maximum stress either.

## Why we might have edge

- **The wind-down math is genuinely uncomputed.** Roughly six more quarters of old-regime earnings accrete onto a book worth more than twice the market cap. If run-off value plus a modest terminal business exceeds $1.61 billion, the market is overshooting. This is checkable work no one has done.
- **The bears double-counted one cash outflow.** The convertible-bond repurchase (US$577 million of face value retired at a 13% discount, ~US$113 million left) is the same money as the "vanished cash floor" and the "no buyback capacity." That spend is finished and non-recurring: the 2030 refinancing cliff is gone, and cash rebuilds at roughly RMB 2 billion of operating cash flow per quarter.
- **The scariest data point is disputed and resolves imminently.** Caixin reported the loan balance falling below RMB 90 billion by mid-March; the company reported RMB 114.4 billion at March 31 — both using the same metric. One of them is wrong, and the Q2 report settles it. If the company's number holds, the most aggressive bear scenario is refuted.
- **"Net cash negative" was overturned.** The bear team netted out RMB 11.1 billion of trust borrowings, but those fund matching loan assets on the balance sheet and are already reflected in book value.

## Why it might be priced in

- The cash-floor and fee-rate arguments — the only two legs of the original pitch — are both dead, and everything above is a *residual* case, not the pitched one.
- The 12%-by-2027 regime is not a speculative draft: regulators are already enforcing the 24% cap and compressing balances now.
- The apparent credit improvement (early delinquencies 6.1% → 5.7%) is a side effect of lending much less, and the 90-day-plus delinquency rate rose from 2.71% to 3.50% on a shrinking loan base — worse than it looks.
- There is no buyer of last resort under the stock, and the semi-annual dividend decision at this report is genuinely binary.
- Only RMB 55.66 billion of the RMB 114 billion book is the newer "capital-light" model; the majority still carries RMB 3.17 billion of guarantee obligations into the transition.
- Three months after the downgrade, price sits at the Street's target. Nothing here is undiscovered.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry now, at any price, ahead of the report.** Sizing today: 0% of book. There are no exit or trim levels because there is no position. What follows are the **reopen conditions**, taken from the review teams' own math:

**Reopen for a full re-hearing (a possible buy) only if ALL FOUR print at the Q2 report:**
1. Semi-annual dividend held or raised — at least **$0.78 per share** for the first half.
2. Quarter-end loan balance of at least **~RMB 105 billion** (the bear team's stricter refutation line is RMB 110 billion; either way, a print toward RMB 90–100 billion confirms the Caixin trajectory and kills the case for another ~15–20% of revenue).
3. **Any 2026 share-repurchase authorization** — including one hiding in the unread June 30 filing.
4. **Q3 guidance at or above normal seasonal levels** — this is the first forecast issued under the new disclosure regime and carries more information than the Q2 profit itself, which was already guided to RMB 830–910 million.

**Price-based reopen:** a close below **$11.31** (the 52-week low) converts this from "17% off the low" to a fresh dislocation and triggers a re-hearing — a review, not an automatic buy. The bear team explicitly rejected pre-arming any buy trigger that lacks both a balance-trajectory condition and a concrete capital-return act, and we adopt that.

**Regulatory reopen:** final national rules materially softer than the 12%-by-2027 draft.

**Sizing on reopen was not set by either team and must be set at the re-hearing** — proposing one here would exceed what the court established.

**Catalyst dates:** Q2 2026 earnings, **~Aug 13, 2026** (vendor-sourced, unconfirmed — window Aug 13–20), carrying the dividend decision, the balance print, and the Q3 guide. Longer-dated: the **end-2027** deadline for the 12% rate cap.
