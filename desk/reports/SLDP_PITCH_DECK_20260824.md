# PITCH — SLDP — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position and no entry at $2.31; both benches land on flat with a *named* reason, and the reopen is event-gated, not price-gated alone.

---

## What we believe

Solid Power is a solid-state battery developer in Colorado with about **$419 million of cash and investments, no borrowed money**, and roughly **228.2 million shares** ($1.84 per share of net cash). At $2.31, the market is paying about **$110–119 million** for everything the company does besides hold cash.

We believe that $110–119 million cannot be justified from anything the company has published, and we believe the reason is specific rather than vague:

1. **The revenue is gone, not shrinking.** In the June 2026 quarter the company said it *completed* the line-installation agreement with SK On and collected the final payment, and in the same quarter it **reversed $1.2 million of revenue it had already booked** because it no longer expects to collect certain milestones. First-half 2026 revenue plus grants was **$2.8 million against $13.6 million a year earlier — down 79%**. Contract assets went from **$7.49 million to zero**. Non-government revenue in Q2 was **negative $1.0 million**.
2. **The reversal hit the part of the business the whole valuation rests on.** The $1.2 million was written off against milestones under the **SK On R&D *license* agreement** — the licensing leg. Management's own stated plan is "to sell its electrolyte **and to license its cell designs and manufacturing processes**." The licensing half is where nearly all the theoretical value sits, and it is the half where management just lowered its own collection assumption.
3. **You cannot price the prize from the company's own documents.** Solid Power has published *capacity* — a 45 tonne/year pilot line, a 75 tonne/year target by end-2026, first output Q1 2027 — but has never published a price per kilogram, a capital cost per tonne, or a royalty structure. There is no techno-economic study. Every input you would need to value the business must be assumed rather than cited.
4. **The cash is a spending schedule, not a floor.** Real cash consumption is about **$70 million a year** (first-half operating cash flow −$27.2M plus capital spending −$7.9M = $35.1M per half), inside the company's own $85–100M guide, and interest income of roughly $16.7M a year already offsets about a quarter of it. That is roughly **20 months** of stub value before the cushion is spent. The board is obligated to spend it.

Put plainly: **the above-cash value of this company is an option whose strike price has never been disclosed, and the seller just told us the one contract with a strike attached is less collectible than they thought.** That is why we are flat, and it is a specific reason rather than a shrug.

## What the market believes

- **Analysts still model $13.02 million of 2026 revenue** (five analysts, consensus price target ~$6.92). That requires roughly **$10.2 million in the second half** from a company whose only cash-paying contract just finished and whose grant income runs about $0.75–0.86 million a quarter. Our number is about **$4.6 million** for the full year — roughly 65% below the street.
- Sell-side coverage of the June quarter read the $1.2 million reversal as an accounting quirk. We read it as management lowering the odds on the licensing leg.
- A structured-financing desk (CVI/Heights) put $130 million in during January 2026 at **$5.70 a share plus 45.6 million warrants struck at $7.25 running to 2033**. That is a financing structure, not a strategic endorsement.
- **Short interest is 24.98 million shares — 13.64% of float, 6.5 days to cover.** A meaningful part of the shareholder base is positioned against the story, and some of that is hedging against the January warrants.
- The stock is down **74% from its 52-week high** ($8.86) and sits **17% above its 52-week low** ($1.98). The market has already de-rated it hard; what it has *not* done is mark the revenue line to what the filing says.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the filing sentence the coverage skipped.** "We completed the line installation agreement and received the associated final milestone payment," combined with a reversal of previously recognized revenue, is a specific, quotable retirement of the revenue stream. The consensus number is annualizing a finished one-time build.
- **We identified which leg the reversal hit.** The bearish framing everywhere else is "equipment build finished." The harder fact is that the reversal was booked against license milestones. That distinction is the difference between a timing gap and a valuation problem.
- **We tried to build the valuation and documented that it can't be built.** The absence of any published unit economics is itself a finding — a developer whose project cannot be valued from its own filings. Most processes stop at "hard to value"; we can name exactly which four inputs are missing.
- **We have a dated fork.** The initial phase of the joint evaluation agreement **expires September 30, 2026**, and a Korea joint venture is targeted for year-end 2026. Each is a chance for the option's strike to become visible for the first time.

**Honest limits on that edge:** the revenue miss is only tradable if consensus is actually stale — we could not confirm the $13.02 million was refreshed after the August 4 print. And a 65% revenue shortfall at a company where revenue is 1% of market cap may simply not move the stock.

## Why it might be priced in

- **A 74% drawdown is not the tape of a market that missed something.** Anyone who cares knows this is a cash-and-a-dream story.
- **13.64% short interest means the bear case is crowded.** We would be the last bear to the table, and the shorts get paid on the same catalysts we would.
- **The revenue line may be economically irrelevant.** Nobody buying SLDP is buying $13 million of milestone revenue. If the market already treats revenue as a rounding error, being 65% below consensus on a rounding error earns nothing.
- **The cash is genuinely there, and the burn is genuinely slower than we first said.** Twenty months of runway plus a partner negotiation in progress is not a distress setup. Management asserts twelve-month sufficiency and there is no going-concern language.
- **The "20% dilution cap" from the January warrants is largely illusory** — the $7.25 strike is 206% above today's price, and if the stock ever got there, exercise delivers **$330.7 million of cash** against about 20% dilution, roughly a wash on a triple. So the upside is not structurally capped the way a quick look suggests. That cuts against our bearish framing.

## What could go wrong

*(This is the pre-mortem. We are currently flat, so "losing money" means either losing money on a starter position we later take, or paying the opportunity cost of standing aside.)*

**Failure 1 — Silent technical disqualification inside a partner's lab. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
The value of this company rests on whether Samsung SDI, SK On, or BMW conclude that the sulfide electrolyte works at automotive cycle life, fast-charge, and cost. Those verdicts are formed inside private test programs and are never published. A joint evaluation agreement that lapses on its own terms on **September 30, 2026** triggers no filing obligation — there is no 8-K for something that simply expires. We would not learn about it until the Q3 report in early November, and possibly not even then. There is no data feed, no permit, no satellite image, and no filing that reveals a partner's internal engineering judgment.
**Unknown-unknown class: counterparty-internal verdicts.** Decisions made by a private third party, on evidence we will never see, that determine the entire value of our position. This class also covers competitor progress we can't observe (a rival chemistry clearing an OEM gate we don't know exists) and unpublished failure modes in the material itself. We have no instrument for any of it, and we should stop pretending a filing watch substitutes for one.
*Partial mitigation, honestly labeled as weak:* the asymmetry runs one way. A **signed** Phase 2 agreement would be a material contract and would get announced. So silence past mid-October is *evidence*, not proof — a probability update, not a trigger.

**Failure 2 — We buy the cash and the cash walks out the door.**
We anchor on $1.84 a share of net cash and treat it as a floor. It isn't. At $70 million a year of consumption, the floor erodes roughly $0.31 a share every year, and the company is contractually and strategically committed to spending it — SP2 buildout, the Korea JV capex share, continued R&D. Two years of no partner signing and the "floor" is $1.20 with a company that still has no priceable product. Worse, the most likely response to a cash cushion falling below roughly a year of runway is an equity raise, which resets the share count against us.
*Tripwire (this one is real):* quarterly cash and investments balance in each 10-Q; any S-3 shelf, 424B5, or at-the-market program filing; year-over-year share count on the 10-Q cover page.

**Failure 3 — We are right on the revenue and it doesn't matter, then a headline runs us over.**
FY26 revenue comes in at $4.6 million against a $13.02 million consensus and the stock doesn't move, because nobody was valuing the revenue. Then a Samsung SDI or SK On agreement gets announced with a headline dollar figure, the 13.64% short base covers into 6.5 days of volume, and the stock is up 60% in two sessions on a contract whose economics are still undisclosed. If we are flat, we miss it. If we are short (we are not, and short-selling this is separately disqualified by the crowding), we get hurt badly.
*Tripwire:* 8-K Item 1.01 / issuer press release watch on Samsung SDI, SK On, BMW; short-interest re-pull each settlement cycle.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own footprint, our predicted flow, and our own rules working against us:**

- **Size versus liquidity is not the problem here, and we should say so plainly.** At roughly $9.2 million of average daily dollar volume, a starter position sized at a fraction of a percent of the book is well under 1% of a day's volume and can be built or unwound in days. Exit risk is not a reason to stay away. We should not hide behind a liquidity excuse we don't have.
- **The price level we want is the price level that means bad news — and it is our own predicted flow.** Our natural buy point is at or below $1.84 (net cash). But the realistic paths to $1.84 are (a) an equity raise being marketed, (b) a September 30 lapse becoming known, or (c) tax-loss selling in December. In every one of those, we would be bidding into supply that our own analysis predicts. A price-only trigger here is a trap: it fills us exactly when the option we're buying has gotten cheaper *for a reason*. **Any price trigger must be paired with a disclosure event, never fired alone.**
- **Our own "don't be flat without a named reason" rule pushes toward a token position, and that pressure is a bias, not an insight.** The discipline exists to stop lazy avoidance. It is not a mandate to own something we've explicitly established we cannot value. The named reason here — an option with an undisclosed strike whose only priced milestones just got written down — is exactly what that rule asks for, and we should not let discomfort with a zero talk us past it.
- **A subtler conflict: a small loss here is tax-useful to us.** Our core book harvests losses. That creates a quiet incentive to take a starter in a name we don't believe in, because the downside has a tax rebate attached. That is a bad reason to own something and we are naming it so it can't operate silently.
- **If we do buy and it works, we may misread why.** With 13.64% of the float short and 6.5 days to cover, a 40% rally could be entirely mechanical. We would be tempted to grade that as thesis validation and add. It would not be.
- **We do not have a live price.** Broker market-data calls were permission-denied in both benches. Every number in this deck anchors on the evidence pack's $2.31 and a secondary source's August 21 close. Our bands should be treated as approximate until a live tick confirms.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we bought an option whose strike price the company has never disclosed, mistook the $1.84 of cash behind it for a floor when it was a two-year spending schedule, and got no warning of the failure because the decision that killed it — a partner's private conclusion that the technology doesn't clear their bar — is made in a lab whose results are never filed.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry. There is no buy at $2.31 and no limit order to leave working.** What follows are the conditions that reopen the file.

**Why no price-only entry exists.** The stock is $2.31 against $1.838 of net cash. The most the *disclosed, funded* business supports on top of that cash — 75 tonnes/year at the company's own $50/kg target, valued at 6x sales — is about **$22 million, or $0.10 a share.** That gives a documented value of roughly **$1.94**, below the 52-week low of $1.98. Everything above $1.94 is the licensing option, and the licensing option has no disclosed strike. We are not able to write a limit price for something we cannot value, and a price-only trigger would fill us precisely on the news that makes it worthless.

### Reopen conditions — the file reopens only when a *disclosure* makes the option priceable

Any **one** of these three reopens the court immediately, regardless of price:

|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | A **signed SK On (or Samsung SDI / BMW) collaboration agreement with a stated dollar value**, disclosed in an 8-K Item 1.01 or issuer release | Restores a cash-paying contract and puts a number on the licensing leg for the first time | Under negotiation now; could land any week |
| **R2** | **Korea JV formalized**, with SLDP's capital contribution and the JV's capacity disclosed | Converts "capacity target" into capital cost per tonne — one of the four missing inputs | Targeted year-end 2026 |
