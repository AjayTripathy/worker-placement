# PITCH — STRL — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position today at $504.80; a ⅙-size starter is proposed only on a pullback into $464 or lower, because the only fair-value math in the record puts the stock *above* the top of its own value band.

---

## What we believe

Sterling is a real, cash-generating heavy-civil and site-work contractor that is growing fast and just raised its full-year outlook. On the company's own numbers, second-quarter revenue was $1.17 billion, up 90% year over year, of which acquisitions contributed $250.8 million — so the underlying business grew about 49% on its own ([company release](https://www.strlco.com/news/sterling-reports-record-second-quarter-results-and-raises-full-year-2026-guidance/)). Full-year guidance is revenue of $4.00–4.15 billion, GAAP diluted EPS of $17.25–17.85, and adjusted EBITDA of $891–916 million ([Q2 8-K, 2026-08-03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874238/000087423826000100/)).

So our belief is narrow and unglamorous: **the business is not damaged, but it is not cheap either.** The stock is down about half from its 52-week high, and that fact alone tempted the first draft of this pitch into calling it a "50% discount." That framing is dead. From the low of $281.58 the stock has already risen 79%. At $504.80 it trades at 28.3–29.3x this year's GAAP guided earnings, 24.9–25.6x adjusted earnings, and about 17.4x the midpoint of adjusted EBITDA. Those are full prices for a contractor.

What we would actually be buying, if we bought, is **durability**: that the order book (combined backlog $5.62 billion, signed $4.33 billion, signed up 116% year over year) keeps converting into work in 2027 and beyond at decent margins. That is a next-year question, not a this-quarter question, and the answer arrives in pieces at the next earnings print.

## What the market believes

The market believes two things at once, and they are not contradictory.

First, the sell side is bullish and loud: eight analysts, a "Strong Buy" consensus, and an average price target of $876. Buying at $505 is not fighting the street — it is **agreeing** with the street at a 42% discount to its own target. That is an uncomfortable place to claim edge.

Second, the tape is skeptical of the *mix*. The company beat and raised on August 3–4 and the shares fell anyway. Five weeks have passed with every number public. The market's working theory appears to be that the growth being bolted on — the acquired units, guided at mid-teens EBITDA margins on roughly $180–200 million of revenue — dilutes the company's ~21–22% blended margin, so revenue growth overstates earnings growth. That is the priced narrative.

## Why we might have edge

Honest answer: **thin, and mostly in the specifics, not the story.**

1. **The guidance basis is genuinely unresolved in public.** The prior outlook (May 4) was revenue $3.70–3.80 billion and EBITDA $801–831 million ([May 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000874238/000087423826000052/a20260504ex991earningsrele.htm)). The new outlook shows two different EBITDA figures circulating — roughly $829–854 million on one label and $891–916 million on another. Which label the May figure matches decides whether the raise was **dilutive (+$25.5M EBITDA on +$325M revenue = 7.8% incremental margin)** or **accretive (+$87.5M = 26.9% incremental, above the 21.8% base)**. Same company, same quarter, opposite conclusions. Nobody in our record actually opened the exhibit and settled it. That is a one-hour job with a real payoff.
2. **Labor hiring is still rising at the newly acquired units.** Record job-req counts step up 3–5x into August–September 2026 (Plateau roughly 2/quarter → 8 then 10; Stone Ridge 1–4/month → 9, 13, 14). If real, a company does not staff up into a slowdown. It is contaminated evidence (see below), but it points the opposite way from the de-rating story.
3. **The valuation kill priced the wrong year.** The 15–17x multiple applied to this year's EBITDA embeds effectively no growth, on a signed book up 116%. If 2027 durability is even half-proven, the multiple is the wrong anchor.

## Why it might be priced in

- **28–29x guided GAAP earnings is on every screen.** There is no secret in the P/E. Our "cheap" call requires the multiple to be wrong, not the data to be unknown.
- **Margin mix was call material.** Management discussed the acquired units' mid-teens margins openly. The market did the arithmetic before we did.
- **Backlog composition is public too.** Signed backlog covers about 1.06x this year's revenue; the unsigned $1.28 billion is 96.9% sitting at the two newest units. Anyone can see it.
- **Insider selling is public.** A Form 144 on 2026-06-25, a run of Form 4s from May through August, and large CEO sales in March 2026 are all on file.
- **Our own scenario math says it is roughly fair.** Expected value on our table is $482 against a $505 tape — a *negative* 4.5% edge at today's price. That is the cleanest statement of "priced in" we can make.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the dilution is real and the label resolves against us (tripwire exists).**
The Q3 exhibit states the outlook on one basis and the raise turns out to be 7.8% incremental margin, not 26.9%. Earnings growth then lags revenue growth for two years while the acquired units are integrated, and 28x becomes 20x on unchanged earnings. *Monitorable:* the Q3 8-K exhibit, the E-Infrastructure segment margin line, and any guide language change. Price consequence roughly $400–430.

**Failure narrative 2 — the order book converts late (tripwire exists).**
The $1.28 billion of unsigned backlog is concentrated at the least-seasoned units. If less than about $400 million converts with named awards by year-end, the "durability" thesis has no evidence for two more quarters while we pay a full multiple to wait. *Monitorable:* named award announcements (8-K/PR), signed-backlog line each quarter, Austin-area permit pulls for the electrical unit's work-in-place (our permit read timed out and was never restored).

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
Roughly 92% of the E-Infrastructure book sits inside a single capital-spending cycle driven by a small number of very large private customers whom the company does not name. A decision made in one customer's capital committee — a pause, a re-phasing, a change of contractor, a change of data-center design that needs less earthwork — reaches us only *after* it has already reached the backlog line, one quarter later. There is no filing, no permit, no satellite pass and no hiring series that front-runs it, because the customer is private and the parcels belong to the customer, not to Sterling.
**Unknown-unknown class: "private counterparty capital-allocation reversal at an undisclosed concentrated customer" — an off-tape, off-filing decision at an entity we cannot name, cannot monitor, and cannot subpoena.** Adjacent named class: *undisclosed-customer concentration risk in percentage-of-completion contracting.* We accept that this one is un-sensored, which is itself an argument for small size rather than for a wider stop.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
Our own size is not the problem; the *company we would be keeping* is. Average daily volume is not in the pack — our estimate is 0.6–1.2 million shares, roughly $300–600 million of notional a day — so even a full 3%-of-book target is a small fraction of one day's turnover. We move nothing. The reflexive risk runs the other way:

- **Predicted flow is crowded and one-directional.** Consensus "Strong Buy" with an average target of $876 means the marginal seller on bad news is a disappointed bull with a published target, not a valuation buyer. Air pockets under crowded longs are deeper than the fundamentals justify, and our proposed add band ($460) sits directly in that air pocket.
- **Doctrine interaction 1 — mechanical adds into a re-rate.** Our add gates are written as "resolve the guide basis, show $800M converted, show the net-debt bridge." All three resolve on the *same* 8-K. If they resolve well, the stock gaps and our gates fire at a worse price than the band; if they resolve badly, none fire and we hold only a starter. The gate structure is therefore asymmetric against us in the good case — we should expect our average entry to be above the band, not at it.
- **Doctrine interaction 2 — "damage-absent" vs "divergence-not-coverage."** The classification doctrine says the business is intact, so hold. The divergence doctrine says agreeing with a "Strong Buy" street is not edge, so pass. Both fired here and they point in opposite directions; the resolution we chose (own it small, only below fair value) is a compromise, not a proof, and a compromise sized at ⅙ can lose money in a way that neither doctrine would have flagged as a doctrine failure.
- **Structural overhang.** Insider distribution (Form 144 on 2026-06-25, six Form 4s May–August) plus 13G/A churn on 2026-07-30 and 2026-09-04 means supply is present but unquantified — we never pulled net-holdings deltas or 10b5-1 adoption dates, so we cannot tell programmed selling from discretionary selling.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid roughly 29x guided earnings for 2027 durability that arrives a year later than the tape needs — the guide basis resolved to the dilutive label and the unsigned $1.28 billion converted slowly — and we did it while agreeing with a crowded "Strong Buy" street that had nothing left to upgrade.

**EV = 0.20(600) + 0.30(510) + 0.25(465) + 0.18(400) + 0.07(300) = $482.25.**
Against the tape of $504.80 that is **−4.5%** — a negative edge at today's price. At the $463.90 starter band it is **+3.9%**; at the $434.90 add band, **+10.9%.** This is the arithmetic that produces the no-entry-today verdict; the bands are not preferences, they are where EV turns positive.

**Dispersion:** σ_table = **$81.40 = 16.1% of spot** over ~52 days to the print (T = 0.1425 yr). Annualized equivalent = 0.161 / √0.1425 = **42.7%**. Measured to a January 2027 expiry (T = 0.345), the same dispersion annualizes to **27.4%**.

**Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated on a single filing — A (20%) and D+E (25%) sit on opposite sides of one 8-K, with B and C clustered at the anchor. That is a genuine two-humped distribution, which is exactly the shape that makes options attractive **if** they are cheap.

**Expression math (candidate strikes, priced off the table itself):**
- C_ours(K = 520) = Σ pᵢ·max(Sᵢ − 520, 0) = 0.20 × 80 = **$16.00**
- C_ours(K = 550) = 0.20 × 50 = **$10.00**
- C_ours(K = 600) = **$0.00** — the table has no mass above $600, so anything at or above the $600 strike is worth nothing to us regardless of price
- P_ours(K = 460) = **$22.00**; P_ours(K = 430) = **$14.50** (relevant to a cash-secured-put route into the starter band)

**Where the arithmetic stops — and the instrument output:**
**No options chain reached either bench.** We therefore have **no chain-level midpoint IV and no real quotes for the $520/$550 calls or the $460/$430 puts**, which means we cannot compute IV-vs-RV, the IV percentile, or the variance risk premium (IV − RV). We will not substitute the underlying's aggregate IV feed for chain midpoints — that is precisely the TSSI lesson, where the aggregate feed read 71 against a chain that was really 101, a 30-point error that would flip any of these decisions. The honest form of the comparison is a **threshold**: our table implies 42.7% annualized to the print. If chain-midpoint IV prints **materially below ~43%**, the $520 call at under $16 is cheaper than our own distribution and gated adds could be expressed in calls; if it prints above ~43%, the two-humped table is already paid for and the route is premium-selling (a $460 cash-secured put against our own $22 valuation) rather than buying. **Until that number exists, the arithmetic cannot output an options trade.**

**Tax overlay:** the only listed expiries that span both the print (Nov 2, unconfirmed — possibly Nov 9) and the gate resolutions run into **January 2027**. That puts the loss branch — 25% combined probability on D+E — **past December 31**, deferring the loss out of the current harvest year. Under the harvest-year rule that excludes the call route unless the edge numerically dominates, and it does not: EV at spot is **negative 4.5%**, so there is no dominating edge to override the deferral. A November-expiry call would keep the loss in-year but expires on or before the print date itself under the unconfirmed-date risk — buying an instrument that can expire before its own catalyst.
