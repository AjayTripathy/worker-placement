# PITCH — CSGP — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-08 — DECLINE. No position, no resting order: the "damage-absent" story is damage-arriving in disguise (net new bookings −26% year over year, organic growth ~8.5%, profit "inflection" = a marketing spend cut off a crushed base) at ~15.5–16.5x forward EV/EBITDA, which is not obviously cheap.**

---

## What we believe

CoStar owns a genuinely good data business — commercial real estate information, Apartments.com, LoopNet — with almost no debt and a franchise that is hard to copy. That much survived both sides of the argument.

What we do not believe is the case that was brought to us. The original pitch said: revenue is growing ~18%, profit doubled, guidance went up, so whatever hurt the stock (it is down ~66% from its 52-week high) has already happened and is behind the company. We tested each of those three legs and each one broke:

- **The growth is half bought, not earned.** Management said on the Q2 call that roughly half of this year's revenue growth is organic — so ~10% year-to-date, not ~20% — and guided the second half to come in about a point and a half below that, i.e. ~8.5%. The Domain acquisition (A$1.92bn, closed August 2025) sits inside the year-over-year comparison until Q3.
- **The profit jump is a spend cut, not a demand recovery.** Homes.com inside sales headcount went from ~660 to ~400; Ten-X was restructured. Cutting the marketing budget on a money-losing growth bet makes this year's profit look better and next year's growth look worse.
- **The forward-looking number is falling hard.** Net new bookings — what the company sells today that becomes revenue later — were $69M in Q2, down ~26% from a year ago. Full-year 2025 bookings of $308M barely exceeded 2022's $305M, on a revenue base about 1.7x larger. Three years of no growth in the leading indicator.

So: real company, real balance sheet, decelerating engine, and a price that already reflects it. We pass.

## What the market believes

Roughly the same thing we concluded, and it got there before we did. CoStar reported a headline-record quarter on 28 July 2026 and the stock fell after hours. Eight sessions later it had not recovered. The market read past "revenue +18%, profit doubled" to the bookings line and sold it.

This is a large, heavily covered company. There is no plausible story where a well-followed mega-cap's most important disclosed metric falls 26% and nobody notices. When we ask "why would we be right and everyone else wrong," the honest answer here is: we probably wouldn't be.

For reference, the market is currently paying about 15.5x this year's guided adjusted profit measure (about 16.5x once the pending Zonda acquisition is funded), and roughly 55x reported earnings, for a business growing organically around 8.5% and slowing.

## Why we might have edge

Stated fairly, because the case is thin:

1. **The reported growth number is misleading and stays misleading until Q3.** Anyone screening on "revenue +18%" is looking at a number that is roughly half acquisition. That distortion mechanically washes out when Domain laps in Q3 — which is a real, dated event, not a guess.
2. **Some of the bookings decline is self-inflicted, and self-inflicted damage is reversible.** Cutting the Homes.com sales force and restructuring Ten-X *causes* bookings to fall. A managed retreat from a losing bet is a different animal from customers walking away. If the commercial business is intact underneath, the leading indicator is understating the franchise.
3. **The balance sheet is not the problem.** Even after paying $800M cash for Zonda, we estimate pro-forma net debt of roughly $400–600M — about half a year's profit. This company will not be forced to do anything.

That is the whole positive case. Note it is a case for *looking again later*, not for buying now.

## Why it might be priced in

- The stock fell on the print and stayed down. The information is public and was acted on.
- Guidance moved the wrong way at the actual Q2 print: the revenue outlook was **lowered** to $3.715–3.755bn. The company itself told the market growth is slowing.
- 15.5–16.5x forward profit for ~8.5% organic growth is a fair-to-full price, not a distressed one. There is no discount to argue about.
- The 52-week low of $25.89 implies roughly 14x on the same math — meaning the market has already visited a price that treats the bookings decline as real and permanent. We are above that level, not below it.
- The one piece of evidence that could rescue the case — a commercial-only bookings figure showing the weakness is confined to residential — **is not disclosed**. We cannot run our own decisive test on today's information. An untestable bull case is not an edge; it is a hope.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these are load-bearing:

1. **Whether the bookings collapse is seasonal, trend, or self-inflicted in what proportion.** The seasonality tool never ran. Our best read — mostly trend with a real managed-retreat component — is a judgment, not a measurement.

5. **How consensus/crowded this bearish read already is.** The crowding check never ran, so "the market already knows" is supported by the tape but not measured.

7. **A minor unresolved data conflict:** the evidence pack lists a 52-week high of $91.89 while our red bench found $97.12. Different sources or intraday-versus-close basis, most likely. Nothing in the verdict turns on it, but the discrepancy is unexplained.
