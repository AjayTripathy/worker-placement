## 0. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH RULING

Walmart's Q1 FY27 (May-21) was a **beat-and-reiterate**, not a miss: adjusted EPS $0.66 vs a $0.63-0.65
guide, net sales +5.9% cc vs a +3.5-4.5% guide, and the FY27 outlook was left **unchanged**. Estimates
have not fallen. So this is textbook **multiple normalization, not estimate derailment** — the pattern
the court frame calls a winner. **But the frame's second half fails.** WMT is not at an own-history
discount: it trades at **39.5x** the FY27 adjusted-EPS guide midpoint and **41.9x** trailing FY26
adjusted EPS, versus a verified own-history band of **22.6x–26.4x** as recently as Feb-2024. The
-17.6% drawdown has unwound roughly **one quarter** of a re-rate that took the multiple from 22.6x to
48.5x in three years on **7.5%/yr** adjusted-EPS compounding. The other three quarters of the re-rate
is still in the price. There is no honesty gap here — management disclosed every adverse datum on the
same page as the flattering ones. The mispricing was, and still is, in the market's own multiple.
**Buying this is buying the tail end of a de-rate with ~6 turns still to go.**

## 2. CAUSE — what actually broke on May-21 (primary: 8-K 0000104169-26-000095, EX-99.1)

**It was not the guide and not the EPS line.** Reconstructed against the Feb-19-2026 guide:

So a beat took the stock down 7.3%. **Four things did it, and all four are in the release:**

**(a) Reiterate-not-raise = an implicit H2 cut.** Q1 landed $0.01-0.03 above the guide top; Q2 is
guided to $0.72-0.74 vs $0.68 LY. H1 FY27 ≈ $1.38-1.40 vs H1 FY26 $1.29 (+7.0-8.5%). Holding FY27 at

company banked a beat and gave none of it forward.

**(b) Negative operating leverage, in the flagship segment.** Walmart U.S.: gross profit **+29bps**,
operating expense **deleveraged 56bps** → segment operating margin **contracted ~27bps** on +4.5% sales.
Named causes: **higher depreciation** (the automation capex program) and **healthcare costs for
associate benefits**. Consolidated: revenue +7.3%, operating income **+5.0%** — revenue grew faster
than profit.

**(c) A fuel shock inside its own logistics.** The CEO's own paragraph: operating income "negatively
affected by **250 bps from higher fuel costs in distribution and fulfillment**." Fuel also shows up in
the Sam's Club inventory build (+14.9%, "primarily related to higher fuel costs and volumes") and in

**(d) Cash conversion.** Operating cash flow **−$0.7B YoY**; **free cash flow −$1.9B, a $2.4B YoY
deterioration**; inventory **+8.9%** against revenue +7.3%. On a name capitalized as a cash compounder,
a $2.4B FCF swing in Q1 is the quality-of-earnings tell.

**Consumer/trade-down mix:** Walmart U.S. average ticket decelerated to **+1.1%** (from +2.8% LY) while
transactions **accelerated to +3.0%** (from +1.6%) — more trips, smaller baskets. Sam's Club average
ticket was **−2.2%**. That is a trade-down signature, and it is share-positive but margin-neutral-to-negative.

**Drift since:** no 8-K after 2026-06-05 (an annual-meeting 8-K, not guidance). **No guidance revision
has occurred.** The drift is de-rating, not news. Today's move is an **Oppenheimer downgrade** (08-04,

**Two disclosure asymmetries worth naming (neither is a lie; both bias the reader):**

1. **The advertising base is not disclosed.** The single most-cited bull leg is presented as a growth
   rate (+37%) with no dollar denominator in any primary filing. The reader *cannot compute* its

2. **The membership metric selects the flattering slice.** The release headlines "membership fee revenue
   grew 17.4%"; the 10-K shows the line item it sits in grew **4.7%** in FY26, because other income
   (e.g. recycling income) fell. Both true; the release picks the sub-component.

**GLP-1 / health-and-wellness — the mix channel, quantified (10-K + 10-Q segment tables):**

| Walmart U.S. Health & Wellness net sales | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 | Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $M | 54,898 | 62,092 (+13.1%) | **69,547 (+12.0%)** | **16,355 vs 16,244 = +0.7%** |

Health & wellness (pharmacy) supplied **~$7.5B of Walmart U.S.'s $20.6B total FY26 net-sales increase —
36% of all segment growth** — and it is retail's structurally lowest-gross-margin category. **In Q1 FY27
that engine went from +12.0% to +0.7%, and Sam's went negative.** This cuts both ways and I will not
pretend otherwise:
- **Bear:** a $69.5B category that carried a third of the growth has flatlined; the top line must be
  replaced from elsewhere. It was this quarter (comp +4.1%), but it is a live forward risk.
- **Bull:** the *lowest-margin* category decelerating is gross-margin **accretive** — and Walmart U.S.
  gross profit did print **+29bps**, its best in several quarters.

The mechanism (GLP-1 list-price compression deflating dollar sales while scripts grow) is **PLAUSIBLE,
not confirmed** — Walmart does not disclose the driver.

**The synthesis, and the reason the multiple is wrong.** Advertising +37%, membership fees +17.4%,
eCommerce +26%, and a favorable pharmacy mix shift — and consolidated **gross profit rate rose 6bps**
while Walmart U.S. **operating margin contracted ~27bps**. Every dollar of high-margin mix gain is being
consumed by depreciation, healthcare costs and fuel. **Six-plus quarters of "margin transformation" have
produced +5.2% (FY26) and +6.1%E (FY27) adjusted-EPS growth — statistically indistinguishable from the
+5.7% WMT posted in FY24, before any of it.**

## 5. THE TARIFF REGIME SWAP — the differentiated leg (primary-sourced, and it is a WASH)

### 5a. What management actually assumed — verbatim

**8-K EX-99.1, 2026-05-21, guidance preamble:**
> "The following guidance reflects the Company's expectations as of May 21, 2026, and **does not assume
> any impact from IEEPA tariff refunds.**"

**10-Q (filed 2026-05-29, period ended 2026-04-30), MD&A Overview:**
> "The Company is participating in the process established by the U.S. Customs and Border Protection for
> refunds of tariffs that the Company paid as the importer of record under the International Emergency
> Economic Powers Act. The timing, amounts and ultimate resolution of any refunds remain uncertain and
> subject to ongoing legal and administrative developments. **Accordingly, the Company did not recognize
> any amounts related to these claims in the three months ended April 30, 2026.**"

> "While we operate in a highly dynamic tariff environment, **less than one third of what we sell in the
> U.S. is imported**, with most of our imports coming from China, Vietnam, Mexico, India and Canada."

### 5b. Findings

**FINDING 1 — WMT is a clean house-pattern "B_skew," and it is MISSING from our own cohort.**
Refund **unrecognized** as a gain contingency **and** the guide explicitly excludes it = the exact
`B_pattern` in `TARIFF_REFUND_COHORT.json` ("refund unrecognized (gain contingency) and/or guide assumes
the tariff cost persists — the ISRG/ARHS pattern"). Yet **WMT does not appear in any of the 168 issuers**
(A_fades 66 / B_skews 86 / unclear 11), despite the cohort's own query set including `"IEEPA" refund`
(608 hits). **Root cause is almost certainly the fetch cap: `efts_docs_matched: 1214` but
`docs_fetched: 900`** — 314 matched documents were never read. **The generator is silently missing the
largest importer in the United States.** This is a coverage defect to fix in the cohort builder, not a
finding about Walmart.

**FINDING 2 — the "America's largest importer" framing is REFUTED as a *sensitivity* claim.**
True in absolute dollars, misleading as exposure. **Under one third of U.S. sales are imported**, per the
10-Q, because ~60% of Walmart U.S. is grocery (domestically sourced), and sourcing is already spread
across China/Vietnam/Mexico/India/Canada after six years of diversification. WMT's *rate* sensitivity to
a tariff regime is **materially lower** than the discretionary/general-merchandise cohort — DG, DLTR,
FIVE, TGT, ROST, KSS, ANF, URBN, all of which *are* in the 168.

**FINDING 3 — the §122 → §301 swap is directionally two-sided and the tape has already priced it flat.**
A clean natural experiment, since both dates fall in the post-print drift with no company news:

| Window | Event | WMT |
|---|---|---|
| 07-22 → 07-28 | **Section 122 surcharge lapses 07-24** (cost release) | 109.33 → 113.10 = **+3.45%** |
| 07-28 → 08-04 | **Section 301 forced-labor basis substitutes** (cost re-imposition) | 113.10 → 110.56 = **−2.25%** |
| **07-22 → 08-04** | **net regime swap** | 109.33 → 110.56 = **+1.13%** |

The market gave WMT a small bounce on the lapse and took most of it back on the substitution. **Net ≈ flat.**

On the basis question the principal asked — does a forced-labor §301 hit general-merchandise sourcing
harder or lighter than the flat §122 surcharge? **Harder per-unit on the affected lines, lighter in
aggregate for WMT specifically, and structurally worse for the sector.** §122 was a *flat, uniform,
statutorily time-limited* (150-day, max 15%) balance-of-payments surcharge — unsourceable-around but
self-terminating. A forced-labor §301 basis is *targeted and product/supply-chain specific* (apparel,
textiles, cotton, polysilicon, seafood), *has no sunset*, and is *sourceable-around over 6-18 months*.
For a retailer whose imported share is <1/3 and whose imported mix is already diversified away from the
targeted lines, the swap is **relatively favorable vs. the general-merchandise cohort** and roughly
**neutral in absolute terms** — which is precisely what the tape printed.

**FINDING 4 — informed caution, per the ISRG lesson: this is NOT an informational moat.**
The cohort file says it plainly: *"a management that guides 'tariffs persist' while its own 10-Q prints
the sunset date has counsel who read the statute. Positive skew on a public fact is not an informational
moat."* Walmart's guide excludes the refund because GAAP gain-contingency rules **require** exclusion —
that is compliance, not conservatism, and every analyst covering the name has read the same sentence.
The refund is real optionality (unquantified; §122 sunset and the Federal Circuit's 2026-06-11 stay
extension both cut against a fast resolution), but it is **public, priced-in-principle, and — for an EDLP
retailer whose entire cultural DNA is passing cost savings into price — likely to be reinvested into
price rather than dropped to EPS.** I carry it at ~$5/share in the bull case only.

**Net tariff verdict: this leg is a WASH. It is not the cause of the drawdown, it is not a differentiated
long catalyst, and it does not rescue a 39.5x multiple.**

## 6. MODE B — the first-principles pass (led, per doctrine), and the honesty read

Stripping the promoter framing entirely, the decisive independent questions were:

**Q1: Has WMT ever earned this multiple?** No. Verified band 22.6-26.4x through Feb-2024 on the same
5-6% growth rate it guides today. **The premise "quality at own-history discount" is factually false for
this name at this price.** This is the finding the −17.7% drawdown framing hid.

**Q2: Where does the alt-profit-stream growth actually land?** It doesn't. §4: +37% ads, +17.4%
membership, and operating margin still contracted. The flywheel is real and it is being fully consumed
by depreciation, healthcare and fuel.

**Q3: Is there a masking channel?** **No — and that is the finding.** Every adverse datum is disclosed
in the *same* release as the flattering one: the 250bps fuel drag is in the **CEO's own paragraph**; the
56bps opex deleverage is in the segment bullets; the −$1.9B FCF is in the balance-sheet box; the
reiterate-not-raise is the **headline bullet**. Per the house ELEVATE boundary (takeaway-divergence, not
datum-disclosure), **WMT grades CLEAN on honesty.** The two emphasis asymmetries in §4 (undisclosed ad
base; membership sub-component selection) are **review flags, not ELEVATE** — nothing is misstated.

**Q4: So where was the lie?** **There wasn't one — the divergence was in the market's inference, not
management's disclosure.** Walmart never claimed the margin transformation was flowing to EPS; it
guided +6% and delivered +5-6% for two straight years while the market paid 48x. **The de-rate is the
market correcting its own inference against honestly-disclosed data.**

**The encodable mechanism (this is the transferable output):**
> **Masking channel = NONE (issuer clean) × Signal channel = the market's own multiple vs. the issuer's
> own disclosed growth rate × Latency = quarters-to-years, no event trigger.**
> When the masking channel is empty, there is **no "truth comes out" catalyst** — the truth was always
> out. Such a de-rate has **no floor except valuation**, and it decays on the slow clock of successive
> in-line prints rather than resolving at a single event. **Corollary for the screen: a clean issuer's
> drawdown is a worse long than a dirty issuer's, at equal drawdown, because nothing is left to be
> revealed.** This is the anti-masking case — catalog it (per doctrine, absence of expected masking is
> itself a positive microstructure finding).

**Q5: Does the AI-BREAK@0.45 house call conflict?** No, and I will not average it away. **WMT is an AI
*adopter*, not AI-capex-levered** — automation, agentic commerce, ad-tech. The entry **does not require
the AI cycle to hold**; an AI-capex break would plausibly rotate *into* mega-cap staples, making WMT a
mild **hedge** to the household's ~$5.2M / 26% AI-complex exposure. The offset: part of WMT's own
multiple premium *is* an AI/automation-productivity narrative, so a break is a modest headwind to that
leg. **Net: non-conflicted, mildly hedging, and it does not rescue the valuation.** Hedge value is a
beta argument, not an alpha argument, and does not earn a slot at 39.5x.
