The decisive test resolves. **FY22–23 were an inventory-holding-gain spike and gross margin is the
fingerprint** — 23.75% and 19.59% against a company-stated long-term average of ~17% (management
calls both 16.9% and 16.5% "slightly below our long term average"). Excess gross profit over a 17%
norm ≈ **£26M (FY22) + £11M (FY23) = ~£37M of unrepeatable holding gains**. The screen's 4-year peak
anchor sits *on the second spike year*. **Reject the melting label.**

But the mirror claim fails too. Current GM is only **~0.3–0.5pp below normal** (£1–2M of EBIT), so
there is little normalisation left. And the FY26 "profit growth" is an accounting artifact: the
operating DB-pension charge was **£2,845k (FY25) → £576k (FY26)** (AR2026 note 20: service 421 +
admin 155). Verified by reconciling finance income — FY26 £3,450k = cash interest £2,445k + net
pension interest £1,005k (3,595−2,590); FY25 £4,435k = £3,442k + £993k — so service/admin sit above
the EBIT line and interest below it. **Pre-pension EBIT: FY26 £22.58M vs FY25 £23.04M = −2.0%, on
revenue +7.2%.** Underlying trading profit went *down*. Where it went: gross profit £62.1M → £61.4M →
£64.8M (**+4.4% in two years**) against ex-pension opex £36.0M → £38.4M → £42.3M (**+17.6%**); FY26
alone GP +5.5%, opex +10.1%. **Overhead inflation outrunning gross profit** — 24/5 depot shifts, two
new director hires, WMS rollout, Motherwell depot, NDC pre-opening — with £1.1–1.8M of NDC
depreciation still to land from FY28.

**Mid-cycle EBIT stripped of holding gains** (17.0–17.5% GM, FY26 opex, no pension charge) = £24.5–
26.5M; haircut for the structurally margin-dilutive LDT pack-timber mix (management: lower margin,
much lower overheads) and continued opex growth → **£23–25M, centre £24M** vs £22.0M reported.
~10% of upside, not a doubling.

## Ruling — SCREEN ERROR (primary) + NOT-A-DRAWDOWN (secondary). **3/10 REJECT**

The cheapness does not survive primary verification: **net cash 21.5% → 14.1%**, **EV/EBIT 7.26x →
8.28x**, once the £45M NDC stops being counted as spare cash (Note 26, £19.03M contracted). Both
screen flags are wrong in the direction that *understates* the business — "melting" is an artifact of
anchoring on a holding-gain spike, negative FCF is a one-off warehouse — but the corrections net out
to a fair price, not a cheap one. The third leg is worse than the screen knows: **the reported FY26
profit growth is a pension service-cost artifact; pre-pension EBIT fell 2% on 7% more revenue because
gross profit grew 4.4% over two years while overheads grew 17.6%.**

Nor is there a de-rate to harvest. The market never capitalised the FY22–23 spike (P/E ~5x at peak
EPS) and now pays 10.9x on normalised earnings — correct behaviour throughout. **There is no
own-history discount here.**

Why 3 and not 1: a 269-year-old debt-free family distributor with £51.2M cash, NCAV at 67% of cap, a
freehold estate at historic cost, a de-risked pension surplus, a dividend raised through the
down-cycle and covered 2.5x, a competitor just gone into administration, and a £45M strategic asset
funded without debt or dilution. The permanent-loss tail is small; it is a fine thing to own forever
at the right price. **Not ownable here**: negative computed edge, no independent coverage, no
catalyst, 203bp round trip, close-only quote, $21.8k/day cap.

**No entry band (score < 4).** If the principal wants it anyway as a fairly-paid-risk hold-forever
line, the only defensible zone is bear/NCAV: **GTC limit ≤ 800p**, quarter-size (≤0.19% of $3.3M),
never a market order into a blind touch.
