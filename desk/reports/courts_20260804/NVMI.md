### B2. So is this the HUBS/CTSH/SAP winner pattern? **NO — and this is the ruling.**
By the letter of the contract's test this is a textbook DE-RATE: estimates up, multiple down.
**But the winner pattern does not transfer to a cyclical at the top of the largest capex cycle on record.**

In subscription software, stable forward estimates are evidence of *durability* — the revenue is contracted.
In semi-cap, **guidance is a lagging indicator**: the cycle always turns *after* the last strong guide, because
the order book converts on a 2–4 quarter delay and cancellations show up only once the customer's own capex
plan is cut. Applying "estimates stable ⇒ derailment refuted" to NVMI is a category error. The market is not
disputing the *level* of 2026 estimates; it is repricing their **durability into 2027**. It is right to.

**Encode the lesson:** *the de-rate-vs-derailment test is not diagnostic for cyclicals.* Estimate stability
must be weighted by revenue contractedness, not taken at face value.
