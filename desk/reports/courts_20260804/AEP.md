## 4. Mode B: the register, the probate, and what is really being priced

AR25 Directors' Report — only **two** holders above 3% exist:

| Holder | Shares (pre-split) | % of voting rights |
|---|---|---|
| **Genton International Limited** | 20,247,814 (static) | **51.3% (FY24) → 52.2% (31-Dec-25) → 52.6% (31-Mar-26)** |
| Nokia Bell Pensioenfonds OFP | 6,459,954 | 16.8% → **15.99%** (TR-1, 24-Jun-26, reducing) |

AR25 Note 25, verbatim: *"The ultimate beneficial shareholders of Genton International Limited are
vested in the **estates of Madam Lim Siew Kim** with the **application for probate in progress**."*
Genton plus deemed interests = **53.0%**. Genton's share count has not moved; its percentage rises
purely because buybacks into treasury shrink the voting base.

**This is the answer to the perennial minority-treatment question, and it is not the one the
question expects.** The historic hoard was a living controlling shareholder's preference. She died;
the estate is in probate; and within roughly eighteen months the company re-based its dividend more
than fivefold, ran two buybacks, split the stock, rebranded, spent $171m of the hoard on
acquisitions and put its Kalimantan subsidiary up for an Indonesian IPO. **The +196% is the market
pricing a controlling stake that has become mobile.**

**The creeping-control mechanic, quantified and then honestly bounded.** Buybacks into treasury do
ratchet Genton up (52.93% on today's voting count). But to reach the 75% special-resolution
threshold Genton would need 112.6m shares retired — **29.4% of the voting float, ~£199m, versus an
£8m/year programme: about 25 years.** A buyback-driven squeeze-out is **not** the live risk, and I
will not dress it as one.

The live structure is simpler and sharper: **Genton 53.0% + Nokia Bell 15.99% = 68.9%, leaving a
31.1% genuine public float.** Nokia Bell is a legacy Belgian pension fund that is already selling. A
take-private that Nokia accepts starts at ~69% and needs very little more. Combined with an estate in
probate — estates need liquidity, and heirs need a divisible asset — **the modal terminal outcome for
this equity is a Genton-side offer, not a perpetual public compounder.** That is an option, and it is
plausibly already in the 1.54x P/B.

**Related-party check — clean on the deals.** Both 2026 acquisitions are third-party: Pinago from
unnamed "identified shareholders" of an IDX-listed company; PT JJU from **CB Industrial Product
Holding Berhad**, a listed Malaysian company. Neither RNS names the Lim estate, Genton or any
director, and neither is flagged as a related-party transaction. The only disclosed Lim-related
dealing is an office lease with **Infra Sari Sdn Bhd** at **$178,192** a year (FY24: $166,800) —
trivial. Related-party noise on this name is, on the FY25 evidence, **historic rather than current**.

**Two governance gaps, stated as gaps.** (a) Neither the buyback RNS nor the entire FY25 Annual
Report contains a single reference to the Takeover Code, Rule 9, a concert party or a whitewash,
despite a 52.6%-and-rising controller being ratcheted upward by company-funded purchases. Above 50%
no mandatory offer is triggered, so this is probably correct — but it is undisclosed rather than
addressed. (b) Directors hold essentially nothing: one director holds 3,000 shares, every other
director including the CEO holds **nil**, and AR25 states *"There is no requirement for Directors to
hold shares in our Company."*

## 5. Mode B: estate age and replanting — the palm equivalent of mine life, and it is under-funded

This is the item the task asked for, and it is where AEP's disclosure is weakest.

**What is disclosed (AR25, p.4):** *"AVERAGE AGE OF OUR PALM: 14"* and a four-slice donut —
**Old 22% / Prime 38% / Young 26% / Immature 14%** — with **no hectares and no bracket definitions
anywhere in the document.** The word "Prime" appears exactly once in the entire annual report. There
is **no hectares-by-age-band table**. For a plantation company, the age profile *is* the reserve
statement; publishing it as an undefined percentage donut is the same defect the THX court found in
a miner that would not state remaining mine life.

**Estate areas at 31-Dec-25 (AR25 p.7), verified:** total land 88,362 ha; planted oil palm 64,982 ha
ex-Plasma (69,324 ha incl. Plasma); **mature 56,224 ha, immature 8,758 ha**; 7 mills, 400 t/hr
combined. Indonesia 81,994 ha across North Sumatra / Bengkulu / Riau / Bangka / Central Kalimantan;
Malaysia (Terengganu) 6,368 ha.

**The replanting arithmetic:**

| | ha/yr |
|---|---|
| Steady state to hold the age profile flat (64,982 ha ÷ 25-yr cycle) | **2,599** |
| Actual 2023 / 2024 / 2025 | 1,301 / 1,700 / **2,440** |
| Realised 4-year average (6,500 ha cumulative since 2022 ÷ 4) | **1,625 — 63% of steady state** |
| Stated programme, 10,000 ha over 2026-2030 | **2,000 — 77% of steady state** |
| Identified for 2026 | 2,800 |

**Finding: AEP has been replanting at roughly two-thirds of the rate needed to hold its own age
profile flat, and its stated five-year programme still runs below steady state.** It is falling
behind more slowly, not catching up. The consequence is already on the tape, in the company's own
numbers:

- **Mature area shrank**, 57.2k ha → 56.2k ha (−1.7%) in 2025.
- **Own FFB production fell 2.7%** in the five months to 31-May-2026 (421,900 vs 433,500 mt),
  attributed by the company to *"lower yields in North Sumatra and Riau, where a replanting
  programme on ageing palms and the suspension of fertiliser application in replanting areas reduced
  output."* Replanting suppresses output for 3–4 years, so catching up costs production.
- **The Malaysian estate is terminal:** average palm age *"over 25 years"*, FFB yield **4.8 mt/ha**
  against a group average of 19.2 and a North Sumatra 24.1.

**And the growth is bought, not grown.** In 2025, external FFB purchased (1,170k mt, +18.3%)
**exceeded own FFB production (1,081k mt) for the first time**. Third-party FFB milling is a
low-margin toll activity, not plantation economics. The 5M-2026 print continues the mix shift: own
−2.7%, external +6.5%, CPO production −1.8%, ex-mill price −2.2%. Strip Pinago out and the core
business is running roughly 4% behind last year on both volume and price.

Pinago itself is the honest offset — 15,400 ha at an average palm age of ~11–12 years (the RNS says
12 in one paragraph and 11 in the next), a 22.7% extraction rate against AEP's 19.8%, and +25% to
group CPO. It is a genuinely good asset that materially *younger*-weights the estate. It was also
paid for with the entire cash cushion.

## 6. The Indonesian export-levy regime — the RNS is true and incomplete

**RNS 20-May-2026, "Statement Re Indonesian Government Export Proposals"** (the day the shares fell
21.1% on 4.24m shares, 4x normal volume). Verbatim core: *"AEP sells all of its CPO production
domestically to refineries in Indonesia and does not export directly… Accordingly, the proposed
changes are not expected to have a **direct** impact on AEP, although there may be **indirect
effects through pricing adjustments** arising from any new arrangements."* **No quantification of any
kind.** The mechanism was only named in the 15-Jun AGM update: centralisation of strategic commodity
exports under **PT Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI)**, transition period running to **full
implementation from 1 January 2027**, DSI as intermediary/supervisor rather than trader.

**Where the framing understates.** AEP discloses both halves of its own price wedge: FY25 average CPO
**CIF Rotterdam $1,151/mt** versus AEP realised **ex-mill $853/mt**. That is a **$298/mt, 25.9%
wedge**, and it is composed of Indonesian export duty, the export levy, freight and refiner margin —
i.e. it is set primarily by the policy AEP says does not directly affect it. **Every 1 percentage
point of that wedge is ~$4.9m of operating profit, 4.4% of FY25 EBIT.** A 3pp levy increase is ~13%
of EBIT.

The CEO's AGM outlook cites *"stronger domestic demand from the B50 biodiesel programme"* as a
support for prices. Indonesia's biodiesel mandates are funded by the CPO export levy — the same
instrument that widens the wedge. **The company presents the demand side of that policy and not the
cost side.** Not a misstatement; a one-sided emphasis on the single largest determinant of its
realised price. Classified **review-flag, not masking** — the numbers needed to compute it are both
printed in the same report.

**Tape confirmation of a genuine dislocation, though.** Since 19-May-26 (the day before the shock):
AEP **+0.8%**, MP Evans +21.3%, First Resources +16.5%, LonSum +14.6%, SD Guthrie +9.7%. AEP has
lagged the complex by **15–20pp on a policy it credibly says does not reach it**. That lag is the
only genuinely attractive feature of the current price, and it is not big enough.

## 8. Four-idea frame

- **The bull idea:** a debt-free planter at 6.3x EV/EBIT that has just bought 25% more CPO
  production at 9x earnings using cash that earned nothing, is buying back stock, yields 3.6% with
  **0% UK dividend withholding** for a US holder, quotes at a **57bp touch**, and has an IDX listing
  of its Kalimantan arm targeted for Q4-2026 that would put a third-party price on ~35% of its
  planted area.
- **The bear idea:** the cheapness is a peak-commodity artifact. CPO sits at the 90th percentile of
  twenty years, own FFB is shrinking, replanting has run at 63% of steady state for four years, and
  the reported multiple is struck on a cash pile that has since been spent.
- **The idea nobody is pricing:** the **probate**. A 53% controlling stake sitting in the estate of a
  deceased shareholder, with a 16% legacy pension fund already selling alongside it, is the whole
  terminal-value question — and it is a *binary corporate* outcome, not a palm-oil one. Whoever owns
  this equity owns a commodity business as a rider on an estate settlement.
- **The idea that would change my mind:** an age-profile table in hectares showing the "Old 22%"
  slice is smaller than the 22-year-plus cohort I am inferring, alongside a replanting run-rate
  above 2,600 ha/yr. Both are knowable and neither is published.

## 11. Does ANY re-entry band exist? Yes — and it is ~30% below the market

The band must be struck where the commodity risk is *paid for* rather than assumed away. Two
independent anchors converge:

1. **Per-hectare:** buy at **≤$7,000/ha** of pro-forma EV versus the $10,714/ha AEP itself just
   paid. On 80,382 ha that is ~$563m EV, +$75m net cash = $638m cap = **~126p**.
2. **Peak-commodity haircut:** apply the bear-case EBIT ($70m) at a normal 6.5x and demand no
   premium for the corporate option ⇒ **~105–125p**.

**Documented re-court band: 118–132p** (≈$7,000–7,800/ha), i.e. **26–33% below the live price**, and
that is roughly where the stock actually traded as recently as December 2025 (136.97p) — this is not
a fantasy level. **No tranche is authorised at 3/10**; this is a WATCH trigger, not an order.

**Additional re-court triggers, any of which changes the analysis independently of price:**
- An age-profile table **in hectares** published at the interims or in AR26.
- Replanting run-rate above **2,600 ha/yr** sustained.
- The Kalimantan IPO **priced**, giving a third-party mark.
- A firm offer or Rule 2.4 announcement from Genton / the Lim estate (that becomes a special-sit
  court, not a value court).
- CPO below **$900/mt** CIF Rotterdam with the equity not yet adjusted.
