## 0. One-paragraph ruling

The screen premise — "perennial cheap cyber laggard, 88% gross margin, massively FCF-generative
installed base, -46% off its own high, new-CEO refresh + Israel discount" — is **factually true and
analytically wrong**. The -46% is not the market finally noticing that growth was always slow. Slow
growth was never the problem. The problem is that **Check Point's operating income has been flat-to-
down for six consecutive years while revenue grew 32%**, and in 2026 the revenue line finally joined
the operating line. The decisive fact is competitive, and it is not ambiguous: in the June-2026
quarter Check Point's **product revenue fell 14.0%** and its **maintenance annuity fell 3.3% — its
first decline** — while Fortinet, the closest structural comparable, grew total revenue **+25.6%** in
the identical calendar quarter and Palo Alto grew **+31.2%**. The industry is in a firewall refresh
up-cycle. Check Point is losing it. The de-rate is the market pricing that correctly, twice, in
violent single-day gaps (-19.6% on 04-30, -8.2% on 07-30), each attached to a guidance reduction.
Separately, the Q2-2026 release carries a **takeaway-vs-data divergence worth flagging on its own**
(Section 5). Probability-weighted fair value lands at **$125** against a $122.97 spot — **+1.8pp of
edge, i.e. none**. This is an exclusion, not an opportunity, and the exclusion is the product.

the annuity does not recover without a product re-acceleration. **Product revenue is -14%.** This
is the mechanical signature of installed-base erosion, not of a soft quarter.

Second, note that the +1.3% total is **inorganic-assisted**: Lakera (Q4-2025, ~$190M), Cyata +
Cyclops (Q1-2026, ~$92M) and Veriti are all inside the 2026 numerator. **Organic revenue growth in
Q2-2026 is approximately zero or slightly negative.**

## 5. Honesty finding — takeaway-vs-data divergence (ELEVATE)

Nothing here is hidden. Every number below is in the company's own tables. What diverges is the
**marketed takeaway**, and it diverges through three separate channels at once.

### 5.1 Selective retirement of the two metrics that turned

| Metric | Q2-2025 release | Q4-2025 release | Q1-2026 release | Q2-2026 release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calculated Billings** | ✔ $642M, +4% | ✔ **led the highlights** — $1,039M, +8%; FY $2,906M, +9% | ✘ absent | ✘ absent |
| **Products & Licenses** | ✔ $132M, +12% | — | ✘ absent | ✘ absent |
| **RPO** | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ ($2.6B, +7%) |

Calculated Billings was the *lead bullet* of the Q4-2025 release. It vanished from the highlights in
the exact quarter it turned. Reconstructing it from the balance sheets (Billings = Revenue + Δ total
deferred revenue), using figures from the Q2-2025 and Q2-2026 6-K balance sheets:

- Total deferred revenue: 2024-12-31 $2,000.3M → 2025-06-30 $1,892.7M ⇒ **1H-2025 billings
  = 1,303.0 − 107.6 = $1,195.4M**
- Total deferred revenue: 2025-12-31 $2,180.4M → 2026-06-30 $2,025.2M ⇒ **1H-2026 billings
  = 1,342.0 − 155.2 = $1,186.8M**
- **1H-2026 calculated billings: -0.7% YoY**, against FY2025's +9%. A ~10pp swing.

*(Caveat, stated: acquired deferred revenue is written down in purchase accounting and FX moves this,
so treat the -0.7% as approximate. The direction and the ~10pp swing are not in doubt. The company
retired the metric rather than print it — that is the finding, and it is the only reason I had to
compute it.)*

Product revenue was a highlight bullet at +12% and is absent at -14%. **Both retirements are in the
same direction. The one metric retained, RPO, is the one that still reads positive.**

### 5.2 Operating income is being carried by a brand-new government subsidy

A new Israeli R&D incentive law was **enacted 2026-03-30**, and Check Point booked it as a *reduction
of R&D expense*: **$28M in Q2-2026, $55M in 1H-2026** (footnote to the Q2 income statement).

- Non-GAAP operating income Q2-26: **$259.7M as reported (-4.2% YoY)** → **$231.7M ex-grant
  (-14.5% YoY)**.
- Non-GAAP operating margin: **38.6% as reported** → **34.4% ex-grant**, versus **40.8%** a year ago.

The grant is disclosed in a footnote. It is **not** carved out of the non-GAAP operating income the
company markets at "39 percent of total revenues." It is now **~11% of non-GAAP operating income**
and it is a politically revocable sovereign subsidy — a dependency the multiple should discount, not
capitalize.

### 5.3 The headline "+8% EPS growth" contains no operating growth at all

Q2-2026 non-GAAP EPS $2.55 vs $2.37 = **+7.6%**. Decompose it:

| Component | Q2-26 | Q2-25 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-GAAP **net income** | $263.5M | $261.4M | **+0.8%** |
| Diluted shares | 103.5M | 110.4M | **-6.3%** |
| ⇒ EPS | $2.55 | $2.37 | +7.6% |

**Essentially 100% of the EPS growth is the buyback.** And the residual +0.8% of net income growth is
itself entirely non-operating: the **$28M R&D grant** (~$0.27/share) plus **financial income of $41.5M
vs $28.5M (+$13.0M)** — which is interest earned on the $2B of *borrowed* convert proceeds. Strip
both and non-GAAP net income is roughly **$222M vs $261M, about -15%**.

So the marketed construction is: *EPS grows because a government subsidy props up operating income,
interest on borrowed money props up net income, and borrowed money buys back the shares.*

### 5.4 The verdict on honesty

Against the CEO's Q2 quote — *"We delivered second quarter results in line with our expectations
while strengthening our foundation for sustainable growth"* — the same document's tables show:
revenue +1.3% (organic ~0), product revenue -14%, the maintenance annuity in its first-ever decline,
computed billings -0.7%, operating income -14.5% ex-grant, and adjusted FCF margin cut from 39.4% to

**Grade: ELEVATE on takeaway divergence.** This is not a disclosure violation — every datum is in the

## 6. Israel domicile — the three items the queue asked to be stated

1. **PFIC: NOT a PFIC. Confirmed, and the reasoning is non-obvious, so it is recorded here.**
   On a *book* basis the asset test looks alarming — cash + marketable securities of $4,203M against
   total assets of $7,512M is **56% passive**, above the 50% threshold. But a publicly traded foreign
   corporation applies the asset test on the **fair market value** of its assets, under which goodwill
   and going-concern value are active assets. FMV of assets ≈ market cap $12.52B + liabilities $4.78B
   = **$17.3B**; passive assets $4.20B = **24%**. The income test is clean by a wide margin: ~$2.78B
   of active revenue against ~$164M/yr of financial income = **~6% passive**. No PFIC, no QEF or
   mark-to-market election needed, no Form 8621. Clean for the taxable book.
2. **Withholding tax: not currently binding, but state it.** Check Point pays **no dividend and never
   has**; all shareholder return is buyback. If a dividend were ever initiated, distributions out of
   Preferred / Technological Preferred Enterprise income carry Israeli withholding of **15% or 20%**
   (20-F, Taxation), reducible under the US-Israel treaty, and creditable to a US holder via Form
   1116 — a real friction in a taxable account but a second-order one. **The all-buyback policy is
   in fact the tax-efficient structure for this household**; a dividend initiation would be a mild
   negative, not a positive.
3. **Geopolitical tail: real, unhedgeable, low-probability, and *not* the reason to pass.** HQ Tel
   Aviv, R&D concentrated in Israel, material reserve-duty exposure in any regional escalation. The
   company traded through October 2023 without operational impairment, so the base rate of *permanent*
   damage is low. The newer and more specific sovereign exposure is the one flagged in §5.2: the
   **March-2026 Israeli R&D credit is now ~11% of non-GAAP operating income** and is a budget line
   subject to political revision. **Size any position for the tail; do not underwrite the tail as the
   thesis.** In this court it is not load-bearing — the name fails on competitive grounds long before
   geopolitics matters.

## 7. AI-complex conflict test (contract requires an explicit answer)

The queue flags `ai_complex: false`. **Confirmed, and it is the one genuinely attractive property of
this asset.**

- **Does an entry REQUIRE the AI-capex cycle holding? NO.** Check Point sells network security to
  enterprise IT budgets. It has no datacenter capex leverage, no GPU supply chain, no hyperscaler
  revenue concentration. Its "AI" content is marketing positioning (AI Firewall, AI Defense plane)
  and small tuck-ins (Lakera, Cyata). Revenue is not levered to AI capex in either direction.
- **Does the de-rate already price the AI break? Not applicable, and that is the point.** The -47.5%
  is idiosyncratic and competitive. Against the frozen house call **AI-BREAK | 2027-12-31 @ 0.45**
  and the household's ~$5.2M / 26% AI-complex exposure, CHKP is one of the few large-cap technology
  assets whose drawdown is **uncorrelated with that call**. If the AI cycle breaks, CHKP is roughly
  unaffected on fundamentals.
- **This does not rescue it.** An AI-cycle-uncorrelated business that is losing share and whose
  operating income has fallen for six years is still a business losing share. Diversification value
  is not a reason to own a derailing franchise; it is a reason to look for an *undamaged*
  AI-uncorrelated asset instead. **I am recording this explicitly rather than averaging it away:
  the AI-hedge property is real and it moved the score by roughly half a point, no more.**
