## Test 1 — capitalised development: the brief's headline hypothesis is REFUTED

1. **The FCF already deducts it.** `capex_total` £28.73m vs `capex_ppe` £0.26m — ~£28.5m of the
   capex line *is* the intangible spend, so the 13.3% FCF yield is struck **after** development
   cash. Independent corroboration: the company's own **£18.8m FY26 cash generation** (cash 42.5→44.0
   while spending £15.4m on buybacks) is an after-everything, after-lease figure landing within ~£2m.
2. **Over the cycle it charged MORE than it capitalised.** AR2025 note 11: FY24+FY25 additions
   £57.4m vs amortisation+impairment £70.2m — a net **£12.8m headwind**. Cumulative cost £252.97m vs
   accumulated amortisation/impairment £211.0m: **83% of everything ever capitalised is written off**;
   NBV £42.0m = 40% of one year's revenue. A clean intangibles balance sheet, not a bloated one.
3. **EBIT flattery is real but collapsing.** FY25 capitalised £28.6m vs amortised £20.1m = +£8.4m on
   £12.7m IFRS operating profit; **H1 FY26 £13.7m vs £12.2m = +£1.5m on £7.8m**, as the intangible
   base flattened (£35.7m→£42.0m→£43.3m). Honest reading: **the FCF yield is the trustworthy metric
   and EV/EBIT is the flattered one — the reverse of the usual assumption.**

**Amortisation basis is conservative and explains the durability.** AR2025 p.54: game developments
**straight-line over 1–4 years** (technology 1–3), *not* units-of-revenue. Planet Zoo (5m+ units,
**£143m lifetime revenue / £85m cash profit** to 01-Sep-2025, PDLC 43% of it) is fully amortised by
year four and then feeds a lengthening catalogue at zero amortisation cost.

**Write-offs — real, two years behind, de-risked by the auditor.** Note 11: **FY24 impairment
£16.93m** (game developments £15.50m + IP licences £1.43m; title unnamed, "an underperforming
title"). **FY25 £nil; H1 FY26 £nil.** EY **removed** the intangibles-impairment Key Audit Matter in
FY25 *"due to the extent of headroom available which has reduced the risk of impairment."*

## Test 3 — the reset, and whether the durability rescue was deserved

**The reset is structural, not one release.** At comparable revenue: FY24 £89.3m → operating **loss
£28.4m**; FY26 £104.8m → **+£19.0m adjusted**. IFRS opex −42% to £54.6m *and* **adjusted opex −13%
to £54.0m** — the cash-cost line moved, not just the non-cash one. Frontier Foundry closed,
headcount cut. "Adjusted" is honest: H1 FY26 IFRS £7.8m vs adjusted £9.7m — a £1.9m share-based-
payment gap, so it does **not** add back the £12.2m of amortisation.

**Rescue verdict: DESERVED here — but the rule is weakly discriminating.** FDEV was one of the 9
rescued by the untested cash-generation condition. It passes trivially (FY24: £28.4m operating loss,
+£1.2m net cash movement) — but *"generated cash on both sides of the EBIT cross" is nearly
automatic for a capitalise-and-amortise business*, because the loss side is manufactured by
amortising and impairing cash spent in prior years. The corroboration came from revenue, margin and
an independent company-stated cash line, **not** from the rule. **Recommend gating the rule to
exclude names whose loss year is dominated by amortisation/impairment of previously capitalised
spend** — there it carries almost no information.
