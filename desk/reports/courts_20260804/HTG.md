**Why the screen says cheap and the market does not:** tax-plus-minorities take ~36% of adjusted PBT, so 10.3x EV/EBIT becomes a 17.7x P/E. EV/EBIT cheapness on a high-tax business systematically overstates equity cheapness. And a business earning **10% ROCE against a ~10% cost of capital is worth about book** — 1.00x P/B is *correct*, not a discount; the >15% ROCE target is a **2030** goal, and five-year book value has gone nowhere ($871.3m 2021 → $885.3m 2025, with the 2024 impairment consuming two years of profit).

**The 11% FCF yield is a shrinking-backlog artifact.** FY25 FCF $96.6m included an **$18.0m working-capital inflow** (FY24 $53.3m), attributed in the AR to unwinding the bank-acceptance drafts on the completed KOC orders, plus **$9.9m of disposal proceeds** (Dutch property) — ex-disposals 9.9%. Management's own through-cycle target is **≥50% EBITDA→FCF ≈ $75m ≈ 8.5%**. **The cash yield and the growth case are mutually exclusive:** rebuilding the order book consumes the working capital that produced the yield — already visible in the H1-26 swing to net borrowings.
