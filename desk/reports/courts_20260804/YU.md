## The ruling in one paragraph
Every one of the three fields that put this name on the shelf is wrong in the same direction.
EV/EBIT 3.2x is really **6.2x** (the vendor added the recurring bad-debt charge back to EBIT).
Net cash of +32% of market cap is really **+7%** (the filer itself says £74m of the £106m cash is
money owed to Ofgem and HMRC). FCF yield 9.9% is really **~4%** (half the operating cash inflow is
the growth in the amount owed to the regulator, and the company guides that it reverses in 2026).
On top of that the shares are **8% below a six-month-old 52-week high**, not in a drawdown at all.
The underlying business is real and growing; it is simply not a 3x-EBIT bargain, and it carries an
unpriced honesty prior.

## The structural fact that governs the tail
The **Shell Energy Europe Trading Agreement** (Feb-2024, extended 21-May-2026 to **2032**) is why Yü
posts no cash collateral and why the 2024 cash flow released £49.8m. In exchange Shell holds a
**fixed and floating charge over Yü Energy Holding and Yü Energy Retail** — the entire operating
business — plus a parent guarantee, with **covenants on profitability, net worth and liquidity**.
The accounts state that in "extreme and limited circumstances" this "could lead to a claim on some
or all of the assets of the Group." So: a gross-margin/bad-debt shock does not just cut earnings,
it trips a covenant, and a covenant trip re-imposes cash collateral against a **£395.6m** forward
book on a company whose genuinely free cash is **£32m**. Equity is structurally subordinated. This
is the mechanism that produced 30+ UK supplier failures in 2021-22, and it is not remotely
compensated at 8.3x earnings.
