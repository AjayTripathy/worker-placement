## Q1 — the £60.0m pension surplus stripped, and whether it is recoverable
CONFIRMED from AR2025 Note 21 + FY25 Financial Review: scheme closed to accrual (2009), 2,875 retirees + 2,296 deferred, duration 12.0 yrs, **DBO £491.0m, assets £551.0m, recognised asset £60.0m** (FY24 £54.9m), retirement benefit obligations nil.

- **Reported P/B = £562.8m ÷ £258.2m = 2.18x** (matches screen 2.185).
- **Ex-surplus P/B = £562.8m ÷ £198.2m = 2.84x** — a 30% deterioration.
- Crediting the surplus at 75% (net of the UK 25% authorised-surplus-payments charge): **2.31x**.

**IFRIC 14 basis: REFUND, no asset ceiling applied.** Policy note and Note 21 both state, verbatim: *"any surplus on the Costain Pension Scheme would be recoverable by way of a refund, as the Group has the unconditional right to any surplus once all the obligations of the Scheme have been settled."* Recognition is therefore correct — but the condition is **wind-up**. Settling means buying out a £491m obligation discounted at **5.45% (corporate bond)**; insurer pricing sits materially above an IAS-19 corporate-bond measurement, and the company commits only to "continue to review options for restructuring the Scheme with the Trustee." At a 5–10% buy-out premium, £551m of assets covers £515–540m of liability, leaving **£11–36m, then taxed 25% on refund** ⇒ realisable shareholder value plausibly **£0–27m against £60.0m carried**. It is also unstable: **+1yr life expectancy costs £19.5m of surplus, −0.25% on the discount rate costs £11.9m** (Note 21 sensitivities) — one assumption erases a third. And **scheme assets £551m ≈ 98% of the equity market cap**, run through a **63% LDI portfolio (£345.1m) levered so "the equivalent of 95% of the value of the assets is sensitive to changes in interest rates"** — a Sept-2022-shaped tail beside a £563m company.

**Answer: real, correctly recognised, and mostly not shareholder value.** Neither 2.18x nor 2.84x is cheap, so this does not create the ranking — the screen ranked COST on net cash and FCF yield, not book. Which moves the question to the cash.

## Score 3/10 — REJECT
The screen is not wrong about the business: Costain is a genuinely improved, cash-generative UK infrastructure services business with a growing end market, a de-risked contract mix, no debt, a clean provisions book and a newly unlocked capital-return policy. The **frame** is wrong — there is no de-rate here to buy. Two screen defects fire and both cut the same way: the £60.0m surplus flatters P/B by 30%, and period-end net cash overstates the through-year balance by 24% on the filer's own numbers. Neither creates the cheapness; both remove what little cushion the multiple had.
**No entry band, no tranche, no size.** Revisit only on a **≥30% drawdown to ~148p** caused by a market/sector de-rate rather than a contract failure — at which price the FY27 step change would be free.

**Kills (if ever carried):** any onerous- or contract-loss provision appearing; average month-end net cash <~£120m or FY26 year-end materially below the guided £175m; forward work <£6.5bn; FY27 step-change deferred again or the >5% margin ambition cut; a buy-out priced at or below the IAS-19 asset (confirming the £60m is air); material HS2 or AMP8 cancellation.

**Freezable — COST | 2026-08-13 (HY26 results):** H1-26 adjusted operating margin lands **at or below 4.2%** (vs 4.5% FY25), consistent with the ~4.0% full-year H2-weighted guide. **our_p = 0.78.** Companion, same date: H1-26 net cash prints below the FY25 year-end £189.3m (H1-25 was £144.9m) — **our_p = 0.92.**

**Top UNVERIFIABLE:** whether the £60.0m IAS-19 surplus survives a buy-out. No insurer quotation disclosed; the gap between a 5.45% corporate-bond discount rate and insurer pricing is the entire question. Treat the surplus as £0–27m of shareholder value until it is priced.
