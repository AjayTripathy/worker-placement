## The AI conflict, courted as a conflict (house call AI-BREAK|2027-12-31 @ 0.45)
FDM itself escalated the risk: *"Rapid technological change, including AI, is changing client needs
and the Board recognises that the likelihood of this risk emerging is currently higher than in recent
years."* Sector channel is live (SThree named AI as a hiring headwind).

**But substitution is not yet visible in the two metrics that would show it.** Utilisation never fell
(92.8/92.9/92.7/93.6) and revenue per consultant is flat (~£78-80k/yr, FY24 and FY25). Pricing and
deployment intensity held; only volume collapsed. The decline began in **2023** with the rate shock,
and is concentrated in N. America (−50%) and EMEA (−32%) against a UK down only 15% — a geographic
signature that reads as a US bank capex cycle, not a technology.

**Which side does the entry sit on?** Two opposing exposures, not to be averaged:
- **Short AI-substitution** — more AI = fewer entry-level bodies; a break deflates the terminal-value
  fear. This leg drives the **multiple**.
- **Long enterprise IT budgets** — clients are banks/insurers, FDM now sells "AI-enabled talent," and
  10% of 2026 exec bonus is tied to *consultants deployed on AI projects*. A capex break freezes the
  budgets it sells into. This leg drives **earnings**.

Sharpest observation: **FDM already lived through "AI adoption continues" and it cost 76% of EBIT** —
record enterprise tech spend went to AI infrastructure rather than to people.

**Statement of side:** the entry does **not** require the cycle holding, and FDM is one of very few
names whose first-order channel is *helped* by a break — real diversification against a household
already carrying ~$5.2M / 26% AI-complex exposure. **It is not a hedge**; do not size as insurance.

**Priced?** EV £93.2M ex-lease vs peak EBIT £55.0M = **1.7x peak** — heavy permanent impairment IS
priced. Vs TTM adjusted EBIT £10.1M = 9.2x — zero recovery priced. Embedded case: "£10M forever."
