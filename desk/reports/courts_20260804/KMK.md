## 2. THE HEADLINE IS A TTM SPLICE ARTIFACT — "23% FCF yield at book" REFUTED on both legs

The shelf's numbers are **arithmetically correct and economically meaningless**. The window is a
trailing twelve months to **31-Oct-2025**, which is a *half-year balance-sheet date* for an
**April year-end** company. Reconstructed from the primary statements:

**TTM = FY2025 (y/e 30-Apr-2025) + H1 FY2026 (6m to 31-Oct-2025) − H1 FY2025**

| £M | | | | = TTM | screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 26.506 | + 15.0 | − 3.7 | **37.81** | 37.843 ✓ |
| Operating profit | 4.737 | + 3.2 | − (−4.8) | **12.74** | 12.725 ✓ |

The arithmetic ties. **The window does two things at once:** it includes *two consecutive halves
that both booked the same one-off licence*, and it **deletes the −£4.8M half-year**. Roughly half
of the celebrated "flip from −£1.9M to +£12.7M" is simply the removal of six bad months.

### What actually produced the profit: one named contract

**Siemens Medical Solutions USA ("Siemens Healthineers") Enablement Agreement**, signed
30-Jan-2025 — **$37.5M over four years** to enable CZT detector production for SPECT. **$25.0M
received in FY2025** (RNS 17-Feb-2025), **$5.0M** more post year-end (RNS 28-Jul-2025). Revenue
recognised: **£16.5M in FY2025** and **£8.3M in H1 FY2026**.

**The cost line is the tell.** FY2025 cost of sales *fell* from £8,693k to £5,075k while revenue
rose 37%. Gross margin went **55.2% → 80.9%**. A CZT hardware manufacturer does not earn 81% gross
margin. A licence fee does.

**Strip Siemens and FY2025 revenue nearly halved:**

| £M | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced Imaging | 9.0 | 20.3 (~16.5 of it Siemens) |
| CBRN Detection | 10.4 | **6.2** |
| **Ex-Siemens total** | **19.4** | **~10.0** |

### And the company has already told the market the run-rate

**FY2026 Trading Update, 12-May-2026:** *"The Company understands FY26 consensus estimates to be
revenues of **£27.2 million** and PBT of **£2.15 million**"* — in line. Against the screen's
£37.8M / £12.7M. **Revenue ~28% lower; profit ~6x lower.**

Implied **H2 FY2026 PBT ≈ −£0.95M** on ~£12.2M of revenue *(my arithmetic on the company's own

## 4. THE DURABILITY-WATCH RESCUE — REFUTED

The shelf demoted 31 names for earnings durability and **rescued KMK**, on the stated ground that
it *"generated cash on both sides of the EBIT cross"* (`durability_watch: true`,
`cash_generative_2y: true`).

**It did not.** Statutory operating cash flow:

| £'000 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Net cash from operating activities | **(2,802)** | **15,901** |

FY2024 OCF was **negative £2.8M**. The rescue can only have been computed on FY2025 **plus** H1
FY2026 — i.e. **the two periods that both contain Siemens.** The durability test was run on the
artifact it was supposed to catch. *This is an encodable generator bug: the `cash_generative_2y`
guard must span the same lookback as the EBIT history it is rescuing, or it will always confirm
whatever the TTM window already says.*

### Was the £12.65M of "FCF" even free cash? Real, but non-recurring, and already spent

FY2025 OCF of £15,901k decomposes (AR2025 note 30) as pre-working-capital operating cash flow of
£10,307k **plus a £6,547k receivables release** — the unwind of a receivable that had *ballooned
£7,454k the prior year*. And the pre-working-capital £10.3M is itself carried by the Siemens
receipt.

**Where the cash went:** over the TTM window, OCF ~£17.45M − capex £4.80M − interest £1.29M −
**net debt repayment ~£9.79M** ≈ nil retained. The company repaid Polymer N2 Ltd's £5.5M secured
term loan plus £5.9M of short-term facilities in Feb-2025 — *"Total repayment inclusive of interest
and fees of the short-term loans was £12.9m"* (CFO Review, AR p.10). **Cash on the balance sheet at
31-Oct-2025: £1.248M.** That is the whole reconciliation: a finite contract was collected and
handed to lenders.

**The screen's capex "mismatch" resolved.** Screen shows capex £4.8M but PPE £342k + intangibles
£179k. The missing **£4,279k is capitalised development costs** (yfinance `Capital Expenditure
Reported = −4,279`, dropped by the screen). Kromek's dominant capex line is capitalised R&D, and
omitting it is what makes the FCF look free.

## 10. RULING — **PASS 1/10**, overdetermined

Not a de-rate and not a derailment: **the cheapness never existed.** There is no multiple to
compress. The 4.6x EV/EBIT and 23% FCF yield are a twelve-month window that spliced a licence fee
into a hardware company's P&L and deleted its worst half-year; the company's own guidance for the
period *after* that window is **£27.2M revenue and £2.15M PBT**, or **~27x EV/PBT**.

Three independent grounds, each individually sufficient:
1. **Friction.** 859bp touch, £85k median day, a 0.75% position exceeds the daily order cap, and a
   £9,796 director purchase moves the price 16%.
2. **The headline is an artifact**, refuted against the company's own guidance.
3. **"At book" is refuted** — £32.2M of the £56.9M equity is capitalised development that the
   auditor flags as possibly overstated; tangible book puts the stock at ~2.6x, not 0.97x.

**Be honest about what might be real.** Underlying ex-Siemens revenue appears to be growing
genuinely fast: ~£10.0M in FY2025 against an implied ~£17.8M in FY2026, helped by CBRN recovering
from an election-year trough and by the European defence-spending cycle (a £1.7M Home Office
contract in Jul-2025). Kromek may well be a decent dual-use defence-adjacent business. **That is a
growth story at 27x guided PBT with a 10% penny-struck option overhang, undisclosed RCF covenants
and £1.2M of cash — priced, not cheap — and it is not expressible at this book at 10-15% of
round-trip friction.** If the thesis is European defence, there are a dozen instruments that cost
20bp to trade.

**Not a durability-watch rescue that should have held. Not a yield. Not at book. Not enterable.**
