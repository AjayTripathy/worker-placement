## 3. THE FRAME QUESTION, ANSWERED DIRECTLY

**Is the cash reachable?** **No, and it is worse than "not reachable" — it is already spoken for.**
Three independent channels are closed: no dividend for 3¼ years; a 10% buyback authority with zero
shares purchased; a register two-thirds owned by strategic holders with no activist. And the fourth
channel is running in reverse — **$160m explicitly committed to buying Capricorn Energy**, plus $125m
of *new* debt on top of the $127m already outstanding, for a company whose own market cap is $189m.
Genel is not a cash box awaiting release; it is a cash box mid-deployment.

**Is the operating bleed bounded?** **Bounded, but not small, and not self-limiting.** H1-2026 FCF
**−$25.4m**. Run-rate obligations: ~$14m/yr of interest on $127m at ~11%, up to $15m/yr of
pre-production spend on two undrilled wildcats, G&A, against revenue of $13.4m in the half.
**Call it $35–50m/yr of cash consumption ≈ 19–26% of market cap per year**, at management's *chosen*
$31/bbl instead of the available international price. Covenants are miles away (equity ratio 62% vs
>30%), so nothing forces a change. The bleed is bounded only by the size of the pile.

**Verdict on the class:** WO&O/TUYA, aggravated. TUYA's cash was *unverified* as to location and
claim; GENL's is fully verified, sits in treasury bills at major banks, and is **verified to be
leaving**. Under the TUYA doctrine — *a cash-box discount closes only on a verified return-of-capital
mechanism* — GENL fails the same test with better disclosure and a worse answer.

## 5. PFIC — the real number, honestly

The two statutory tests under **§1297(a)** are **DISJUNCTIVE** (income test *or* asset test). The
house screen's "conjunctive" language applies only to its *income-test proxy* (rev/assets <10% **and**
passive ≥40%) — not to the statute. So GENL passing the income test does **not** save it.

For a **publicly traded** foreign corporation the asset test must be run on **fair market value**
(§1297(e)), where the denominator ≈ market cap + liabilities. **Cash is a passive asset even when it
is working capital.** Recomputed on today's figures:

- Passive (cash) **$240m** ÷ (mcap $189m + liabilities ~$245m) = **~55%** → **asset test fails on a spot basis.**
- But the test uses the **average of quarterly measurement dates across the taxable year**, not a spot.
  On the trailing-12m average price (~57p → mcap ~$211m) and average cash (~$210m), the share is
  **~49% — straddling the line.**

**Honest verdict: PFIC-PROBABLE, not PFIC-determined**, and it is not determinable from public data —
it needs the company's own quarterly measurement dates. The shelf flag is directionally right and
methodologically approximate; it is a *spot* proxy for an *average* test.

**The mechanism, stated exactly:** GENL is a candidate PFIC **because** it de-rated. Passive assets
sit fixed in the numerator while the denominator collapses with the multiple. A ~26% re-rate (to
~64p) would take the corporate-level share back under 50% — **but §1298(b)(1) "once a PFIC, always a
PFIC" keeps the taint at the *shareholder* level for the rest of the holding period absent a purging
election.** The cure for the company is not a cure for the holder.

**What it would cost this book (taxable, CA):** LSE Main Market is a qualified exchange, so a §1296
mark-to-market election is available and should be made in year 1 — but MTM gains are **ordinary
income at ~54% combined, annually, on unrealised marks**, with losses deductible only against prior
inclusions. Against ~37% LTCG, that converts roughly **a quarter to a third of the entire re-rating
payoff into tax**, on a name we could not exit inside 3–8 sessions to fund the bill. For a thesis
whose *whole* return is a one-time re-rating, this is not a footnote.

## 8. FOUR-IDEA FRAME

1. **Long here.** No. Negative edge, the cash is committed, and the operator of the only producing
   asset is somebody else.
2. **Long lower.** The only price that would interest this desk is one where you are paid for the
   *uncommitted* cash alone — i.e. **below ~22p**, which requires the Capricorn deal to complete
   *and* the market to write off the operating business. Not a plan; an observation. **No alert set.**
3. **The adjacent expression that is actually cleaner.** **The bond, not the equity.** An 11% 2030
   senior unsecured at ~104–105, 62% equity ratio, $199m liquidity against a $20m covenant. The
   credit is where the balance sheet strength accrues and the equity is where the capital-allocation
   risk lands. *Not recommended here* (no USD HY sleeve mandate, and single-asset Kurdistan credit is
   its own court) — but naming it is the honest form of "the balance sheet is real."
4. **The reusable lesson — encode it.** *A screen's `netcash_r` must be net of **announced but
   unclosed acquisition commitments**, not only of debt.* Guard (o) catches securities-masquerading-
   as-cash; it does **not** catch cash that is real, liquid, and contractually spoken for. GENL is the
   specimen: $240m of genuine cash of which $160m is committed in an RNS the balance sheet cannot
   see. **Proposed feature:** cross the shelf against Takeover Panel offer periods on the **BIDDER**
   side as well as the target side (`in_offer_period` today only tests the target), and demote
   `ncash_r` by any announced cash consideration. This would have moved GENL out of the top 5.
