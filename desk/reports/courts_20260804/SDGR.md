**This is the honesty-alpha finding on the long side: an anti-masking result.** The absence of the

### 2c. Masking-channel check — the presentation changed on the same day the number went bad

On **2026-02-25**, in one release, Schrödinger:
1. disclosed **4.0% ACV growth** and the **113% → 100% NDR** collapse;
2. announced it was **"accelerating its transition to hosted software"**, which converts upfront
   on-premise revenue recognition to ratable — explicitly warning this "is expected to introduce
   short-to-medium term declines in software revenue"; and
3. guided FY26 ACV to **$218–228M, +10–15%** — a re-acceleration from 4%.

In Q1'26 reported software revenue then fell **−21%** and management attributed it entirely to the
transition. And in Q1'26 they additionally **reclassified contribution revenue out of software and
restated prior periods.**

Is that masking? **Verdict: NOT masking, but it demands scepticism.** The hosted transition is
economically real, the accounting treatment is correct, ACV and cash flow are genuinely unaffected by
it, and the company said so plainly in the same release rather than burying it. Under house doctrine
this is disclosed bad news presented honestly, which is CLEAN — not a catch. **But** two presentation
changes landing in the same two quarters as a demand deceleration means the ACV metric is now doing
all the work, and ACV is a company-defined, unaudited operating metric. So ACV must be tested, not
accepted. Which brings us to the one thing that actually matters on Wednesday.

### 2d. THE ARITHMETIC PROBLEM IN THE FY26 GUIDE — the Aug-5 test

- FY25 ACV: **$198.5M**. FY26 guide: **$218–228M** → requires **+$19.5M to +$29.5M** of ACV added.
- Q1'26 ACV $28.4M, +12% YoY → **+$3.0M** of the requirement delivered.
- **Trailing-four-quarter ACV = $201M**, versus ~$195.5M a year earlier → **TTM ACV growth is +2.8%**,
  not 12%. The 12% is a single-quarter figure on the smallest quarter of the year.
- Q2'26 guide: **$19–23M ex-contribution**, versus a comparable $18.3M base (Q2'25 was $23.3M
  *including* $5.0M of contribution ACV) → **+$0.7M to +$4.7M**.

**So H1 delivers roughly +$3.7M to +$7.7M of a +$19.5M to +$29.5M full-year requirement. The FY26 ACV
guide needs H2 to carry +$12M to +$26M.** That is a steep back-half hockey stick, and it is the exact
same structural weakness I found in ZTS's guide this session — worth noting as a pattern, not a
coincidence: both companies are guiding to a recovery their H1 run-rate does not evidence.

The saving difference from ZTS: SDGR's Q2 ACV guide is *checkable on Wednesday*, and SDGR has no debt
and no financing need, so being wrong costs multiple compression rather than solvency.
