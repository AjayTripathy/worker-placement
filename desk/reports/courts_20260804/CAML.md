## 2. THE CENTRAL FINDING — the multiple IS the mine life, and the screen cannot see it

Both producing assets terminate in the **same year, 2034**. This is not inference; it is in the
audited accounts.

| Claim | Source | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kounrad is a finite dump-leach with a hard end date | AR2025 goodwill note: *"The Kounrad cash flows have been projected until **2034, the remaining life of operation**"*; FY25 results presentation: *"In production since 2012; **licensed to 2034**"* | 8 years left | **CONFIRMED** |
| Sasa mine life | Sasa Ore Reserve Statement 03-Mar-2026: *"Sasa mine life, currently based solely on Svinja Reka reserve and resource, **estimated to 2034**"* — a **five-year reduction** | 8 years left | **CONFIRMED** |

| Asset retirement obligation | AR2025 note 36: **$37,190k** provision, *"estimated to be required in 2034"* (Kounrad) and *"primarily commencing in 2034"* (Sasa). Raised **$6,863k** in 2025 *"driven primarily by an updated life-of-mine for the Sasa mine now assumed to be 2034 which accelerates the expected timing of closure cash outflows"* | a real terminal cash call | **CONFIRMED** |

**Why this destroys the shelf's "4.3x EV/EBIT" headline.** EV/EBIT is a perpetuity statistic. For
a wasting asset it must be converted to an annuity. At an 8.5-year remaining life and a 12%
discount rate, the annuity factor is 5.15 against a perpetuity factor of 8.33 — a ratio of
**1.62x**. CAML's 4.3x screened EV/EBIT is economically equivalent to **~7.0x for a going
concern**. It is still cheap. It is not top-decile cheap, and it does not belong ranked alongside
non-extractive names on the same axis.

*Shelf-wide lesson (applies to THX #1, SRB #3, and every miner on the LSE shelf): the deep-value
composite's EV/EBIT term mechanically over-ranks finite-life extractive assets by
perpetuity_factor / annuity_factor. Encode a remaining-mine-life haircut or the miners will keep
colonising the top of the list.*

**Sasa's reserve is not a stable number.** Ore Reserve fell **9.2Mt → 6.9Mt (−25%)** in one year.
The stated causes are not one-offs: *"revisions to the mine design; the application of higher net
smelter return (NSR) cut-off values… revised assumptions for metals prices and concentrate
treatment charges… and normal mining depletion"*, plus — the durable one — *"As mining has
progressed deeper at Sasa, the geology has presented increasing challenges… the orebody has
become **narrower**… **more variable in both geometry and grade**."* A second cut is a live
possibility, not a tail.

**Kounrad's remaining life is disclosed but not quantified in tonnes.** CAML gives a licence date
(2034) and a cash-flow horizon (2034) but no remaining leachable-copper statement. The Eastern
Dumps were *"scheduled to cease production in approximately 2024"* yet delivered **14% of 2025
output** and *"approximately 16% more copper than originally envisaged."* Read honestly, that
cuts both ways: management's dump estimates carry wide error bars in **both** directions, and the
last surprise was favourable. Dump 15 levelling is complete and Dump 16 is being winterised for
2027 — the physical pipeline is being fed.

## 8. RULING — neither de-rate nor derailment: a **mandate change on a wasting asset**

The contract asks de-rate (multiple compressed on stable/rising estimates) vs derailment
(estimates falling with the multiple). **Both signatures are present simultaneously, on different
variables:**

- **De-rate signature:** the 2026 *earnings* trajectory is rising hard — copper +50%, realised
  +39%, MET cut worth ~$13M/yr, net cash +$18M in H1 with a buyback done. Estimates are going up
  while the multiple went down.
- **Derailment signature:** the *reserve* trajectory is falling — Sasa −25% of reserve and −5
  years of life in one statement, grades down 9-10% y/y, orebody narrowing at depth.

The synthesis, and the honest label: **the security's mandate changed.** CAML was owned as a
high-payout runoff yield vehicle. Between September 2025 and June 2026 it cut the dividend a
third, lost five years of asset life, and elected to issue 30% new equity for a pre-production
development project. The holders who owned the old mandate are the sellers; the holders who want
the new one (a Québec copper developer) have not arrived. That gap is the discount.
