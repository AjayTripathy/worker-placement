## 3. MODE B (led) — four things the memo framing hides

### Finding 1 — the "two-engine model" has one engine that makes almost no money
Q1'26 was the first quarter with a separate Overseas reportable segment. The table:

| Q1'26 | Net revenue (RMB k) | Segment op profit (RMB k) | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chinese Mainland | 2,216,096 | 598,747 | **27.0%** |
| Overseas Markets | 948,946 | **45,750** | **4.8%** |
| Others | 50,148 | (38,613) | — |

Q1'25 comparison: Mainland 2,770,160 / 913,142 (**33.0%**); Overseas 705,343 / 24,379 (3.5%).

Overseas is **29.6% of revenue but 7.6% of segment profit**. Mainland segment profit fell
**RMB314.4M**; overseas segment profit rose **RMB21.4M** — the "growth engine" replaced **6.8%**
of what the profit engine lost. Management's framing is entirely in revenue and growth rates
("revenue grew 34.5% year over year", "operating profit … up 87.7%") on a base of RMB24m.

Sharper still: the Indonesian product is capped at **0.3%/day ≈ 110% APR** (20-F, OJK rules).
A ~110%-APR loan book that yields a **4.8% operating margin** has essentially zero margin of
safety — credit losses and acquisition cost consume it. There is no operating leverage story
here yet; there is a thin-margin subprime book being scaled.

### Finding 2 — credit is deteriorating, and their own chart says the worst is unseasoned
90-day+ delinquency ratio (mainland, ex capital-light), from the four quarterly releases:

| Jun-30-25 | Sep-30-25 | Dec-31-25 | Mar-31-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.92% | 1.96% | **2.85%** | **3.11%** |

+62% in three quarters. A shrinking denominator inflates this mechanically — but not by 62%.

The Q1'26 release publishes the vintage curve only as a **JPEG** (`ex99-1_001.jpg`), no table.
Read multimodally (per the chart-only-disclosure rule), the cumulative 30-day+ curves show:
**2025Q3 is the worst vintage on the chart at month 6 (~2.1%, above 2023Q3's ~1.8%), and 2025Q2
at month 9 (~2.7%) is tracking at/above every prior vintage except 2023Q3.** The 2025Q2/Q3
cohorts — originated in the push right before the October rule — are the worst-performing
cohorts in the four-year history and are only half-seasoned. Provisioning is not done.

This is *disclosed*, in their own release, in a form text-grep would have missed entirely. That
is an anti-masking datum and I count it in their favour on honesty — and against them on credit.

### Finding 3 — the regulatory sequence is LIVE, and two rules land inside eight weeks
Every bull note treats the regulation as a 2025 event that has been absorbed. The 20-F filed
2026-04-29 says otherwise:

- **Effective 2026-08-01 (two days ago):** NFRA/PBoC *Provisions on Disclosure of the
  Comprehensive Borrowing Costs for Personal Loan Business* — forces itemization of interest,
  installment fees, credit-enhancement fees, default costs, **and identification of which
  entity collects each**. This is the enforcement mechanism for the 24% all-in ceiling.
- **Effective 2026-09-30:** *Administrative Measures for Online Marketing of Financial
  Products* — bans platforms from routing clients to unlicensed third parties, bans
  "low threshold" / "drawdown in seconds" / "low interest rate" ad language, and **bans using
  algorithmic models to induce consumption**. This attacks customer acquisition directly. The
  20-F's own words: *"a lot of uncertainties about its interpretations and impact on our
  business… we may be required to change our online ads for compliance, which would affect user
  acquisition."*
- **Philippines SEC memorandum, effective 2026-04-01:** 12%/month effective-rate cap on
  consumer loans ≤P10,000 with terms ≤4 months. Not in any reported quarter yet; it hits the
  4.8%-margin overseas segment starting Q2'26.

Three separate pricing/acquisition constraints take effect *after* the last reported quarter.
Buying here is buying ahead of the next two data points about a statute, not after them.

### Finding 5 — the takeaway-vs-data divergence (the honesty catch)
> CFO, Q1'26: *"Early signs of credit recovery in our Chinese Mainland business supported a
> **recovery in loan origination volume** to RMB38.5 billion, driving a 6.9% sequential increase
> in Chinese Mainland net revenue…"*

Checked against the company's own reported series:
- Mainland volume **Q4'25 = RMB38.7bn → Q1'26 = RMB38.5bn**. That is **-0.5% sequentially.**
  There was no volume recovery. **REFUTED.**
- Group volume Q4'25 42.8 → Q1'26 42.6: also down. **REFUTED.**
- "Early signs of credit recovery": 90d+ delinquency went **2.85% → 3.11%** over the same
  quarter, and the freshest vintages sit at the top of their own chart. **REFUTED.**
- "Net revenues … up 6.2% sequentially" (3,023.9 → 3,210.1): **CONFIRMED** — but the driver
  was **net interest income +100.6% YoY**, i.e. moving loans onto the balance sheet and taking
  the credit risk directly (provision for loans receivable +155.4%), *not* volume. The revenue
  gain was bought with balance-sheet risk and then attributed to a volume recovery that the
  company's own table shows did not occur.

**Mechanism (encode this):** masking channel = *causal misattribution of a sequential revenue
gain*, wrapped in a sequential-vs-YoY frame switch (-7.8% YoY revenue and -44.4% YoY profit are
never spoken; "+6.2% sequentially" is). Signal channel = the company's own volume table and the
JPEG vintage chart in the same filing. Signal-to-price latency ≈ **zero** — everything needed to
refute it shipped in the same 6-K, and the tape still paid +16.9% that week.

Per the honesty doctrine this is **review_flag, not EXCLUDE**: the data is fully disclosed, the
guide-down is explicit and quantified, and the new segment break-out plus the self-damaging
vintage chart are *anti-masking*. But the marketed causal story is factually wrong in a
direction that flatters, and that is exactly the divergence the framework grades.

## 4. Ratio triangulation (ADR trap check — mandatory)

- Market cap: 243.3M ADS (Form 144, 2026-05-27) × $4.96 = **$1.207bn**.
- Book equity attributable: RMB16,245.6M ÷ 6.8987 = **$2.355bn** → **P/B 0.51x**.
- TTM net profit attributable (Q2'25 ≈ 697 derived from FY25 RMB2.5bn less the three reported
  quarters, + Q3'25 640.7 + Q4'25 415.5 + Q1'26 415.1) ≈ **RMB2,169M = $314M** → **P/E 3.8x**.
- TTM ROE ≈ 2,169 / ~16,400 = **13.2%**.
- **Consistency test (ROE / PB ≈ 1/PE):** 13.2% ÷ 0.51 = 25.9% earnings yield → implied P/E
  **3.86x** vs computed **3.8x**. **CONSISTENT.** No ADR-ratio or currency-basis corruption.

Forward: Q1'26 annualized net profit = RMB1,660M = $241M → **~5.0x forward**. Note Q1 has
historically been the *strongest* quarter (Q1'25 746 vs Q2 697 / Q3 641 / Q4 416), so 4x-ing Q1
is generous, not conservative.

**Guide arithmetic that the bulls skip:** Q1'26 revenue RMB3.21bn × 4 = RMB12.84bn — already the
**top** of the RMB11.5–12.9bn guide. For the midpoint to hold, Q2–Q4 must average RMB3.00bn,
i.e. **revenue must fall from here.** Either management is sandbagging, or the Aug-1 and Sep-30
rules are expected to bite. The Q2 print resolves which.
