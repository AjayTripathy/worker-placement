## 2. MODE B FIRST — first-principles, no promoter framing

I derived the decisive questions before reading management's story.

### B-1. Is the category actually penetrated out? **NO — REFUTED.**
- 10-K (FY2025, Item 1): household penetration **≈15.2 million US households**; the addressable base is
  **≈98 million dog/cat-food-buying households** (Numerator, cited in the same 10-K).
- **15.5% penetrated. 84.5% headroom.** The "fully-penetrated category" hypothesis in the brief is
  arithmetically refuted from primary source. This is NOT the HUBS pattern's opposite; the TAM is real.

### B-2. Is fridge placement saturating? **PARTLY — but the lever moved, honestly.**
- 10-K: **≈30,235 retail locations**, **≈24% of stores carry multiple Freshpet Fridges**.
- Store-count growth is decelerating against a finite US grocery/mass/pet universe. Management's own
  answer is *cubic feet per store*, not store count: FY25 letter calls island fridges "our most
  significant step change in retail visibility and availability." That is a candid re-statement of the
  runway, and it is a **smaller** lever than a virgin store.
- Verdict: placement saturation is REAL at the store-count level, MITIGATED at the shelf-space level.

### B-3. Where is the return on $700M+ of capex? **THIS IS THE FINDING.**
Primary-source capital intensity:

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capex | $187.1M | $148.2M | **~$150M (unchanged in the May raise)** |
| D&A | ~$85M | $86.4M (XBRL) / ~$97M run-rate (Q1'26 recon ×4) | ~$100M |
| **Capex / D&A** | **2.2x** | **1.6x** | **~1.5x** |
| PP&E, net | — | $1,138.7M (12/31/25) | **$1,143.6M (3/31/26)** |
| **Net PP&E / sales** | — | **1.03x** | **~0.95x** |

A packaged-food manufacturer typically runs 0.3–0.6x net PP&E/sales. **Freshpet runs ~1.0x — roughly
double.** And the build is not finishing: the 10-K says Ennis Phase 1 is complete (3 lines), Phase 2
was "initiated in 2024," and **"the balance of Phase 2 and Phase 3 [is] planned for completion over
the next several years."**

**Normalized ROIC:**
- FY26E Adj EBITDA (guide mid) $210M − D&A ~$100M − normalized SBC ~$40M = **economic EBIT ≈ $70M**
- Invested capital = net PP&E $1,143.6M + net working capital ~$63M = **≈$1,207M**
- **Pre-tax ROIC ≈ 5.8%. After-tax ≈ 4.3%.** Below any defensible WACC.

That is the whole case. Management is deploying $150M/yr into an asset base already earning less than
its cost of capital, and the 2026 guide does not promise the leverage that would fix it (§B-4).

## 4. DE-RATE vs DERAILMENT — the whole job

**Ruling: DE-RATE on the near term, DERAILMENT on the terminal, and the de-rate landed at FAIR.**

- **Current-year estimates are STABLE-to-RISING.** Sales guide was *raised* in May; EBITDA guide held;
  Q1'26 volume **re-accelerated to +14.6%** from Q4'25's +9.7%. This is not the falling-estimates
  signature of a derailment. The HUBS/CTSH/SAP first condition is met.
- **The terminal DID derail.** FY24 +27.2% → FY25 +13.0% → FY26E +8–11%, and management's own PSU
  reversal says the multi-year targets are gone. The growth algorithm reset a full tier.
- **The second HUBS condition FAILS.** HUBS/CTSH/SAP worked because the compressed multiple was cheap
  against intact earning power. Here the compression ran **50x EV/EBITDA (Jan-2025) → 14.2x today** —
  that is a bubble being corrected to a normal multiple, not a normal multiple being pushed to a
  discount. There is no own-history discount worth having when the own-history was 50x.

**Where it trades now:**
| Metric | FRPT |
|---|---|
| EV / FY26E Adj EBITDA (guide mid $210M) | **14.2x** |
| EV / FY26E sales | **2.47x** |
| FY26E FCF (OCF ~$180M − capex $150M) | **~$30M → 1.0% FCF yield** |

| "Street-adjusted" EPS (SBC added back) | ~$1.60 → **~37x** |
| Pre-tax ROIC | **5.8%** |

14.2x EV/EBITDA for a 9%-growing, 1.0x-PP&E/sales, sub-WACC-ROIC food manufacturer with 35% customer
concentration is **fair to full**, not cheap. Packaged-food comps sit 9–14x on 0–3% growth; premium-growth
food sits 15–25x on 15–25% growth. FRPT is priced correctly in between.
