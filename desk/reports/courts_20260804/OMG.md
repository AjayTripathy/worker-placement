## 5. PFIC — the conjunctive test, honestly

**The statute is DISJUNCTIVE.** §1297(a): a foreign corporation is a PFIC if **either** ≥75% of gross
income is passive **or** ≥50% of assets are passive. The house screen's *"conjunctive"* language
applies **only to its income-test PROXY** (`rev/assets < 10%` **and** `passive ≥ 40%`) — a
false-positive suppressor inside our own tool, **not a statement about the statute.** So the shelf's
note *"book test passed — active business, 58% revenue intensity"* does **not** save OMG. The asset
test stands alone, and for a **publicly traded** foreign corporation it must be run at **fair market
value** (§1297(e)), denominator ≈ market cap + liabilities. **Cash is passive even as working
capital, and so are fixed-term deposits** (they earn 4.52% interest — textbook passive income).

| basis | passive | denominator | share | PFIC? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **spot, today** | £31.7m | £42.98m cap + ~£17.5m liabs = £60.5m | **52.4%** | **fails** |
| **statutory basis — average of quarterly measurement dates across the taxable year** | ~£31m | avg cap ~£46m + £17.5m | **~49–50%** | **coin flip** |

**Honest verdict: PFIC-MARGINAL, not PFIC-determined — the shelf's flag is a SPOT proxy for an
AVERAGE test.** A **+7% move to ~41p** takes the spot share back under 50%. Two structural points the
flag cannot express:

1. **The de-rate manufactures the status.** Passive assets sit fixed while the denominator collapses
   with the multiple. OMG is a candidate PFIC *because* it fell 38%.
2. **Capital returns are the cure, and they fix both problems at once.** Paying a £3.7m dividend moves
   the ratio from 31.7/60.5 = 52.4% to 28.0/56.8 = **49.3%**. Because the numerator is smaller than
   the denominator, distributing cash **reduces** the passive share. **The same action that closes
   the discount also walks the company out of PFIC territory** — the structural opposite of GENL,
   which cures its status by converting cash into an acquisition. *(Note the perverse corollary: the
   £10m buyback, being a return of capital, worked the same way — and the dividend rebase will work
   against it.)*

**THE REAL ASYMMETRY vs GENL — and it cuts against OMG.** A §1296 mark-to-market election requires
*marketable stock on a qualified exchange* (Reg §1.1296-2(c)). **GENL is LSE Main Market — clean.
OMG is AIM.** AIM is an SME growth market operated by the LSE (an FCA-recognised investment
exchange); most practitioners treat it as qualified, **but "most practitioners treat" is not a
determination.** If AIM does not qualify: MTM is unavailable, QEF is unavailable in practice (an AIM
smallcap will not furnish a PFIC Annual Information Statement), and the holder falls into **§1291**:
- PFIC dividends are **not** qualified dividends (§1(h)(11)(C)(iii)) — taxed at ~54% combined (CA)
  rather than ~37%.
- A distribution above **125% of the trailing three-year average** is an *excess distribution*,
  throwback-taxed with an interest charge. **A dividend INCREASE is tax-penalised** — the regime
  punishes the best possible development. *(A dividend CUT, ironically, is tax-neutral here.)*
- Gain on sale loses LTCG treatment entirely.

**This is a live, unresolved, decision-relevant tax question and it is a pre-entry item for counsel,
not a footnote. It is the single largest reason this is a 4 and not a 6.**
