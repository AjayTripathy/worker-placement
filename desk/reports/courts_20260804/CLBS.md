## 1. THE DECISIVE TEST — DEFERRED INCOME

If revenue were merely deferred by the move to straight-line licence recognition, contract
liabilities had to rise by something like the revenue shortfall. AR2026 note 5 ($000):

| | 31 Mar 2026 | 31 Mar 2025 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contract liabilities (deferred income)** | **8,836** | **7,128** | **+1,708** |

| Receivables within trade and other receivables | 1,106 | 5,010 | −3,904 |

**Deferred income rose $1.7m while revenue fell $15.1m. The recognition change explains at most
~11% of the decline.** REFUTED.

This is not re-detecting disclosed bad news. Every datum above **is** disclosed, in note 5. The
finding is that the **marketed takeaway diverges from it**, and the reconciling disclosure is
withheld:

| marketed takeaway | the company's own data |
|---|---|
| "Revenue decline masks ARR growth as accounting change hits FY26 figures" | The accounting change explains ~11%; 85% is a collapse in one partner's pass-through and software revenue |

claim. That is the divergence, and it is what makes this an ELEVATE rather than an honestly

## 5. PFIC — WATCH, NOT CONFIRMED, AND THE DE-RATE IS WHAT MOVES IT

The shelf flags `pfic: true`, severity MED, on the **book** asset test (passive assets 62.7% ≥ 50%).
But §1297(e) runs the asset test on **fair market value** for a publicly traded corporation, and the
shelf's own `pfic_fmv_share` is **0.464 — under the 50% line.** The income test passes comfortably
(interest on $32.5m against $23.6m of revenue is ~5% passive, far below 75%).

**So on the governing FMV basis Celebrus is probably NOT a PFIC today — but it is within ~4 points
of the line, and it is the price that moves it.** This is the mechanism from the intake's §4 stated
exactly: a further de-rate raises cash as a share of market value and *manufactures* the status.
The correct label is **PFIC WATCH**, and the correct treatment for a US taxable book is to require a
margin of safety against the line, which at 46.4% does not exist.
