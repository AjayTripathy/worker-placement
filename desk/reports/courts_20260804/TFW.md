## 3. MODE B — THREE THINGS THE "COMPOUNDER AT 7.3x" FRAMING HIDES

**(a) The profit peak is margin-driven, not volume-driven.** Revenue peaked in **FY2023 at
£176.7m** and has been flat-to-down for three years (FY25 £175.2m). All of the FY23→FY25 profit
growth came from margin. Margin expansion on flat volume is finite.

**(b) Cash conversion broke before the profit did.** FY25 **operating cash flow fell 19.7% to
£33.2m** (FY24 £41.4m) while operating profit rose 4.7%. The cash statement turned a year before
the P&L did.

**(c) There is a £9.4m committed claim on the cash pile that no balance-sheet screen can see.**
AR2025 carries a **level-3 redemption liability of £9,355k** (FY24 £10,449k) — the put/call
obligations to buy out the **Zemper and SchahlLED minorities**, which the Group "has committed to
acquiring over the next few years." It sits in payables, not in debt. **This is the THX lesson
exactly: a committed obligation against a cash balance, living in the notes.**

**Corrected net cash and EV** (31-Dec-2025 interim balance sheet):

|---|---|
| Cash and cash equivalents | 46,383 |
| Short-term financial assets (term deposits >3m) | 11,517 |

**EV = £295.3m − £45.4m = £249.9m. On FY25 EBIT of £32.06m that is 7.8x, not the shelf's 7.3x.**
Capital commitments are genuinely small and verified: **£302k** (AR2025 note 30).

**Family control — the vendor figure is wrong.** yfinance reports `heldPercentInsiders` **66.8%**.

shareholder is declared and no holder exceeds 50%.** So takeover optionality is not extinguished —
but at ~46% board-held it is effective negative control, and the 233bp touch is structural.
Substantial non-director holders: Liontrust 5.8%, N Brangwin 4.2%.

## 4. WHERE THE WEAKNESS ACTUALLY IS — AND THE ONE THING TO STRESS

Segment operating profit before acquisition adjustments, FY25 vs FY24 (£000):

| segment | revenue FY25 | FY24 | op profit FY25 | FY24 | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thorlux (incl. SchahlLED DE) | 101,011 | 99,492 | 21,263 | 19,933 | +6.7% |
| **Netherlands (Lightronics + Famostar)** | **34,348** | **37,942** | **6,408** | **8,802** | **−27.2%** |
| Zemper Group (ES) | 21,372 | 19,350 | 3,671 | 2,880 | **+27.5%** |
| Other (TRT, Philip Payne, Solite, Portland) | 18,490 | 19,014 | 2,017 | 718 | +181% |

**The weak assets are Lightronics (Netherlands) and SchahlLED (Germany), not Zemper.** German
revenue fell **25%** (£17,554k → £13,164k) on the "widely acknowledged recession in its German
industrial sector"; Famostar is the strong half of the Dutch segment, so Lightronics standalone is
worse than the −27.2% shows. **Zemper is the standout grower.**

**The stress point:** goodwill of **£14,230k sits on Lumen Intelligence (SchahlLED)** against a
business whose geography just declined 25%, and **no impairment was taken**. The FY25 impairment
test used a **10.0% post-tax WACC, cut from 11.0% the prior year** — a discount-rate reduction that
mechanically increases headroom in the year the underlying deteriorated. Management states headroom
survives even at a 20% WACC. Total goodwill £46,348k (Zemper £18,218k, FW Thorpe Nederland £6,666k,
Famostar £4,331k, SchahlLED £14,230k, Portland £2,618k, Solite £285k). The only impairment in two
years was Thorlux Australasia, £249k in FY24.

## 5. PENSION — THE LSE ADDENDUM §2 REQUIREMENT, DISCHARGED

**There IS a DB scheme** (the FW Thorpe Retirement Benefits Scheme, hybrid DB/DC, DB section closed;
a PwC Key Audit Matter) and it **IS in surplus** — but there is **nothing to strip from book
equity.** AR2025 note 22:

| £000 | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Present value of funded obligations | (16,753) | (28,073) |
| Fair value of plan assets | 20,818 | 32,461 |
| **Surplus** | **4,065** | **4,388** |
| Restriction of surplus (asset ceiling) | (4,065) | (4,388) |
| **Asset recognised in the balance sheet** | **nil** | **nil** |

> "The Company has no unconditional right to a refund or reduction in contributions as the trustees
> have the discretion to use any surplus to augment the benefits… the surplus in the scheme is
> treated as the asset ceiling and not recognised."

**P/B of 1.54 is therefore NOT overstated** — unlike CHH (£7.65m recognised, 12% of equity), COST
(£60.0m) and VTY (£32.2m). This is the correct outcome of the addendum's mandated hand-check, and
it is a positive finding: the intake's §5 defect does not bite here.

**Related guard error:** the shelf's `pension_gross` of £3,352k is a *long-term provisions proxy*.
AR2025 note 23 shows the provision is **100% WARRANTY** (£3,310k at 30-Jun-2025; usual warranty
period 5–10 years), nothing to do with pensions. The module's own note warned the proxy
over-captures; here it captured warranty and labelled it pension.

## 11. UNVERIFIABLE / GAPS

1. **No interim actuarial valuation was undertaken**, so the 31-Dec-2025 pension surplus is unknown;
   FY25 (£4.065m, unrecognised) is the last measured point. Immaterial given the asset ceiling.
2. **Buyback activity since 30-Jun-2025 is unquantified** — the Board says it "will continue to buy
   back its own shares when considered appropriate," and treasury already holds 2,673,682 shares.
3. **Lightronics standalone figures are not disclosed** — it is reported inside the Netherlands
   segment with Famostar, which is the strong half. The true extent of the Lightronics decline is
   masked by aggregation. This is the segment-level flooring lesson: read the mix, not the total.
4. **The 20%-WACC headroom assertion is management's, not tested independently.** Given a 25% German
   revenue decline against £14.2m of goodwill, it is the one claim I would stress before sizing up.
