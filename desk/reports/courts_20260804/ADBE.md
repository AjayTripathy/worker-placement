## 2. MODE B (LEAD) — first-principles, disconfirming pass

I derived the decisive questions without the memo's framing. The memo's framing ("genAI
commoditizes creative tooling") is the *ambient* bear and it is already fully in the price. The
questions that actually determine the outcome are different.

### B-1. Is the leading indicator diverging from the marketed takeaway? **YES — the finding.**

Adobe's revenue and EPS are *lagging* indicators of a subscription book. ARR is the leading one.
Trace the FY26 ARR target across two press releases:

| Release | Date | FY26 "Total Adobe ending ARR growth" target | Semrush included? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q4 FY25 (8-K ex-99.1, `adbeex991q425.htm`) | 2025-12-10 | **10.2%** | **NO** — "do not include any contributions from Semrush Holdings, Inc." (verbatim) |
| Q2 FY26 (8-K ex-99.1, `adbeex991q226.htm`) | 2026-06-11 | **10.2%** | **YES** — "These targets factor in the acquisition of Semrush Holdings, Inc." (verbatim) |

Semrush closed 2026-04-28 for $1.87B (10-Q Note 3) and contributed **~$480M to ending ARR**
(Q2 FY26 release, verbatim: "Total Adobe ARR exiting the quarter was $27.10 billion, **including
approximately $480 million from Semrush**").

**The same 10.2% headline now absorbs ~$480M — roughly 1.9 points of growth — of purchased ARR that
was explicitly excluded from the original target.** The organic FY26 ARR growth target has therefore
been cut from 10.2% to approximately **8.3%**, a ~19% reduction in organic net-new ARR dollars. It
was never stated as a cut. The press release headline instead reads:

> "Adobe Reports Record Q2 Results — **Adobe Raises FY26 Total Revenue and Non-GAAP EPS Targets**"

Both of those raises are true. Both are also substantially mechanical: the revenue target rose
$550M at the midpoint ($26.00B → $26.55B) against ~$290-300M of Semrush revenue plus a ~2-point FX
tailwind (Q2 grew 13% reported vs **11% constant currency**). Strip acquisition and currency and the
organic constant-currency revenue plan is roughly unchanged-to-lower. **This is the divergence: the
marketed takeaway is acceleration; the leading metric decelerated and the deceleration was funded
with $1.87B of shareholder cash.**

Corroborating pattern, same direction, same quarter:
- **The ARR denominator was revalued upward.** Q4 FY25, verbatim: "We revalued ending ARR at the end
  of FY2025 which resulted in a **$460 million increase** to Total Adobe ARR, from $25.20 billion to
  $25.66 billion entering FY2026, primarily from foreign exchange rate changes."
- **RPO growth disclosure was dropped.** Q1 FY26 headline bullet: "Total subscription revenue and
  RPO increases **13% year over year**." Q2 FY26: RPO stated as a level ($22.27B) with **no growth
  rate given** — and the level is *below* the FY25 year-end $22.52B while now including Semrush.
- **GAAP did not participate in the raise.** FY26 non-GAAP EPS target went $23.30-23.50 → $24.35-24.45
  (+$1.00). FY26 GAAP EPS target went $17.90-18.10 → $17.90-**18.00** — *trimmed at the top*. The
  non-GAAP-to-GAAP gap widened from $5.40 to **$6.45, now 26% of non-GAAP EPS**.

### B-2. Was the disclosure surface reduced at the moment of maximum scrutiny? **YES.**

10-Q (`adbe-20260529.htm`), verbatim:

> "In the first quarter of fiscal 2026, we combined our former segments—**Digital Media, Digital
> Experience and Publishing and Advertising—into a single operating and reportable segment**."

Digital Media ARR — the single most-tracked Adobe metric for a decade, and the exact series the
queue asked me to trend — **was retired in Q1 FY26**, the same quarter the CEO announced his
departure and the disruption debate peaked. It is replaced by "Total Adobe ARR," a metric whose base
was revalued +$460M and which now silently absorbs acquired ARR.

I want to be fair about this: ASC 280 permits segment aggregation when the CODM genuinely changes
how it reviews results, customer-group subscription revenue is still disclosed at two-group
granularity, and the stated rationale (unified selling motions) is coherent. This is **not**
accounting misconduct. But the net effect is that the specific granularity which would let an
outsider locate where erosion is occurring was removed exactly when it became valuable. Cataloguing
it as a masking channel, not as fraud.

### B-3. Does the entry require the AI-capex cycle to hold? **NO — it is the rare inverse. This is the strongest finding for the name.**

Against the frozen house call **AI-BREAK | 2027-12-31 @ 0.45**:

- **If the cycle BREAKS, Adobe is a fundamental beneficiary.** The bear case requires venture- and
  hyperscaler-subsidised generative competitors (Sora, Midjourney, Runway, Canva's model spend,
  Figma) to keep offering near-free model-native creation. That is capital-cycle-funded. A capex
  break removes the subsidy, forces inference to be priced at cost, and de-funds the challenger
  cohort — while Adobe's enterprise contracts, IP-indemnified training corpus, and distribution
  persist. Adobe additionally becomes a rotation destination: 10.4% FCF yield, 89% gross margin,
  near-zero capex.
- **Adobe carries almost no AI capex exposure of its own.** PP&E capex was **$179M on $23.77B of
  FY25 revenue — 0.75%** (XBRL `PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment`). There is no
  depreciation cliff, no stranded GPU fleet, no take-or-pay compute commitment of consequence. The
  entire AI build is opex and third-party cloud.
- **If the cycle HOLDS, the bear intensifies** — better models, more challenger capital.

**Household context makes this decisive.** The book already carries ~$5.2M / 26% AI-complex exposure
that is *long* the cycle holding. ADBE is one of the few liquid, cash-generative, quality names
whose fundamental payoff is **negatively correlated to the house's largest concentrated risk**. That
is a portfolio-construction reason to own it that is independent of the standalone edge.

Honest caveat, and I am not going to average it away: in the first weeks of a capex break,
correlations go to one and ADBE sells off with everything else. The negative beta here is
**fundamental, not price-path**. A break is also a growth-software multiple event in the near term.
This is an 18-month hedge, not a two-week one.

Cap structure pulled **before** any valuation claim (10-Q balance sheet + Note 11 + Note 14):

  framing would be wrong. EV ≈ **$101.3B**.
- Buyback: March 2024 $25B authority (to 2028) **plus a new April 2026 $25B authority (to April
  2030)**. **$26.78B remained as of 2026-05-29 = 26.7% of the market cap.**
- Execution is verified, not promised: H1 FY26 open-market repurchases **16.6M shares for $4,588M**.
  Q2 monthly average prices paid: $260.24 / $241.27 / $245.90 — **the company bought through the
  drawdown at an average below today's price.**
- Share count is actually falling, net of SBC: 441M (Nov-24) → 413M (Nov-25) → 406M (Feb-26) →
  **399M (May-26)**. −3.4% in two quarters, −9.5% over 18 months.

**Caveat I will state plainly:** FY25 buybacks were **$11.281B against $9.852B of FCF** — funded
partly by drawing cash from $7.613B to $5.431B. The pace is above free cash flow and is not
indefinitely repeatable without leverage. FY26 H1 is more disciplined at $4.589B vs $5.028B FCF
(91%). And roughly **6.6 of the ~16.6 points of FY26 non-GAAP EPS growth come from the share count
alone** (427M FY25 → ~399M guided FY26), so "EPS +17%" overstates business growth by a wide margin.

## 6. FOUR-IDEA FRAME

1. **The estimate path settles the queue's central question, and it settles it for the bull.** 28-29
   upward revisions and zero downward revisions in 30 days, with FY26/FY27 consensus at 12-month
   highs while the multiple went from ~28x to 9.2x. This is the HUBS/CTSH/SAP signature: the
   *multiple* was destroyed, the *numbers* were not. Every historical instance of that pattern in
   this book has been a de-rate, not a derailment.
2. **The de-rate's cause is a leadership vacuum, not model competition — which is both better and
   worse than the narrative.** Better, because a vacuum is fillable and the fill is a dated,
   high-probability, binary-up catalyst. Worse, because a CEO-less company five months into a search
   with an interim CFO, a retention-lettered CLO and a 50.65% say-on-pay cannot execute a bold AI
   pivot, and a new CEO inherits $26.78B of buyback authority that could instead become a
   transformational acquisition. That single un-hedgeable capital-allocation risk is the reason this
   is 0.65% and not 1.2%.
3. **The honesty finding is real but narrow, and it is priced-adjacent rather than priced.** Holding
   the FY26 ARR growth target at exactly 10.2% while newly absorbing ~$480M of purchased ARR is a
   ~2-point organic cut delivered under a "raises targets" headline, alongside a revalued ARR base,
   a dropped RPO growth rate, and a retired segment. None of it is misstatement; all of it is
   framing. It does not make the business worse than the market thinks — the market has already
   marked the leading indicator down harder than Adobe has. It makes *management's communication*
   less trustworthy as a forward guide, which is a reason to underwrite the tape and the revisions
   rather than the guide.
4. **The portfolio reason outranks the standalone reason.** With ~$5.2M / 26% of the household long
   the AI-capex cycle holding, a liquid, 10.4%-FCF-yield, 89%-gross-margin business whose *bear case
   requires the cycle to hold* is a structurally scarce asset. Bought small and staged, ADBE is the
   offset. Bought large, it is just another 2026 falling-knife software long.
