## Mode B — LED. The masking channel (this is the finding)
**Every headline claim in Coinbase's own Q2 deck is a VOLUME or SHARE metric at an all-time high.
Every revenue metric fell.** Verbatim from the deck:

> "3rd Consecutive Quarter of Record Crypto Trading Volume" · "Reached ATH in Coinbase Crypto
> Trading Volume Market Share; gained share in both Spot and Derivatives" · "Q2 Report Card —
> Strong fundamentals despite macro headwinds — **The business is healthy**"

Set against the company's own income statement: transaction revenue **−31%**, total revenue
**−18.5%**, operating income **−$113M**, third straight net loss.

**Record volume with revenue down 31% is arithmetically a take-rate collapse.** The mechanism is
disclosed in the deck's own product commentary and is *structural, not cyclical*: COIN is winning
volume share by moving into structurally low-take products —
- crypto **perpetuals/derivatives** (TTM volume $4.2T, "new ATH global derivatives market share",
  "3rd consecutive quarter of derivative share gain") — perps carry roughly an order of magnitude
  lower take rate than retail spot;
- **prediction markets** ("$100M+ annualized revenue... >2x growth Q/Q") — near-zero take rate;
- institutional mix over consumer.

The same channel runs on the stablecoin line, and it is the cleaner illustration: the deck says
**"Stablecoin revenue of $292M driven by new ATH in Average USDC Held in Coinbase Products offset
by declines in interest rates & off-platform balances."** Record balances, falling revenue.
**COIN's USDC line is a short-rates bet marketed as a crypto-adoption metric** — and it is 24% of
total revenue, down from a $331M peak.

**Signal-to-price latency:** ~zero. The market has already marked this (−65%). Per the honesty-alpha
doctrine, re-detecting a disclosed problem is not alpha — the *contribution here is the mechanism*
(metric substitution: volume/share substituted for take-rate/revenue, on two independent lines),
which generalizes to any marketplace whose mix is shifting down the take-rate curve.
