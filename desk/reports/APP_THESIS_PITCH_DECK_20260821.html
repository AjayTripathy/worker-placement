Why the spread and not more shares — the desk's opinion

ReasonThe argument
Gate complianceAdds are evidence-gated — more shares today would be an un-gated add, a doctrine violation. The spread is the one instrument that pre-pays for the gate-firing scenario without front-running the gate: conditional exposure, where shares are unconditional.
Subsidized convexityImplied 64 vs realized 83 means the market is undercharging for convexity. Shares carry none; when optionality trades below the process's actual movement, take the subsidized instrument. At the 80th IV percentile this answer inverts: buy shares, skip options.
The tail a stop can't capA stop is not a floor — a 5%-a-day stock can gap through $299 on a headline and fill lower. The stock leg wears that; the spread cannot lose beyond $730 under any gap. On a no-floor name, capping the left tail is what the theta buys.
Payoff shaped to the handoffThe 340 cap costs the far-right tail — but above $340 the basing gate adds shares anyway. The spread maxes out exactly where the stock position takes over: it is a bridge between the two share tranches, not a substitute for them.
The honest costs: the spread time-boxes the thesis — it is a bet that a gate fires by Jan 15 (the tenor spans the 11/04 print plus two months of basing runway, but a re-rate arriving in March pays shareholders and zeroes the spread) — and any gain is short-term income where shares held through could go long-term. If the gates were undated, shares would win; because the gates are near-dated, the spread does.

Why only starter size — the honest slide

  • Growth durability is genuinely unresolved. The guide-miss regime marker is real; dollar-adds are flattening against +77% comps. The bear needs no new information to be right — which is also why he is fully priced.
  • There is no floor. Positive net debt, no asset value, no yield: if growth breaks, the downside is a multiple on a falling number. Hence the tight stop rather than a wider band.
  • The pivot is unaudited. The e-commerce engine — the entire second act — has never appeared in a filing. We pay zero for it and demand paper before adding.
  • Management has never bought a share in the open market. When that changes, so does the size — automatically, via the gate.

What this position reliably delivers — and what it doesn't

Reliably: exposure to an elite cash engine at a consensus-priced discount, with total risk ~0.32% of book, a 1.8% stop on the stock leg, defined risk on the option leg, and every upgrade path dated and watched.

Not reliably: the second act. The e-commerce pivot may never reach a filing; growth may fade exactly as the crowd expects.
