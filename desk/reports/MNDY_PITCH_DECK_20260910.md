## What we believe

monday.com is a real, cash-generating software business trading at a fraction of its old price. The stock is down about 62% from its 52-week high of $220.80 and sits at $84.40 (pack tape). On the company's own FY26 guidance — revenue $1,466–1,474M and adjusted free cash flow of $280–290M — the enterprise (about $2.80B, using 45.9M basic shares and $1.07B of cash and securities) is priced at roughly **1.9x sales and 9.8x free cash flow**. That is a value multiple on a business still growing revenue 22% year over year, with committed backlog growing *faster* than revenue: current RPO $750M (+27%) and total RPO $937M (+34%) ([6-K EX-99.1, 2026-08-10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001845338/000117891326003971/exhibit_99-1.htm)).

The large-customer engine is not broken. Customers paying more than $100k a year grew 37% to 2,019; more than $500k grew 68% to 114; net dollar retention in the >$100k cohort held at 115%. Management cut about 20% of headcount (announced 7/22) and took a $21.4M charge, and non-GAAP operating margin still hit a record 17%.

So the belief is narrow and specific: **the price now pays us to own a decelerating-but-durable business, and it pays us much better a rung or two lower.** We already hold 105 shares at $76.21 (cost $8,002, market value $8,862, unrealized +$860).

## What the market believes

The market believes growth is halving and the cheapness is a mirage. It has three concrete reasons, all in writing:

1. **The Q3 guide is $368–370M, +16–17%**, against +22% just delivered — and below the street's ~$372.8M. Full-year guidance was *held* at $1,466–1,474M after a ~$9M beat, which quietly cuts the second half by about $9M. Implied H2 revenue is $750–758M, roughly +17% versus H1's +23%.
2. **Cash generation is going the wrong way.** Q2 adjusted free cash flow was $52.3M — a 14.3% margin — versus $64.1M and 21.4% a year earlier. First-half FCF fell 10.7% year over year ($155.1M vs $173.6M) *on 23% revenue growth*. To hit the $280–290M guide, the second half must produce $125–135M while restructuring cash actually goes out the door.
3. **The bid is gone.** The company bought 9.6M shares for $735M in H1 at roughly $76 — about 19% of the shares out — and the $870M authorization is now **fully used with no shares available for future repurchase**. Cash and securities are down to $1.07B. The buyer that absorbed every selloff in Q1–Q2 has stopped.

The market also believes the "AI work platform" story is being used to change the subject: the headline metric is "AI ARR doubled from Q1, 17% of net new ARR" — a ratio of a ratio with no dollar base — while overall net dollar retention slipped from 110% to 109% and the >$50k cohort from 116% to 115%.

## Why we might have edge

- **We know the price history is being mis-told, in both directions.** The "unexplained collapse" version of this trade is dead (see below), but so is the tidy bear version. The 8/28→9/10 decline is about **−15.8%** off the pack tape (not the −17.6% in the original brief, which used a stale $82.65 quote), roughly 2.4x the sector ETF's −6.5%, and it *ends at* the post-print close of $84.12. The move is the round-trip of a late-August rally, not fresh damage. Knowing that keeps us from paying up for "damage" that isn't there and from selling into "collapse" that isn't there either.
- **We priced the right multiple.** The cheap-looking earnings number (about 12x) is non-GAAP: GAAP operating loss was $1.5M and GAAP EPS $0.08 versus non-GAAP $1.48. Our own bear bench claimed the free-cash-flow gap was a stock-comp add-back; that is wrong — non-GAAP operating income already excludes stock comp, so the gap is interest income on the cash pile plus deferred-revenue float. Getting that mechanism right is why our valuation leg survived audit while the bench's rebuttal of it didn't.
- **Backlog is the least-watched line.** RPO +34% against revenue +22% is not what a demand break looks like. The market is trading the guide; the contracts say something slower to turn.
- **Position math.** We hold 105 shares. That is trivial against average volume (estimated 1.2–1.6M shares/day; not in the pack). We can wait for our price without moving it.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: mostly, it is.

- Every element of our original "dislocation" case failed on primary evidence. The centerpiece — "EDGAR shows no 8-K, therefore no cause" — **cannot fire**: monday.com is a foreign private issuer that files 6-K and has never filed an 8-K in its life. That test had zero information content.
- The guide cut, the 20% headcount reduction, the exhausted authorization, and the FCF margin compression are all *filed and dated*. A stock that fires a fifth of its staff and guides growth down to the high teens is supposed to re-rate.
- $84.40 is 47% above the 52-week low of $57.50. Even taking the more favorable framing — 16.5th percentile of the 52-week range — this is not a washout print.
- Our own adjusted scenario table (below) puts expected value at **+4.6%** from here over 6–12 months. That is inside noise. The same table pays **+34%** from $66. The edge is in the *entry level*, not in the current price.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the deceleration keeps going and 1.9x sales was never cheap (monitorable).** Q3 lands at or below the $368–370M guide, Q4 guides to low teens, and net dollar retention breaks below 109%. Then the "value" multiple re-rates to 1.2–1.4x sales, which is the mid-$60s to low-$70s on today's share count. *Tripwires:* 11/09 print — revenue, overall NDR, >$100k NDR, FY27 framing. Blue's explicit exit line: FY26 revenue guided below $1,466M, or >$100k NDR below 110%.

**Failure narrative 2 — the free-cash-flow leg breaks and the whole cheapness case goes with it (monitorable).** The 11% FCF yield is the entire reason to own this. It requires $125–135M in the second half while restructuring cash lands and while management has said the ~$100M of savings is largely being reinvested in AI and product, with temporary disruption and lower retention expected. If FY26 adjusted FCF is cut, the stock is not cheap on anything — GAAP earnings are essentially interest income. *Tripwire:* any FCF guide reduction at the 11/09 print; H2 FCF margin below 18%.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** The unit we are paying for silently stops being the unit that gets sold. Work-management is billed per seat; if agents replace seats, or if a platform incumbent bundles a good-enough work OS at zero marginal price, the damage shows up as *retention that never recovers* rather than as an event. There is no filing, no permit, no contract award, no dataset we can subscribe to that fires before the revenue line does — and for a foreign private issuer there is no continuous 8-K duty, so even company-known developments can surface a quarter late at management's discretion. **Unknown-unknown class: "silent substitution of the priced unit," compounded by FPI disclosure latency.** We accept this class un-hedged; the only mitigation is position size and the willingness to be wrong at a re-derive band rather than averaging into it.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** Our own size is not the problem — 105 shares plus two planned rungs of ~35 shares is roughly 0.01% of estimated daily volume; we are invisible. The reflexive risk is the flow that *left*. For two quarters a price-insensitive buyer absorbed 9.6M shares (~19% of the float) at about $76. That buyer is gone, the authorization is exhausted, and stock-comp dilution now runs uncontested against a thinner book. That is exactly the mechanism our bear bench named (`exhausted_authorization_air_pocket`), and it has a nasty interaction with our entry plan: **without the corporate bid, gaps get bigger, so a limit at $66 may not fill at $66 — it may fill at $61 in one session, or be jumped entirely.** Two doctrine interactions cut against us as well: `null_form_test_foreign_private_issuer` (we must never again treat "no 8-K" as exculpatory here), and `stale_catalyst_reattribution` (our bear bench blamed the September decline on an 8/10 filing that the market initially rallied ~16% on — we are equally capable of blaming the next move on the last piece of paper we read). And one supply item is sitting unread: an 8/31 Form 144 (a proposed insider sale notice) ahead of the 9/2–9/3 Form 4s.

The most likely reason this loses money is that growth keeps sliding toward the low teens while the free-cash-flow guide gets cut, so the "cheap" multiple re-rates lower on a shrinking numerator, with no buyback underneath to slow the fall.
