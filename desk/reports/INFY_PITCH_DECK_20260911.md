# PITCH — INFY — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — PASS — FX-subsidised margin (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

---

## What we believe

We believe three things, and only the third is strong.

1. **The idea that got INFY on the page is dead.** The pitch started as "a −63% drawdown in a $46bn cash-generating services franchise means the market has priced terminal AI disruption." That input does not survive. The 52-week-high rail of $29.36 is arithmetically incompatible with the local listing: ADSs are 1-for-1 with equity shares, the NSE 52-week high was ₹1,728, and at 84.5–95 rupees to the dollar a $29.36 ADR implies a local price around ₹2,480 — roughly 44% above a high that never happened. The honest drawdown is local −34.4% times FX −10.6%, or about **−41%** (range −38% to −45%). The "excess drawdown" of −28pp that generated the idea was manufactured by a bad data field.
2. **At ~14.7x trailing earnings on flat-to-2% growth, the stock is roughly fair.** Not cheap, not dislocated. We cannot claim mispricing when the multiple matches the growth story.
3. **The one real deterioration in the file argues bear, not bull.** The rupee fell about 11% year-over-year into the June quarter. Management has historically quantified roughly 1.2pp of margin from a 4.9% average rupee move — about 24bp per 1% — which implies a **+250 to +400bp** currency tailwind. Reported margin went up **30bp** (20.8% → 21.1%). The gap, roughly **200–370bp of underlying operational or pricing erosion**, is the only confirmed damage signal in the court, and it is hidden by the currency rather than revealed by it.

So: we believe there is a real story here, and it is a *short-quality* story on margins that we are not equipped to trade, not a long-dislocation story.

## What the market believes

The market appears to believe INFY is a stable, low-growth, high-cash-return exporter worth mid-teens earnings: 14.5–15.4x on the local line, 14.74x on the ADR as of 2026-09-04. That is a coherent view and it is consistent with the company's own guidance of 1.5–3.0% constant-currency growth for FY27 after 2–3% for FY26 — flat at the midpoint, a halving only at the low rail.

**What we cannot say is whether we disagree with anyone.** Neither bench could pull a street consensus revenue or EPS point-and-range for the September quarter. Without that anchor we are describing the market's view from the multiple, not measuring a divergence from it. That is an asserted consensus, not a measured one, and it blocks any accept on its own.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thin, and mostly analytical rather than informational.

- **Decomposition discipline.** Very few holders of a dual-listed ADR separate the drawdown into local price, currency translation, and receipt basis. Doing that turned a "−63% catastrophe" into "−41%, most of it a rupee and a normal bear market." That is a repeatable process edge on every ADR/GDR screen we run, and it is now a detector candidate.
- **The FX-adjusted margin lens.** The street reads 21.1% and calls margins resilient. Adjusting for the currency tailwind that should have delivered 250–400bp says the underlying business gave back 200–370bp. If that view is right and not yet widely held, it is a genuine edge — but it points at avoiding or shorting the name, and we do not currently have the FY27 sensitivity disclosure needed to size it.
- **Rail integrity as a screen input.** We caught a corrupt data field before sizing. The edge is in *not* taking the trade.

That is the full list. There is no proprietary channel work, no client-check, no supply-chain read.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost everything in this file is either public or arithmetic anyone can do:

- The guidance range, the 21.1% margin, the $5,082m quarter, the $3.6bn of new deal value with 61% net-new, and the 8.2% AI revenue mix all sit in the press release exhibits of the 6-K, not just a transcript. Everyone has them.
- The rupee's slide from ~84.5 to ~94.5–95.6 is the most-watched macro series in the Indian market. Nobody is surprised by a currency tailwind to margins.
- The ADR-versus-local basis is arbitraged two ways by the depositary. Whatever gap exists today — somewhere between 0% and −8% depending on which timestamps you pair — is a plumbing and timing artifact, not a re-rating opportunity waiting to be harvested.
- The 85%-of-free-cash-flow return policy is published, and the withholding tax treatment of dividends and (post-2024) buyback consideration is on the company's own tax page. The advertised ~8.4% gross free cash flow yield is roughly **6.5–6.7% net** to a dollar holder before further currency drift. That is a fine carry and it is fully known.

The only plausibly-not-priced item is the ex-currency margin erosion, and even that is a calculation off public numbers.

## What could go wrong

*(the PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)*

**Failure narrative 1 — We enter on the reopen bands and the rupee turns.** The bull case for a re-entry requires the September quarter to print margin at or above 21.1% *with the rupee flat-to-stronger*. If instead the rupee strengthens back toward 88–90, the 250–400bp tailwind reverses into a headwind and reported margin drops toward 18.5–19.5% while nothing changes operationally. We buy an "FX-unsubsidised margin beat" that becomes an FX-punished miss two quarters later. **Tripwire: monitorable** — a USD/INR level gate and a margin-ex-FX tracker both catch it.

**Failure narrative 2 — The guide walks to the low rail and the multiple follows the growth down.** FY27 guidance is 1.5–3.0% constant currency with the first quarter at +2.4%, so the remaining nine months must run 1.2%–3.2%. If the mix drifts to the bottom, a 14.7x multiple on a 0–1% grower compresses to 12x or below — which is our own deep-value entry band at $8.18–8.92, roughly 20–25% under spot. We would have bought "fair value" into a de-rating. **Tripwire: monitorable** — the guide revision itself, plus deal-value-to-revenue conversion.

**Failure narrative 3 — the one with NO monitorable tripwire.** The instrument we would own is not the company; it is a depositary receipt whose economics depend on custody, fungibility, registration mechanics, and the tax/treaty treatment of a cross-border payout — the same layer that already handed us a corrupt 52-week-high field. A change at that layer (a depositary fee or ratio change, a fungibility restriction, an Indian regulatory change to ADS conversion, an India–US treaty rate change, a data-vendor adjustment that silently re-bases our own history again) moves the price of our claim without moving a single line of the business, and **we have no sensor that would fire on it.** We would not learn about it from the tape, from a form we watch, or from a price gate; we would learn about it from the P&L. **Unknown-unknown class: instrument-plumbing / data-rail integrity risk** — the class where the *representation* of the asset fails rather than the asset. This class already bit us once in this very file, which is the strongest possible argument that it is under-defended. Note it is compounded by a second, adjacent class we also cannot monitor: **counterparty-side demand shock in labour arbitrage** — client-captive insourcing or a visa/immigration regime change that re-prices the delivery model, with no filing, form, or price level that flags it in advance.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our size versus liquidity.* We hold zero. The proposed reopen starter is 0.75% of book, maximum 2.0%. Against an estimated ADR average daily volume of 6–12m shares (roughly $70–130m notional), that size is immaterial to price — but note the ADV figure is **estimated, not measured** (our price tooling was unreachable from both benches), and it is listed as an open item below. We cap any fill at 10% of trailing 20-day volume per day so that this stays true even if the estimate is 3x too high.
- *Predicted flow.* The flow that would be alongside us is the wrong flow. A −41% ADR drawdown on a widely-held large-cap is exactly what screen-driven dip-buyers and yield-reaching retail buy, and a re-rating requires an institutional re-underwrite that a single in-line quarter does not deliver. Meanwhile the depositary arbitrage continuously erases any basis we might be paid for. We would be adding to a crowd that cannot move the multiple.
- *Doctrine interactions.* Three of our own doctrines collide here. (a) The **excess-drawdown screen** generated this idea and will generate it again next week from the same corrupt field unless the rail validator is armed — a reflexive loop inside our own process. (b) The **print-proximity doctrine** did not trigger (the print is ~24–29 trading days out), which means the deck was written without the discipline that a near-print reconstruction would have forced. (c) The **"no accept without a measured divergence"** doctrine is what actually binds; the original brief recommended a starter position while simultaneously calling the currency split "the first job of a court" — sizing ahead of its own binding unknown. That failure mode is the real risk to this deck: we build reopen bands, the rail gets cleaned, and we treat "the data is now correct" as though it were "the thesis is now true."

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a fairly-priced, flat-growth exporter because a broken 52-week-high field made it look dislocated, and the currency that was quietly subsidising its margins turned against us before the operational erosion we already measured became visible to everyone else.

**Both courts say no entry. Size today: 0.0% of book. No orders.** What follows are the **reopen bands**, priced off the benches' own arithmetic (trailing EPS back-solved from 14.74x at $10.96 ≈ **$0.74**).

**Reopen Gate C — the quality print (blocking, event).** At the Q2 FY27 print, require **USD revenue > $5.25bn** (breaking the ~$5.08bn plateau) **and operating margin ≥ 21.1% with USD/INR flat-to-stronger (≤ 94.5 on the quarter average)**. **If A, B and C all clear:**
- **Starter: 0.75% of book, paid up to $11.50** (≈ 15.5x trailing EPS). Above $11.53 the multiple exceeds what a 1.5–3.0% grower supports — **no entry at any size above $11.55**.

**If A and B clear but C fails (margin quality confirmed deteriorating):**
