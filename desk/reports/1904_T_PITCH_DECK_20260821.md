# PITCH — 1904.T — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED  — STARTER**  (conviction 7.5/10 — buy a minimum starter at market; the original thesis is struck from the record and replaced)

## What we believe

Taisei Oncho (1904.T, Tokyo Stock Exchange Standard) is a Japanese mechanical contractor — heating, ventilation, air-conditioning and plumbing installation, mostly domestic, with small operations in the US, China and Australia. Roughly ¥61.7bn of revenue and ¥3.98bn of operating profit last fiscal year.

We believe four things, none of which is the thing the original write-up believed:

1. **The company is not a 6x-earnings bargain.** Its own forecast for the year ending March 2027 says net income will be ¥5,300m while *pre-tax ordinary profit* will be ¥3,870m. Net income cannot exceed pre-tax profit from normal operations. The gap is a one-time gain of about ¥4.0bn from selling its Oimachi headquarters building, under a contract signed 2025-11-21. Strip it out and normal earnings per share are about **¥429**, so at ¥5,130 the stock is on **12.0x recurring earnings**, not 6x. Every screener quoting "PER 6.0x, ROE 17.25%" is quoting a property sale.

2. **But the business underneath is doing better than management's own explanation says.** Management blames the guided profit decline on material and labour cost inflation. Its own first-quarter filing contradicts that: construction gross margin rose from 15.14% to **16.66%** (+152 basis points). Operating profit fell only because operating expenses rose 14.7% — a discretionary ¥1bn spend on people. That is a choice, not an erosion.

3. **Work in hand is at a record and nobody read the page it's on.** The headline said orders fell 31%. The supplementary table on page 10 of the same filing shows parent-company orders fell only 3.5%, and carried-forward work rose 14.2% to **¥56,758m** — about **89% of the ¥64.0bn full-year revenue forecast already booked at the end of Q1**. The 31% is one lumpy US order in the prior-year base.

4. **The balance sheet is genuinely unlevered and the dividend is genuinely covered.** Cash ¥17,849m plus investment securities ¥4,897m less short-term borrowings ¥3,000m = **¥19.75bn net financial assets, 62% of the ¥31.91bn market value**. Dividend ¥198 = 3.86% yield at about 46% of recurring net income.

Net: this is **fairly-priced carry with a modest, identifiable information edge**, not a dislocation. Book value ¥4,939.80 per share means we are paying **1.04x book**; when the building gain lands, book steps to roughly ¥5,390 after tax and we are at **0.95x**. We are buying a boring, cash-rich contractor with a record order book at about one times its own equity, and we are being paid 3.9% to wait.

## What the market believes

The market appears to hold two incompatible views at once, and both are wrong.

**View A (the screen buyers):** "PER 6x, ROE 17%, 0.6x book, cash fortress." This is what free screeners publish, and it is arithmetic built on a one-off building sale. Anyone buying this is buying a number that vanishes in April 2027.

**View B (the headline sellers):** "Orders down 31%, operating profit guided down 7%, ordinary profit guided down 16%." This is the narrative page of the same filing, and it is a base effect plus a discretionary spending decision.

The tape suggests neither view is under stress: ¥5,130 against a 52-week range of ¥4,050–¥5,630 — down 8.9% from the high and **up 26.7% off the low**. This is not a distressed price. There is no forced seller, no dislocation, no crowding. It is an uncovered small cap that nobody is thinking about very hard in either direction.

## Why we might have edge

The edge is narrow, specific, and comes from reading two pages of one PDF that neither the screeners nor, apparently, the sell side opened.

- **The supplementary information table (page 10) is the leading indicator for a contractor and it is not in any database.** Revenue for a construction firm is recognised as work progresses, so carried-forward backlog tells you next year's revenue with far more reliability than this quarter's orders headline. Record backlog at +14.2%, covering 89% of the revenue guide at Q1, is a fact that exists only in a Japanese-language PDF table.
- **Management's stated reason for the profit decline is contradicted by its own filed margin.** If the ¥1bn people-spend is a one-year investment rather than a permanent cost base, recurring earnings normalise upward from ¥429 without any revenue help. That is a real, testable divergence from the company's own guidance — and the company is the only "analyst" here.
- **The orders headline is a trap we can see through.** Consolidated −31% vs parent −3.5% is exactly the kind of number that makes a screen-driven holder sell. If the market takes the headline at face value into the November print, the divergence is monetisable.
- **Nobody covers it.** No broker research, no conference calls in English, no ADR. Uncovered names can stay mispriced in both directions for a long time — which is also the risk (below).

Honest calibration: this is an edge of the "we read the filing carefully" kind, not the "we know something unknowable" kind. It supports a starter. It does not support conviction sizing.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because several of these are strong:

- **1.04x book is not cheap for a Japanese contractor.** A large fraction of the TSE Standard market trades at or below book. We are not getting an asset discount; we are paying full book for an average-quality construction business.
- **The EV/EBIT of 3.3x is flattered by cash that isn't fully ours.** ¥6,699m of the ¥22,746m cash-and-securities pile is *customer advances on uncompleted contracts* — money paid up front for work not yet done. Net of that, financial assets are ~¥13.0bn (41% of market value), still good, but the "62% of the cap in net cash" figure overstates it. Also, ¥3.0bn of short-term borrowing appeared during Q1 from zero at year-end — working-capital swings are real and seasonal.
- **Selling the headquarters converts a free asset into a permanent bill.** The company owned its Oimachi building; it is moving to leased space at OIMACHI TRACKS in autumn 2026. The ¥4bn gain is one-time; the rent is forever. **Neither of our benches quantified this**, and it pushes recurring EPS below ¥429 by an amount we have not yet sized. This is the single most likely reason the stock is *correctly* priced at 12x.
- **The book-value step is fully public.** The disposal was disclosed in November 2025. Anyone can see it. There is no private information in the 0.95x post-gain book figure.
- **Orphan stocks stay orphaned.** "Cheap, ignored, fine business" is the classic Japanese value trap. There is no catalyst that forces the market to re-rate this — no activist, no buyback announcement, no going-private transaction (see below), no index inclusion.
- **The China segment is structurally negative.** Revenue −42.7% to ¥106m with the operating loss widening to −¥118m. Small, but it is deteriorating, not stabilising.

## What remains unverified

Treated as a work queue, not as footnotes. Ranked by how much each could move the decision.

1. **The rent bill after the headquarters sale.** Unquantified by both benches. Converting an owned building into leased space adds a permanent operating cost that lowers recurring EPS below ¥429. If it is large, 12.0x becomes 13–14x and the starter is a mistake. **Highest-priority open item.**
2. **Whether the ¥1bn staff spend is a single investment year or a new permanent cost base.** Determines whether recurring earnings normalise upward.
3. **The headquarters transfer date.** Disclosed as undecided; management holds the guidance and is moving this autumn, so it expects delivery this fiscal year. Unverifiable until it is announced.
4. **Tax rate applied to the gain.** Red used 32%, blue used 31%; we used blue's. The difference is ~¥40m of net income and ~¥6 of book value per share — immaterial, but the assumption is ours, not the company's.
5. **Backlog margin quality.** Record work in hand is only bullish if it was bid at reasonable margins. The filing discloses the amount, not the profitability. The +152bp gross margin is one quarter of evidence pointing the right way; it is one quarter.
6. **Taisei Oncho USA's project addresses.** Not disclosed. With them, the desk's US permit connectors become usable on the ¥2.84bn/quarter US segment, which currently we can only read at the segment line. This is the specific missing evidence that would turn three "uncheckable" detectors into live ones.
7. **The half-year report date.** 2026-11-13 is inferred from prior-year cadence, not confirmed by the company. Treat the dated pack accordingly.
8. **Average daily volume and free float.** Not pulled. Our sizing constraint is therefore an estimate. Before the second add, verify that the ladder stays under 1% of average daily volume.
9. **Whether the two full-year filings were read directly.** The red bench worked from aggregators and admitted it; the blue bench read the Q1 filing byte-for-byte. The full-year FY3/26 filing has still not been opened directly by either bench.
10. **Any read on the ¥1,031m of goodwill** beyond "legacy, amortising ¥25.6m/quarter." Not tested for impairment risk.
