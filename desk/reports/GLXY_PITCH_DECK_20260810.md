## What we believe

Galaxy Digital is two businesses wearing one stock ticker. The first is a crypto trading and asset-management franchise. The second — and the reason this case exists — is Helios, a giant data-center campus in Dickens County, Texas, being built and leased to a single customer: CoreWeave, an AI cloud-computing company.

The original pitch was that the market is pricing Galaxy as a crypto company in a crypto downturn (the stock is down 57% from its high of $45.92, trading around $19.50–19.85), while ignoring that a large, contracted, rent-paying data-center business is switching on underneath it. CoreWeave has committed to lease the full ~800 megawatt approved campus on a roughly 15-year lease averaging over $1 billion per year, with CoreWeave bearing most operating costs — a finding our second bench added that neither the original brief nor the first bench had fully credited. Phase I (133 MW) has been delivered; a much larger Phase II is under construction, funded by $3.507 billion of newly issued bonds ([pricing press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/galaxy-digital-inc-announces-pricing-of-3-507-billion-of-senior-secured-notes-302833899.html)) plus a separate $1.4 billion construction loan for Phase I ([press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/galaxy-closes-1-4-billion-project-financing-facility-to-accelerate-helios-ai-datacenter-development-302530804.html)).

What we now believe, after the court fight: the data-center story is real but it is **entirely a bet on CoreWeave staying solvent**. In the near term, owning Galaxy stock is close to owning CoreWeave's credit risk with extra steps. CoreWeave is visibly distressed — credit markets imply roughly 50% odds of default within five years, and a recent $2.6 billion financing attempt failed to find buyers. CoreWeave reports earnings tomorrow. Buying the day before that report, with none of our monitoring in place, is not a trade — it's a coin flip.

## What the market believes

The market is treating Galaxy primarily as a crypto company caught in a crypto drawdown, with a growing discount for the CoreWeave concentration. The stock fell only about 5% on Galaxy's own second-quarter results (Aug 5), and has already bounced roughly 19–21% off its 52-week low of $16.43 — so the "nobody has noticed the data center" version of the story is at best only partially true. Some of the dislocation has already been bought.

The market cap is roughly $7.8 billion (391.4 million shares). Against that: about $7.2 billion of total debt after the new bond deal, and $2.459 billion of cash — of which roughly $1.6 billion appears to be held in stablecoins, meaning even the cash cushion carries crypto-infrastructure risk (this split is from a secondary source and not yet confirmed against the filing itself).

## Why we might have edge

1. **The bond structure is more protective than the bear case claimed.** Our own first bench tried to kill this name on the argument that the new bonds' ~$487M/year of debt service swamps the ~$320M/year of Phase I rent. The second bench showed this was an accounting error: Phase I rent services Phase I's *separate* $1.4B loan, the new bonds pre-funded their own interest reserve out of the proceeds, and principal repayment doesn't begin until at least 10 months after Phase II is finished. When Phase II turns on, contracted rent should exceed the bond coupon. The strongest public bear argument in circulation (e.g. the [$346M coupon math](https://cryptoslate.com/the-brutal-346m-math-behind-galaxys-high-stakes-race-to-build-coreweaves-texas-ai-mega-center/)) makes a version of this same silo mistake.
2. **The contracted base is bigger than either bench initially credited.** CoreWeave's commitment covers the full 800 MW approved campus; the "830 MW unleased" figure floating around refers to *additional, newly approved* capacity beyond that.
3. **The bonds are largely walled off from the parent.** The notes are obligations of the project entity, secured by the project's assets — not a general claim on Galaxy. If the project fails, Galaxy's direct loss is its completion obligation plus its equity in the project (~15% of cost), not the $3.5B principal.
4. **There is a defined research path to an answer.** The remaining questions (does the parent guarantee survive if CoreWeave walks away? how big is the pre-funded reserve? does the Phase I loan cross-default?) are answerable by reading the bond offering documents. That is cheap, decisive work.

## Why it might be priced in

- The stock has already bounced ~20% off its low, and dropped only 5% on its own earnings — the market is not asleep on this story.
- The bull case's central premise — that Galaxy trades with a crypto peer group rather than a data-center peer group — was **never verified**. The comparison data we needed was inaccessible to both benches. The original 8-out-of-10 conviction score rested on this unverified premise; by the brief's own sensitivity math, if it's wrong the score drops to ~4.
- The real risk transmission isn't the coupon math the bears cite — it's (a) an uncapped parent guarantee obligating Galaxy to *finish building* Phase II for a possibly-defaulting tenant, and (b) the Phase I loan maturing around 2028, which must be refinanced against whatever CoreWeave's credit looks like then. Neither has a visible floor price attached today.
- CoreWeave reports in ~24 hours. Whatever edge exists is dominated, right now, by a binary event we have no differentiated view on.
