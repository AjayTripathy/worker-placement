# PITCH — SUJA — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: BUY a small first tranche now (~⅓ of intended size, at or below $6.43 — the top of the sponsor's own August buy range), with the remaining ⅔ deliberately held back until the November 2 lockup expiry / November 10 earnings collision resolves. ## What we believe

Suja Life (juice and healthy-soda brands, IPO'd January 2026 at $21, now $6.48) is cheaper than its business deserves, and the reason it looks expensive-to-fairly-priced on the surface is an accounting-of-effort illusion, not a broken company.

The stock fell 46% in one session on a soft Q2 and "near-term softness concentrated in the grocery channel." The bear reading is that management's full-year profit guide ($70–72M of adjusted EBITDA on $360–369M of sales) requires a miraculous second-half margin jump — roughly 8.5 points of improvement over the same half last year — and should not be believed a week after a crash.

Our review found that the "miracle" is actually simple arithmetic. The IPO prospectus discloses that in fiscal 2025 the company's new-brands segment (chiefly the Slice healthy-soda relaunch) **lost $26M**, described in the filing as deliberate start-up investment — launch costs, initial distribution, consumer trials — and, importantly, those costs were *not* excused out of the adjusted-EBITDA figure. Strip that launch drag out and the core Suja business earned roughly **$66.5M, about a 21% margin, in fiscal 2025**. This year's $70–72M guide therefore only requires the core business to hold steady while last year's one-time launch spending rolls off — which is exactly what the company's own Q2 release credits for its improving costs ("lapping of one-time startup costs in Emerging Brands"). The prospectus is here: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001934114/000110465926057504/tm2530822-13_424b4.htm

If the guide is arithmetic rather than hope, then at $6.48 the company trades at roughly 5.4–6.0x this year's guided EBITDA (about 7.7x after burdening the value with a large tax-payment obligation owed to the pre-IPO owner, described below) — a genuine discount for a 46.7% gross-margin consumer brand growing sales.

Our independent consumer-demand lane corroborates the demand side with one caveat: search and channel data show core Suja demand up ~6.6% year over year (consistent with the shift out of grocery), but interest in Slice looks **spent** (down ~64.8% year over year), which trims how much the new brand can contribute in Q4.

## What the market believes

At $6.48, down 65% from the $18.48 high and 16% off the $5.59 low, the market is pricing some combination of:

1. **The guide is fiction.** The stock crashed on the very quarter that made the second-half math look heroic; buyers a week later are catching a knife in front of a possible guide-down.
2. **A wall of stock is coming.** About 27.8M shares — **256% of the freely trading float** — unlock around November 2, one week before the next earnings report. Two banks (Goldman Sachs and Jefferies) can waive that lockup early, at their sole discretion, at any time.
3. **The private-equity seller has to get out.** Paine Schwartz owns ~14.8M exchangeable units it must eventually sell through a 10.85M-share float, and a tax receivable agreement pays it up to ~$163.6M more (two-thirds of the current market cap) as it exits.
4. **Legal overhang.** A Section 11 investigation related to the IPO was announced August 12.

Notably, the market does *not* lack coverage: four analysts initiated in June (Goldman $31, Jefferies $25, Evercore $24 targets). The bearishness is positioning and supply-driven — short interest reportedly rose 75.5% in June (secondary source, unverified) — not obscurity.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the segment footnote the sellers apparently didn't.** The entire "impossible margin jump" bear case dissolves once the $26M disclosed launch drag is separated from the core business. Our own bear-side reviewer initially made this exact mistake — treating last year's 9.6% second-half margin as the run rate — and named the decomposition as the thing that would change its mind without running it. Our bull-side reviewer ran it, from the primary prospectus. For the bear case ($56–58M of EBITDA this year) to be right, the company would have to *repeat* launch-level spending it has explicitly said it is lapping.
- **The crash was one week ago; the correcting disclosure has existed since January.** This is a "read the filings while the tape panics" situation, and the tell that the panic overshot is that the company held its full-year guide on the same release that caused the crash (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001934114/000162828026052600/sujalifeq22026earningsrele.htm).
- **We have independent demand data.** Our consumer-products tracking (built this month) reads core-brand demand as intact and shifting channels, not shrinking — a check most sellers of a $250M-cap recent IPO won't have.
- **The structural negatives are dated and watchable.** The lockup has a known date, the waiver has known holders, and early supply must arrive via filings we monitor daily. Supply risk of known timing is a reason to stage purchases, not to abstain.

- **Four covering analysts already carry $24–31 targets.** "The guide is achievable" is not a secret thesis; the *Street's* view is closer to ours than the tape is. What the tape is pricing is supply, and the supply is real: 256% of float in eleven weeks is not a subtle overhang.

## THE BODEGA DISTRIBUTION QUESTION — why it's load-bearing (methodology note)

Two models move juice into an independent's fridge, and the answer sorts the whole thesis:
- **DSD (direct store delivery):** distributor truck on a route, rep stocks/rotates, **credits back expired bottles** — owner bears no shrink, holds real depth.
- **Cash-and-carry:** owner buys cases at a wholesaler with their own cash and **eats the shrink** — rational owners stock a 90-day HPP product shallow.

Mapped to SUJA's fork ("channel shift, not share loss"): the WF Oakland audit produced the physical signature of a distribution transition (sold-out shelf, no refill). The bodega answers extend or kill that read:
1. "Truck comes weekly, rep's solid" → independents healthy; softness is grocery-specific or demand. Neutral-to-bearish for the transition excuse.
2. "Switched distributors / rep stopped coming / we run out" → **fill-rate hypothesis confirmed at the distribution layer** — operational, fixable, management's story gains evidence. Clock attached: unreliable suppliers lose bodega cooler slots to competitors within months.
3. "I buy it myself" → the owner IS the demand signal; follow-up = has the WHOLESALER had it in stock (upstream out-of-stocks = supply problem above store level).

Plus "sells vs sits" = per-SKU velocity from someone with their own cash in the fridge — which flavors move, do $3 shots outsell $8 juices (margin mix), what they'd drop. Unpurchasable data.

**The script:** "Who do you get Suja from — truck or pick it up yourself?" · "They keep you stocked or do you run out?" · "What happens when bottles expire — do they credit you?" (silently reveals the model: credits = DSD) · "Which ones actually sell?"

**Sizing implication regardless of branch:** if the indie channel is cash-and-carry, bodega depth is STRUCTURALLY capped for a 90-day product — bounds how far "independent channel strength" can scale; input for the November add.
