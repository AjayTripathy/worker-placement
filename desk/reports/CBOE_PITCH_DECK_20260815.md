# PITCH — CBOE — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY at the current price (~$293). Move to WATCH with two reopen triggers: price at or below $255–265, or the September 3 August-volume report confirming index-options growth held through a full calm-VIX month.**

## What we believe

Cboe is a genuinely excellent business — the owner of the S&P 500 (SPX) and VIX options franchises, with a fast-growing data subscription arm (Data Vantage, guided to low-teens growth and raised last quarter). The daily-expiration options boom (0DTE, XSP) is real customer adoption, not a fad, and July's index-options volume was up 34% year over year.

But the last twelve months of earnings — adjusted EPS of $12.99 — were earned during a stretch of historically violent markets. When markets are turbulent, options trading explodes and Cboe's revenue explodes with it. That turbulence has now ended: the VIX (the market's fear gauge, which Cboe itself publishes) sat at 14.5 in mid-August, the calmest reading of 2026. Both of our review benches agree that a fair "normal-conditions" earnings number is roughly **$11–12 per share**, not $12.99. At ~$293–296, the stock trades at 24.7–27x that normalized figure — the very top of its own historical 22–25x range. The stock is down 21% from its high, but that decline is the froth coming out of the multiple, not a bargain appearing.

So: great company, wrong price, and one cheap, dated piece of evidence (the September 3 volume report) about to tell us whether we're wrong.

## What the market believes

The market is treating the drawdown as largely done and the earnings as durable. Fourteen analysts cover the stock, it's an S&P 500 member, and reported estimate revisions have been upward (we could not independently verify that claim and gave it no weight — but nothing suggests downward revisions either). The market's implicit position: the shift to daily-expiration index options is a permanent step-change in how people trade, so the elevated earnings are the new baseline, and ~23x trailing earnings is reasonable for a toll-booth business of this quality.

One notable dissent from the company itself: **Cboe's own board repurchased stock at an average of $256.61 in the second quarter** — about 13% below today's price. The issuer's revealed opinion of fair value sits below the market's.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We dated the earnings, not just measured them.** The entire jump in trailing earnings sits in the specific quarters when markets were wildest (Q1-26 adjusted EPS $3.70 and Q2-26 $3.56, versus $2.67 in Q3-25). First-half 2026 adjusted EPS was up 47% year over year. Meanwhile the input that drove it — market turbulence — has already reverted to a 2026 low. The earnings haven't caught down yet. That's a timing asymmetry: we can simply wait for the September 3 report, which covers the first full month traded at VIX ~14.5, and learn the answer nearly for free.
2. **The court process caught and corrected its own worst error** (detailed below), so the surviving valuation bracket ($11–12 normalized) is one that both an attacking bench and a defending bench signed off on. It is deliberately built to be fair to the bull case — it grants 10–13% structural growth on top of a record year.
3. **A watch-item the market likely isn't tracking:** management is guiding to $838–853M in operating expenses, justified partly by a workforce reduction — while job postings at headquarters have been accelerating (50 open roles, including senior AI-engineering hires). If the savings are being quietly re-absorbed, it shows up in the October 30 report's expense line.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the other side, and it's strong:

- **The "windfall" might actually be a ratchet.** July's +34% index-options volume printed while volatility was *already falling*. If August confirms 20%+ growth in a fully calm month, the bull case wins the argument: customers who adopted daily options during the storm kept trading them in the calm, the $12+ earnings base is real, and today's ~$290 price is defensible. Our own red (attacking) bench conceded exactly this in its "what would change my mind" section.
- **Nothing here is undiscovered.** Fourteen analysts, an index member, a widely publicized 0DTE story. The 2032 expiration of the exclusive SPX license — which our red bench raised as a risk — has been public since **March 2013**; thirteen years of models already contain it.
- **The apparent share loss in the equities business is softer than it first looked.** Cboe's own market-wide on-exchange volume figure fell 12.4%, but Cboe's multi-listed options volume actually *grew* 28% — the honest read is "losing share of a growing pie," which is a much weaker bear point than "shrinking."
