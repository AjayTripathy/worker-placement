Western Alliance (WAL)

Edge — scar-discount retirement$83.65 · 52w $65.83–96.57

The claim. The market still prices WAL off its 2023 identity — the fragile venture-deposit bank that nearly ran — while the audited numbers describe a diversified $82B franchise printing the best returns in its peer group. That divergence is the alpha, with an expected two-to-three-quarter re-rate as consecutive clean prints retire the scar.

What we verified from the Q2 release and 10-Q. Return on tangible common equity 15.3%. Tangible book $63.24/share, up 13.2% in a year. CET1 capital 11.0%. Deposits up 15.1% year over year. At $83.65 the stock trades at 1.31× tangible book and about 8× forward earnings. The peer comparison is what makes this an edge rather than fair pricing: Zions trades at 1.55× on lower returns, Comerica at 1.70×, East West at 2.05× on similar returns. WAL is the cheapest of the set while earning the most. Our base fair value of $98 is nothing more exotic than Zions-parity on WAL's own tangible book; bull $106, bear $70.

Red team — sustained with a haircut (7 → 6.5)

The adversarial pass could not break the thesis but corrected the record in three places, and about a third of the discount turns out to be earned, not scar:

  • Fraud losses last quarter, omitted from the court record. Q1-2026 charge-offs printed 1.45% unadjusted — $208.5M including $152.5M of explicitly fraud-related losses on the Leucadia and Cantor fund-finance loans. The clean 0.37% the court quoted is the adjusted series. Recent counterparty surprise in exactly the lending engine (commercial & industrial, +$4.8B of growth) driving the franchise.
  • Uninsured deposits are rising, not falling. FDIC data: 28.2% → 36.5% of deposits over five quarters ($30.4B). The level is roughly peer-normal and far below the ~55% pre-crisis configuration — but the growth the court credited is coming disproportionately from the deposit type that ran in 2023. Deposits also fell 1.0% in the quarter; the +15.1% is year-over-year only.
  • Say "criticized improving, nonperformers rising" — not "normalizing." Nonperforming loans climbed 0.76% → 0.92% over the year while special-mention and classified-accruing improved; reserve coverage of nonaccruals slipped to 96%.

FS Bancorp (FSBW)

Fair risk + mean-reversion optionality$43.65 · 52w $36.20–45.85

The claim. An under-followed Puget Sound community bank at 1.00× book ($43.57) with a five-year record of 11–16% returns on equity, currently earning a cycle-trough 9.9%. You are paying book for a franchise that has historically earned well above its cost of capital; if returns mean-revert you get paid, and if they don't you own it at book. The 7.3M-share float is why the market leaves it here — and why size is capped, not conviction.

The court's worry, resolved in our favor. The screen flagged deposits down 7.2% in the quarter as the wobble to watch. The red team decomposed it from the earnings 8-K: $201M of brokered CDs rolled off by design (12.5% → 5.2% of deposits) while retail deposits actually grew $12M and the cost of deposits fell. That is funding hygiene, not flight — the original kill trigger would have false-fired on good management, so it now reads retail-only.

Red team — sustained at small size (red 4/10)

  • First construction charge-off landed: $2.3M on a commercial construction loan, driven by office-market appraisals. The remaining office-construction book ($7.2M) is entirely nonperforming — bounded at 1.7% of construction, but the watch is now armed.
  • Construction concentration sits at ~105% of bank capital — above the 100% regulatory guidance line that draws supervisory attention (total CRE ~219% vs the 300% line). This caps growth appetite and justifies the small sizing by itself.
  • The balance sheet is now wholesale-supplemented: loans/deposits jumped to 108.6%. Mitigant: capital is thick (CET1 11.3%, tangible book +10.2% YoY through buybacks).

Kalmar (KALMAR.HE)

Quality at a fair price€38.40 · ~3% off the 52w low

The claim. The container-handling equipment maker spun out of Cargotec, orphaned in the lower third of its range at ~14× earnings for a 24% return-on-capital business with essentially zero net debt, 99% cash conversion, and a reaffirmed >12.5% margin guide. This is the TINA case — for quality, fair value is the entry — with the cycle as the honestly-priced risk, not a hidden one.

The owed item, resolved clean. The court sized this off "net debt €13M" with the balance-sheet line still owed. The red team pulled the H1 report: total interest-bearing debt €295M (including €91M of lease liabilities) against €280M cash — net debt €13M inclusive of leases, gearing 1.8%, €480M of liquidity. The number survives the strictest read; the fair-value band stands.

Red team — sustained (red 4/10), bear re-marked

  • One strike on the cycle. Q2 book-to-bill was 0.94, not the 1.0 the court quoted (that was the H1 ratio); the order book is −4% YoY and Q2 sales growth partly drew it down. Industry throughput forecasts have Q2 running at +2%, below the court's glide path. A new tripwire: two consecutive quarters below 0.95 forces a re-court — Q2 is strike one.
  • The recession bear is €24–27, not €31. €31 survives as the mild-deceleration bear; a 2020-style −15/20% equipment year with 25–30% decrementals, half-offset by the €49M savings program, marks the true-recession branch at €24–27. Size as carry, not edge.
  • No evidence of Chinese share loss — electric units rose to 12% of equipment orders and Kalmar sold four electric reachstackers into China. The eco-order tripwire (<35%) stays armed at 40%.
  • Governance churn the court missed: the CFO leaves Sept-30 and the Services president hands over Sept-1 — two transitions inside the exact half-year the margin guide depends on. Watch the Oct-29 Q3 report.

Verallia (VRLA.PA)

Deleveraging carry, band now live€18.96 · 52w €15.50–29.00 · −2.7% today

The claim. Europe's #1 glass-packaging maker near its 52-week low, 38% below the €30/share its own controlling shareholder (BWGI) offered in last year's tender, on trough margins the market extrapolates as permanent. The trade doesn't need the old 24–25% margins back: at a ~21% structural margin you collect roughly 10%/year of enterprise-value transfer from debt to equity plus a 5.4% dividend, with the controller's own €30 mark as a soft floor.

Tonight's H1 print — the court's binary — passed all four bars. The court refused entry today precisely because H1 was printing tonight. It did, at 18:45 CET, and every kill-bar passed: volumes stable (bar: not negative) · margin 20.7%, up 33bps (bar: hold above 19.9%) · leverage down to 2.6× with nothing significant maturing before 2028 (bar: below 3.0×) · full-year guidance confirmed. Free cash flow of €102M ran 54% ahead of last year — the deleveraging engine is ahead of plan. The wait-then-enter verdict converts to enter.

Red team — attack failed (red 3/10), two legs re-shaped

  • The headline is the opportunity: net income halved to €27M — on €43M of disclosed one-off restructuring (€70M ex-item). "Profit halved" wires plus tomorrow's 9:00 CET analyst call could gap the stock from €18.96 into our band. The ladder is resting where the headline sellers exit.
  • The covenant fear is refuted: the sustainability-linked financing carries a 5.0× leverage covenant, tested semi-annually — nowhere near 2.6× (medium confidence: press summaries, not the registration document itself).
  • Float verified at 12.26% (~€292M) — the court's correction of the screen's €500M was right. Size stays capped, limits only.
  • The exit catalyst is deferred, and that changes the trade's shape: BWGI committed to no squeeze-out and a maintained listing for three years (~mid-2028), and its ownership creep via scrip dividends is only ~0.4pp/year. Minority holders are confirmed not-trapped — but this is a carry-and-re-rate position, not a tender play. Next catalyst: Capital Markets Day, Nov 18.
  • New fragility, stated by the company: guidance is explicitly conditioned on no material Middle-East deterioration.
