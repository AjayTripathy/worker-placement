# PITCH — ESRT — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no purchase today: stay flat, arm the reopen gates below, because at $4.77 the stock sits inside our own $4.10–$6.40 value band and the two facts that would settle the argument (is the Observatory losing customers or losing a tourism cycle; is the 87.4-million-share registration a sale or a formality) are both unread.

---

## What we believe

Empire State Realty Trust owns the Empire State Building and a collection of pre-war Manhattan office buildings it has spent a decade renovating. It has two businesses: renting offices, and selling tickets to the observation deck.

Four things we believe, in order of how sure we are:

1. **The observation deck is deteriorating faster than the headline suggests, and faster than last quarter.** Revenue fell 20.1% in Q1 and 28.5% in Q2 versus the same quarters a year earlier. But profit fell much harder: deck operating income was $12.4M this quarter against $24.1M last year, down 48.5%. The margin went from 71.1% to 51.2% in twelve months. That is what happens when visitor counts drop against a cost base that does not shrink — you keep the elevators, the staff and the insurance whether 3 million or 2 million people come through.

2. **The office side is not the clean, undamaged half we originally described.** Leasing *volume* is genuinely strong — the leased percentage rose from 93.2% to 94.9%. But the number that measures whether the same buildings earn more cash than a year ago went from +1.3% to **−3.2%**. Volume up, economics down.

3. **Management's own 2026 forecast says the second half will earn less per share than the first half — during the deck's busiest season.** They earned $0.20 in Q1 and $0.21 in Q2 ($0.41 for the half) and guide the full year to $0.75–$0.79. That leaves $0.34–$0.38 for the back half, or $0.17–$0.19 a quarter, below what they just printed, in the six months that contain the summer and the holidays. Last year the second half of the deck earned $51.0M against $39.1M in the first half. So the seasonal tailwind is real and the guide still goes down through it.

4. **After all of that, the stock is roughly fairly priced — not cheap, not expensive.** Working from the balance sheet: 173.1M listed shares plus the 39.0% of the operating partnership held outside the listed company gives ~283.8M total units. At $4.77 that is $1.353B of equity and about $3.51B of enterprise value against $2,240,571K of debt. Subtract the deck at 8× a $55M profit assumption ($440M) and you are paying about a 7.5% yield on roughly $230M of non-deck property income. Move that yield and you get: 8.0% → **$4.08**; 7.5% → **$4.77**; 7.0% → **$5.53**; 6.5% → **$6.42**. Renovated, non-trophy pre-war Manhattan changes hands in roughly the 6.5%–8.0% range. **The tape is inside the range, not through it.** Our original claim that this trades "well wide of recent Manhattan comparables" was wrong, and we are withdrawing it.

Net: this is a fairly-priced asset with one unresolved binary attached (is the deck's decline cyclical or permanent), and our rule is that we do not buy fair with an unresolved binary. We buy fair when fairness has been *verified*. It has not been.

## What the market believes

The sell side has not followed the stock down. Before the Q2 print, consensus for 2026 was **$0.86** per share — above the company's *old* guide, let alone the cut one. After the print, price targets moved from $8 to $7, with an average of **$7.26** across five analysts and a low of **$6.30**. Even the most bearish published target is 32% above today's price.

Read backwards, those targets require something close to a 6.0%–6.25% yield on the office portfolio *and* a recovery in observation-deck traffic. Neither is impossible. Both are assumptions, not observations.

The tape disagrees with the analysts. The stock is down 40% from its 52-week high of $7.94 and sits 3.5% above its 52-week low of $4.61 — a month after the guidance cut, it has not bounced. When published targets and the tape are 50% apart, one of them is carrying a stale number. In this case we think it is the targets: consensus was still modeling $0.86 against a company guide of $0.75–$0.79.

**We agree with the tape, not the targets.** That is an uncomfortable place to be when writing a buy pitch, and it is why this deck does not propose a buy.

## Why we might have edge

Four specific things we did that the published research appears not to have done:

- **We decomposed the quarters instead of reading the headline.** The company's press release reports the deck's second quarter. We backed out the first quarter from the six-month figures and found the decline *accelerating* (−20.1% → −28.5%), not stabilizing. The published notes we can see treat $55M as a trough. It is a waypoint — the first half already produced only $23.1M of deck profit.

- **We ran the guidance arithmetic against the seasonal calendar.** The industry headline was "ESRT cuts 2026 outlook by $0.10." The actual signal inside the cut is that the implied back-half quarterly run-rate falls *below* the quarter just reported, through the strongest season. Nobody frames a cut that way, so the seasonal inversion goes unpriced. We have written this up as a reusable screen.

- **We sized the share registration.** On July 30 — one day after the stock gapped down 10% — the company filed a shelf registration and two prospectus supplements. The main prospectus is a 1MB document neither of our reviewers could read. But the filing-fee exhibit is two pages and says exactly what is registered: **87,412,804 Class A shares, $487,763,446, priced off a $5.58 reference from July 24, fee of $67,360 paid.** That is 50.5% of the listed share count and 30.8% of all units, registered the day after a crash. Whether it is legacy partners obtaining the right to sell (an overhang) or the company issuing new stock (real dilution) is the single largest unread fact in this file.

- **We found the accounting metric that is doing the work.** Every number in the original write-up, and every number in the analyst notes, is FFO — a REIT profit measure that adds back depreciation. It does *not* subtract the cash the landlord pays to fit out a tenant's space. ESRT just signed over 250,000 square feet at an average 12-year term in buildings undergoing repositioning. Those leases consume cash on day one and pay it back over a decade. The cash-adjusted measure (AFFO) appears nowhere in the company's release, nowhere in the analyst notes, and — honestly — nowhere in our own work either. We flagged the hole; we did not fill it.

There is also a free, dated, monthly read available on the central question that neither we nor the street has used: U.S. government overseas-arrivals data for New York, Port Authority international passenger counts, and hotel revenue-per-room for New York City. If tourists are returning to the city while the Empire State Building's traffic keeps falling, the deck is losing share to newer competitors and this is a permanent impairment. If arrivals are also down, it is a cycle. That question is answerable before the next earnings report, at zero cost.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- **Down 40%, sitting on the low.** A stock does not fall from $7.94 to $4.77 and hold there because nobody noticed the deck. The market has repriced this.
- **Our own valuation says fair.** Our $4.10–$6.40 range centers near $5.30 against a $4.77 tape. That is roughly 11% of upside to the midpoint — inside the error bars of a cap-rate assumption we cannot verify.
- **The refinancing risk is well known and smaller than we first said.** Our own first pass claimed $38M a year of extra interest. That applied a 170-basis-point increase to the *entire* $2.24B of debt, most of which is not maturing. Only $738M matures 2027–2029 ($159.3M / $149.6M / $428.9M). And the company's actual borrowing cost as of July is 5.99% on $130M of new notes, not the 6.4% we assumed. The correct figure is roughly **$9.5M a year by 2029, about $0.03 a share** — and part of that step is already inside the 2026 guide, so counting it again would be double-counting. This is a known, small, consensus item.
- **The deck decline is in every headline.** "Observatory revenue down 28.5%" is not a discovered fact; it is the reason the stock is at $4.77.
- **The one genuinely undiscovered item cuts against us.** The share registration is an overhang, not a hidden asset.

The most likely truth is that the market has this approximately right and we have no edge worth capital today. That is not a failure of the analysis — it is the analysis.

## What could go wrong

*(This section applies to the position we would take if the reopen gates below trigger — a 0.25% starter near $4.10–$4.30 — not to a position we hold today. We hold nothing.)*

**Failure narrative 1 — The deck is losing customers, not waiting for them.** *(Monitorable.)* Two newer observation decks opened in Manhattan in the last few years with better sightlines and newer marketing. If the Empire State Building's traffic is falling while New York's overall visitor count rises, the $55M profit assumption is not a trough — it is a stop on the way to $35M, and the $61.4M of goodwill on the balance sheet goes to zero. Note that the segment already loses money on a full accounting basis: −$2.3M in the quarter and −$4.5M for the half, versus profits a year ago. **Tripwire: two consecutive months of New York international arrivals up year-over-year while Empire State Building app-review velocity is still down.** If that pattern appears, this is a permanent pass, not a lower entry.

**Failure narrative 2 — The registration is a sale, and we are the bid underneath it.** *(Partially monitorable.)* 87.4 million shares — half the listed float — were registered a day after the crash. If those are long-time partnership holders who now intend to sell, and we are buying at $4.10, we are absorbing supply we ourselves predicted. Partnership-unit holders exchanging into stock and selling do not always generate a timely public filing, so we would learn about it from the tape before we learned it from a form. **Tripwire: partial only** — ownership filings and insider forms catch some of it, late.

**Failure narrative 3 — The profit we underwrote never becomes cash.** ***(NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)*** Every number in this deck — ours, the company's, the street's — is FFO, which ignores the money a landlord spends to build out a tenant's space. ESRT is signing long leases in buildings being repositioned, which is exactly the situation where the cash cost per square foot is highest. If tenant-improvement and leasing-commission spending runs $80–100M a year against $230M of property income, then the "6.3× earnings" framing is fiction and the dividend is being funded from the balance sheet. **There is no tripwire because the disclosure does not exist at quarterly frequency** — per-square-foot leasing costs are not in the earnings release and were not in the portions of the quarterly report we retrieved. We would learn this at the annual report, if then, after four quarters of being wrong.

> **Unknown-unknown class named: *metric decoupling.* This is the family of failures where the number everyone quotes (FFO) is structurally disconnected from the number that pays you (cash), the disconnection is caused by an activity management is publicly celebrating (record leasing), and the reconciling item is disclosed annually or not at all. We cannot enumerate what lives in this class for ESRT, only that it is the class we are exposed to and cannot watch.**

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own predicted flow, and our own rules working against each other:**

- **Our size is not the problem; our timing is.** A 0.25% starter is a small order against a $1.35B-equity, NYSE-listed REIT, and would not move the price. *(Average daily volume is not in the evidence pack and we have not verified it — flagged as unverified below. The claim "our order is small relative to ADV" is an assumption, not a measurement.)*
- **The flow problem runs the other direction.** We have *ourselves predicted* the supply: we sized the registration at 87.4M shares. Our standing rule is never to place a resting bid underneath flow we have forecast — that is buying the exact liquidity event we identified as the risk. Proposing a $4.30 limit while holding an unread half-float registration violates our own rule. This is the single strongest argument for the "read the document first" gate below, and it comes from inside the house.
- **Two of our rules disagree here, and we are resolving it explicitly.** Our default is that a clean adversarial review — one where no reviewer names a specific killing fact — should end in a small starter rather than a pass, because refusing to act is itself a decision that goes ungraded. But this review is *not* clean: two named, sustained, novel kills survived (the accelerating deck decline, the sub-seasonal back-half guide). Meanwhile our fairness rule says an asset priced fairly is ownable only when the fairness is *verified* — and ours rests on an 8× multiple for the observation deck for which **neither reviewer could produce a single comparable transaction.** Fairness unverified plus two live kills equals no starter. If the adjudicator disagrees, the disagreement should be on the deck multiple, because that is where the whole valuation is load-bearing.
- **No tax or wash-sale interaction.** We hold nothing in this name and nothing correlated to it that we have identified; any entry starts a fresh short-term holding period.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the Empire State Building's traffic decline turned out to be permanent share loss to newer observation decks rather than a tourism cycle, and that we financed the wait on an earnings figure that added back depreciation while the company was spending real cash to fit out twelve-year leases.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both reviewers reached the same action: no position, no order, no price alert armed.** We are flat and we stay flat. What follows are the conditions under which we reopen the file.

**Reopen condition A — permanent pass (kill the name).**
Two consecutive months where New York international arrivals are **up** year-over-year while Empire State Building visitation indicators are still **down**. That combination means the deck is losing share to newer competitors, not waiting out a tourism cycle. In that case the $55M deck assumption is wrong in the direction that breaks the valuation, and we close the file rather than lower the price.
*Data: monthly U.S. National Travel & Tourism Office overseas arrivals; monthly Port Authority JFK/EWR international passenger counts; New York hotel revenue-per-room. All free, all monthly, all available before the next earnings report.*

**Reopen condition B — 0.25% starter.**
**Both** of the following, together:
1. The July 30 prospectus body is read and confirms it registers **resale by existing partnership holders only** — no new shares issued by the company; **and**
2. Third-quarter deck visitation is down **less than 10%** year-over-year (versus −28.5% in Q2), which would mean the back-half guide was conservative rather than descriptive.

Then: **buy 0.25% of the book at or below $4.30.**

> **Flagged for the adjudicator:** the $4.30 figure was written as "the 8.0% cap floor," but that floor computes to **$4.08** on the same table. The two are not the same number. Our recommendation is the stricter reading — **$4.08** — with $4.08–$4.30 as the widest defensible band. This should be settled before any order is written, not after.

**Additional add levels:** none proposed. A second tranche should not be sized until cash-adjusted earnings (AFFO) exist as a number, because that is what would determine whether a lower price is a discount or a warning.

**Exits / trims:** not applicable — we hold nothing.

**Upper boundary of interest:** above **$5.53** (a 7.0% yield) the remaining upside to our band does not compensate for an unverified deck multiple and an unread half-float registration. If the stock recovers there before the questions are answered, this becomes a pass for this cycle rather than a chase.

**Downside reference:** the 52-week low is **$4.61**. A clean break below it before condition B is met is confirmation that the tape has not based, and argues for waiting rather than for a better fill.
