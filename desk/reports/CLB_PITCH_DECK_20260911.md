## What we believe

We believe the *bull story we were handed is dead at the price it was told at*, and that what is left is a small, thin oilfield-lab company that is not cheap, not dislocated, and has a governance crack in it.

Specifically, what we now believe:

1. **There is no dislocation to buy.** At the pack tape of $12.55, CLB is **+28.2% above** its 52-week low of $9.79 and 38.3% below its high. The original thesis was built on a price of $9.51 described as a "fresh 52-week low." That price does not exist in any evidence we hold and was never sourced. Every derived number in that thesis — the $450M market cap, the $544M enterprise value, the "7.6x EBITDA," the "2.8% free-cash-flow yield," the "management bought back stock 24% above today's price" flourish — dies with it.
2. **At the real price the name is mid-single-digit-yield, not cheap.** Market cap $594.4M, enterprise value ~$688.0M, **9.56x** the thesis's own $72M EBITDA, **2.0%** free-cash-flow yield.
3. **The cash quality is poor and neither bench's story fixes it.** Operating cash flow margin of ~5.4%, stock comp running at ~62% of operating cash flow, and buybacks that are smaller than stock comp — diluted shares fell only 0.8% year over year despite "seven consecutive quarters of repurchases." Shareholder return here is a press release, not a cash return.
4. **The governance event is real, documented, and only half-read.** A director resigned over strategic direction and governance practices, effective 10/1/26. We have the 8-K. We have *not* read his resignation letter, which is sitting free in the same filing.
5. **The one genuinely new forward signal is ambiguous, not bullish.** A hiring wave went from 2 openings in July to 22 in August and 21 in September (50 open). The only posting we can actually see is a compliance-and-data-privacy lawyer, dated five days after a director verbally quit over governance practices. That is as consistent with cleanup staffing as with a lab ramp.

**So: flat, with a defined price at which the bull math becomes true rather than asserted.** The thesis's "7.6x" exists at ~$9.58; its "2.8% yield" exists at ~$9.05. We would buy the thesis at the thesis's price — not 30% above it.

## What the market believes

The market believes, roughly, what management guided: a normalization quarter. Guidance of $128.5–135.5M revenue implies a midpoint of $132.0M, or **+5.9% sequentially** off a soft $124.6M base. The market appears to have taken that at face value — the stock is 28% off its low, not making new ones.

We cannot say more than "roughly" with honesty, because **we never pulled consensus**. Both benches flagged it. The only forward number in this entire court is the company's own guide. Agreeing or disagreeing with a guide is not a view on what the market believes; it is a view on what management said. That is a hole, and we are labeling it as one rather than dressing it up.

What the market does *not* appear to be pricing: the governance rupture as anything other than one director leaving; the possibility that the hiring wave is remediation rather than growth; and the fact that the buyback is sub-anti-dilutive.

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- The drawdown from $20.33 to $12.55 already happened. Whatever the market thinks is wrong here, it has had time and price to express it.
- The governance resignation was filed 8/25 and the stock is still well off its low. The market read the 8-K and shrugged.
- The guide is public. There is no informational asymmetry in a number the company published.
- 9.6x enterprise value to EBITDA on a 5% cash-margin services business is not obviously mispriced in either direction — it is roughly where a low-growth, capital-light, cyclical lab business trades.

The strongest version of "priced in": our entire remaining case is *unread documents*, not *misunderstood facts*. Unread is not the same as unknown — others may have read them already.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy the reopen band and it is not a dislocation, it is information.**
We trigger at a confirmed close below $9.79 because that is where the bull math works. But the reason price got there is that something in the 10-Q, the print, or the governance situation is genuinely worse — an impairment, a lost contract, a further resignation. We buy the thing that caused the price, not the price. *Tripwire exists:* 8-K Items 4.01/4.02/5.02, an NT 10-Q, revenue print below the $128.5M guide low. Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — The hiring wave was the tell and we read it backwards.**
Fifty open requisitions resolve as compliance, privacy, audit, and legal. Instead of a ramp, it is remediation for something not yet disclosed. Price does not fall on a headline; it bleeds for two quarters as costs land in operating expense and the "normalization" guide keeps slipping. *Tripwire is weak but exists:* requisition mix re-scan, operating expense line in the next two 10-Qs.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
The resignation letter (Exhibit 17.1) is a private communication from a director to a board that we then read as a public exhibit. But the *substance* of the disagreement — what "research priorities" and "succession planning" actually mean inside that boardroom — is unobservable. If the real dispute is about something the board has chosen not to characterize, no filing, no price gate, and no detector will surface it before it becomes an event. There is no sensor that can be armed against a board's private disagreement.
**Unknown-unknown class: PRIVATE-DELIBERATION RISK — value-relevant facts that exist only inside a governance body's non-public record, disclosed at the body's discretion and on its timetable.** Adjacent named class: **NARRATIVE-CAUSE RISK** — the "exogenous, reversible cargo shock" explanation for the weak quarter rests entirely on a management press-release narrative. The one physical test we ran (customs consignee lookup) returned an **empty set with no prior-year baseline** — a null, not a refutation. We cannot verify the cause of the weakness we would be buying the recovery from.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- **Our own size versus liquidity.** We do not have average daily volume in the pack. Our estimate is 500k–900k shares/day, roughly **$6–11M notional**. A 0.75% starter on a book of typical size is likely low-single-digit percent of one day's volume — tolerable. But the reopen band sits *below the 52-week low*, which is precisely where liquidity thins and market makers widen. Our fill could be materially worse than the gate price, and the gate is the whole thesis. **This is our single largest execution risk and it is unmeasured.**
- **Predicted flow.** Flow is currently buyback-direction, but the buyback is only ~$2.7M/quarter — under one day's volume. It will not support the stock. Against it: three 13G/A amendments in nine days of unknown direction. If passive holders are reducing, the supply is larger than the demand, and we would be adding to a one-sided tape.
- **Doctrine interaction.** Our reopen trigger is a *price* gate below the low. Our own pre-mortem narrative 1 says a price below the low is likely to be *informative*. Those two doctrines fight each other. We resolve it by requiring the event gates to be CLEAR at the moment the price gate fires — but we concede the sequencing may not cooperate: price often moves before the 8-K.
- **Bench contradiction we inherit.** The red bench argued the guide was 5.4 points above seasonal norm (bearish) and separately that the hiring wave was a missed bullish-or-bearish signal it did not resolve. The blue bench pointed out that a 10x requisition wave is the best available corroborant of the very guide red called heroic. **Red cannot hold both, and we have not resolved it either.** We are carrying an unresolved internal contradiction into a reopen band.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ that we bought a confirmed new low as a valuation event when it was actually the market pricing information from the governance rupture that we never read out of Exhibit 17.1 — and we filled it thin, below the 52-week low, at a worse price than the gate.
