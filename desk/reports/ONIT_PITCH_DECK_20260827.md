# PITCH — ONIT — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position. Both benches independently reached "do not buy," and the deck agrees, but they killed it for *different* reasons and one of them was wrong on the numbers; the name is kept alive with pre-registered reopen conditions at the Q3 report (≈2026-11-05).

---

## What we believe

Onity Group is a mortgage servicer — it collects payments on other people's home loans for a fee. The thing that makes it interesting is that the stock trades at roughly **half of the company's stated book value**: $34.505 a share against $72.55 of book value per share at June 30, 2026 (equity $609.9M ÷ 8,406,047 shares), which is about **0.48x book** and a total market value of roughly **$290M**.

The original idea was: the stock fell 36% from its 52-week high while the book value it is priced against was *growing*, so the damage was in the share price and not in the business. That would be a dislocation worth buying.

**We no longer believe that.** Working through the company's own quarterly report, the honest version of what we believe is narrower and less attractive:

1. **The book value did not grow during the fall.** It shrank slightly. Book value per share went from $73.68 at year-end 2025 to $72.55 at June 30, 2026 — down 1.5%. The "+22%" that the company markets is a year-over-year figure that was earned in the second half of 2025, *before* the stock's decline. The entire 36% drawdown happened in 2026, and in that window book value went down, not up.
2. **The damage is real, disclosed, and getting worse.** The company recognized **$139.5M of writedowns on its servicing rights in the first half of 2026**, versus $66.2M in the same half of 2025 — the loss roughly doubled. That single line more than explains the swing from a $43.6M profit to a $4.2M loss. This is not a hidden problem; it is a printed and accelerating one.
3. **Almost the entire company is one model-estimated asset.** Mortgage servicing rights are carried at **$3,208.8M — about 5.3 times the company's total equity of $609.9M**. There is no market price for them; the value comes from the company's own assumptions about interest rates, how fast borrowers prepay, and how many of them go delinquent. The company's own disclosure says a **10% adverse move in just the discount rate wipes out $118.5M — 19% of all shareholder equity** ([10-Q, sensitivity table](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/873860/000162828026054426/R65.htm)).

So the belief we can defend is: *this is a cheap-looking company whose cheapness is a fair payment for a book value that one modeling assumption can move by a fifth, and whose earnings are currently going the wrong way.* That is a reason to watch it, not to own it today.

## What the market believes

The market believes the discount is deserved and permanent-ish. Three things support that reading:

- **The price is sitting on its low.** The stock is 36.2% below its 52-week high of $54.10 and only **4.5% above its 52-week low of $33.00**. This is not a stock that fell hard and then stabilized — it is a stock still at the bottom of its range with no reversal yet.
- **Both legs down were reactions to the company's own earnings reports.** In our experience, when a stock falls on its own results, the results *are* the news — there is no misunderstanding to arbitrage. We have graded this exact setup seven times and gotten it wrong all seven.
- **The number everyone is arguing about is management's own adjusted-return target**, which has been cut twice (from 13–15%, to 10–15%, to the low end of that) while the actual accounting return on equity is negative. Both the bull case and the market's skepticism rest on the same adjustments. We are not seeing something different from the market here — we are looking at the same disclosure and drawing a similar conclusion.
- Servicers like this one have traded below book for most of a decade. A sub-book multiple is the base case for the industry, not an anomaly.

## Why we might have edge

Three genuine pieces of work, all sourced from the company's own filings rather than commentary:

1. **We took the marketing apart against the filing.** The press release leads with "book value per share up 22% year over year." The quarterly report shows book value per share **down 1.5% sequentially**. The company chose a comparison window that flatters; that choice is itself information, and we caught it. Neither number is a lie, but only one of them describes the period during which the stock fell.
2. **We reconciled where the servicing-rights value came from.** The carrying value of servicing rights rose $383.5M in six months, which looks at first like the company marked its main asset up. It didn't — it wrote it **down** by $139.5M and the carrying value still rose because the company originated a record $15.5B of new loans and kept the servicing, adding roughly $523M of new assets ([income statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/873860/000162828026054426/R4.htm)). Getting this right requires reading the income statement next to the balance sheet. Our own bear analyst got it backwards, and so would most quick screens.
3. **We sized the fragility instead of describing it.** Using the company's published sensitivity brackets, we can say precisely what a bad assumption is worth: 19% of equity for the discount rate, 14% for a 10% prepayment shock, 27% for a 20% prepayment shock. That converts a vague "it's a Level 3 book" worry into a number we can size a position against.

The honest limit on all three: this is edge on *understanding*, not obviously edge on *price*. We now know why the stock is cheap. Knowing why does not make it a buy.

## Why it might be priced in

Nearly all of it, probably.

- Everything above comes from a public quarterly report filed 2026-08-06. It is three weeks old and available to everyone. Nothing we found required a non-public source.
- The stock is at its low, which is what a market that has already read this report would produce.
- We could **not** reach any analyst estimate feed this session, so we cannot state what the published expectations actually are. That is a real hole: without it, "we diverge from the street" is an assertion, not a finding. Our bear analyst rested part of its rejection on this and then admitted it could not check it — a method error we are flagging rather than burying.
- The buyback that was cited as a management conviction signal is **115,589 shares, about $4.6M, 0.75% of equity**. Both analysts agree that is too small to signal anything.
- The one insider transaction on file (2026-08-25) is an accounting officer having 1,844 restricted share units **settled for cash** at $36.03, with no shares purchased ([Form 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/873860/000149315226040050/ownership.xml)). No insider is buying with their own money.

## What could go wrong

*(This is the pre-mortem. It assumes we buy at a reopen band and then lose money.)*

**Failure 1 — The discount is correct and never closes. (Monitorable.)**
We buy at half of book, and the market keeps paying half of book forever, because a servicer's stated book is a model output that no one trusts. Meanwhile the servicing-rights writedowns keep running at $70M a quarter and the book value we anchored to erodes 3–5% a year. We lose slowly and blame it on "the multiple." *Tripwire:* quarterly servicing-rights valuation adjustment stays worse than −$40M, and book value per share falls in two consecutive quarters. Both are visible on report day.

**Failure 2 — The financing structure bites before the value shows up. (Partly monitorable.)**
Servicing rights are financed with borrowings secured by those same servicing rights: those facilities went from $1,285.2M to $1,566.1M in six months, and now equal 48.8% of the servicing-rights balance versus 45.5% at year end ([fair-value schedule](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/873860/000162828026054426/R62.htm)). Senior unsecured notes also rose $203.6M. If rates move against the mark, the lenders can demand more collateral, and the company must sell servicing into a weak market to meet the call — turning a paper loss into a permanent one. Financing costs already consume 46.2% of revenue. *Tripwire limitation:* facility utilization is only disclosed quarterly. A collateral call happens between reports. We would learn about it after the fact, from an 8-K, or from the price.

**Failure 3 — A servicing relationship or an agency approval goes away. (NO monitorable tripwire.)**
The company services roughly $341B of unpaid loan balances, much of it for third parties, and its ability to do so at all depends on remaining an approved issuer/servicer with the government mortgage agencies. **The filings do not name the counterparties.** We attempted to verify the client roster against those clients' own filings and could not — there is no published list to check against. So if the single largest servicing client leaves, or an agency approval is conditioned, we have **no instrument that fires before the announcement**. There is no price gate, no filing pattern, and no external dataset that reaches it.

> **Unknown-unknown class: counterparty concentration that disclosure does not enumerate.** We cannot build a sensor for a relationship whose existence is not disclosed. A related, smaller instance of the same class: the value of servicing rights depends on where the underlying homes are (hurricane, flood, and insurance-cost exposure), and the quarterly report **does not break the $341B down by state**. We can see the company's own offices sit in West Palm Beach, Houston, and St. Croix — all high-hazard — but that is a business-continuity fact, not a valuation one. The valuation-relevant geography is simply not published.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own predicted flow, our own rules:**

- **Our size versus daily volume is UNMEASURED.** The broker contract-lookup permission was refused to the bear analyst, the bull analyst, and this deck. We have no average daily volume figure, so we cannot certify our own rule that a ladder never exceeds 1% of a day's volume. With 8.4M shares outstanding and a ~$290M market value, the tradable float is small enough that this is not a formality. **Measuring average daily volume is a hard precondition to the first order, not a nicety.**
- **Our own entry levels require the outcome we say is bad.** Every reopen band below is *under the 52-week low of $33.00*. We only get filled if the stock breaks to new lows — which is exactly the "cause unresolved, no stabilization" scenario we are using as the reason not to buy today. We would be bidding into the continuation we ourselves predicted. Stated plainly: **our buy price is only reached by our bear case coming true.** If we ever find ourselves filled quickly and in size, that is evidence against us, not for us.
- **Rule interactions.** Our "a clean court with no named objection defaults to a small starter position" rule does *not* apply here — both benches named a specific, quantified objection, so flat is the correct default. Our "don't buy a stock that fell on its own earnings" rule points the same way. But a third rule cuts against us: if we later reopen on *price alone* without the fundamental numbers below improving, we would be buying purely because the multiple got lower — which is the precise mistake the bull analyst identified in the bear analyst's reasoning. **The price gates below are permission to re-examine, not permission to buy.**
- **Tax.** Any entry starts a short-term holding clock into a catalyst about ten weeks out. An exit on a good Q3 report would be taxed at short-term rates. Size and expected holding period must be set with that in mind before the first order, not after.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a stock at half of a book value that is itself a model output, and the model kept revising downward — the servicing-rights writedowns continued at their current doubled pace, book value per share ground lower every quarter, the market never re-rated the multiple, and the discount we paid for turned out to be an accurate price for the fragility rather than a mistake.**
