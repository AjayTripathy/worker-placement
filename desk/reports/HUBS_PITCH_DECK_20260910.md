# PITCH — HUBS — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-10 — HOLD — NO ADD (conviction 7/10). The regime/drawdown thesis was REJECTED as actionable for this name; see the EVENT ADDENDUM at the end for the ruling. Any earlier 'ADJUDICATED' line in this deck referred to a prior court and is superseded.**

---

## What we believe

HubSpot is a real business that got expensive on the wrong line. It is GAAP-profitable, carries no long-term debt, grows mid-teens in constant currency, and sits 57% below its 52-week high at $224.51 (32% above the $169.65 low). That combination is why we owned it and why we still own half of it.

But the thing we were being paid for — accelerating customer growth after the April price cuts — has not shown up, and the company's own August 5 guide says it probably won't this year: 5,000–6,000 net adds, net revenue retention flat at ~102%, roughly 3 points of the reported growth coming from a weak dollar rather than from selling more software. Strip the currency help and the exit rate is about 11% constant-currency. You are paying roughly **57x GAAP operating income** ($9.86B enterprise value against a ~$173M annualized GAAP EBIT run-rate) for an 11% grower whose pricing model was deliberately made cheaper per customer with the payback still unproven.

Two things keep us from going to zero. First, deferred obligations longer than one year grew about 31% year over year against 15% constant-currency revenue — customers are signing longer, which is not what churn looks like. Second, the balance sheet is unlevered and the share count is *shrinking* (52.7M → 49.3M diluted), so the buyback has more than covered dilution.

Net: this is a **size problem, not a solvency problem**. Business risk cuts size; it does not force an exit. Half on, cap re-derived from 1.2% of book to 0.5%.

## What the market believes

The market believes almost exactly what we believe, and that is the uncomfortable part. The sell side models ~13% annual revenue growth for three years. The company guided Q3 to $924–925M against a $941.2M consensus — it guided **below** the street, and the street did not revolt. Roughly 10% of the shares outstanding are sold short (about 5.0M shares, ~4 days of average volume), so the bear case is not an orphan idea; it is a crowded one.

So the market believes: decent company, decelerating, no longer a premium-multiple story, and enough people are short that any clean beat hurts. The gap between us and consensus is not about the forecast. If we have anything, it is about the **multiple**, not the numbers.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The long-dated backlog signal.** >1-year remaining obligations up ~31% while constant-currency revenue grows 15%. Consensus is extrapolating the reported revenue line; the contract line says customers are committing further out. This is the single piece of information that is not in the 13% model.
2. **We can build a billings read before the print, and one of our own benches proved it.** HubSpot does not report RPO or cRPO, and our red bench declared the check impossible. That was wrong. The deferred-revenue note discloses quarterly invoicing directly (Q1-25: $766.8M invoiced against $714.1M recognized), so a billings series is derivable from the primary filings. That gives us a pre-11/04 read most screens will not have.
3. **Positioning asymmetry.** 10% short + a $1.0B buyback authorization + shrinking share count means a merely-okay Q3 can move this violently upward. Crowding is a reason to size down, never a reason to reject.
4. **Cost discipline that the bear case understates.** Stock comp is growing ~5% a year while revenue grows mid-teens — SBC/revenue is ~13.3% and falling from ~17%.

Honest scoring: item 2 is process edge (we can see something a day or a week early). Item 1 is the only real analytical edge, and it is mix-confounded by the April multi-year contract push — which is exactly why it bought half a position and not a full one.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is. At $224.51 you are already paying only ~2.68x EV/sales, versus a bear case at $170 that is itself 2.0x sales. The de-rate has happened. The street already models the deceleration. The short book already exists. And the pieces of the bull case that felt strongest turned out to be arithmetic errors on our side:

- The "6.4% free cash flow yield" was a pre-stock-comp number. Net of ~$490M of annual SBC, owner free cash flow is roughly **$167M, about 1.7% of enterprise value**.
- The "$1.34B of net cash as a cushion" and "the $1.0B buyback as an unspent catalyst" are the same dollars. You can credit one or the other, not both.
- The "implied vol below realized vol means the option market hasn't repriced this" claim was backwards. Realized vol contains the −20.2% earnings gap. Implied falling below post-event realized is the ordinary post-catalyst vol crush, not a dislocation.

What is *not* obviously priced: the multi-year backlog mix, and the possibility that a 5,500-add quarter with 103% NRR reads as an inflection rather than a miss.

## What could go wrong

**Failure 1 — The slow one: AI erodes seat-based CRM and NRR never gets back above 103%.** Agentic tools reduce the number of human seats a customer needs; HubSpot's per-seat model bleeds a point of NRR a year, adds stay in the 5–6k band, and 57x GAAP EBIT compresses toward 2.0x sales ($170) with no single bad quarter to point at. *Tripwire:* NRR and net adds each quarter; billings growth derived from the invoicing line. Monitorable. This is also the factor we already own twice via MNDY — courting them together is one bet, not two.

**Failure 2 — The fast one, and it hurts because we trimmed: the squeeze.** 5.0M shares short (~4 days of volume) plus a buyback retiring ~2.4M shares a quarter meets an in-line-to-good Q3. The stock goes 224 → 300+ in a week and we own half of what we owned. Losing money you already had is still losing money. *Tripwire exists but we never armed it* — we have a downside price alert at 169.63 and no upside alert at all. Fixed below.

**Failure 3 — No monitorable tripwire: our own book state is not trustworthy.** The court adjudicated a "trim-50 on 83 shares." The book holds **42 shares**. The armed instruction (149) says **SELL 51** — more than we own. Somewhere between the brief, the instruction generator and the custodian record, the position was described three different ways, and *no market data feed fires when our internal state is wrong*. There is no price, no filing and no 8-K that alerts us to this. Unknown-unknown class: **internal-state / reconciliation drift (data-integrity failure in our own plumbing, not in the issuer)** — the sibling class of the "which lot did we actually sell" and "which tape did the bench use" errors that both benches committed in this very case. The only defence is a scheduled reconciliation, which is a process, not a sensor.

**The reflexive and structural leg.** Our own size is irrelevant to the tape: 21 shares against roughly 1.2M shares of average daily volume is **0.0018% of a day** — we have zero market impact and we should not pretend our exit is a risk factor. The reflexivity that matters is other people's. The company itself is the largest predictable buyer: $531.9M of Q2 repurchase is ~2.4M shares, about two full days of volume every quarter, and it is price-insensitive. Against that sits the 5.0M-share short book, ~4 days to cover. That is a market where the marginal buyer is mechanical and the marginal seller is levered to a single data point on 11/04 — thin two-sided liquidity into an event, fat tails both ways. Doctrine interactions we are consciously accepting: (a) **CROWDED sizes, never rejects** — so 10% short cut us to half instead of to zero; (b) **business-risk → size cut, not exit** — same direction; (c) the **no-double-credit rule** (cash *or* buyback support) which killed the valuation leg; and (d) the honest tension between (a) and (b): both doctrines push toward "hold something," which means the doctrine set has a structural bias against ever going flat. We should name that rather than pretend the half-position was independently derived twice.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the April price cuts bought customers who never upgrade — Q3 lands 5,000–6,000 adds with NRR stuck at 102%, the constant-currency exit rate confirms ~11%, and a 57x-GAAP-EBIT multiple grinds toward the $170 / 2.0x-sales trough while we are still holding the other half.**

**Instrument recommendation — output of the arithmetic: STOCK ONLY. Execute the trim in shares. No options.** Reasons, in order:
1. **The 80-vol breakeven is inside the noise between an aggregate feed and a real chain.** Our table only pays for the Dec-26 260 call if chain midpoint IV is below ~80. The aggregate feed says 63.6, which would imply ~$8 of edge — but that feed is the exact input doctrine forbids for strike pricing (TSSI: 71 on the aggregate, 101 at the strike). A post-crash SaaS name with an earnings print inside the expiry can easily carry 75–85 vol at a 40-delta strike, which erases the entire edge. **No real quotes were pulled by either bench, so the edge is unmeasured, not present.**
2. **Buying calls now front-runs our own gate.** The re-add is conditioned on 11/04 data. A pre-print call is a bet on the fork, not on the gate — it contradicts the adjudication's business-risk→size-cut logic.
3. **Covered calls are mechanically impossible.** Post-trim we hold **21 shares**; a contract needs 100. Cash-secured puts would add exposure we just voted to cut.
4. **Tax overlay.** The loss branch must land in the intended tax year. A **Jan-2027** expiry pushes any loss into 2027 and is therefore **excluded during a harvest year** — and the numeric edge does not dominate, since it is unmeasured (point 1). The only tax-compliant expiry that still contains the 11/04 fork is **Dec-2026**, and at Dec-26 the model price is *lower* ($19.52 vs $22.13), i.e. less premium to be wrong with. Separately, the share trim realizes a **gain** of roughly $792, not a loss, so harvest-year exclusion does not block the trim itself — but lot selection does matter: sell the highest-cost lots first, and confirm the long-term/short-term split before the ticket goes in (unverified item 4).
