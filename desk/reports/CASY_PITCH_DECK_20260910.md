# PITCH — CASY — VERDICT: **PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

No position, no order, no immediate entry: at the pack's live $635.93 our own scenario table prices the stock at $537 (−15.5%), so the proposal is a laddered buy program starting $565 and below, not a buy today.

---

## What we believe

Casey's is a good, boring, slowly-compounding retailer that just printed a quarter whose headline growth was mostly rented from a fuel margin it does not control, and the market correctly took the multiple down for it.

Three things we think are true:

1. **The store is fine.** Inside same-store sales +3.2% against a hard 4.5% year-ago comp, inside margin up ~30bp to about 42.2%, prepared-food and dispensed-beverage +4.8%, guidance untouched. Nothing broke.
2. **The earnings did not really grow 27.7%.** Fuel margin went from 41.0¢ to 47.8¢ a gallon on essentially flat volume (same-store gallons −0.3%). Roll fuel margin back and the quarter grew somewhere between +1.7% and +4.3% at the EBITDA line and +5.3% to +8.8% at the EPS line. Call it high-single-digit, not high-twenties.
3. **The price still has not adjusted enough.** $635.93 is 33.2x reported trailing EPS of $19.16 and roughly 34x our fuel-normalized FY27 estimate. That is a compounder multiple on a mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit compounder whose recent upside came from a commodity spread.

So: the business is not damaged, but the stock is not cheap. Down 31.5% from the high is a fact about the high, not a fact about value. We want to own this — lower.

## What the market believes

The market believes two things at once, and they are both defensible.

The seller believes what we believe: that a beat built on street fuel margin is low-quality, that the reaffirmed guide implies the back nine of the year grows only +4.5% to +7.3% at EBITDA, and that a stock at 38-48x (where insiders were buying back $45.6M of it last quarter) had nothing priced in but perfection. That view has already moved the stock 31.5% off the high.

The buyer believes the fuel margin step-up is at least partly permanent — that the Fikes/CEFCO acquisition brought a fuel terminal in Waco, Texas, and with it real supply-cost integration, so 46-48¢ is the new floor rather than a 2026 spike. That buyer also believes ~500 new stores of unit growth and a 10-million-member rewards program keep the inside box compounding, and that S&P 500 membership plus a defensive-consumer bid supports the multiple.

Neither bench could find the sell-side consensus number for FQ2 fuel margin or FY27 EPS this session. That is a real hole: we are arguing about whether something is priced in without the price-in rail. We say so rather than pretend.

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the normalization arithmetic and, unusually, we did it in both directions.** Our bear bench rolled fuel margin back to last year's 41.0¢ but left the credit-card fees inflated by $3.99/gallon retail sitting in operating expense — a $10M item it had itself sized two findings later. Our bull bench caught that and re-ran it symmetrically. Then we caught our bull bench: card fees track the *price* of fuel, not the *margin* on it, and margin can compress with crude flat. So the honest answer is a state-dependent band (+1.7% to +4.3% EBITDA), not either bench's single number. Most people will carry one of those two numbers around; we carry the fork.
- **We know the industry mid-cycle, and both benches' baseline was generous.** A NACS explainer citing an OPIS weekly report puts the average retail fuel margin at "35.7 cents per gallon" (https://www.convenience.org/Media/conveniencecorner/Who-Makes-Money-Selling-Gas). So the 41.0¢ "normal" both benches used is already above the industry average, and 47.8¢ is way above. Normalizing to 41¢ is the *friendly* case.
- **We have a named, testable structural question that nobody has answered.** If ≥4¢ of the +6.8¢ comes from the Waco fuel terminal, the bear case is half wrong and the stock is worth ~$610. If it comes from crack-spread luck, it is worth ~$450-530. That is one question with a bounded answer, and there is an independent instrument for it (terminal throughput imagery) that nobody on the street runs.
- **We are patient and unlevered on this name.** No position, no expiry, no funding cost. The only thing we have to do is not buy at 34x.

## Why it might be priced in

- The stock is already down 31.5% from its high and 24 hours of the internet has already said "the beat was fuel." Our bull bench graded three of the bear bench's four load-bearing kills as **consensus, not novel** — the original research brief itself opened by conceding that the beat was fuel-driven and pre-quantified more than 55% of the incremental gross profit as coming from it. If our best insight is on page one of the bear thesis, it is in the price.
- Revenue +24.3%, gallons +2.6%, $3.99/gal, same-store gallons −0.3%: all of this is in the company's own press release. Nothing here is proprietary.
- The 33x multiple is public. Anyone can divide.
- The genuinely non-consensus part of our work is the *symmetry* correction and the mid-cycle band — and those make us *less* bearish than the loudest bear, which is not an edge that pays unless we buy. And we are not buying today.
- Honest possibility: the market has already discounted mean reversion and $636 *is* the right price for ~$18.85 of normalized earnings at a defensible 33-34x for an S&P 500 staple. Our base case says 28x. If we are wrong about the multiple by five turns, we are wrong about the whole deck.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the fuel margin reverts harder and faster than mid-cycle, and the inside box slows at the same time.** Crude falls, street margin goes to the high-30s (which the NACS/OPIS series says is normal), FQ2 inside comps come in below 3% against that hard 4.5% base, and the guide gets cut in the spring. Earnings and multiple fall together — the classic double. Price outcome: $415 area. *Tripwire: monitorable.* Weekly EIA/OPIS retail margin series below a 40¢ four-week average; FQ2 print 2026-12-08 showing cpg <42¢ with inside SSS <3%.

**Failure narrative 2 — nothing happens to earnings and the multiple keeps going anyway.** The "quality compounder" bid rotates out and CASY drifts toward the c-store peer band, which our bull bench flagged as historically mid-to-high teens and which **neither bench ever actually pulled**. On $17-19 of earnings a 19-22x multiple is $325-415, and there is no earnings event to argue with. Price outcome: $322-415. *Tripwire: weak.* We can arm a peer-multiple rail (we have not), but multiple compression has no announcement date, so the tripwire lags the damage.

**Failure narrative 3 — the inside store was never as good as the reported comp, and we find out four to eight quarters late. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** Two mechanisms, both invisible from outside: (a) competitive substitution — Buc-ee's/QuikTrip/Wawa-style large-format and grocery/dollar-channel encroachment eating the rural prepared-food moat store by store, with no published share series to watch; (b) the 42.2% inside margin being held up by vendor allowances, promotional accounting and rewards-program deferral choices rather than pricing power. Neither shows up in a filing, a permit, a plume, or an app review until it appears as a comp miss that gets blamed on weather. **Unknown-unknown class: slow-variable, unobservable-state erosion in a market with no independent share or unit-economics series — a state variable, not an event.** There is nothing to arm. The only defense is position size and the willingness to be wrong cheaply, which is why the first tranche is small and low.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size is not the problem.* CASY is an S&P 500 constituent with multi-hundred-million-dollar average daily volume. A full 3.0%-of-book position is a rounding error against one day's ADV; we can enter and exit in a single session with no impact. There is no reflexivity from us.
- *Crowding at our own levels is the problem.* Our entry bands (30x, 27x, 24x normalized) are the same bands every quality-at-a-price fund uses on this name. We will be filling into a bid made of people running our doctrine, and that bid disappears at exactly the moment it is tested.
- *Gate correlation is ~1.* All three of our release conditions (cash conversion, structural margin attribution, FQ2 comp) resolve on or around one date, 2026-12-08. We do not get staged information; we get one binary. That makes the position path-dependent on a single print in a way the position size must respect.
- *Doctrine interactions cut both ways and are unstable.* Two competing pattern candidates came out of this court: the bear bench's `rented_margin_beat_derate` (which, if ratified into the atlas, would auto-suppress this very entry) and the bull bench's `asymmetric_passthrough_normalization` (which, if ratified, weakens the bear case that suppresses it). If either is adopted before 12/08, our sizing changes for reasons that have nothing to do with Casey's.
- *Passive float.* Index ownership means a meaningful share of the float is price-insensitive. That cushions the down branch and thins the effective float on the way down — the same fact makes moves in both directions larger than the fundamentals justify.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid a compounder multiple for a business whose recent growth came from a pass-through commodity margin, and the multiple — not the earnings — did the damage, with no dated event to warn us.
