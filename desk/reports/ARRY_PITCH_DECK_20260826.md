# PITCH — ARRY — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position, no orders, no entry today; re-open the file on the Q3 print (est. 2026-11-04) against four pre-registered gates.

---

## What we believe

Array Technologies makes the steel-and-motor tracker systems that tilt solar panels toward the sun. The stock is $4.67, down 62% from its 52-week high of $12.23 and 4% above its 52-week low of $4.48.

Three things we believe after working the filings:

1. **The business is not deteriorating the way the original write-up said it was.** The write-up's core claim — that gross margins are collapsing 540 basis points in the second half because of commodity costs and international mix — is overstated. Management's own CFO said the first half carried roughly 200 basis points of one-time benefits, so the normalized step-down is about 340bp, not 540bp. Second-quarter gross margin was 30.8% versus 27.8% a year earlier — up 300 points on *lower* revenue. Management raised the full-year EBITDA floor ($200–230M → $210–230M). Margins are getting better, not worse.

2. **The real problem is the revenue line, and it is arithmetic, not opinion.** Revenue in the first half of 2026 was $565.5M, down 14.9% from a year ago. Third-quarter guidance of $310–330M is down 16–21% year over year. But the full-year guide of $1.40–1.50B is *up* 9–17% over 2025's $1,284.1M. Every bit of that reversal has to land in the fourth quarter: $504.5–624.5M, against $226.0M in Q4 2025. The midpoint, $564.5M, would be the largest quarter in the company's history — the prior record is $507.7M from Q2 2023. A company shrinking 16% through nine months is guiding to a record quarter to finish the year.

3. **There is a new, permanent cash drain that started 16 days before the stock made its low.** The $506.4M Blackstone preferred stock had an option, through August 10, 2026, to pay its dividend in kind — adding to the balance instead of writing a check. That option expired. It is now cash-only, at 6.25%, roughly $31.7M a year, stepping up 50–100 basis points on future anniversaries to about 10.25% by 2031. It has no maturity, is not convertible, and we found no company right to call it. Against a common stock worth roughly $715M, that is about a 4.4% annual cash bleed ahead of shareholders, forever.

Put together: a franchise trading at roughly 7.2x enterprise value to EBITDA with genuinely improving margins and a 1.5x book-to-bill, whose entire full-year story depends on one unprecedented quarter, and which just started paying out a fifth of its free cash flow to a security shareholders can never retire. **That is not a buy today. It is a well-specified bet that resolves on one date.**

## What the market believes

The market believes the fourth quarter does not happen as guided. That is the honest read of a stock at 62% off its high, sitting 4% above a 52-week low, at about 7x EBITDA.

We could not verify this against sell-side estimates. Neither bench could obtain a Q3 or FY26 consensus revenue number through available tools — searches returned company guidance only. So "the market believes X" is inference from price, not from a measured gap between our number and anyone else's. That is a real weakness in this deck and we are not dressing it up.

What we *can* say the market has plainly seen: the CFO told everyone on the August 5 call that the preferred dividend "will transition to cash pay in the third quarter," roughly "$12 million for the remainder of 2026." That is disclosed, quantified, and on the tape. We do not have edge there.

## Why we might have edge

**The revenue arithmetic.** The required Q4 is an all-time record, and nobody in this file — not our first analyst, not our red bench — assembled that until late. It required pulling the quarterly revenue series directly from SEC XBRL ([data.sec.gov companyconcept](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001820721/us-gaap/RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax.json)) because the evidence pack handed to both benches contained no 2026 quarters and no Q3/Q4 2025 — the exact rows the whole question turns on. If our own pack was missing them, it is plausible some of the market is anchored on the same convenient framing ("guide reaffirmed, margins raised") without doing the subtraction.

**The stock-versus-flow distinction on the preferred.** Most models carry the $506M preferred as a claim in the capital structure. Fewer have adjusted the cash flow for the fact that roughly half of the $31.4M of "preferred dividends and accretion" booked in the first half was non-cash accrual that has now ended. The balance-sheet number didn't change; the cash number did.

**The specificity of the resolution.** This is an unusually clean setup: four checkable numbers, one date. Most theses don't resolve; this one does.

## Why it might be priced in

Substantially, it probably is.

- **The preferred flip was quantified by the CFO on the earnings call.** Our red bench flagged it as a novel discovery; our blue bench correctly demoted it. When management gives you the dollar figure on a webcast, you are not early.
- **A 62% drawdown to within 4% of the low is not a stock the market thinks is fine.** The setup is priced *somewhere* between "Q4 misses badly" and "Q4 misses and the balance sheet becomes a problem."
- **We could not check whether this is company-specific or sector beta.** Our conditioning layer (`discovery_state`) and a comparable quote for NXT were both unobtainable — IBKR access was denied to both benches. The signal that put ARRY in front of us in the first place was an excess-drawdown trigger that turns out to have been generated off a wrong sector tag in our own data (ARRY is classified SIC 3674, semiconductors; it makes steel structures). **The name may have entered our funnel through a defect in our pipeline rather than through a real dislocation.** We have not ruled that out.
- **Orders are up 37% while deliveries are down 15%** — a 1.5x book-to-bill on a $2.5B executed-and-awarded book. That is a well-known bull argument and it is the company's own framing. If it's right, the Q4 concern is timing, not demand, and the market may be discounting a timing problem as a demand problem — or may already have figured out it's timing and priced it correctly.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The record quarter doesn't convert, and the guide comes down at the print.**
Nine months of revenue is running down about 16%. Utility-scale solar revenue depends on projects reaching mechanical completion, which depends on interconnection queues, permits, and customer capital that we cannot observe. If Q4 lands even at the low end of the implied range ($504.5M), the year is $1.40B — but if the company revises the year down on November 4 instead, the stock's entire remaining valuation support (7.2x EBITDA on a *growing* base) evaporates, and it becomes a shrinking-revenue levered stub. *Tripwire: monitorable.* Q3 revenue below $310M, or any cut to the FY revenue range, at the November print.

**Failure narrative 2 — The preferred gets restructured off-tape, and we learn about it as a completed fact. THIS ONE HAS NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
There is a $506M non-callable, non-convertible perpetual preferred held by a single sophisticated counterparty, whose cost to the company ratchets from 6.25% toward 10.25%. There is an obvious deal to be done — exchange it for converts, for equity, for a lower coupon with warrants — and every version of that deal is negotiated privately between the company and the holder and announced complete. There is no filing, no price move, no data series that leads it. An exchange struck at a discount to a $4.67 stock could be severely dilutive and we would read about it in an 8-K on the morning it is done.
**Unknown-unknown class: off-tape negotiated capital events with a control-adjacent security holder.** We have no detector for this class and no realistic way to build one — the information does not exist publicly before the announcement. The only defense is *sizing*, not monitoring. Related and equally unmonitorable: the quality of the "awarded" half of the $2.5B backlog. "Awarded" is not "signed"; degradation inside it is invisible to us between quarterly disclosures.

**Failure narrative 3 — The Section 232 tariff, effective 2026-12-04, cuts the wrong way, or freezes customers before it lands.**
We have assumed a domestic-content tailwind (the book is >95% domestic). The second-order effect is the risk: customers who defer projects while waiting for final rules push revenue right, out of exactly the quarter that has to be a record. Note the sequencing — the tariff takes effect *after* the November 4 print, so a clean print does not clear this risk. *Tripwire: partial.* We can watch the proclamation text and effective date; we cannot watch customer deferral behavior until it shows up in a guide.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own footprint, our own flow, our own rules.**

- *Size versus liquidity:* not a constraint, and also not verified. Broker access was denied this session, so we have **no confirmed average daily volume for ARRY**. A starter at 0.5% of book is small in absolute dollars against a $715M-market-cap Nasdaq name, and we do not expect impact. But we should not place an order on an unverified liquidity assumption — pull ADV before any ticket.
- *We would be bidding into our own predicted flow.* Our thesis says the stock is cheap *if* November 4 clears. If it clears, the stock gaps and we buy higher — we do not get our price. If it fails, the stock breaks the $4.48 low and we get filled precisely in the scenario we said was the kill. This is the classic shape where a resting order is a bet you didn't mean to make: **do not leave a good-till-cancelled bid under this name through the print.** A limit under the low is not a value entry here; it is a short volatility position on an event.
- *Wash-sale and account interaction:* the household's Parametric sleeve is an indexed, tax-loss-harvesting account. A small-cap solar name is exactly the kind of position that sleeve can hold and harvest. Buying ARRY in the personal account inside 30 days of a Parametric harvest in the same name creates a wash sale we would not see until the 1099. **Check the current Parametric holdings file for ARRY before any purchase** — this is a mechanical pre-trade check, not a judgment call.
- *Doctrine interaction:* our standing default is that a clean court with no named kill produces a minimum starter rather than a flat. That default does not apply here — narrative 1 is a specific, dated, named kill, which is exactly the condition under which flat is the correct disposition. We are not using "wait" as a way to avoid a decision.
- *Taxable account, short-term clock:* an entry now that we exit on a bad print is a short-term loss at full rates; an entry that works is a short-term gain for twelve months. That asymmetry is another argument for entering *after* resolution rather than before it.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a cheap multiple on a guided year that never arrived — the fourth quarter failed to deliver the largest revenue quarter in the company's history, the full-year number came down, and what looked like 7x EBITDA on a recovering franchise turned out to be a shrinking business paying $32M a year, forever and non-callably, to someone ahead of us.**
