# PITCH — ALAB — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no orders, no entry at $303.44; both review teams land on reject, and the one honest reopen trigger is Credo's 2026-09-01 guide, not anything Astera itself is scheduled to do.

---

## What we believe

Astera Labs makes connectivity silicon that sits between AI accelerators and the rest of a data-center rack. It is fabless — TSMC makes the wafers, outside houses do assembly and test — so it carries almost no factory risk. Capital spending in the first half of 2026 was $28.1M, about 4.0% of revenue.

The business is growing very fast and it is genuinely cash-generative. First-half 2026 revenue was $700.8M, up 99.4% year over year. Operating cash flow was positive in every reported period. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $1,201.9M, operating cash flow $335.7M, capital spending $59.0M, free cash flow $276.7M.

But we do **not** believe this is a buy here, and we did not arrive at that by disliking the company. We arrived at it because the reason ALAB was on our desk at all turned out to be wrong. ALAB entered this review as a *comparison name* — the "clean" one — in an argument that the market had unfairly punished Credo (CRDO) on 2026-08-18 while sparing Astera. That argument does not survive. Once it fails, ALAB has to stand on its own, and on its own it is a very expensive stock whose cash conversion is compressing.

Two things we now believe with high confidence:

1. **The original comparison was not a fair one.** Astera had already reported (8-K on 2026-08-04, 10-Q on 2026-08-05) and had already taken its punishment — a single session of −11.96%, from 361.67 to 318.43. Credo had *not* reported; it reports 2026-09-01 after the close, for a quarter that ended 2026-08-01. A company that has already told you its results and a company nine sessions away from telling you are not comparable on any single day.
2. **The one-day gap was mostly a giveback, not a verdict.** Credo rose 8.82% on 2026-08-17 (259.90 → 282.82) the day before the comparison, while Astera was −0.45%. Measure two sessions instead of one and Credo actually fell *less*: −5.36% versus Astera's −5.65%. Measure three and Credo is worse, but by 1.5 points, not 7.8. The headline gap lives almost entirely in one closing bar.

On the company itself: cash conversion is decompressing. Operating cash flow as a share of revenue ran 33.9% (Q3'25), 35.2% (Q4'25), 24.2% (Q1'26), 22.3% (Q2'26). Trailing free cash flow is 23.0% of revenue; the audited FY2025 figure was 33.1%. Stock compensation is $112.9M in the first half, 16.1% of revenue and up 44.9% year over year, so free cash flow after paying employees in stock is $21.3M for the half — 3.0% of revenue.

At $303.44 on roughly 183.34M diluted shares, the company is worth about $55.6B, or 46× trailing revenue, 202× trailing free cash flow, and 681× free cash flow after stock compensation. (Cash is not subtracted from that market value; doing so would move it by roughly 1.6%.)

## What the market believes

The market is paying 46× revenue, which is not a price you pay for cash conversion. It is a price you pay for a belief about the size of the end market and Astera's durable position inside it — that AI rack build-outs keep compounding and that Astera's connectivity content per rack rises with them. At that multiple, the marginal buyer is underwriting a revenue curve several years out and treating current-year cash flow as almost irrelevant.

The market also does not appear to be alarmed by the compression we just described. The stock is 39.2% below its 52-week high of $499.48 but 2.1% above its 52-week low of $97.89 — so the drawdown is real, but from an enormous base. The 2026-08-04/05 report produced one −12% session and then stabilized; there is no evidence of a rolling repricing.

Insiders have been steady sellers: eight Form 4s and five Form 144s in 47 days (144s on 7/1 ×4 and 8/6; 4s on 6/26, 7/2, 7/6 ×3, 7/22, 8/10). We did not pull the share counts, so we can describe the *pattern* but not the *size*.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: on ALAB specifically, thin. The genuine edge produced by this review is negative-knowledge — we now know an argument that looked good is wrong, and we know it before putting money behind it. That is worth something, but it is not a position.

The three places where we may know something the consensus write-up does not:

- **The comparison-day arithmetic.** Almost nobody who cited the 2026-08-18 move gap checked whether Credo had spiked 8.8% the day before. We did, and that reframes the whole event as an unwind rather than a repricing.
- **The event-position asymmetry.** Ranking peers on a single day when one has reported and one has not is a repeatable error. We now have it written down as a rule.
- **Cash quality decomposition on a matched basis.** Comparing Astera's audited FY2025 conversion (33.1%) against Credo's trailing-twelve-month conversion is apples to oranges. Put both on trailing twelve months and Astera converts 23.0% against Credo's 30.5% — the "clean" name converts *worse*. That inversion is not in the narrative anywhere.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is. The stock is classified as heavily-covered and heavily-owned — this is a widely followed $55B AI name, not something under a rock. Specifically:

- The compression in cash conversion is visible in a 10-Q filed 2026-08-05 that any analyst can read. It is not hidden.
- The stock compensation load is disclosed quarterly.
- The −12% reaction on 8/04–8/05 says the market already processed the quarter and the guide.
- The 46× revenue multiple means the buyer has already decided conversion is not the variable. If conversion doesn't sort the buyer, our conversion work doesn't produce an edge either — it produces an explanation.

The one thing that may *not* be priced is the China exposure. The 10-Q separately discloses revenue by country including China, and we never pulled the actual numbers or the customer-concentration note. For a company selling into hyperscaler-adjacent hardware, an export-control cliff there would matter more than anything argued above. We flagged this and did not close it. That is our biggest open gap, and we are stating it rather than burying it.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry.** Both teams say reject, and neither produced a fair value. The comparison thesis that brought ALAB here is dead; the standalone long was never argued. Buying now would mean paying 46× revenue on the strength of an argument we just disproved.

**Position: 0% of book. No orders staged, none to unwind.**

Because we have no fair value, the levels below are **arithmetic, not valuation** — they are trailing-twelve-month revenue ($1,201.9M) divided across 183.34M diluted shares at stated multiples. They are re-examination triggers, not bids. We would not buy at any of them without doing the valuation work first.

**Reopen conditions — any ONE reopens the file; none is a buy signal by itself:**

1. **Event trigger (primary): Credo's 2026-09-01 after-close report and guide.** This is the actual re-court trigger both teams named. If Credo guides and the connectivity group reprices on real information, the comparison can be rerun on matched event position for the first time.
2. **Price trigger: ALAB below $196.70** — 30× trailing revenue, −35.2% from $303.44. At that level the multiple is low enough that cash conversion could plausibly become the sorting variable, which is the precondition for our conversion work to be worth anything.
3. **Deeper price trigger: ALAB below $163.90** — 25× trailing revenue, −46.0% from here. This sits above the 52-week low of $97.89 and roughly a third below the current price. Full valuation work owed before any bid.
4. **Fundamental trigger: operating cash margin re-expands above 30% for two consecutive quarters** (it ran 35.2% → 24.2% → 22.3%). That would falsify the decompression finding and change the standalone case.
5. **Fundamental trigger the other way: China revenue disclosure or a restricted-party finding.** If we pull the geographic table and find material China concentration with export-control exposure, this becomes a short candidate to court, not a long.

**Trims / exits: none applicable — we own nothing.**
