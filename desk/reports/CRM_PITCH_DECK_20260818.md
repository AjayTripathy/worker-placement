# PITCH — CRM — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — OWNABLE ONLY IF EARNINGS DURABILITY IS PROVEN; we own zero shares today, the only live exposure is a resting 53-share bid at $175, and nothing gets added until the August 26 quarter passes a three-part test.**

## What we believe

Salesforce is a very profitable, slow-growing software company whose share price ($190.97 as of this pack; $189–190 during the court on 2026-08-07, versus a 52-week range of roughly $146–269) now embeds an extremely low growth assumption. Working from the company's audited cash-flow figures: fiscal 2026 operating cash flow of $14,996M less $594M of capital spending gives $14,402M of free cash flow — which ties exactly to the company's own stated "$14.4 billion, up 16%." Guide the next year up 4–5% and subtract roughly $4.1B of stock compensation, and owner earnings are about $10.9B. Against a $155.6B market value (819M shares on the 10-Q cover) that is a 7.0% owner-earnings yield, which implies the market expects about **2% nominal growth forever** — roughly zero after inflation.

That is a lower bar than even our own bear reconstruction clears. If the ~30% of the subscription base that was last seen growing 1.5–3.7% never grows again, and the healthy 70% decays from 11% to 7%, blended growth is still about +4.9%. To get down to what the price implies you have to assume the weak products actually *shrink* 5%/yr and the core decays to 6%.

Three things are genuinely working, and we say so without hedging:

- **The share count is really shrinking.** Diluted shares went 970M to 871M year over year, −10.2%.
- **Stock compensation is disciplined** — 7.7% of revenue last quarter versus 8.5% for the prior full year, about half of ServiceNow's and falling rather than rising.
- **The forward order book is still growing faster than revenue.** Last quarter with full disclosure, current remaining performance obligations grew about 9% excluding the Informatica acquisition, against organic revenue of about 6%. Businesses that turn into value traps have a *flat or shrinking* order book. This is the single cleanest thing separating Salesforce from the companies that looked cheap and then destroyed capital.

## What the market believes

The market believes the AI agent story is a cost story for Salesforce's customers before it is a revenue story for Salesforce — that software sold per seat gets less valuable when the seat is automated, and that Salesforce sells the most seats of anyone. The stock is down about 28% year to date with implied volatility above 50%, which is a market pricing a structural argument, not a bad quarter.

The market also believes, correctly, that management is telling a better story than the numbers support. The company said "$60 billion plus **organic**" in one quarter and "$63 billion in revenue in FY30" the next, with the word "organic" quietly gone. At the growth rate management itself guides, the company reaches about $57.2B by FY30 — roughly $5.8B short. And the market can see that the *reported* growth rate is flattered by an acquisition: Informatica contributed about 4.5 points of the last quarter's growth, and the next quarter is guided to only about 5.9% growth once you strip it out.

Finally, the market believes the buyback was bought, not earned — which is right. The $27.2B of repurchases last quarter was funded by $25B of new notes plus a $6B term loan, not by cash flow. Long-term debt went from $10.4B to $39.3B and book equity fell from $59.1B to $34.2B.

## Why we might have edge

**One.** The bearish view is being expressed as a multiple opinion ("13× is fair for a 7% grower") when the price is actually set for a 2% grower. That gap — between the bear's own arithmetic and what the price embeds — is the whole opportunity, and almost nobody computes both sides.

**Two.** The bear case is being applied to the wrong products. When we reconciled the company's new two-bucket revenue disclosure back to the five product lines it replaced, the *weaker* of the two decaying cohorts (integration and analytics — MuleSoft, Tableau — 15.8% of the base, last seen growing 3.7%) sits in the bucket that is **not** named after the AI product, and that bucket grew **10.3% excluding the acquisition** — faster than the company average. The decaying products are real, but they are about 30% of the base and are dragging roughly 0.74 points of growth, while the accelerating platform/data/Slack cohort is adding roughly 3.9 points. That is 5× more growth added than lost, and it was not in either the bear write-up or, as far as we can tell, in the consensus framing.

**Three.** The newer business is carried at zero in the bear's price band. Agentforce subscription revenue on an annualized basis went from $800M to $1,200M in a single quarter (+50% sequentially). Excluding the acquired Informatica piece, the AI-and-data annualized base is about $2.3B, up from ~$1.4B two quarters earlier. Even at a distressed 5× that is ~$14/share; the bear's entire fair-value band is $21 wide. Discounted hard for the fact that this metric bundles "features" and has no reconciliation to reported revenue, it is still $9–14/share carried at zero.

**Four.** Management has committed, twice, in filed documents, to organic revenue reaccelerating in the second half of this fiscal year — once in February and again in May. That is a dated, gradeable promise with a real credibility cost, not a vague aspiration.

## Why it might be priced in

**One — and this is the strongest argument against us.** A cheap multiple on earnings only helps if the earnings hold. We pulled the record of mature software companies that entered at 14× or less with flat-to-negative revenue: one clear win (IBM in 2021, and only because growth actually inflected), one wash, four traps, and one still open. The decisive case is Cognizant in December 2022 at 12.6×: the multiple **re-rated upward** to 15–17× and holders still underperformed by 33 points over three years, because earnings fell faster than the multiple rose. A cash-flow-yield argument cannot tell "cheap" from "about to earn less." Ours is exposed to exactly that.

**Two.** Adobe entered at 13.7× in November 2025 on the same "incumbent with an AI terminal-value problem" logic and was cut to 7.8× in two months. There is no evidence a low multiple provides support in this cohort.

**Three — the concentration problem.** We already hold about $112k of software across seven names, which is 10.9% of current account value, and we already have Adobe bids staged at $238 and $195. Adding Salesforce does not diversify this book; it doubles down on the identical bet.

**Four.** The company is removing the numbers that would settle the argument. It discloses no seat counts, no customer counts, and no net revenue retention. It collapsed product-level revenue from five lines to two in the very first filing after two of those five lines printed 1.5% and 3.7% growth. It stopped attaching the Informatica adjustment to the order-book figure while still attaching it to revenue in the same press release. And a metric it marketed as accelerating one quarter ("net new annual order value") simply had no number attached the next quarter, while the definition stayed on the page.

**Five.** There is an interest cost not yet in the printed numbers. Last quarter's $317M of interest expense captured only about six weeks of the new debt. The full run-rate is closer to $420M per quarter, meaning roughly $100M/quarter — about $0.30/share annualized — of drag still to arrive.

**Six.** Reported earnings per share was up 52% year over year, but $621M of that pre-tax was gains on privately held investments swinging from a loss to a gain — 85% of the entire increase in profit. Excluding it, profit grew 5.5%, and most of the per-share gain was the shrinking share count. The company's own filing says these private valuations "require judgment due to the lack of readily available market data."
