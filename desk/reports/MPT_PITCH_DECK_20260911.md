## What we believe

Medical Properties Trust owns hospital real estate and leases it to operators. It nearly failed on its debt wall and on 8/10/26 it fixed the near-term maturities by issuing $2.4B of new 9.25% secured notes due 2032 — part new cash, part swapping old unsecured bonds into the new secured ones ([8-K, 8/10/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1287865/000119312526342713/d135389d8k.htm)).

Three things we actually believe:

1. **The refinancing is real and dated.** The 2026 notes get redeemed in full; 2027 gets a partial redemption; roughly $1.5B of 2027–2031 unsecured paper was exchanged. That is a primary-document fact, not a management claim.
2. **The price of that fix is large and is not in any reported quarter yet.** The new coupon is $222M/year. Against the old paper it replaced, the *incremental* cash interest is roughly $96–151M/year (central ~$122–136M) — about $0.20/share, or a third of the earnings run-rate the stock looks cheap against. The first cash coupon is 12/15/26.
3. **Therefore the stock is not 6.2x. It is about 8.4–9.5x pro-forma.** Annualized Q2 cash earnings (NFFO) of $0.60 becomes roughly $0.39–0.44 after the coupon step-up. At $3.70 that's 8.4x–9.5x, with a $0.36 dividend (9.7% yield) eating 82–92% of a non-cash metric.

That is still a discount to healthcare-REIT peers at 12–18x. But it's a *narrower* discount than the bull case claimed, and the remaining gap is compensation for one question we have not answered: **does the rent actually arrive in cash?**

**Our honest stance: this is a maybe, not a buy. And we have not yet read the one document that decides it.**

## What the market believes

- The street models FY26 cash earnings of about **$0.63** with Q1–Q3 at $0.14 / $0.15 / $0.15 — which forces a rising Q4 of roughly **$0.19**. That is the opposite of a coupon step-down.
- Published headlines have been negative: two consecutive quarters framed as earnings misses.
- The tape agrees with the bears, not the bulls: the stock printed **new 52-week lows after the 8/10 financing closed** ($3.99, then $3.89), and sits at $3.70 against a 52-week low of $3.665 — 1% off the low, 41% below the 52-week high. The market read the fix and kept selling.

So the market believes: survival was bought, not solvency; the operators are still shaky; and the dividend is on borrowed time.

## Why we might have edge

**One clean, gradeable disagreement.** Street FY26 is $0.63. Our pro-forma run-rate is ~$0.40 — a **36% gap with a named mechanism** (a full year of the 9.25% coupon that no reported quarter contains, and that the street's rising-Q4 shape flatly contradicts). Somebody is wrong in a way that will be settled by arithmetic on published statements, not by opinion.

Note the direction: **our divergence is bearish on earnings and only conditionally bullish on price.** The edge is not "it's cheap." The edge is "the number everyone is quoting is the wrong number, in both directions," which means the re-rating path is knowable before it happens.

**Second, a timing edge that is free.** The first cash coupon is 12/15/26. Anyone using a single quarter's cash flow as a health check will get a false positive in Q3 (coupon-light) and a false negative in Q4 (coupon-heavy). We know the shape of the distortion in advance.

**Third, a document nobody in this court read.** The 10-Q filed 8/10/26 contains H1'26 cash flow and the debt footnote that says whether the 2027 residual is actually gone. It is public, free, and same-day available. Both of our own benches set a cash-flow bar without it. That is an embarrassing edge, but it is an edge.

## Why it might be priced in

Plainly: most of it probably is.

- The stock fell *after* the financing closed, holding the indenture in hand. The coupon is public. A 9.25% secured coupon on a hospital landlord is not a secret.
- Peer-discount arguments are the most crowded trade in this name and have been wrong for three years.
- The dividend yield of 9.7% is itself the market's statement that it does not believe the dividend.
- Our own table, run at the current price, gives negative expected value. That is the strongest "priced in" evidence in this deck, and it comes from us.

There is also a logical trap we should not fall into, which our blue bench caught: you cannot simultaneously claim "it's fully priced" *and* "the street's $0.63 is the right anchor we agree with." If the coupon step-up is unpriced by the street's model, then "priced in" is not established; if it is priced, the street number isn't the anchor. We resolve this by treating the *price* as informed and the *published estimates* as stale — which is why we're flat and not short either.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The rent was accrued, not received (tripwire exists, but it lags).**
The step from $0.14 to $0.15 was attributed to escalating rent from two recovering operators (HSA/Noor). The coverage metric supporting them (roughly 2.0x cash rent coverage) is **management-sourced and unaudited**. If that rent is being booked and not banked, cash earnings are materially below reported cash earnings and the dividend is funded by asset sales. Tripwire: deferral-balance and cash-receipt disclosure in the Q3 and Q4 10-Qs — which means we learn one to two quarters late, after the price has moved.

**Failure narrative 2 — The new lenders own the asset sales (tripwire exists, weakly).**
The new notes are **secured**. Standard secured-note covenants require proceeds from selling pledged collateral to be applied to redeeming the notes. If that is the case here, then the "$172M of asset sales = liquidity for the dividend" leg of the bull case is wrong, and the dividend is riding on operating cash flow alone. We have **never seen the indenture's collateral schedule or the asset-sale covenant** — only the 8-K's summary paragraphs. Tripwire: the indenture exhibit, if filed; otherwise the next 10-Q's debt footnote. This is a better kill than anything our red bench produced and red missed it.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
A private operator's liquidity fails, or a single foreign government changes hospital reimbursement, and the stock gaps 20–30% before any US filing exists. MPT's tenants are private companies; their bank lines, payor mix, and supplier terms are not disclosed anywhere we can subscribe to. There is no filing, no permit feed, no satellite image, and no XBRL tag that changes state before this happens. Our own detector sweep confirms the hole: the geospatial and permit detectors were all UNCHECKABLE or NOT-FIRED here, and the one detector that fired (tenant credit) fired only *partially*, because tenant operator financials do not exist publicly.

**Unknown-unknown class, named:** `unmonitorable_private_counterparty_state` — the solvency and payor-regime state of a non-public counterparty whose lease is load-bearing for our earnings, observable only after it breaks. Sub-class: `unseen_collateral_pool` — we are underwriting a secured structure whose pledged-property list we have never read.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**

- *Our size vs. liquidity:* We do not have ADV or short interest in this pack — that is a real coverage gap, charged to both benches. Base-rate estimate for a $2.2B-cap NYSE REIT with this news flow: **15–25M shares/day, $55–90M notional**. A 0.35x-normal starter is a rounding error against that; **we are not the flow.** We would need to be ~10x this size before our own footprint mattered.
- *The flow that does matter:* this shareholder base is yield-seeking — income funds, REIT index products, and retail. A dividend cut is not a valuation event for that base, it's a **mandate-violation event**, and the selling is price-insensitive and fast. That is why our downside scenario is a multiple *and* a yield reset at once. Symmetrically, short interest in these names typically runs high (estimate 10–18% of float), so a clean 10-Q can squeeze upward just as violently. Both tails are flow-driven, not fundamentals-driven.
- *Doctrine interactions, stated plainly:* (a) **Crowding is a sizing and timing input, never a reason to reject** — we followed that, and it is also why our unknown crowding does not veto the trade, only shrink it. (b) **Share-count series are a known audited error class**, so red correctly refused to call the missing post-refi share count fatal — it left it UNVERIFIED. Good discipline, but it means a real dilution risk is sitting in the "unknown" bucket rather than the "priced" bucket. (c) The **quotation-scope doctrine** (only quote what the document says, not what the excerpt truncates) vacated a fatal finding in this court — see the next section. (d) The **single-quarter cash-flow gate** doctrine now says: never set a bar on one quarter of a semi-annual-coupon issuer. Both benches violated it before blue caught it.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be **that the rent we underwrote was booked and never collected in cash — and the filing that shows it arrived two quarters after the price did.**
