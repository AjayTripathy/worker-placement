# PITCH — 7254.T — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-20 — WATCH — melt bounding but a Y2.8B below-the-line hole is unexplained; PULL the at-tape ladder**

Univance Corporation (7254, Tokyo Standard Market) — maker of drivetrain units: transfer cases, transmissions, gears for cars and trucks. Last price ¥795. No position. Nothing to buy today.

---

## What we believe

Univance is a small, unlevered Japanese auto-parts maker trading well below the accounting value of its own assets, whose business stopped shrinking. That is the whole case, and it is only half-built.

Three things we think are true:

1. **The shrinkage stopped, at least for one quarter.** The desk's own rule since July was: don't spend time on this name unless the decline flattens out. In the April–June 2026 quarter it did — sales ¥13.8B, up 7.8% from a year earlier, and operating profit ¥1,109M, up 38%.
2. **The price is low against assets.** Book value is ¥1,482.91 per share. At ¥795 the stock trades at 0.54× that — you pay 54 yen for a yen of accounting net worth. The company carries essentially no debt (debt-to-equity 0.02).
3. **Something large and unexplained happened below the operating line.** In the January–March 2026 quarter the company earned ¥1,679M at the operating line and still reported a net **loss** of ¥1,146M. That is roughly ¥2.8B that disappeared between the two — and it is not a rounding item, it is roughly 17% of the company's entire stock-market value.

We cannot recommend buying a company when we cannot explain where 17% of its market value went in one quarter. That is the entire reason this is a WATCH and not a starter position.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a melting ice cube and is pricing it accordingly — and, importantly, **the company's own management appears to agree with the market, not with us.**

This is the single biggest thing neither of our two benches mentioned. Univance's guidance for the year ending March 2027 is for revenue to **fall 11.6%** (¥56.5B → ¥50.0B) and operating profit to **fall about 40%** (¥5.01B → ¥3.0B). The good quarter we are excited about is a quarter the company itself is telling investors will not last.

The bear logic behind that is straightforward and does not require any cleverness: electric cars do not need multi-speed mechanical transmissions or the kind of 4WD transfer case Univance sells. As the vehicle fleet electrifies, the addressable market for these parts shrinks. On top of that, Japanese parts suppliers sell to a handful of very large automakers who squeeze prices every year. A shrinking market plus a pricing squeeze is a good reason for a stock to trade at half of book value, and it is not a mistake.

The market also, presumably, saw the January–March net loss. We haven't.

## Why we might have edge

**The quarter is running far ahead of the company's own forecast, and nobody seems to have marked that.** Take the June-quarter numbers at face value and annualize them: about ¥55B of revenue and ¥4.4B of operating profit, against management guidance of ¥50B and ¥3.0B. That is running roughly 10% above the sales guide and roughly 45% above the profit guide. Either (a) management is being deliberately cautious — common among Japanese suppliers, especially with tariffs and the yen moving around — and guidance gets raised later in the year, or (b) the second half genuinely falls off a cliff. Those are very different stocks. Knowing which one is true before the market does is a real, ordinary, unglamorous edge.

**Management's own arithmetic implies the ¥2.8B hit was a one-off.** The company guides net profit to *more than double* next year (EPS ¥44.26 → ¥95.84) while guiding operating profit *down* 40%. That combination only makes sense if the past year's profit was crushed by a charge management does not expect to repeat: guided net profit of ¥2.0B on guided pre-tax profit of ¥3.0B is a perfectly normal ~33% tax rate, with nothing unusual below the line. So management is implicitly telling us the hole was a one-time event. That is a strong hint — but a hint from a forecast is not the same as reading what the charge actually was, and we have not read it.

**The plumbing here is fine.** No debt, and the stock is cheap on book. If the operating business has genuinely stabilized and the ¥2.8B was a non-cash write-down of an asset that was already impaired in economic reality, then the market is pricing a dying business that isn't currently dying.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The bear case is the company's official forecast.** When management guides to an 11.6% revenue decline, "the market is too pessimistic" is a much harder argument to make.
- **Cheap on book is the normal state for this cohort.** Dozens of Japanese small-cap suppliers trade below book. Being one of them is not, by itself, information. Historically that discount has persisted for years without closing.
- **The return on that book is poor.** Return on equity was 4.14% last year. A business earning 4% on its equity *should* trade below the value of that equity — the discount is arithmetic, not mispricing, unless returns improve.
- **The dividend does not pay you to wait very much.** Forecast yield is 2.52%.
- **The good quarter may be a pull-forward.** Auto parts orders are lumpy; one strong quarter against an easy comparison is weak evidence.
- **We have no unique information.** Everything above is derived from public filings and public aggregators. If the ¥2.8B charge was clearly disclosed and explained in the May filing, then the market has already read the explanation and we're the last ones to the document, not the first.

## What remains unverified

- **What the ¥2.8B actually was.** Impairment of plant and equipment? A loss provision? A write-off of deferred tax assets? A subsidiary loss attributed to minority shareholders? These have completely different implications — a non-cash write-down of already-dead assets is close to irrelevant to value; a cash provision or a tax-asset write-off is not. We know the hole exists, we know management's own forecast implies they don't expect it to repeat, and we do not know what it was. **This is the binding constraint on the whole position.**
