## What we believe

BioAge Labs is a clinical-stage drug developer whose entire market value is currently less than its bank account. At $9.69 a share the whole company is worth about $445M. It holds $381.3M in cash and securities, has no debt, and management says that money funds it into 2029. Subtract the cash and the market is paying roughly **$63M for everything else** — the drug program, the people, the Novartis partnership.

The reason for that is a single day. On July 31, 2026 the stock fell 63.6% in one session (from $24.85 to $9.04) — not on its own news, but on somebody else's trial. Novo Nordisk's ZEUS study of ziltivekimab undercut the idea that lowering the inflammation marker hsCRP reliably translates into fewer heart attacks. BioAge's lead drug, BGE-102, is an oral NLRP3 inhibitor that acts further upstream in the same inflammation pathway. The market treated one company's result as a verdict on the whole pathway.

We think that is an overreaction of *degree*, not a certainty of *kind*. The company has a Phase 2 trial, QUELL-CV, already dosing: 160 adults with a BMI of 32–42 and elevated hsCRP, three doses versus placebo, 12 weeks. It finishes its primary measurement window on **November 30, 2026**, meaning a topline result realistically lands in December 2026 or January 2027. That is about five months of waiting, costing roughly $1.10–$1.30 per share in cash burn. Downside is buffered — but not floored — by $8.31 a share of net cash.

The most honest version of our belief: **this is a cheap option on a five-month readout, bought below the value of the cash backing it, and it deserves the smallest position we take — 0.25% — not a real one.**

## What the market believes

Two different things, and the gap between them is the trade.

The **tape** believes ZEUS killed the pathway. It assigns BGE-102 about $63M — near-zero, and less than the value of the company's own cash. It has done this once before: in the fall of 2025 the stock traded at $4.42 when cash alone was $6.01 a share. This market has shown it will pay less than 75 cents on the dollar for BioAge's liquid cash.

The **one analyst we could actually verify** believes ZEUS killed a *node*, not the pathway. Citi cut its price target from $52 to $21 on August 5, 2026 and **kept a Buy**. At $21, the company would be worth $964M, implying roughly $583M of value for the drug program — about nine times what the tape assigns. Management's own post-ZEUS language is consistent with that framing: hsCRP "appears to be not as strong of a biomarker for risk reduction as previously published data had suggested," while the upstream IL-1β mechanism remains intact.

We want to be precise about what we did *not* establish. Both of our benches cited additional analyst actions — a Piper Buy on 8/3, a Roth reaffirm on 7/31, a Morgan Stanley Underweight, a William Blair Market Perform, a Roth cut from $36 to $25. **None of those could be sourced.** Only Citi is load-bearing. Our divergence case rests on a single external anchor, and we should say so out loud.

## Why we might have edge

**1. The market may be pricing the wrong thing.** ZEUS tested an IL-6 antibody. BGE-102 inhibits NLRP3, which sits upstream. If the market has collapsed "hsCRP is a weaker CV surrogate" into "inflammation drugs don't work," that is a category error with a named mechanism and a dated test.

**2. There is a readout coming that is broader than people think.** The trial registration lists only one measured outcome that matters — percent change in hsCRP — plus safety. But the company has publicly committed in writing to reporting more: the *proportion of patients normalizing hsCRP below 2 mg/L*, plus "additional inflammatory, **cardiometabolic**, and **imaging** biomarkers." That language is in the June 16, 2026 trial-initiation release and repeated in the Q2 release. In a 160-person obese population dosed for 12 weeks, that basket could contain something the pathway story does not currently get credit for.

**3. This same stock has already shown what it pays for this kind of evidence.** Inside the last twelve months BIOA went from $4.42 to $25.96 — 5.9x — and the only company-specific drivers were BGE-102 biomarker data and the January 2026 financing. We have an in-name, in-window observation of exactly how much this tape will pay for exactly this class of result. That is a better prior than any spreadsheet.

**4. Financing is not the pressure point.** $381.3M, zero debt, roughly $26–29M a quarter of net outflow after interest income — about thirteen quarters of runway. The company does not have to raise into weakness before the readout. That is unusual for a broken biotech and it is why the downside is a valuation question rather than a survival question.

## Why it might be priced in

We hold these against ourselves with equal weight.

**1. The one endpoint that is actually pre-specified and powered is the one ZEUS just devalued.** The registry lists percent change in hsCRP as the primary outcome and adverse events as the only secondary. It explicitly says: *"Other/Exploratory Outcome Measures: None listed."* An NLRP3 inhibitor lowering hsCRP is close to a foregone conclusion — and after ZEUS, lowering hsCRP may simply not be worth anything. The only *registered* readout that can surprise is safety, and safety only surprises to the downside.

**2. The broader basket cuts both ways.** Everything interesting the company promised to report — cardiometabolic, imaging — is unregistered, not pre-specified, not multiplicity-controlled, roughly 40 patients per arm, and **selected by the company after it sees the data**. That is a reason to *discount* a good-looking print, not a reason to be confident one is coming. Our red bench found this gap and then reasoned as if the eventual print would be clean. It won't be.

**3. There is a large, underwater, unlocked seller.** In January–February 2026 the company placed 6,782,050 shares at **$19.50** — about 14.8% of shares outstanding, $132.3M gross. Those buyers are down roughly 50% and free to sell. Any rally has known supply above it. (How much that actually caps the move is unproven — see the errors table.)

**4. The "second shot" may not exist yet.** Both benches leaned on a second trial, QUELL-DME in diabetic macular edema, as ZEUS-independent optionality. The company guided to starting it "mid-2026." **We could find no trial registration for it.** As of today that is a plan, not an enrolled study, and it should be valued at zero until a registration appears.

**5. The cash floor is not a floor.** The market already paid 0.74x liquid cash for this company once, at $4.42. If the readout is bad, "trades to cash" is not the downside case — "trades to half of cash" is what actually happened last time.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

Nothing here is live. This is a proposal awaiting sign-off, and no orders exist.

**Proposed starter**
- **Buy 0.25% of the book** — our minimum position size, the one we use when the evidence supports owning something but not believing in it.
- **Limit $9.10 or better.** That is 0.94x the $8.31 net cash per share plus a margin — it makes the entry cheaper than the balance sheet even after five months of burn. At today's $9.69 this does **not** fill; it is a resting bid, not a market order.
- **No adds before the QUELL-CV topline.** None. Not on weakness, not on strength.

**One design flaw we are flagging rather than hiding:** a resting bid that sits below the market for five months, through a binary readout, fills *exactly when the news is bad*. It is a bet against volatility that nobody intended to make. If this is approved, the order must carry a **hard cancel date of 2026-11-20**, before the readout window opens, and be re-decided on the actual result.
