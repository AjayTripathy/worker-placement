# PITCH — FA — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position. Both benches land on flat; the binding reason is that neither bench produced a fair value, so the name cannot be sized at any price. Reopen after the ~September 10 insider lock-up passes.

---

## What we believe

First Advantage runs employment background screening — criminal checks, drug testing, verifications — charged per order. It bought its largest competitor, Sterling, in October 2024 and is now the scale player in a fragmented business.

The operating story is not in dispute and it is good:

- Revenue grew **14.9%** year over year in the June quarter ($448.8M vs $390.6M), and both periods are *after* the Sterling deal closed, so that is real growth, not merger arithmetic.
- Cash generation recovered hard: **$123.0M** of operating cash flow in the first half of 2026 versus **$56.8M** in the same half of 2025.
- Management raised guidance in August and is paying down debt voluntarily ($70M prepaid in the June quarter).
- The company is asset-light. It owns almost nothing physical; one headquarters in Atlanta.

The original idea was that hiring volume is turning up after a multi-year white-collar hiring recession, and that the market had failed to pay for a beat-and-raise. We now believe the second half of that sentence is false, and the first half is unproven.

## What the market believes

Roughly the same thing we do, and slightly more optimistically.

- Wall Street rates the stock a **Buy**, with an average price target of **$26.25**.
- Street forward earnings estimates (~$1.37/share) sit **above** the company's own raised guidance midpoint ($1.26).
- The stock trades around **$21–22**, roughly **13–17% below its 52-week high of $25.15** — but also **+137% to +147% above its 52-week low of $8.82**.

So the market is not skeptical of this company. It is already long, already paying up for a recovery, and already forecasting above management.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: we have not established that we do. The three candidate sources of edge, stated fairly:

1. **Forced supply is temporarily setting the price, not fundamentals.** Silver Lake, the private-equity owner, sold 12.5 million shares at $22.20 on August 11 — four sessions after a beat-and-raise had pushed the stock to its 52-week high. The float (shares actually tradable) grew about 20%, from ~82.2M to ~94.0M. Prices set by a seller who *must* sell are not prices set by information.
2. **The overhang decays.** Silver Lake still owns **73,555,240 shares, or 42.8%** of the company. Each block it sells and the market absorbs removes some of the discount that exists *because* everyone knows those shares are coming. Mechanically, the *next* block pushes the price down; the *last* block is what lets the multiple re-rate. Our red-team bench priced only the first half of that.
3. **The short side is crowded into a known date.** About **11.77 million shares, 12.5% of the float**, are sold short into a lock-up expiry that every participant can read in the same public filing. That is a two-sided setup, not a one-sided warning — but it is a reason to be careful about size and timing, not a reason to own something.

None of these is a claim about the business that the Street does not already have.

## Why it might be priced in

This is the stronger side of the argument, and we should say so plainly.

- **Our headline number was borrowed.** The original pitch said "cheap at 16.6× next year's earnings." The Street is at **15.3×** — a *lower*, more optimistic multiple — and publishes it. We were restating consensus with slightly worse math and calling it a discovery.
- **That 16.6× used an adjusted earnings figure, not the audited one.** Reported (GAAP) diluted earnings were **$0.10** in the June quarter and **$0.11** for the first half; full-year 2025 was a **loss of $0.20**. On audited numbers the trailing multiple is about **144×**. The adjusted figure is roughly 3.5× the audited one. The gap is mostly non-cash amortization of Sterling intangibles plus interest — defensible to adjust for, but we must never quote the adjusted multiple without saying so.
- **There is meaningful debt.** Long-term debt was **$2,033.8M** against **$237.9M** of cash at June 30 — about **$1.80B net**, or **3.76×** the midpoint of full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance ($479M). On enterprise value the business trades at roughly **11.3–12.8× EBITDA**. That is a normal price for a levered rollup, not a bargain.
- **The best-informed seller told us their price.** Silver Lake has owned this company since before its 2021 IPO, sits on the board's side of the table, and sold at **$22.20** with full knowledge of the next several quarters. Any argument that the shares are worth materially more than $22.20 has to explain what the sponsor knew and got wrong.
- **The drawdown framing was misleading.** "Down 16.5% from the high" sounds like a fallen stock. It is a 13–17% giveback on a 137%+ run off the trough. That is a pause in a rally, not a dislocation.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry. There is no buy level in this deck.** Not a starter, not a token position. Below are the reopen conditions, taken from the benches' own arithmetic.

**Why flat rather than a minimum starter.** Our default when a court comes back clean is a small starter, and flat requires a *named* kill. Two are named here:

1. **No fair value exists on either side of this court.** We cannot size a position against a number we never produced.
2. **We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict.** Silver Lake holds 42.8%, its shelf registration is live and effective, and its lock-up permits distributing up to 4.2 million shares *during* the lock-up to limited partners who are not themselves restricted. Buying ahead of supply we forecast is the error we most want to avoid.

## What remains unverified

Stated without softening:

1. **We have no fair value.** Not a range, not a point. Every other item on this list is downstream of that.
2. **We have no verified live price in this document.** Quote permissions were denied to both benches. The two benches worked from prices two sessions apart ($20.95 and $21.78) and one of them built an argument on the staler figure. Any decision must start with a fresh quote.
3. **The hiring-volume inflection is management's word alone.** The independent check we designed for exactly this returned nothing, twice. What we need is the specific base-revenue growth figure from the quarterly deck, checked against the filing — if it is above 4% and accelerating, the story becomes a number.
4. **Whether AI compresses price-per-screen is completely unexamined.** We named it as the central risk and then never looked. It is the only finding that would make this a structural short rather than a fairly-priced compounder.
5. **We cannot separate volume growth from price growth.** No customer disclosure, no vertical split, no physical shipments to triangulate against. This is a permanent instrument gap for this company, not a research to-do.
6. **The August 14 Form 3 and Form 4 are unread.** Cheapest open item in the case.
7. **Whether Silver Lake markets another block, and at what price, is unknown.** The "each block that clears removes the discount" argument is logical but has zero forward evidence behind it.
8. **Whether the 4.2 million in-kind shares have already been distributed and absorbed is unknown.**
