# PITCH — AGL — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — DECLINE. No long, no short (the setup is squeeze-shaped: 16.8M shares, 96.8% implied volatility). Watch to the Q3 print.**

---

## What we believe

agilon health runs Medicare Advantage risk contracts with primary-care physician groups. It nearly died — the stock hit $7.48 intraday in February 2026 — and then reported the two best quarters in its history: $53.8M and $69.6M of Adjusted EBITDA, against a full-year guide that started the year at breakeven.

We believe three things, and none of them make this a buy today.

**First, the turnaround is real but its quality is unproven.** The full-year guide went $0M → $25M → $85M at the midpoint. Of the most recent $60M raise, **$49.6M (83%) is the second quarter beating its own guidance — money already earned, looking backward.** Only $10.4M is a better view of the second half. That's fine; it's just not a forward story yet.

**Second, the second-half loss everyone panicked about is normal for this company.** The guide implies a second half of roughly −$38M. That triggered a −19.5% single-day drop. But the second half has been materially worse than the first half in **four years out of four** (average gap −$181M), because agilon books favorable claims development on last year's reserves in the first half and eats the physician profit-share true-up and Q4 utilization in the second. The 2026 implied swing of −$162M is *better* than the 2023–25 average. Our own first-pass screen flagged this as management hiding something. It was wrong, and both benches said so.

**Third, what actually stops us is price and a 2027 hole.** Strip out the government ACO programs and strip out the favorable reserve releases, and the core Medicare Advantage business earned roughly **$21.6M in 2026 on a development-neutral basis**. A generous 2027 core, assuming zero ACO recovery, is $50–60M. Against today's enterprise value of about **$1.38B** (16.785M shares at $95.40, plus $31.5M of debt, less $257M of cash and securities), that's **23–27× EBITDA** for a company that has never produced a positive year of operating cash flow. The two cleaner comparables trade at 15.7× and 16.6×. We would be paying a premium for the messier balance sheet.

Nothing here is a short. A name up more than tenfold in six months on 16.8M shares at 97% implied volatility will hurt anyone who fades it on valuation.

## What the market believes

The market believes the Medicare Advantage book has turned. And it has a decent case: capitation rates per member per month rose about 19% year over year against a reserved cost trend in the low-7% range. That spread is the whole thesis. Membership is down 10% (Medicare Advantage down 12%), and the bull reading is that this is deliberate pruning — agilon walked away from bad markets and bad payors, and what's left prices correctly.

The market also believes the second-half loss is seasonal, not deterioration, which is correct. And it believes the ACO REACH program ending on 2026-12-31 is a known, absorbed fact — the annual report disclosing it was filed 2026-02-25, and the stock went from $7.91 to $30.94 in the twelve sessions after it. The market read that risk factor and re-rated the equity 291% anyway.

The August 6th sell-off was not about honesty. It was the third-quarter guide: **−$5M to +$5M, right after delivering $70M.** That was a shape surprise to anyone who hadn't run the four-year history.

## Why we might have edge

**1. The 2027 gap is specific to this company, and we sized it.** About $25–30M of the $85M full-year guide comes from ACO model entities. That program terminates 2026-12-31. Its replacement, the LEAD Model, starts 2027-01-01 with undefined benchmarks and risk adjustment, and agilon's own annual report says those unknowns "will have a significant impact on **whether** agilon will participate." We checked whether this was a sector-wide problem. It isn't: Privia Health's Q2-26 10-Q contains **zero** occurrences of "REACH" and zero of "LEAD Model." Our blue bench went looking to prove the risk was shared and proved the opposite — against its own argument.

**2. The comparison everyone runs is the wrong one.** On headline numbers AGL looks like the cheap one in the group (~14.5× versus PRVA 16.6× and ALHC 15.7×). Take out the ACO contribution — which has a statutory expiry — and it's **21.4×**, roughly a 30% premium, with the weakest growth and the only negative free cash flow in the set.

**3. There's a quality-of-earnings wrinkle in the ACO number.** The $34M of first-half "ACO contribution to Adjusted EBITDA" sits on top of $4.1M of actual GAAP net income at those entities. The bridge is **$29.7M of add-backs eliminating administrative fees agilon charges the ACOs it doesn't consolidate.** Those fees went from $4.3M in Q2-25 to $12.4M in Q2-26 — roughly triple. The method is disclosed and eliminating intercompany charges is defensible. But it means most of that $34M is a fee-elimination artifact, not economics that survive the entity going away.

**4. The banks tightened, quietly.** Two credit-agreement amendments in four months (2026-02-12 and 2026-04-09). The February one requires **103% cash collateralization of every outstanding letter of credit** and **a minimum $50.0M of total cash at the end of each business day**. Revolver availability is down to $20.8M against $69.2M of letters of credit outstanding. None of this is a cliff — maturity is February 2028 and the company was compliant at 6/30 — but lenders did not require this before.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, most of it may be.

- **The termination date has been public since February** and the stock nearly quadrupled after it was published.
- **The ACO programs funded none of the raise.** The footnote said "$25–30 million for fiscal year 2026" in *both* the May and the August releases. The guide rose $60M; the ACO assumption didn't move a dollar. All of the raise came from the core Medicare Advantage business.
- **The migration path is already demonstrated.** agilon moved three of its ACOs from REACH to MSSP — a permanent statutory program, not a demonstration — a full year before the deadline, and elected the Enhanced Track with real two-sided risk. The 2027 transition is a maneuver they've already run.
- **The claims development is disclosed with a number**, in the standard actuarial rollforward, both directions, with the prior-year comparative beside it.
- **The cash held up.** Total cash including restricted *rose* $5.1M in the first half of 2026, against a $22.4M decline in the first half of 2025. And Q1-2026 operating cash flow was **+$23.7M — the first positive first quarter in the company's public history.**
- **The working-capital shape is industry-standard.** Receivables peak in Q2 and drain through Q4 every year; the second half is the collections half. PRVA — net cash, no debt, raising every metric — also runs negative first-half operating cash flow (−$11.5M) and names the same driver.

The strongest bear case that remains is simply **price**: 23–27× a generous, development-neutral 2027 core. That is a valuation objection, not a fraud objection, and valuation objections are resolved by waiting, not by shorting.
