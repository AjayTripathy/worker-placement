# PITCH — SEZL — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY. Both benches reject the starter position at $118.02. Sezzle is a good business that just repriced correctly, not a bargain — and it carries an unresolved governance problem (an audit-committee director quit effective immediately in April, followed by a law-firm fraud investigation) that makes the downside impossible to bound at any reasonable size. Reopen only on the specific evidence gates below.**

## What we believe

Sezzle is a buy-now-pay-later company that has converted its free users into paying subscribers with remarkable success: revenue grew 51.7% year-over-year last quarter ($149.7M vs $98.7M), EBITDA margins are 38.8%, the company generated $141M of operating cash flow in the first half, and management has raised guidance four quarters in a row. The stock fell roughly 34% after the August 6 earnings report and now sits 40% below its 52-week high.

The original thesis was that this drop was an overreaction to a mechanical slowdown in the growth rate — that the market punished a comparison problem (this year's quarters lapping unusually strong ones) rather than a real deterioration.

After the adversarial review, we no longer believe the drop was an overreaction. The math both benches agreed on: 87% of the monetizable user base (854k subscribers out of 982k monthly active users) has already converted to the paid tier, so the engine that produced 50%+ growth is mostly used up. Management's own full-year guidance (~$608M, up ~35%) implies second-half growth of about 31%, down from 51.7% — a deceleration management itself is forecasting. The market read that correctly and repriced the stock from a hyper-growth multiple to roughly 22.5x forward earnings. That is a fair price, not a dislocated one.

Where we part from the harshest version of the bear case: growth is not collapsing to the mid-teens. Purchase frequency is still rising (7.2 transactions per user vs 6.1 a year ago, a company record) and total payment volume grew 37.9% — there are growth channels beyond converting free users. A 20–30% durable grower at these margins is arguably cheap at 22.5x. But "arguably cheap" is not enough when the risk below cannot be bounded.

## What the market believes

The market believes the growth story just broke, and a large, informed group believes something worse. Short sellers hold 25.6% of the float (about 4.0M shares, per Fintel/MarketBeat — though that figure fell 4.2% in the latest period, so bears are trimming, not pressing). The bear case has institutional history: a Hindenburg short report in December 2024, a live securities class action, a Pomerantz fraud investigation opened in April 2026, and a November 2025 letter from the Senate Banking Committee questioning exactly the subscription-fee model that drives all the revenue growth ([the letter itself](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/20251118lettertosezzlerebnpl.pdf)).

One consequence: the 52-week high of $195.71 was probably set partly by short squeezes, so "40% off the high" overstates how cheap the stock is relative to any level informed buyers ever endorsed. (Both benches flag this as plausible but unverified — our live-quote check was unavailable this session.)

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the deceleration math ourselves from primary filings**, rather than taking either the company's framing or the bears'. The reconstruction (guidance implies ~31% second-half growth, not a cliff and not 50%) is confirmed against the [Q2 2026 release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/06/3340662/0/en/Sezzle-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.html) and the [full-year 2025 release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/25/3244959/0/en/Sezzle-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Results.html).
- **We caught the bear case overstating its strongest point.** The prosecution's "growth converges to 14%" claim ignored rising purchase frequency and used a stale user-growth figure. If Q3 shows growth genuinely running above conversion — faster user growth, frequency still climbing, revenue yield holding — the stock is cheap at today's price and most of the market is anchored to the broken-story narrative.
- **We know precisely which disclosures would prove it**: subscriber churn/renewal (currently undisclosed and the single most load-bearing unknown), a quantified revenue figure for the new SezzleCash/Send products (currently transcript-talk only), and loan-loss provisions coming in at or below 2.5% of volume.
- **The Shopify lawsuit is misunderstood.** Bears cite it as proof Sezzle is locked out of the biggest small-merchant platform. True — but Sezzle's antitrust suit survived Shopify's motion to dismiss in May 2026, so it is also a removable ceiling with settlement/recovery upside. Two-sided, not a clean negative.

## Why it might be priced in

- The repricing already happened, and it matched the math. A 22.5x forward multiple for a business decelerating to ~31% and possibly lower is fair. Nobody in our own court — including the original thesis author — produced an argument that the market's read is *wrong*, only that the risk might be acceptably paid. That is a weaker claim, and it fails on the next point.
- The tails aren't bounded. An audit-committee director resigned effective immediately on April 9, 2026 (8-K; the stock fell 13.6% the next day), which is what triggered the Pomerantz investigation. Until that resignation is explained or the investigation closes, "fairly paid" cannot be certified — a fraud outcome isn't a valuation input, it's a different distribution.
- A quarter of the float is held short by people who have been directionally right about the mechanism since 2024. They may be wrong from here — short interest is now declining — but this is a contested battleground, not a neglected name.
- Management's own provision guidance (building toward 2.5–3.0% of volume; the implied second-half rate of 3.0–3.9% exceeds any recent actual) says credit costs rise from here. The original thesis tried to count that guidance as both a safety cushion and hidden upside; it can only be one.
