# PITCH — NMG — PROPOSED VERDICT: DO NOT BUY BEFORE THE AUG-12 REPORT; RE-OPEN ONLY AFTER THE MID-SEPTEMBER SHARE UNLOCK IF AT LEAST TWO OF THREE CONTRACT MILESTONES ARE MET — **PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

## What we believe

Nouveau Monde Graphite (NMG) is building a graphite mine at Matawinie, Québec, plus a battery-materials plant at Bécancour. The original pitch said: the company just raised US$309.5M of equity at US$1.84 per share — backed by Canada Growth Fund, Investissement Québec, and Eni — declared a final go-ahead on the mine, and holds a Government of Canada purchase agreement that puts a floor under future revenue. The stock trades at $1.38, down 77% from its 52-week high and 9.5% above its 52-week low, so the market is supposedly ignoring roughly US$300M of cash (~65% of the market cap) plus a government-backed mine.

After the adversarial review, what we actually believe is narrower: the cash is real, the go-ahead decision is real, and sophisticated policy investors did commit money at $1.84 *after* the US trade ruling went against graphite tariff protection ([USITC, 12-Mar-2026](https://www.usitc.gov/press_room/news_release/2026/er0312_68281.htm)). That bounds the downside. But the two pillars the pitch leaned on hardest are **not signed contracts**:

- The "government purchase agreement" is a **binding term sheet**, not a definitive contract. The company's own press release says it is still "working toward definitive transaction agreements" and remains subject to "completion of governmental approvals and appropriations processes" ([company announcement](https://nmg.com/government-of-canada-offtake-2026/)).
- The "fully funded" label depends on a **US$335M loan commitment letter** that only becomes real money after final documentation and lender due diligence ([financing announcement](https://nmg.com/nmg-announces-equity-financing-package/)). Cash actually in hand covers less than half the ~$635M package.

## What the market believes

The tape says the market is unimpressed: the $309.5M raise closed and was announced on May 15 ([closing press release](https://nmg.com/financing-and-matawinie-fid/)), and three months later the stock sits near its 52-week low. The straightforward reading — the one our prosecution bench took — is that the market already knows about the cash and is pricing the post-tariff-ruling graphite economics plus the risk that the loan and the government contract never close.

The defense bench added an important complication: the seller pressing the price down may be mechanical, not informed. The raise increased the share count by roughly half; 52.4M of the new shares are already free to trade and sit 25% underwater versus their $1.84 purchase price, and holders dumping stock they bought in a deal (rather than holders with a view) can hold a price down regardless of fundamentals. So "the market has decided" is weaker than it looks — but that cuts both ways, because another 115.8M shares (about 35% of all shares outstanding) come free to trade around **mid-September** when their four-month resale restriction expires.

## Why we might have edge

- **A verified downside anchor.** Canada Growth Fund, Investissement Québec, and Eni put US$309.5M in at $1.84 *after* the adverse US trade ruling — informed, deep-pocketed buyers underwriting the story at a price 33% above today's. The prosecution never engaged this fact; the defense flagged that omission as the review's one real evasion.
- **The current seller may be forced, not smart.** If the pressure is deal-stock flowback, the price is not a clean referendum on the business, and a genuinely funded mine trading 25% below what sovereign-linked investors just paid is a legitimate thing to buy — later.
- **Clear, checkable milestones.** The loan converting from commitment letter to executed credit agreement, the government term sheet converting to a signed contract, and the cash balance holding above US$280M are all public, binary events. When they land, the "two contingent documents" objection dissolves on its own schedule.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The cash is three months stale as news.** Every input to the Wednesday balance-sheet math (gross proceeds, escrow release, go-ahead decision) was in the May 15 press release. A quarterly report confirming ~$300M of cash tells the market nothing it disputes. The prosecution called this the strongest single kill and the defense sustained the core of it: an in-line cash number is not a catalyst.
- **The bear case is about the documents, not the cash.** If the market's true concern is that the loan and the government contract are unexecuted — and both benches confirmed from primary sources that they are — then today's price already reflects exactly the risk we verified.
- **Known supply wave incoming.** The mid-September unlock of 115.8M shares is public information; buyers ahead of it are volunteering to absorb it.
- **Spending is accelerating on the unfunded half.** Job postings show Bécancour ramping (29 postings in July, 13 in August) before its financing exists — the burn gets bigger while the funding stays conditional.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

The courts say **no entry now** (prosecution: reject, conviction 7/10; defense: sustained, flat before the print). Reopen conditions, from the benches' own math:

- **Do not buy before the Aug-12 report** (date itself unconfirmed) — the cash number is pre-computable and resolves nothing.
- **Do not buy before the ~Sep-15 unlock** of 115.8M shares (~35% of shares outstanding) has passed.
- **Reopen gate (all from the defense's closing position):** after the quarterly report AND at least **two of three** milestones: (1) the US$335M EDC/CIB credit agreement **executed**, not committed; (2) the definitive Government of Canada purchase agreement **signed** with the appropriations condition cleared; (3) reported cash **≥ US$280M** with construction spend tracking inside the feasibility-study estimates (the benches modeled a $25–60M quarterly draw).
- **If the gate opens (~late September):** buy **one-third of the originally proposed starter size**, valuing the company on the Matawinie mine only, with the Bécancour plant carried at **zero** until its financing exists.
- **Reference prices for the reopen:** the US$1.84 deal price is the verified sovereign-underwrite anchor — anywhere at or below it preserves the discount to what informed policy investors paid; today's $1.38 is 25% below it; the 52-week low is $1.26. A forced-selling flush through $1.26 during the unlock window, with the milestone gate already satisfied, is the best version of the entry. The benches specified conditions rather than a single limit price, so these are anchors, not triggers.
- **Exit/kill:** if the appropriations process fails or the lenders' due diligence terminates the credit commitment, the thesis object no longer exists — no position survives that.
