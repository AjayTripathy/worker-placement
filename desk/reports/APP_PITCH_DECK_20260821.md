## What we believe

AppLovin sells advertising software. Advertisers give it money, its model decides where the ads go, and it takes a cut. Two things are true at once:

1. **The business is real and clean.** In the last reported quarter it generated real cash, not accounting cash. Cash on hand is about $3.05 billion, free cash flow runs at roughly $4.6 billion a year, share-based compensation is honestly reported, and the share count is *shrinking* — 373 million diluted shares in early 2023 down to 342 million by mid-2025 ([10-Q, filed 2026-08-05, accession 0001751008-26-000059](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001751008&type=10-Q)). We swept for the usual accounting traps and found none. That is an unusual result and it should be said plainly.

2. **The stock has been cut by more than half and is sitting on its lowest price of the year.** $745.61 high, $303.17 low, $305.42 now — down 59% from the high, three-quarters of one percent above the low.

What we believe is narrow: **the price now embeds a growth-collapse story that the reported numbers do not yet show.** Ad revenue grew 52.8% year over year last quarter ($1,924M vs $1,259M). The company guided the next quarter to 46–48%. That is a business slowing down from extraordinary to merely excellent — not a business breaking. If it stays merely excellent, this price is too low.

We do **not** believe we have found something the market missed. We believe we have found a name where every objection is public, the objections are all fair, and the price already pays us for them. That is worth a token position, not a real one.

## What the market believes

The market believes the growth rate is unknowable and therefore untrustworthy.

The specific fear is legible. The company's growth depends on periodic improvements to its ad-targeting model. Management explained last quarter's shortfall by saying model improvement was "lighter than normal." Nobody outside the company can observe model improvement. So the market is repricing a number it cannot verify, and when you cannot verify a number you assign it a lower multiple. At least seven analysts cut their targets on the same day — this is a crowded, informed, coordinated markdown, not a panic.

Two more things the market is weighing:

- The quarter missed **the company's own guidance** ($1,924M against a $1,930M midpoint) — the first time. A miss against the Street is noise. A miss against your own forecast, when your forecast is the only window outsiders have into the model, is a different kind of event.
- An SEC inquiry into the company's data practices was reportedly closed, but the closure was announced **verbally on a call** and we could not find it in writing in any filing or press release.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thin, and I want to be exact about how thin.

- **We priced the deceleration correctly and most commentary did not.** Coverage framed last quarter as a "0.85% miss." The real content is 52.8% growth guiding to 46–48% against a prior-year quarter that itself grew 77%. Against that comparison, the *dollars* added per quarter are roughly flat (~$660M). "The engine broke" is not what the arithmetic says. That reframing came from our own benches arguing with each other, and it is the one genuinely non-consensus thing in this file.
- **The trap sweep is clean and that is a positive finding, not a null result.** Most stocks down 59% have something wrong inside them. We looked for the usual things — cash that isn't cash, dilution hiding in the share count, revenue recognized before it's earned, an obligations backlog quietly draining — and found none. Absence of rot, verified, is information.
- **Structural, not analytical:** we are small enough that a 0.25% position is free to hold and free to be wrong in. Institutions cannot express "probably too cheap, unknowable timing" at this size. We can.

## Why it might be priced in

This is the stronger side of the argument and both of our benches landed here.

- **Nobody is forced to sell.** Our original framing called this a dislocation — a forced, mechanical, price-insensitive seller creating an artificial discount. We could not name one. What we found instead was seven analysts independently deciding growth durability is worth less. That is the market working, not the market breaking.
- **The cheapness argument is circular.** "It trades at about 21× next year's earnings" only holds if next year's earnings hold, which requires the 46–48% guide to hold, which is precisely the thing in dispute. The multiple is cheap *if you assume away the question*.
- **Insiders sold and none has bought.** The CEO sold 104,166 shares at $485–495 in June. Other executives sold at $504, $566, $600. There is **not one open-market purchase in the company's history**. At $305, the people who can actually observe the model's cadence are not buying. (Our first read mistakenly implied a large August sale; it was a **gift** of 213,675 shares — code G, not a sale. Corrected below.)
- **The "it's below the analysts' floor" argument is worthless.** The same analysts had $700+ targets and lowered them all the way down. Their floor has followed the price, not led it.
- **We never ran the competition check.** "Model improvement lighter than normal" is also exactly what it looks like when Meta, Google, or Unity close the algorithmic gap. Nobody on this desk did that work. It is an open hole and it is the hole most likely to contain the real answer.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Now — the only unconditional action:**
- **Buy 0.25% of the book (~$2,500) at market, ~$305.42.** This is a minimum starter. It exists to convert a paper opinion into a position that generates a real, scored track record. It is sized so that being completely wrong costs nothing that matters.
- **Stop: a weekly close below $299.** Not an intraday touch — a weekly close. $299 sits about 1.4% below the 52-week low of $303.17, deliberately: a stop placed exactly at a widely-watched low gets taken out by noise. If the stock closes a full week beneath it, the thesis that this price is a floor is simply wrong and we leave.
- **The honest problem with this entry, stated plainly:** we are buying 0.7% above the level that invalidates us. There is no version of this where that is comfortable. It is tolerable only because the size is trivial. At any real size this entry would be indefensible, and both benches were right to say so.

**Adds — three gates, taken verbatim from the court. Nothing else qualifies.**

1. **Q3 print ≥ $2,055M in advertising revenue AND the e-commerce vertical quantified *in the filing* — in writing, in the revenue note, not spoken on the call.** This is the one that matters most. It converts the unverifiable model-cadence story into a disclosed number. Oral quantification does not count and never has.
2. **Any open-market insider purchase (transaction code P).** There has never been one in the company's history. The first one would be the single strongest signal available, because it comes from the only people who can see the thing the market cannot price.
3. **Four full weeks of basing above $340.** That is ~11% above the current price and clear of the invalidation level. It converts this from a falling knife into a base, at which point the position can be sized on the business rather than on the tape.

**A fourth condition our attacking bench asked for and we are keeping as a partial add-trigger:** written 8-K or press-release confirmation that the SEC inquiry is closed. Alone it does not justify an add; combined with any of the three above it justifies a larger one.

**Trims/exits:** none proposed above the stop. At 0.25% there is nothing meaningful to trim. The position either grows through a gate or it dies at $299.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **~2026-11-05 — Q3 2026 earnings. Date is vendor-derived and UNCONFIRMED.** No company announcement names it. Treat the date as approximate until a press release fixes it. This is ~11 weeks out, so nothing resolves in the near term and there is no pre-earnings positioning decision to make today.

## What remains unverified

Stated without softening:

- **The competition question was never examined.** No pass was run. The single most plausible benign-sounding explanation for the slowdown — rivals catching up — has zero evidence for or against it on this desk.
- **The earnings date is a vendor guess.** No company source confirms 2026-11-04 or 2026-11-05.
- **The SEC inquiry closure exists only as spoken words on a call.** We could not find it in writing. Direct SEC document fetches returned 403 errors during the court, so the legal-proceedings section of the 10-Q was read once, by one bench, and never independently re-verified.
- **Customer concentration is unknown and undisclosable.** The filing breaks out geography only. We cannot rule out that a small number of advertisers drive an outsized share of revenue.
- **Whether the insider sales were pre-scheduled (10b5-1 plans) is unverified.** If they were scheduled, the "insiders are selling" signal weakens considerably. We did not check.
- **Live broker pricing was blocked twice during the court.** Both benches ran on vendor prices that were already ~5% stale by the time the verdict landed. All prices in this deck come from the evidence pack, not from a live broker quote.
- **The seasonality check that our own process mandates was not run** (no shell access in either bench). Q3 ad-spend seasonality is a live, unexamined variable in the $2,055M threshold.
- **The biggest unverifiable of all:** the growth rate depends on a proprietary model whose improvement cadence is, by construction, invisible from outside. No amount of diligence fixes that. We are buying a small amount of a thing we cannot fully see, at a price that appears to acknowledge it. That is the whole trade.
