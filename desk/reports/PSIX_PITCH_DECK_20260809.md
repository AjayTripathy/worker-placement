## What we believe

Power Solutions International makes engines and power systems, including generators for data centers. The stock has fallen 66% from its high of $119.70 to $40.82, though it has already bounced 61% off its low of $25.28.

Two separate questions decide this name, and they got opposite answers in court:

**The profitability question — the bears were wrong.** The bear case said margins would collapse as the company ramped its new Wisconsin plant. The opposite happened: second-quarter gross margin printed 27.1%, *above* the company's own 25% long-term target, and it did so while ramp costs were still elevated. That leg of the bear case is refuted by the company's own primary numbers.

**The revenue question — the company's own guidance is a stretch, dressed up as a modest one.** Management guided second-half 2026 revenue to roughly $395 million, described as "approximately in line with" the second half of 2025. That framing hides what it actually demands. The second half of 2025 was the best two quarters in the company's history — Q3 2025 revenue grew 62% year-over-year and Q4 grew 33% (source: the company's own Q3 2025 earnings release, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1137091/000162828025050205/psiearningsrelease-septemb.htm). Meeting the guide means repeating that record surge, which requires a 29.5% jump from Q2 2026's $152.5 million — while revenue is currently *declining* at an accelerating rate (down 5.1% year-over-year in Q1, down 20.5% in Q2). No company in this revenue series has gone from −20% year-over-year to +30% sequentially without a disclosed order book. The company says larger orders are "moving into production" but has put no number on them, its business depends heavily on one customer (referenced in the singular), and a new CEO starts August 17.

**Bottom line:** a real, profitable company whose bear case on margins is dead — but whose guided second half is an unverifiable inflection we cannot confirm through any public channel, sold to the market as a conservative anchor.

## What the market believes

At $40.82 the stock trades at 13.9× trailing earnings of $2.94 per share. The market has already digested two things: the 66% crash priced in a bust of the data-center generator boom, and the sharp bounce since the August 6 earnings report paid the margin thesis back — the "margins hold" discovery is no longer a secret. The Weichai overhang (the Chinese parent owns 46% and sold shares in August 2025) and single-customer concentration are prominent in every public write-up of this name. What the market is effectively haggling over now is exactly the question we can't answer: does the second half repeat the record, or not? At 13.9× trailing, the price roughly assumes the guide lands.

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the comparison math the guidance language hides.** "In line with 2H-2025" sounds conservative; it is actually a demand to repeat a +62%/+33% record surge from a starting point 25% below it. Both benches verified the quarterly numbers independently to the dollar ($203.8M + $191.2M = $395.0M — the guide matches exactly).
- **We caught the direction of travel.** The year-over-year decline is *accelerating into* the guided half. The guide needs an inflection, not a continuation.
- **The margin finding is stronger than even our original thesis claimed** — 27.1% gross margin above target *during* the expensive part of the ramp.
- **We corrected the record on the insider overhang.** Weichai's last reported sales were August 2025 at average prices of $83–91 — roughly double today's price — and no 2026 sales have been filed. The overhang is real but not currently active, and the controlling shareholder last sold at ~2× the current price.
- **We found the cash-flow tell.** Six-month operating cash flow of $75.7 million came mostly from working capital being released (inventory and receivables shrinking). A company about to grow revenue 30% sequentially builds working capital; it doesn't release it. The strong cash flow and the guide landing are mutually exclusive — one of them is telling the truth about the second half.

## Why it might be priced in

- Half the dislocation is already closed: the stock is up 61% from its low, and the post-earnings rally already rewarded the margin proof.
- If the guide *does* land, 13.9× trailing (13–14× forward) is not expensive — the market may already be discounting a partial miss.
- Our "zero backlog" framing was too strong: the earnings release says larger orders are "moving into production." Orders exist; they're just unquantified. (This was the defense bench's correction of our own prosecution.)
- The known risks — single customer, Chinese parent, cyclicality — are in every published note on this name. We have no informational monopoly on the bear points, only on the comp arithmetic.
- If the second half misses, realistic forward earnings are about $2.00–2.20 per share, which puts today's price at roughly 19× a shrinking business — meaning the current price only works in the guide-lands scenario, which is also the consensus scenario.
