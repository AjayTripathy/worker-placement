# PITCH — SPGI — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: BUY a small initial position (~0.4% of book, one quarter of an intended 1.5% full position) near $418.80, explicitly labeled "fairly-paid quality carry, not edge" — with the full position gated on the October 29 third-quarter report passing all three tests below. Both benches converged here: red recommended accept-with-cuts (kill-case conviction only 5/10), blue sustained the cuts and affirmed the carry at 8/10.**

## What we believe

S&P Global is a clean, honest, dominant business trading at a fair — not cheap — price, and fairness is enough to own it as a compounder if the tail risks are sized for.

Specifics both benches confirmed from primary documents:

- **The business is confirmed clean.** Balance sheet, dilution honesty, and disclosure all checked out. Full-year 2026 guidance is $17.50–$17.75 adjusted EPS ($16.35–$16.60 GAAP), 6–8% organic constant-currency revenue growth, and more than $7B of buybacks — confirmed against the company's own [Q2 2026 earnings release](https://s29.q4cdn.com/690959130/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/S-P-Global-2Q-2026-Earnings-Release-and-Exhibits-7-28-2026.pdf) and the [10-Q filed 2026-07-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/64040/000006404026000045/spgi-20260630.htm).
- **At ~$418.80 that is roughly 23.7–23.8× forward adjusted earnings (25.4× GAAP), with a free-cash-flow yield around 4.0–4.2% for the company as it exists after the July 1 Mobility spin-off.** With mid-to-high single digit organic growth and roughly 3.5% per year of sustainable buyback capacity on top of the dividend, the math clears a money-market hurdle without needing the multiple to rise.
- **The correct downside frame is a ratings duopoly in a debt-issuance downturn — painful but recoverable — not a broken business.** Blue's most important correction of red: cyclicality is not the same as disruptability.

What we no longer believe (our own original pitch got these wrong — see the table): that ~65% of revenue is recurring "toll road" revenue (it's closer to 45%), that the stock trades at a meaningful discount to its own history (unprovable on a consistent basis), and that the free-cash-flow yield is 4.5% (it isn't, post-spin).

## What the market believes

The stock is down about 21% from its 52-week high of ~$529 (a figure partly contaminated by the July 1 Mobility spin-off, so treat the drawdown as overstated) and sits 16% above its 52-week low. The Q2 print on July 28 was received as a disappointment — adjusted EPS of $4.83 against a consensus that aggregators report inconsistently ($4.95 by one count, $4.75 by another; we could not resolve which is right). The market is treating SPGI as a fully-discovered mega-cap quality name inside a data-and-analytics cohort that is actively de-rating on AI-disruption fear (FactSet, a peer, is down ~58%), and is pricing modest skepticism about the Market Intelligence segment (+6% organic, the slowest leg) while giving little separate thought to *where* the Ratings growth is coming from.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: **limited, and the courts said so.** This is proposed as fairly-paid carry, not as a mispricing. The genuine content is:

1. **We know precisely what the growth mix is, and most commentary doesn't.** From the 10-Q revenue note: Ratings transaction revenue was $746M vs $597M a year ago (+25%), and roughly 41% of the entire quarter's revenue growth was this single cyclical line. That cuts against the bull story — but it also means we know exactly what to watch on October 29, and we have named, checkable instruments (the company's own accelerated buyback contracts, struck at $426.70 and $414.76) to verify the capital-return promise.
2. **We have a defensible downside taxonomy.** An issuance downturn hits revenue but not the duopoly franchise; that distinction — recoverable downturn vs permanent impairment — is what lets us own it through a bad quarter rather than being shaken out.
3. **The three-gate re-entry plan is specific.** Most holders have no falsifiable test for the thesis; we do, with dates and thresholds.

## Why it might be priced in

- **It is a mega-cap covered by everyone; the "fair price" conclusion is close to the consensus conclusion.** We claim no informational advantage on the headline numbers.
- **The supposedly AI-safe leg is AI-funded.** Management itself said Q2 issuance strength was driven by tech infrastructure and hyperscaler debt: $169B of hyperscaler issuance billed in H1, with the full-year assumption *raised* to $250–300B from ~$200B. An AI-capex halt would hit Ratings transaction revenue, the Market Intelligence narrative, and index assets simultaneously — one macro event, all three legs. The market's de-rating of the whole cohort may be pricing exactly this.
- **The 5.7% buyback yield the tape sees is a spin-year one-off, funded by $1,986M of new senior notes and spin proceeds, not by free cash flow.** The durable pace is ~3.5%/yr. Anyone doing the balance-sheet work reaches the same haircut.
- **Our household already carries heavy AI-correlated exposure** (large US index beta, a tech-tilted separately managed account, and a concentrated GOOGL position), so even the fair carry is worth less *to us* than to a diversified buyer — hence the size haircut.
