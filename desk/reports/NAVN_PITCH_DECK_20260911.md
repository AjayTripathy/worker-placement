# PITCH — NAVN — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — WATCH — band $17.50 (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Navan sells corporate travel-and-expense software, but roughly 92% of what it books as revenue is a cut of travel transactions — commissions and usage fees, not subscriptions. The original brief said: bookings are accelerating, the company raised its full-year guide, the stock fell 21% on a good print, therefore this is a healthy business caught in an indiscriminate software/AI de-rate. Buy the dislocation.

After both benches, that is **not** what we believe anymore. What we now believe:

1. The business is real and solvent. $653.0M cash plus $167.0M short-term investments = $820M, restricted cash correctly excluded. Positive operating cash flow last fiscal year. This is not a runway story.
2. The growth path bends down, and the company's own numbers say so. Revenue growth went +40% → +35%, the Q3 guide is +30%, and the implied Q4 is +25% ($930M full-year guide − $453.0M first half − $254M Q3 guide = **$223M vs $178M last year**).
3. The valuation argument that justified buying — "4.8x sales is a floor" — was computed on basic shares. On a fair diluted count it is roughly **5.5x** enterprise value to sales, and this same market marked the stock at **$8.11** inside the last twelve months, which is about **1.7x**. There is no floor.
4. The one thing the bull case had — that the stock is cheap relative to the street — is not an edge. Fifteen analysts cover it, rating is Strong Buy, average target $29.4. Our number is *lower* than theirs, not different-and-higher. Being quietly bearish with no position is not a trade.

So: interesting name, wrong price, and no stated variant view. We want it on the sheet with gates, not in the book.

## What the market believes

The market believes Navan is a fast-growing travel-tech platform that deserves a software-like multiple, and that the September de-rate was a growth-quality scare rather than a re-classification. Evidence: 15 covering analysts, Strong Buy consensus, a $29.4 average target (TD Cowen at the high end, 8/13), a FY27 consensus of $911.3M, and a Q3 consensus of $248.3M sitting *below* the company's own $254M guide — the street is taking the guide as a ceiling-ish base case rather than underwriting independently.

Two important qualifiers our blue bench forced in:
- That $29.4 target is a **pre-print** anchor. Nobody has published a post-9/09 target in our evidence. So we genuinely do not know what the street believes *today*. We are comparing our fresh view to a stale one.
- Consensus for Q2 was $220.5M, which is exactly the company's guide midpoint. For a company four quarters public, "consensus" is often just the guide restated. That means there is no independent street model to be right against — which is the *precondition* for a mispricing, but is not itself a mispricing.

There is also a visible seller. Four Form 144 notices on 8/27, five Form 4s on 8/24, and a founder-family trust converting Class B shares into Class A on 8/31 ([SEC Form 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001639723/000185373026000011/form4-08312026_080847.xml)). Class B→A conversions create sellable float that never showed up in the reported float, so a standard lockup calendar misses it.

## Why we might have edge

Honest answer: thin, and mostly of the "we did the arithmetic others skipped" variety.

- **Denominator work.** Most published multiples on this name use 254.3M basic shares. The reserved pool — 37.5M options plus 18.8M RSUs — is 22% on top, and RSUs roughly doubled in a single quarter. Anyone quoting 4.8x sales is quoting a number that does not exist.
- **Classification work.** This is a transactional, take-rate business wearing software clothes. The right comp set is payments/marketplace intermediaries, not SaaS. Both benches agree this is the single strongest analytical point in the file.
- **Take-rate math nobody publishes.** Revenue ÷ gross booking value: 8.31% a year ago, 7.75% this quarter. That 56bp compression is the whole story of why volume growth doesn't convert to revenue growth, and the company leads its press release with the volume number.
- **Supply mechanics.** The B→A conversion sitting between five Form 4s and four Form 144s, two weeks ahead of a print that de-rated the stock 21%, is a float-creation tell that lockup-calendar screens do not catch.
- **Patience as edge.** We have no position and no orders. We can require the December print to hand us the confirmation for free.

## Why it might be priced in

Also honest: probably most of it.

- Fifteen analysts, a liquid $40–80M-a-day tape, and a 21% single-day move say the market already re-rated this name for exactly the reason we identified. We are describing the de-rate, not anticipating it.
- The deceleration ladder is computable by anyone with the press release and a calculator. It is not private information.
- The dilution is in the filings. The 22% overhang is disclosed, not discovered.
- The IPO securities class action (McCown v. Navan, No. 26-cv-01550, N.D. Cal.) was publicized with a lead-plaintiff deadline of 2026-04-24 — six months of public digestion.
- Our blue bench's counterpoint cuts the other way too: the implied +25% Q4 is a residual off a guide that has already been raised twice in the company's first public year. First-year managements sandbag. That residual is a floor, not a forecast, and the street may be right to look through it.

## What could go wrong

**Failure #1 — The sandbag unwinds and we are short-of-nothing while the stock re-rates up (opportunity loss, then chase loss).**
Q3 prints a take rate back near 7.9% on $3.3B+ of volume, the Q4 guide comes in at $235M+, and the "ladder" evaporates as first-year conservatism. Stock goes $20.67 → $27+ and our reopen band at $17.50 never trades. The real damage is behavioral: we chase at $25 with a worse basis than the one we just refused at $20.67.
*Monitorable tripwire:* yes — the Q3 press release's own take-rate and Q4 guide numbers, plus a price gate at $24 that tells us the discount thesis is dead and we stand down rather than chase.

**Failure #2 — We buy the $17.50 band and it keeps going, because there is no valuation floor underneath.**
This is blue bench finding A and it is the one that should scare us. The market marked this equity at $8.11 within twelve months — about 1.7x sales. Our $17.50 band is 4.5x. Between 4.5x and 1.7x there is nothing but sentiment. Add a travel-spend slowdown and the warehouse credit facility becomes a problem: that facility funds card and receivable balances, so the "$695M net cash" is not all unencumbered, and credit losses in a downturn hit a line neither bench modeled.
*Monitorable tripwire:* partial and lagging — the allowance-for-credit-loss line and the debt note, but only quarterly, on a ~40-day delay. We would learn about the credit cycle after the stock did.

**Failure #3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE. Demand rolls over on a surface we cannot see.**
Navan's booking activity sits behind corporate single-sign-on. There is no public availability panel, no slot inventory, no shipping manifest. Our scheduler-exhaust detector came back UNCHECKABLE for exactly this reason, and our customer-identification detector is structurally unable to help because Navan imports no physical goods — there is no customs trail to triangulate. The app-review detector resolved the app but returned a lifetime rating only (4.81297) with no 90-day cohort series and no Play package, so even the crude "is the product degrading" leg is dark. Meanwhile the company does not disclose remaining performance obligations at all, so the usual forward-demand proxy is absent.
Net: between one print and the next, we have **no instrument whatsoever** that would tell us a large customer left, that Ava's automation is degrading service, or that corporate travel budgets are being cut. We would find out on the December press release at the same second as everyone else.
*Unknown-unknown class, named:* **unobservable-demand-surface risk** — sub-class: undisclosed counterparty concentration in a services issuer. We have no tool that covers it, no vendor that covers it, and no plausible workaround short of expert calls or a corporate-buyer walk-around. This is a permanent blind spot in this name, not a temporary data gap.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size.* Average daily volume is roughly 2–4M shares, about $40–80M. A full 1.5%-of-book position fills inside a day at under 5% participation. **We are not the flow here and cannot move this tape.** That is good for execution and bad for excuses — if it goes against us, it is the thesis, not our footprint.
- *Predicted flow, which is not ours.* Insider supply is live and mechanical: post-lockup window open, S-8/resale registration effective, four 144s and five 4s in one week, and founder-family B→A conversions manufacturing new Class A float that float-based screens under-count. No follow-on is on file — but a four-quarter-old IPO with a $29 street target and a stock 155% off its low is an obvious secondary candidate, and a follow-on announcement is the single fastest way to break our $17.50 band from above.
- *Crowding.* Fifteen analysts long with a stale pre-print target is a downgrade cascade waiting for a trigger. That is a *timing* instrument for us: it argues for entering after the anchors reset, not before.
- *Doctrine interactions.* (a) **DIVERGENCE-NOT-COVERAGE**: red bench tried to convert "15 analysts cover it" into a reject. That measures that the name is *measured*, not that it is *priced*. Blue overturned that leg and we adopt blue's version — crowding sizes and times, it does not veto. (b) **Anchor-and-adjust**: our prize table below is anchored on the company's own guide ladder, then adjusted; we should expect our own numbers to be sticky to that anchor and are flagging it. (c) **TENX lesson** (basic-share artifact) is what killed the "4.8x floor." (d) The **III precedent** (rounded, seasonal series are not trends) is what killed red's FATAL demand call. (e) The **TSSI 71-vs-101 lesson** governs the vol section: aggregate IV feeds and chain-level midpoint IV diverge badly, and we will not price an expression off the aggregate.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a decelerating, 92%-transactional travel book at a software-style multiple on the belief that $17.50 was "cheap," and the market — with no valuation floor between 4.5x and the 1.7x it already paid this year — finished re-rating it toward that trough on demand information we had no instrument to see coming.

**Instrument math — and what is missing to complete it.** There is **no options chain in the evidence pack**, so the following is the arithmetic set up for the moment the chain is pulled (item 9), not a conclusion.

Candidate strikes priced off the table itself, C_ours = Σ pᵢ × max(Sᵢ − K, 0), December expiry:
- **C($22.5) = $0.90**
- **C($25.0) = $0.40**
- **C($20.0) = $1.85**
- **P($17.5) = $1.25** (relevant because $17.50 is our reopen band)

The decision rule, stated in advance so it is not retrofitted: pull **chain-level midpoint implied volatility for the December expiry on the specific strike** — never the underlying's aggregate IV feed, which is the TSSI lesson where an aggregate read of 71 sat against a real chain at 101. Then:
- If the December $22.5 call trades **below ~$0.90** and chain IV is meaningfully **below 47.8%**, the chain is cheaper than our own uncertainty and the gated add belongs in calls.
- If chain IV runs **materially above 47.8%** — which is the more likely case for a four-quarter-old IPO that just moved 21% on a print and rallied ~49% in six months, implying realized vol plausibly in the 55–70% band — then implied minus realized is likely **negative or near zero**. A negative variance risk premium means options are *not* generously priced for sellers either, and buying them is paying above our own table. In that case the expression is **stock at the reopen band, or nothing**.
- Note our table sits *below* a plausible realized-vol read. That is a warning about the table, not just the chain: our four-point discretization compresses the tails, so C_ours is a floor on option value, and we should not treat "chain > C_ours" as automatic proof the chain is rich.

**Bimodality.** The table is fork-dated on 2026-12-14 and the mass is genuinely split — 0.20 at $27 and 0.50 combined at $16.50/$11.50, with a thin middle. That shape argues against selling premium straddling the event and in favor of directional or no expression. One specific conflict to name: a cash-secured put at the $17.50 strike would pay us to buy at our own reopen band, which is superficially attractive — but the reopen gate requires the *company-confirmed date* leg as well as the price leg, and an assigned put delivers us the stock into the fork without that confirmation. A CSP is therefore permissible **only** at starter size and **only** after the date PR lands.

**Tax overlay.** Today is 2026-09-11 and the fork resolves 2026-12-14. Any option expression must use the **December 2026 expiry**, not January 2027: a January expiry pushes the loss branch past Dec 31 and defers the loss into the following tax year, which is excluded during a harvest year unless the edge numerically dominates — and a +10.6% EV at starter size does not dominate anything. Stock bought in the November band and stopped on the fact-stop also lands its loss branch inside 2026, which is the clean outcome. Nothing here should be structured across the year boundary.
