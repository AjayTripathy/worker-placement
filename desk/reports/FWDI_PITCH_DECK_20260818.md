# PITCH — FWDI — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-08 — DECLINE. No position, no order. The 22% discount that justified the idea was wrong on both halves of the fraction; the corrected discount (~16–23%) is real but thin against a stock that is essentially 100% unhedged Solana, and the quarterly report that decides it is now filed and unread by us.**

---

## What we believe

Forward Industries is a former accessories company that turned itself into a Solana holding vehicle. Its stock trades at $4.54 against a 52-week high of $46.00 — down 90%, and 30% off the $3.48 low.

We believe three things, in descending order of confidence:

1. **There is some discount to the coins.** The company owns roughly 7.55 million SOL as of 6/30/2026 plus about $16.6M of cash, against debt of at least $57.1M (stale — the newest borrowings are not yet visible to us). Marked at SOL $72.895, the shares trade somewhere between about 0.77x and 0.95x of what the company owns per share, depending entirely on which share count you use and where liabilities actually land.
2. **The discount is not the thesis we were originally sold.** The idea arrived as "22% off asset value with a buyback closing the gap." That number was built on a coin count that was too low and a share count that was too low. Correcting both, the honest range is a 16–23% discount on the more favorable share basis, 5–12% on the harsher one. Two errors that partly cancelled made a broken calculation look plausible.
3. **There is an unresolved honesty question that outranks the valuation question.** The company's own marketing dashboard showed $585.5M of holdings at 3/31 while the filed quarterly report showed $507.3M of total digital assets — a roughly $78M gap between the promoted number and the audited one. If the newest quarterly report shows that gap again unexplained, this name is permanently excluded regardless of price. That test matters more to us than the discount does.

Our position today: flat, and staying flat until someone reads the 8/12 filing line by line.

## What the market believes

The market appears to believe the equity is worth modestly less than the coins it holds, and prices it accordingly. That is not obviously irrational:

- The exposure is a levered, unhedged bet on one cryptocurrency. Anyone can buy SOL directly without paying a corporate wrapper, without the debt, and without management.
- The average cost of the company's coins is around $232/SOL against a spot near $73 — the treasury is roughly 69% underwater. Growth in "SOL per share" has come from borrowing to buy more coins, not from buying back cheap stock.
- The discount is advertised by the company itself. It publishes the multiple on its own website, in two different flavors. A gap the seller points at is not a gap the market failed to see.
- Comparable crypto-treasury companies trade at similar discounts. This is a sector price, not a single-name mistake.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thin, and mostly of the "we did the arithmetic and others repeated a press release" kind.

- **The two published multiples differ and almost nobody reconciles them.** The company shows both a fully-diluted figure (0.827x) and an in-the-money-shares figure (0.702x). Roughly 30 million warrants sit between them. Whether those warrants are pre-funded (effectively shares already) or genuinely out-of-the-money (and therefore should not be counted as free shares) is the entire difference between "cheap" and "not cheap." The answer lives in the equity note of the quarterly report, which most commentary does not open.
- **The $78M gap between the promoted holdings number and the accounting number.** We found it; the sell-side narrative does not discuss it. If it is a definitional difference (staked/wrapped coins, timing, cost-vs-fair-value), it is nothing. If it recurs unexplained, it is a disclosure problem — and finding companies whose promoted numbers do not tie to their filed numbers is the thing this desk is actually good at.
- **The buyback story is misread in public.** Nearly half of the $65.4M repurchase was a single negotiated block — 6,164,324 shares at $4.445 (about $27.4M) bought from an exiting private-placement investor and financed with a $40M loan from Galaxy. That is a borrowed-money exit ramp for one holder, not an ongoing per-share value engine. It reads in headlines as "retired 14% of the company."

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it probably is.

- Every headline number was public before the quarterly report: 7.55M coins, 73.85M basic shares, 0.0729 SOL per fully diluted share. The report's only genuinely new information is the debt stack and the warrant schedule.
- **Management's own behavior says the stock is not the bargain.** In the June quarter they spent roughly $40M buying SOL at about $79 (now $72.9) instead of buying back stock they publicly describe as trading at a discount. They also bid for another company in all stock — you issue stock you think is expensive, not stock you think is cheap. And while repurchasing shares, they simultaneously *sold* 93,642 shares through an at-the-market program.
- **The buyback is decelerating.** Basic shares went 76,314,617 → 73,846,883, about −3.2%, versus −14% in the prior stretch.
- **A 16–23% cushion is about two bad days in SOL.** Buying a 20% discount to obtain 100% exposure to a volatile asset you could buy directly is not an edge; it is a leveraged directional bet with a coupon attached.
- One mild point in the company's favor: borrowing at ~3.4% to hold coins yielding 6.4–7.3% from staking is positive carry. Small, but real.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry today.** The verdict was decline-before-the-report, and the report has since been filed but not analyzed. The reopen conditions below are taken verbatim from the adjudication and are the only path to a position.

**Reopen gates — all three must pass in the 10-Q filed 2026-08-12:**

1. At least 7.5M SOL, unencumbered, reconciled to the audited digital-asset figure (not just the press release);
2. Total liabilities of $100M or less;
3. A warrant schedule that confirms the in-the-money / out-of-the-money split.

All three pass → asset value is verified at roughly 0.75x → **starter long permitted, after the report only.**
**Any one fails, or the promoted-vs-reported holdings gap is left unreconciled → permanent exclusion.** That is an honesty exclusion, not a price exclusion; it does not reopen at a lower price.

**Price levels (valid only at SOL = $72.895; recompute before acting):**

|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | ≤ 0.75x verified in-the-money asset value | **$4.28** | **$3.91** |
| Add | ≤ 0.65x | $3.71 | $3.39 |
| Trim / exit | ≥ 0.95x | $5.42 | $4.96 |

Derivation, so it can be rebuilt: 7.55M SOL × $72.895 = $550.4M, plus $16.6M cash, minus liabilities, divided by ~89.5M in-the-money shares (inferred from the company's own 0.702x figure). Asset value lands at $5.70/share on the light-debt case and $5.22 on the heavy-debt case. Because the asset is a coin that moves daily, **the multiple is the rule and the dollar figures are only today's translation of it.** Rough conversion: every $10 move in SOL is about $0.84 per share of asset value.

**Note the awkward fact:** at today's $4.54, the stock is *above* the starter level in both cases. Even if all three gates pass cleanly, there is no buy at this price. The idea needs either a lower stock price or a higher coin price to become actionable.

**Sizing if it ever triggers:** 0.5% of the book as a starter, 1.5% absolute cap. The exposure is essentially unhedged single-cryptocurrency risk with corporate debt in front of us; a 20% discount does not pay for that at size. No premium selling, no leverage, no averaging down below the add level without re-verifying the debt.
