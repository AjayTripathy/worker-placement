# PITCH — BRBY.L — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION.** Hold the existing 940 shares (~£9.9k), add nothing, and arm no price-triggered action gate; the only decision point is the 12 November half-year results, with a re-open to full review at roughly 800p or on a printed operating-margin slope above 9%.

---

## What we believe

Burberry is a turnaround that is genuinely turning — but slowly, and the shares are not cheap on what has actually printed.

The believable part of the story is cash. In the year to March 2026 the company generated £141m of free cash flow against £65m the year before — more than double — on essentially flat constant-currency revenue of £2.42bn. That is margin repair showing up in the bank account rather than in a slide deck. And the balance sheet, which was the thing that could have killed this, is fine: £614m of cash against £511m of borrowings is **£103m of net cash** before lease obligations ([FY26 preliminary results press release](https://www.burberryplc.com/content/dam/burberryplc/corporate/documents/investors/results-reports/2026/prelims/Burberry%20FY26%20Preliminary%20Results%20Press%20Release%20v2.pdf.downloadasset.pdf)). There is no solvency problem here and no forced-financing risk.

What we do **not** believe is the version of this idea that was originally written up. The write-up said the recent 7.7% three-day fall was "not fundamental — it is index-boundary flow," meaning mechanical selling by index-tracking funds ahead of a possible demotion from the FTSE 100. Our own adversarial review took that apart, and we accept the correction: nothing in the record demonstrates that mechanism. It remains a possibility, not a finding.

So the honest belief is narrow: **a recovering but expensive business, at a price that offers no margin of safety, in a position too small to matter.** Holding costs us nothing. Adding requires evidence we do not have.

## What the market believes

The market is pricing Burberry as a recovery that will substantially complete. At 1050p the whole company — equity plus debt plus lease obligations, less cash — is worth about **£4.61bn**. Against £160m of adjusted operating profit that is **28.8 times**; against the £115m the company actually reported under standard accounting it is **40 times**; and the free cash flow yield on that whole-company value is **3.1%**, with no dividend being paid.

That is not a distressed price. It is a price that already assumes the margin goes a long way back toward where it was at the peak. Set against that, the stock is 23.7% below its 52-week high of 1376.5p and only 7.6% above its 52-week low of 976p — so the market is simultaneously demanding a recovery multiple and marking the shares near the bottom of their year. Both benches read that tension the same way: the recent fall coincided with a broker downgrade of LVMH, and the parsimonious explanation is the whole luxury sector being re-priced for weaker demand, not a technical flow event in one stock.

Here is the arithmetic that frames the whole debate, and neither of our benches produced it until the second pass. If Burberry gets all the way back to a roughly 20% adjusted operating margin (its 2023 peak) on today's revenue, that is £484m of operating profit; at 15 times whole-company value, the shares are worth about **1790p — up 70%**. If it stalls at a 12% margin, the same method gives about **977p — down 7%**. The margin actually printed for FY26 was **6.6%**.

So the upside is real and large, but it only clears +50% somewhere above a **17–18% margin**. The market is not mispricing an obvious recovery; it is pricing a hard, multi-year climb roughly halfway to done.

## Why we might have edge

Three candidates, stated in descending order of how much we actually trust them.

**1. The balance-sheet misread (real, and the strongest thing we have).** Many screens and commentators quote Burberry's "net debt" of £852m, which includes £955m of lease obligations on its stores. Excluding leases, the company holds £103m of net cash. If other investors are pricing a leveraged turnaround, they are pricing something that does not exist. Our own review found this fact while arguing *against* the position, which is the best evidence we have that it is not motivated reasoning.

**2. The FTSE index question (weak, and we have downgraded it).** Burberry currently ranks 102nd by market value at £3,944m. The demotion threshold — rank 111, held by RIT at £3,555m — corresponds to roughly **992p** per share. So the stock sits about **5.8% above** the level at which demotion becomes live. The data date for the September review is **1 September**, effective **18 September**. The original idea was that forced selling would depress the price and create a buying window afterward.

We now think this is not edge, for three reasons. Nobody is force-selling at rank 102; demotion is a conditional 5–6%-away scenario, not something happening now. Index-review boundary lists are published free every week and this is one of the most heavily arbitraged trades in the market. And Burberry itself was demoted and re-promoted in 2024–25 in full public view ([CNBC, 4 Sep 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/04/burberry-to-return-to-ftse-100-after-a-years-absence-amid-overhaul.html)) — the pattern is known to everyone.

There is also a rule of our own that applies here: **we do not bid into flow we ourselves predicted.** If we think the September review pushes the price down, buying ahead of it is buying from the very sellers we forecast — the worst possible fill.

**3. Patience on the margin slope (untested).** If the cash-flow doubling continues into the half-year results, most of the market will still be anchored to the 40x reported multiple. That is a possible edge, but it depends on a print we have not seen.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly, most of it probably is.

The recovery narrative is universally covered — Burberry is a FTSE 100 name with full sell-side coverage; there is no information asymmetry. The index story is public and crowded. The valuation is demanding on every measure that uses actual results rather than hoped-for ones. And the one quarter of good news we have is flattered by an easy comparison: first-half FY26 like-for-like sales were **flat** and second-half was **+4%**, so the +5% reported for the June quarter was measured against roughly zero. The second half of this year laps the +4%. That is the hard test, and it lands on 12 November.

Worse, we have no independent read confirming that demand is accelerating. Our own consumer-demand instrument returned **no data**, with a below-median heat score of 35/100 and no retail-rank or social-velocity signal at all. That is not evidence of deterioration — it is the absence of corroboration for the single claim the whole bull case rests on.

And there is a risk nobody has sized. The Americas — up 12% and the growth engine — is served with product made in the UK (Castleford) and Italy. US tariffs on those goods would compress the margin on exactly the region driving the recovery. We could not check this: our origin-tracking tools rely on ocean shipping records, and luxury goods travel by air. It is a **named blind spot**, not a cleared one.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry. This is a hold-and-wait, and the re-open conditions are below.**

**Current position:** 940 shares at 1065.83p average cost, ~£9.9k, currently −1.5%. **Hold. Do not add. Do not trim.** The position is too small to influence anything; forcing a decision on it in either direction is activity, not investing.

**No price-triggered action gate is armed.** This is a deliberate reversal of the original write-up. The reason is a rule we have already learned the hard way: a trigger level that moves with the sector is not a trigger, it is a coin flip. The 1000p alert was derived from an index rank threshold, and that threshold falls when the whole luxury sector falls — so if the sector sells off together, the alert fires while Burberry's actual demotion risk is unchanged. It sits 4.8% from the current price and *above* the 52-week low of 976p, meaning it is close to a near-certain fire that would demand a decision on the tape's schedule rather than ours. **Re-specify it as review-only: it notifies, it does not act.**

**Re-open to full review — whichever comes first:**

- **Price: below ~800p.** This is roughly 15 times whole-company value on about £248m of forward operating profit — i.e. it requires believing operating profit rises ~55% from the £160m just printed. Below 800p the recovery is available at a price that does not require the recovery. *(Flagging plainly: the benches wrote this level alongside a £310m profit figure that implies ~1060p instead. We keep the lower, harder level and note the inconsistency rather than paper over it.)*
- **Fundamentals: half-year adjusted operating margin above 9%,** with like-for-like sales accelerating against the harder +4% base. That is roughly a 2.4-point improvement on the 6.6% just printed, and it is what a real slope looks like.

**Explicitly not a buy trigger:** the 18 September index effective date on its own. A calendar is not a price. If demotion happens and the shares are still above 800p, we do nothing — and we do not buy into the run-up either, because we are the ones forecasting that selling.

**Sizing if a re-open ever converts to a buy:** start at no more than 1% of the book, in two tranches, with the second contingent on a printed margin figure rather than on price alone. This is a wide-outcome recovery (−7% to +70% on plausible margin paths), which argues for small and staged.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **1 September 2026** — FTSE index review data date (market values measured). Non-print event, ~10 sessions out.
- **18 September 2026** — index changes take effect. Flow event; we are prohibited from bidding into it.
- **12 November 2026** — half-year FY27 results. **This is the decision point.** Note: derived from a data vendor, *not* confirmed by a company announcement.

## What remains unverified

- **The cause of the August fall.** Both benches hit the same wall: no exchange volume decomposition and no index-fund flow data were obtainable. Brokerage history access was permission-blocked this session. Sector-demand repricing and index-boundary flow both remain possible; neither was proven. *This does not change the verdict* — hold-and-wait is correct under either explanation — but it means the original write-up's headline claim is unsupported, not merely unproven.
- **US tariff exposure on Americas margin.** Uncheckable with current tools. The growth region is supplied from the UK and Italy, and we cannot size the drag.
- **The half-year profit split.** We know full-year adjusted operating profit was £160m and that H2 like-for-like sales ran +4% against a flat H1, but the actual H1/H2 profit split is not disclosed in what we pulled. If H2 ran materially hotter, the annualised multiple is closer to 20–22x than 28.8x — a meaningful difference we cannot currently resolve.
- **Share count (358.2m).** Every valuation number in this deck depends on it and it was not traced to a filing.
- **The 12 November results date.** Vendor-derived, not company-confirmed.
- **The re-open price level.** 800p is carried forward as the conservative reading of two mutually inconsistent bench figures; it should be re-derived from a forward profit estimate we actually stand behind before it is treated as final.
- **Forward operating profit itself.** We have no independent estimate — the £248m implied by the 800p gate and the £310m the benches wrote are both assertions, not models.

Confidence that hold / no-add is the right disposition: **high**. Confidence in the *reasons* originally given for owning this: **low** — the write-up's central claim did not survive our own review, and the position survives on a valuation floor and its own immateriality rather than on the thesis that put it there.
