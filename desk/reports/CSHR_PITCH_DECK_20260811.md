## What we believe

CoinShares (CSHR) is a European crypto asset manager that came to the US market through a SPAC merger completed March 31, 2026. The stock, at $4.88, is down 53% from its 52-week high — but that high ($10.43) was an artifact of the SPAC trust structure, not a real trading level, and the stock has already bounced 39% off its $3.51 low. This is not a fresh panic; it is a bounced tape.

The original bull case — that the business earns fees on assets under management, trades near book value, and is being sold off mechanically by SPAC arbitrageurs rather than on fundamentals — was substantially dismantled by our own adversarial review:

1. **The book value is not a hard floor.** Stated "available capital" is $481.3M (~$3.65/share), but $280.0M of that — 58% — is accrued management fees owed to CoinShares by its own legacy XBT crypto products. That receivable is economically linked to crypto prices, not liquid, distributable capital. Truly liquid capital is about $176.7M, roughly $1.34 per share. In a crypto downturn, both the earnings and the book shrink together. There is no hard-asset floor at $3.51.
2. **Earnings are more crypto-levered than the fee arithmetic suggests.** The simple model "revenue = ~170bps × AUM" omits the Capital Markets segment — $73.1M of revenue and gains (~37% of the total), including staking ($21.9M) and lending ($10.7M). That is the leg that reverses hardest in a downturn, and net income already fell from $162.4M to $114.3M (−30%) in an *up* year for crypto.
3. **The supply overhang is real and enormous.** A resale registration covering 105,610,080 shares plus 10,999,993 warrant shares (strike $11.50, currently out of the money) went effective in June — roughly 80% of the ~131.8M share count registered for sale, against very thin daily volume.

What we still believe positively: this is a real, profitable, founder-run business with a genuine European ETP franchise, a new UCITS platform launched in July, and management seeking buyback authority. None of that is enough to own it today at 1.34x a soft book, blind on flows since June.

## What the market believes

The market is pricing CSHR as a small, thinly covered, crypto-beta stock with a massive registered share overhang: down 53% from the (artificial) high, up 39% off the low, and stuck below where the SPAC priced. Forward analyst estimates exist (the name is thinly covered, not uncovered), and the market appears unwilling to pay for the fee stream until the selling-shareholder supply clears and post-merger reporting establishes a track record. Implicitly, the market treats the balance sheet as crypto-linked — which our review confirmed is the correct treatment.

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the balance-sheet decomposition most screens won't.** Anyone reading "available capital $481M" against a ~$643M market cap sees ~0.75x price-to-capital and calls it cheap. We traced 58% of that capital to an accrued fee receivable inside the company's own crypto vehicles. Knowing *why* the cheapness is illusory is edge on the short side of others' mistakes — it tells us where the real re-entry level is, and what disclosure (the H1 balance sheet) resolves it.
- **We have a specific, dated verification calendar the market may not be watching:** the monthly AUM tracker (updated by the third business day of each month, per the company), the H1 report due by September 30 ([company press release, July 30, 2026](https://investor.coinshares.com/pressreleases/coinshares-confirms-quarterly-reporting-cadence-seeks-shareholder-approval-for-buyback-authority)), and a pending shareholder meeting on buyback authority. Buybacks executed against a 105.6M-share overhang near liquid-capital value would be genuinely accretive — a two-sided setup our review flagged that pure bears miss.
- **The overhang is quantified, not vibes:** 105,610,080 + 10,999,993 shares, verified against the effective registration statement. When that supply is absorbed or locked up past H1, the setup changes measurably.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: mostly, it already is. The market's implicit view — crypto-beta earnings, soft book, huge supply — matches what our review concluded. Our "edge" is currently *diagnostic*, not *actionable*: we can explain the discount better than we can refute it. Two of the three things that would make the stock ownable (book quality, flow trajectory) are unverifiable until the company publishes its next AUM datum and H1 balance sheet. Until then, buying here is just a smaller version of the same crypto bet with no informational advantage — which is why the proposed verdict is no entry rather than a contrarian buy.
