## What we believe

ClearPoint Neuro sells the hardware, disposables and service work that let other people do brain surgery and deliver drugs into the brain — including the delivery step for other companies' gene therapies. We believe the *business* is real and the *stock* is not cheap.

Three beliefs, plainly:

1. **The company is not about to run out of money.** At 6/30/26 it had about $29.4M of cash and was burning roughly $7.5M a quarter, which alone is under four quarters. But the 2025 note agreement with Oberland still contains an **undrawn $25.0M tranche that the company can take at its own option any time before 12/31/26** — a company decision, not a negotiation. That pushes practical runway to roughly seven quarters *if they pull it*. This is the one place our own red bench got it badly wrong (see the checked-ourselves table).
2. **The price already assumes the good version of the story.** At $14.05 with ~30.5M shares, the equity is ~$429M and, adding ~$55M of notes and netting ~$29.4M of cash, enterprise value is ~**$454M — about 9.1× the midpoint of a revenue guide the company just cut to $48–52M**, and ~12.3× actual FY25 revenue of $37.0M.
3. **The next three months are a guide-versus-print test, not a story test.** Guidance implies H2 revenue of $25–29M, i.e. $12.5–14.5M per quarter, against $10.88M in Q2. Our own quarterly estimate is $11.0–12.0M — below the guide-implied floor even though it would print as +24–35% growth versus a soft year-ago quarter. That combination (headline growth, missed guide) is how this name can look fine and still de-rate.

Net belief: **right business, wrong price, and the cheap-money leg of the bear case is dead.** We want it lower, or we want proof the demand is booked rather than merely possible.

## What the market believes

- The sell side carries a Buy and a **$30.00 target** — roughly double the tape — and Morningstar publishes a price/sales of about 9.9. So the multiple we are calling expensive is not a secret we discovered; it is a published, consensus-visible number. Our red bench labelled this a "novel" kill and that label was wrong.
- The market believes the H2 inflection happens. Consensus for the June quarter was $13.0M against $10.88M actual — a **16.3% miss** — and the name has beaten on revenue only once in the last four quarters. The full-year street number of $53.1M (dated 7/18/26) sat *above* the new $48–52M guide, but that print is **pre-cut and therefore stale**; post-print consensus almost certainly resets near the guide. Anyone still quoting "street above guide" as evidence of complacency is using a dead anchor.
- The tape says the panic is over: down 53.3% from the 52-week high of $30.10, but **up 69.9% from the $8.27 low**. The market has already re-rated this once, upward. Buying "a 53% drawdown" here is buying a bounce that has mostly happened.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We decomposed the guide instead of accepting it.** The H2 ask ($12.5–14.5M/qtr from $10.88M) is a **$1.6M–$3.6M sequential jump**. The best organic sequential gain in company history is roughly **+$0.5M** — and the seasonality check kills the obvious rescue: reported Q4'25 rose ~$1.5M sequentially, but the company's own January 8-K said the 20% comparable growth *excluded* IRRAflow, which backs organic Q4'25 into ~$9.3M, i.e. ~+$0.44M organic sequential. There is no reliable fourth-quarter seasonal lift, and 2024's fourth quarter actually fell sequentially.
2. **We have a gradeable number.** Our $11.0–12.0M versus the guide-implied $12.5–14.5M is a specific, dated, falsifiable disagreement — and the mechanism is named: consensus and guidance both assume the biologics/drug-delivery line inflects on service work that is *capacity*, not *backlog*. The filing discloses no backlog for that segment and contract-liability releases of only ~$0.7M a quarter.
3. **Guide credibility is measurable and deteriorating.** Combined guidance went **$54–60M (11/6/25) → $52–56M (3/17/26) → $48–52M (8/3/26)** — two cuts inside two quarters of setting the number. That is a stronger argument against management's "return to growth in the third quarter" line than any modelling we can do.
4. **We priced the upside instead of gesturing at it.** A 50% move to **$21.08** implies ~$668M enterprise value, which needs **~$73M of revenue at today's 9.1×**, ~$111M at 6×, ~$67M at 10× — a 2028-ish number at guided growth. The roof isn't in the room.
5. **A leak in the enterprise value nobody was modelling:** the IRRAS earnout runs over three one-year periods at **25% of net sales** of certain acquired products above thresholds. The inorganic revenue carrying the "18% growth" headline arrives with a contingent claim attached, so the true sales multiple is worse than 9.1×.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it.

- The 9.9× sales is **published**. The Buy/$30 target is published. The guide cut is public. The 16% miss is public. Four of the five surviving arguments are consensus-class, not novel. Post-audit tally on the bear case was **1 novel finding / 4 consensus / 2 overturned**.
- The financing scare — the part that would have made this a real dislocation — **isn't true**. The tranche is undrawn and unilateral.
- The one thing our work is genuinely early on (that the H2 sequential ask exceeds anything the company has ever done organically) is reduced, not confirmed: we applied a first-quarter IRRAflow revenue figure to the second quarter, where it is **not disclosed**, so "organic −7%" is *plausible*, not proven. And the year-ago comparison contains a management-acknowledged one-time single-customer order, which flatters our own decomposition.
- The bear case is a **valuation and pace** case. Valuation cases do not pay on a schedule; they can be wrong for a year while a partner headline re-rates the multiple. Which is exactly why we are flat rather than short.

## What could go wrong

*(the PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)*

**Failure narrative 1 — the cheap price arrives with a cheaper business (tripwire: YES).** We buy the $8.50–9.00 reopen band, and the reason it got there is a fourth guide cut. Revenue resets to $42–45M and "5× sales" recomputes to 6.5× on the new number — the multiple never actually compressed, the denominator fell. *Monitorable:* any guidance 8-K/PR, and the quarterly print itself. This is the single most likely way we lose money, because our entry rule is written in multiples and multiples move when the denominator does.

**Failure narrative 2 — the tranche is not the free option we think (tripwire: PARTIAL).** The $25M is described as a company option, but **neither bench read Note 8 or the note-purchase-agreement exhibit** (blue hit a 403; red inferred from a cumulative-proceeds line). If there is a material-adverse-change clause or a minimum-revenue condition precedent, the option evaporates exactly when it is needed, and the next dollar is the untouched **$50M at-the-market program into a ~30.5M-share float** — ~12% of market cap of issuance sitting above the tape. *Monitorable:* an 8-K, a 424B5, or the next 10-Q — but only *after* the fact. Pre-event, this is unreadable until we fetch the exhibit.

**Failure narrative 3 — the partner-side reversal, WITH NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** ClearPoint's biologics revenue is a derivative of other companies' private go/no-go decisions on brain-delivered therapies. A partner can quietly deprioritise a program in an internal portfolio review, or a safety signal in someone else's trial can freeze the whole delivery approach class-wide, and **nothing files, nothing scans, nothing prints**. There is no backlog disclosure, no referral-quality data (that detector was unusable here — the company is the supplier, not the referrer), no trial-registry read on a supplier, and no satellite or facility read that separates "possession of a building" from "booked work." We would learn about it two or three quarters later as service revenue that simply failed to renew. **Unknown-unknown class: counterparty-internal capital-allocation reversal at a private third party — silent partner attrition in a supplier-of-record with no disclosed backlog**, with an adjacent sub-class of *off-tape scientific reversal* (a class-wide safety event at a sponsor we don't cover). We cannot arm a sensor for this. We can only cap size against it.

**The reflexive / structural leg (our own size, predicted flow, doctrine interactions):**
- **Liquidity:** ~726k shares/day at ~$14 ≈ **$10M of average daily volume**. A 0.5%-of-book starter is comfortably inside a day for us; the problem is the exit, not the entry — in a post-miss tape this name's volume concentrates and the spread widens, so anything above ~1 day's volume should be treated as a multi-day unwind and sized accordingly.
- **Predicted flow against us:** the $50M at-the-market program is the structural ceiling. Any bounce is the issuer's best window to sell, so our upside path has a seller in front of it. Insider Form 4s (six on 7/01–7/06/26, one on 9/03/26) plus 144 notices (6/17, 6/29/26) plus 1.325M shares issued in the IRRAS deal add supply, though the July cluster is consistent with the annual grant/vest window and the 144 amounts are unverified — this is supply *context*, not a kill.
- **Doctrine interactions:** we nearly lost money to our own process here. The **stale-dislocation error class** almost got us long a "−53%" name that is +70% off its low. Two detector patterns fought each other on the financing question — *committed_tranche_exhaustion_diff* (red: reconcile announced tranches to cumulative proceeds) fired and produced a false kill, and *tranche_identity_misattribution* (blue: force the net/gross ratio to choose between candidate tranche sums; 87% smells wrong, 96% is fee-consistent) corrected it. Both must be armed as a **pair**, never singly, or the same error recurs. Separately, the evidence pack's own coordinate for the "San Diego Facility" (32.71742/−117.16277, downtown) is **not** the Torrey Pines parcel the thesis leans on (~32.90/−117.24) — any imagery scan run on the pack geocode would have tested the wrong building and returned a confident, wrong answer.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a multiple, not a business: we paid ~9× a twice-cut revenue guide for a device company whose organic base was flat-to-shrinking, the third cut came before the inflection did, and the "cheap" level we waited for turned out to be the same multiple on a smaller number.
