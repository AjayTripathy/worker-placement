# PITCH — 005387.KS — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line; label clearly "PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION")

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY TODAY.** The red bench's "this idea is dead" was overturned, but its "don't buy at this price" survived: after-tax income is ~3.1% (below our 3.5% bar), there is no forced-convergence edge yet, and the price is mid-range — the position is **gated, not killed**, and reopens at ≤₩180,000 or on a cancellation event.

## What we believe

Hyundai Motor's 2nd-series preferred shares (005387.KS, ₩201,500) are the same economic claim on Hyundai's cash flows as the common stock (005380.KS, ₩408,000), minus the vote — yet they trade at a **50.6% discount** to the common. Our own sum-of-the-parts work on Hyundai (completed the same day, live Korean prices, share counts reconciled to Korean regulatory filings) says the *common* is above fair value in every scenario we ran (fair ₩282,000–372,000), while the preferred is 28–46% *below* fair. In plain terms: the distortion at Hyundai is not the car business — it's the gap between the two share classes.

Three things make the preferred worth watching rather than owning today:

1. **A committed dividend.** Hyundai's 2025–2027 shareholder-return plan promises at least ₩10,000/year per share (₩2,500 quarterly, plus a 25%+ payout commitment and a ₩4 trillion buyback). The July 23 quarterly dividend was held at ₩2,500 even though operating profit fell 20.8% — the policy held under stress.
2. **A new legal clock on buybacks becoming real value.** A March 2026 amendment to Korea's Commercial Code now *requires* companies to cancel repurchased shares — newly bought shares within one year of purchase, pre-existing treasury stock by September 5, 2027, with personal fines on directors for non-compliance ([Ministry of Justice implementation guide, 2026-03-11](https://www.moj.go.kr/bbs/moj/182/491790/download.do)). For the first time, the "buybacks never actually shrink the share count" objection has a statutory expiration date.
3. **The discount itself is unstable in our favor's direction.** It went from 28% at end-2025 to 53.4% in June 2026 to ~50.6% now — the widening was driven by index flows that only touch the common. That mechanism can unwind.

What we do **not** believe: that this is a buy at today's price. The income isn't high enough after taxes, and the company's buyback execution so far has short-changed the preferred class (preferreds got 8.45% of buyback spending versus their ~10–11% weight in the capital structure), so there's no active engine closing the gap yet.

## What the market believes

The market prices the preferred as a permanently discounted, second-class instrument: a family-controlled voting structure where the non-voting class never gets treated equally, in a market (Korea) with a long history of exactly that. The 50%+ discount says investors expect (a) buybacks to keep favoring the common, (b) the generous dividend policy to be a temporary "Value-up" gesture that can be rewritten after 2027, and (c) no mechanism that ever forces the two classes toward each other. The recent widening also reflects mechanical selling pressure: passive index flows land on the common only, so when Hyundai de-rated ~39% off its 52-week high on tariff costs and a cooling AI/robotics narrative, the preferred fell alongside it without its own bid.

## Why we might have edge

- **The statutory cancellation clock is new and under-appreciated.** The red (skeptic) bench itself initially got this law *backwards* — reading it as "cancellation got easier, so the absence of cancellation is damning" — when the law actually *mandates* cancellation on a schedule. If a careful adversarial bench misread it, the market plausibly hasn't fully priced it. The ₩33.8bn of preferred shares Hyundai bought in 2026 must, by statute, be cancelled within a year.
- **Our valuation work is independent and recent.** The sum-of-the-parts giving "common above fair, preferred well below fair" was built from live prices and filing-reconciled share counts, and our own documented bias runs *conservative* (we've measured our fair values coming in ~13% low), which strengthens, not weakens, the "preferred is cheap" side.
- **Two dated, checkable catalysts.** The Company Investor Day on August 26, 2026 will address the post-2027 return policy and could announce per-class cancellation details; September 5, 2027 is the legal deadline for cancelling pre-existing treasury stock. Both are events we can grade, not vibes.
- **The discount's own history shows it can close fast** — it was 28% seven months ago.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated symmetrically, the bear case is respectable:

- **No convergence engine exists today.** Buyback execution is *below* the preferred's pro-rata share. The thesis's own numbers concede this. A discount with no closing mechanism can stay wide indefinitely — Korea offers decades of examples.
- **The dividend floor is a policy through 2027, then a question mark.** The charter minimum is ₩100/year. The same August 26 event we count as a catalyst is also where the post-2027 regime gets rewritten — it's two-sided.
- **The income doesn't clear our own bar.** ~5.0% gross is ~3.1% after federal, California, and Korean withholding taxes for this account — below the 3.5% we require for "get paid fairly to wait" positions carrying full automaker equity risk in an unhedged currency.
- **If the common falls to fair value, the preferred can fall with it.** Red's version of this (a locked ratio implying −9% to −31%) was overturned as internally inconsistent — you can't freeze a ratio that moved 25 points in 7 months *because of* the very flows whose unwind you're modeling. But the softer version stands: the direction of the preferred over any given quarter is dominated by the common, and our own model says the common is expensive.
- **Entry isn't distressed.** The preferred is already +22.2% off its 52-week low. Part of the snap-back happened without us.
