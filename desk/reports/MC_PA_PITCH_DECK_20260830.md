# PITCH — MC.PA — ADJUDICATED  — Ownable at a fair price, not a mispricing: a quarter-size starter (0.25% of book) authorized inside the €440–448 band at a €443 limit, balance held for the October print — because the single variable that decides this trade (mainland China demand) is not disclosed by the company and we have no instrument on it.

*(LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton, Paris listing. No position held, no orders working. Adjudication outranks both benches; where a bench is wrong, it is named below.)*

---

## What we believe

LVMH is a very good business going through a real, ordinary downturn in its largest division, and the downturn is getting less bad rather than getting worse.

The facts from the company's own half-year release ([LVMH H1 2026, 27 July 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/27/3333733/0/en/LVMH-Accelerating-growth-in-the-second-quarter-solid-first-half-results.html)):

- Group sales grew 2% on a like-for-like basis in the half, and 3% in the second quarter — a sequential improvement.
- Fashion & Leather Goods, the division that matters most, did **€18.146bn, −5% as reported and −1% like-for-like** for the half, with the second quarter at **+1%** after a first quarter at −2%. So the biggest division crossed from shrinking to barely growing inside the half.
- Profit from recurring operations was €8.7bn at a 22.5% margin. Net profit attributable to shareholders was €5.7bn, flat against last year. Free cash flow was €4.1bn in six months. Net financial debt was €8.245bn — roughly one year of free cash flow against a ~€228bn company.

So: earnings in euros are flat, not falling; the margin is intact at a level nobody else in the industry reaches; the balance sheet is not a factor either way. The stock, meanwhile, is roughly 32% below its 52-week high of €654.70, and last traded around **€447.40 (27 Aug close)** — about 1.7% above its 52-week low of €440.00.

What we are actually buying is a durable franchise at roughly 20–21x flat earnings, with the option that Fashion & Leather Goods keeps inflecting. That is a fair price for a good asset. It is not a bargain, and we are not claiming one.

## What the market believes

The market believes the same thing we do about the next quarter, and something better than we do about the next three years.

- 26 analysts have a mean target of **€568.12** against a €453.80 reference price — about 25% above the market. The Street is not bearish on this name; it is patient on it.
- Consensus for the second quarter was +2.3% like-for-like. LVMH printed +3.0%. The company **beat** the number the market cared about, in the period that just happened.
- Yet the shares kept de-rating. That tells you the market is not trading the quarterly beat; it is trading a view about how long Chinese luxury demand stays weak, and about whether the 2021–22 price increases permanently pushed the aspirational customer out.

This is a crowded, widely-owned, index-constituent mega-cap. Everything in the previous two paragraphs is known to everyone.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thin, and mostly of the "patience and construction" kind rather than the "we know something" kind.

1. **Division-level rather than brand-level comparison.** Against Kering's same-window release ([Kering H1 2026, 28 July 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/28/3334524/0/en/kering-2026-first-half-results-back-to-growth-performance-improvement-strategy-execution-on-track.html)), LVMH's Fashion & Leather Goods at −1% like-for-like sits four points ahead of Gucci at −5%. At the group level the gap is only one point (LVMH +2% vs Kering +1%). Both comparisons are legitimate basis choices; we think the like-for-like unit is division-vs-division, and on that basis LVMH is inflecting earlier. This is a modest, arguable edge — not a dispersion trade.
2. **Time horizon.** We can hold through two or three bad quarters where a fund reporting to consultants cannot. The de-rating from €654 to €447 has already happened; the incremental buyer at these levels is being paid to wait rather than to be right about the next print.
3. **Sizing discipline as the actual edge.** We know we cannot observe the decisive variable, so we are buying a quarter of the target rather than pretending to conviction. Getting the size right on a name we do not have an information edge in is worth more than a false thesis.

We do **not** claim divergence from the Street. We sit 25% below its target with no mechanism we can name for the gap. Agreeing with the consensus at a discount to its target is not an edge; it is agreement at a discount.

## Why it might be priced in

Very likely most of it is.

- **21x for flat earnings is not distressed.** The company is doing roughly €21.9 of earnings per share and the shares are ~€447–458. There is no valuation dislocation here — just a quality business that used to trade at 30x trading at 20x.
- **The chart says the de-rating is still in progress.** Being 32% off the high but only 1.7% off the low means the stock has not found a base. Buying into that gives us almost no cushion before new lows.
- **The inflection is public.** The half-year release is titled "accelerating growth in the second quarter." Every fact we lean on was in a press release read by thousands of people on 27 July.
- **The bad news is public too.** Swiss watch exports to mainland China fell **18.5% in July 2026**, with Hong Kong at +0.5% ([Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry data via trade press](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/swiss-watch-exports-july/); [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/posts/swiss-watch-exports-plummet-china-hong-kong-markets-lead-industry-decline)). This is the first hard datapoint from inside the current quarter and it is negative. Note this is a secondary source — the Federation's own page returned errors on every direct fetch attempt.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — China does not turn, and October confirms it.** Mainland demand keeps deteriorating; the October revenue print shows Fashion & Leather Goods back below zero; the shares break €440 and base in the €380s. We are then down 12–15% on a position we entered 1.7% above the prior low. The precedent for how violent this print can be runs both ways: LVMH rose 12% in a day on its Q3 2025 numbers ([CNBC, 15 Oct 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/15/lvmh-3q-results.html)). *Monitorable:* yes — monthly Swiss watch exports, Hong Kong retail sales, Kering and Richemont commentary. This is the risk we can actually watch.

**Failure narrative 2 — the aspirational customer is gone, and we find out over five years, not five quarters. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** After roughly five years of double-digit price increases, the entry-level customer LVMH added in 2021–22 does not come back at current price points. The damage does not appear as a bad quarter; it appears as management defending the 22.5% margin by cutting marketing, store investment and product development, so reported earnings stay flat while brand equity quietly erodes. By the time it is visible in an organic growth number, five years of compounding have already been lost.

We have no instrument for this at any useful cadence. There is no public price index for handbags; the organic growth number blends price and volume and the company chooses the blend; there is no cohort or repeat-purchase disclosure; and the transmission is through a margin that looks *healthy* the whole way down. **Unknown-unknown class: latent-variable erosion with no external instrument** — a variable that is real, economically decisive, and observable only through an aggregate that the issuer defines, computes and controls. This is the same family as the disclosure defect our red bench correctly identified: LVMH reports an "Asia excluding Japan" bucket wide enough to absorb mainland China entirely, so the country the whole thesis turns on has no line item in any filing. Kering, in the same window, said mainland China "remained challenging." Both statements are true at once. That is the point.

**Failure narrative 3 — the reflexive and structural leg: we get filled precisely when we are wrong.** Our own size is irrelevant to the market — 1.0% of this book is roughly €40–50k, about a hundred shares, against a mega-cap trading hundreds of millions of euros a day (daily volume not pulled this session — treat the ratio as obviously immaterial rather than measured). Market impact is not the risk. The structural risk runs the other way:

- **A resting bid at €443 into a known event is accidental short-volatility.** The €440–448 band sits 1.7% under market. The most likely way it fills is a gap down on the September watch-export release or the October print — meaning we buy from someone who just learned the thing we admitted we cannot observe. We would be selling the market a free option on the exact news that decides the trade.
- **We would be bidding into our own predicted flow.** Our own base case for the September China datapoint is negative. Parking a limit in the band we ourselves expect the news to push the stock through is the ladder mistake, not the ladder.
- **Doctrine interactions.** (a) Currency: this is a euro asset in a dollar-reporting book. Our blue bench is right that the −5% currency drag on LVMH's *reported* numbers is a base effect that decays at an unchanged EUR/USD of 1.1583 — and wrong to treat that as neutral for us. LVMH's translation effect fades; our dollar return on a euro share does not. (b) Tax: a new lot starts a short-term clock and carries no harvest value for about twelve months, and any harvestable loss is a dollar loss after translation, not the euro loss on the screen. (c) Crowding: as a heavily-owned index constituent, our entry and exit are entirely price-taking and our fill quality is set by other people's flow.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that mainland Chinese demand for Fashion & Leather Goods kept deteriorating through the second half, and we could not see it — because LVMH does not disclose the number, our detector suite has no telemetry inside China, and the only external proxy we found (Swiss watch exports, −18.5% in July) was already pointing the wrong way when we bought.**
