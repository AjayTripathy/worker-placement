**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No entry today at $47.34; the stock is roughly fairly priced against our own scenario math (EV $49.6 vs tape $47.34, +4.8%), and both the stock and the options are unattractive, so we pre-register reopen levels and arm sensors instead of buying.

## What we believe

Structure Therapeutics is developing aleniglipron, a pill you swallow once a day that does what Ozempic and Zepbound do by injection. In its Phase 2 trial it produced 15.3% absolute weight loss at 44 weeks, and the extension study showed 16.2% at 56 weeks with the curve still going down. That is, on the evidence available, the most effective oral weight-loss drug in development. It also cleared the trap that killed Pfizer's version of this drug: the liver. The company reports no drug-induced liver injury, no persistent enzyme elevation and no heart-rhythm signal across more than 625 people dosed.

We also believe three things that cut against owning it today:

1. **The money arrives too late.** The trial that decides this company finishes in **September 2028**. We verified that directly against the trial registry rather than accepting it from either side of our own debate: ACCOMPLISH-1, 3,600 patients, primary endpoint at week 76, primary completion September 2028, sponsor Gasherbrum Bio (a wholly owned Structure subsidiary) — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07654361](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07654361). A drug approval would follow in 2029 and a launch in 2030, as the fifth pill of its kind to market.
2. **The price of the product collapsed before the trial started.** Lilly's pill was approved on 2026-04-01 and immediately sold at $25/month to insured patients and $149–349/month cash-pay depending on dose. Novo's pill launched in January 2026 at the cheapest cash price the category has ever seen. The revenue per patient that everyone modelled two years ago no longer exists.
3. **The company will need more money, and it will raise it into any rally we participate in.** It has $1.34 billion, which it says lasts through end-2028 — *excluding* the cost of preparing to sell a drug. It also has an untouched $400 million at-the-market share-sale program sitting over the stock.

Netting those out: this is a good drug in a bad-priced category, owned by a company that runs out of money the same quarter its decisive data arrives. At $47.34 you are paid about fairly for that. You are not paid *well*.

## What the market believes

The stock is down 50% from its 52-week high of $94.90 and up 156% from its low of $18.49. That shape tells you the market already lived through the repricing — this is a halved former momentum name, not something nobody has looked at.

Published analyst targets average **$110.91**, with a wide spread: HCW cut from $100 to $70, Citi sits at $97. The number doing the work in those models is peak annual sales of $3–4 billion. Ours is $1.3–2.4 billion, because we use the *post-April-2026* prices actually being charged rather than the prices that existed when those models were built. We think a meaningful part of that $110.91 average is stale marks that predate the price collapse, but we could not establish that for every contributor and we say so below.

Separately, the market appears to assign **zero value** to the company's second drug, an oral amylin agonist in Phase 1 — and so did both sides of our internal debate until one of them caught it.

## Why we might have edge

**The takeover floor is much higher than a first read suggests.** Pfizer paid **$10 billion** for Metsera, a company whose lead drug was an *injectable* about to begin Phase 3, plus a Phase 1 amylin and a Phase 1 oral. Roche paid $1.65 billion up front ($5.3 billion total) for a Phase 2 amylin. Structure, at roughly **$2.1 billion** net of its cash, owns a Phase-3-started, best-in-class *oral* plus the most advanced oral amylin in the industry. Novo lost the Metsera auction and still has no oral small molecule. Every comparable transaction sits above where this trades.

**The liver question is closed and we do not think that is fully priced.** The entire class carries the memory of Pfizer's danuglipron, which died on liver toxicity. Structure has now dosed 625+ people with no drug-induced liver injury and discontinuation rates of 3.7% (Phase 2b, higher doses) and 2% (extension). The market has watched one oral GLP-1 after another fail on tolerability; a clean one is worth more than a discount rate implies.

**Two dated, unscheduled catalysts sit in front of us, not in 2028.** The company guided 72-week extension data and the Phase 1 amylin results to the second half of 2026. Either can print on any morning. Neither is on a calendar the market can position around.

**And the honest counterweight:** none of these are secret. The registry date is public. The comparable deals are public. This is a name with real coverage and real dispersion — we are not the only ones who have done this arithmetic.

## Why it might be priced in

The bear case here is **consensus**, not discovery. HCW cut its target to $70 on precisely the pricing argument we make. The stock has already halved. The trial date is one registry lookup away from anyone. When we ask "what do we know that the price does not," the honest answer for most of our findings is: nothing.

Our valuation says the same thing arithmetically. Taking the scenario set below at our own probabilities, expected value is **$49.6 per share against a $47.34 tape** — about 5% of upside, which is inside the error bars of every assumption feeding it. A fairly-priced asset with a two-year fuse and a guaranteed share issuance in the middle is not an edge; it is a coin flip you pay a spread to enter.

The one place we may genuinely differ from the tape is the takeover floor — but that is a tail we cannot handicap from outside, and we should not pretend a 15% probability we invented is knowledge.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the tolerability number regresses and takes the thesis with it.**
The 3.7% discontinuation rate that anchors the "clean drug" story comes from **one patient out of 27**. There is no confidence interval around that. The earlier, faster titration schedule produced 10.4%. In a 3,600-patient Phase 3 the true number will land somewhere in between, and if it lands near 10% the "best-in-class *and* best-tolerated" claim narrows to "roughly the same as everyone else's." *Tripwire: exists but slow* — the 72-week extension data, then Phase 3 safety updates. This is monitorable, and it is the failure we would see coming.

**Failure narrative 2 — the price war goes deeper than the maintenance dose.**
Our valuation now rests on patients paying $2,400/year, which is the *observed maintenance* cash price. That price is two years old and set by two companies competing for share. If Lilly and Novo push net realized price toward the $900/year end — and both have every incentive to lock the category before a fifth entrant arrives — the base case drops from $79.90 to $21.50 per share and no clinical result rescues it. *Tripwire: exists* — Lilly and Novo disclose obesity net price movement quarterly, and pharmacy-benefit formulary decisions are public.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE — the class resets from outside the company.**
The decisive datum is 24 months away, which means 24 months of pure narrative in between. In that window the thing most likely to destroy this position is an event with **no issuer-side observable at all**: a rare liver or oncology signal emerging in a *competitor's* marketed oral, generalized by regulators into a class-wide monitoring requirement or REMS that lands on a drug we own but did not cause; or, symmetrically, Lilly or Novo announcing an oral GLP-1/amylin *combination* that makes a single-mechanism oral pill commercially obsolete before it ever launches. Neither shows up in Structure's filings, its trial registry, its Form 4 cadence, or any price gate we can arm. The tape moves first and the explanation arrives second.

**Unknown-unknown class, named: EXOGENOUS CLASS RESET — a third party's data or regulatory action revalues our asset with zero observable on our issuer.** We have no sensor for this class and we should stop pretending a filing-watch substitutes for one. The only defence is position size and the fact that we own nothing today.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

*Our own size is not the problem.* At a 0.4–0.8% starter this is a few thousand shares against a name that trades a nine-figure notional daily — well under 1% of a day's volume. We flag that our volume figure is estimated, not pulled (item 8 below), but no plausible value makes our footprint matter.

*The company's size is the problem.* The $400 million share-sale program is untouched. At $47 that is about 8.5 million shares — roughly 12% of the shares outstanding. At $71 it is 5.6 million, about 8%. **We would be buying a stock whose issuer has a standing, pre-registered incentive to sell into exactly the rally our thesis predicts.** Our own doctrine says never bid into flow you are forecasting; here the forecast flow is the issuer's, it is already documented in a filed prospectus supplement, and it is triggered by our being right. The December-2025 buyers who paid $65.00 are down 27% and are the natural supply on any move back up.

*Doctrine interactions, stated plainly.* Three of our own rules point the same way and we are following them rather than arguing around them: this is human-gated development-stage biotech, so nothing stages regardless of how the debate resolves; 2026 is a tax-harvest year and this position's loss branch would not land in it; and the holding period required to reach the decisive data (September 2028) is two years, which makes any near-term exit short-term-taxable on a thesis that has not yet resolved.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a clinically excellent drug into a category whose price had already collapsed, and were then diluted at the bottom by the company's own share sale while waiting two years for data that arrived after the economics stopped mattering.**

### Tax overlay

2026 is a harvest year for this book. The loss branch of this position — scenario A, at 32% probability, the single most likely individual outcome — needs to land in the tax year we intend to use it.

- **A stock position entered today** does not resolve until at least the 72-week extension data and realistically not until September 2028. Its loss branch lands in 2027 or 2028, not 2026. It is also short-term-taxable for the first twelve months, on a thesis that cannot resolve inside twelve months. **Wrapper mismatch.**
- **Any option long enough to span the September-2028 readout** expires in 2029. Its loss defers three tax years. Under harvest-year rules that is excluded unless the edge numerically dominates — and the edge here is *negative* (we would be paying 75% volatility for a 55% distribution). **Excluded on both counts.**
- **A short-dated option positioned for the 2H-2026 catalysts** would land its loss in 2026 correctly, but it is not the thesis — it is a bet on the timing of an unscheduled press release, and it is being sold at 73–81% volatility against 70–90% realized. No edge, wrong question.

The tax overlay and the volatility arithmetic point at the same door. **No position.**
