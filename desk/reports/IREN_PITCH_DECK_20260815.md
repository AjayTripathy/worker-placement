# PITCH — IREN — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Buy a half-sized starter (~0.5% of the book) near $44, with the second half strictly gated on the August 27 fiscal-year results passing three tests; both benches independently landed on this same half-size.**

## What we believe

IREN is an Australia-incorporated, Nasdaq-listed data-center company (formerly a bitcoin miner) that has signed roughly $2.8 billion of new AI-compute contracts, headlined by Microsoft. The bearish story that dominated the tape earlier this year — "the backlog is vapor and the buildout can't be financed" — is now refuted by dated primary documents:

- On July 20 the company announced contracted annualized revenue above $4 billion, roughly 85% of it contracted, on four-year weighted terms with about 45% customer prepayment on new contracts.
- On August 13 an 8-K announced Horizon 1 was delivered to Microsoft and accepted, plus an NVIDIA "Exemplar Cloud" certification ([SEC filing index for IREN, CIK 1878848](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=1878848)).
- Deferred revenue grew from $0.9 million to $120.4 million in nine months per the [Q3 FY26 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001878848/000187884826000026/iren-20260331.htm) — prepayment cash is actually landing, which vapor doesn't do.

So we believe the *demand* is real. What we do **not** believe is that the stock is cheap on the delivered plan. The live risk has shifted from "is the business real" to "can they build and deliver on schedule, and what is left for shareholders after they pay for it."

## What the market believes

The stock trades at $44.06 (52-week range $17.22–$76.87), which is 43% below its high but 2.5x its low, and it rose 15.6% on the Microsoft-acceptance announcement. Short interest is very large — three independent sources put it at 22–32% of the float — so this is a genuine battleground: a big crowd is betting on execution failure or dilution, and part of the recent rally was those shorts covering. Meaningfully, this is **not** a neglected or misunderstood name where we'd have an information advantage from simply reading the filings; both sides are heavily engaged.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The dead bear case is still partially in the price.** With a fourth of the float short, some of the market is still positioned for a "backlog is fake / can't be financed" outcome that primary documents now contradict (signed contracts, prepayment cash on the balance sheet, a dedicated GPU financing facility).
2. **A confirmed near-term catalyst with a clear pass/fail test.** Fiscal-year results on August 27 (confirmed by company press release) will restate the contracted-revenue figure, hopefully date the next Microsoft delivery phase ("Horizon 2"), and address second-half funding. We wrote down the three gates in advance, so we can't rationalize a bad print after the fact.
3. **We know exactly what caps the upside.** Our review of the 10-Q debt note found $2.3 billion of convertible notes that convert at $51.40 — only 15% above today's price — plus NVIDIA warrants on 30 million shares at $70. That gives us disciplined trim levels most of the crowd isn't using.

## Why it might be priced in

This is the strongest finding of the whole court, and it survived both benches: **the company's own fully-delivered plan, valued generously, roughly equals today's price.** The math: at ~$22 billion enterprise value (market cap, plus converts corrected for the deep-in-the-money portion, plus the GPU facility, minus cash), the company's own $4 billion contracted-revenue target at a 60% margin and a full 9x multiple works out to about $43–44 per share — i.e., today. To get +50% you need either a premium multiple or the **2027 leg (1.2 gigawatts of additional capacity), which is uncontracted, unfunded (~$3.5 billion+ open, with the company itself citing "additional financing initiatives"), and physically unverified by us.** Add to that:

- The contracted-revenue metric assumes 100% utilization (contracted price × 8,760 hours/year) while actual AI-cloud revenue last quarter was $33.6 million — the market is being asked to underwrite a ~30x revenue ramp in nine months.
- Share count grew 32% in nine months (258.1M → 341.0M). Issuing stock into strength is documented behavior, not a hypothesis.
- The convert strikes at $51.40 and warrants at $70 create standing sellers overhead.
- The "acceptance by Microsoft" exists only in IREN's own press release — no Microsoft quote, no filed contract, no billing trigger — and the NVIDIA certification comes from a party holding warrants in the company, so it is not independent validation.
