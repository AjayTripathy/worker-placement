## What we believe

O-I Glass makes glass bottles and jars. Its European business fell apart this year — segment operating profit margin went from about 12% to about 1% — and the stock is down roughly 60% from its 52-week high, sitting about 10% above its 52-week low.

We believe the European collapse is mostly a **timing problem, not a permanent cost problem**. O-I is running the same kind of plant-closure program its European competitors ran two years ago. Verallia said in its own words that its European margin expansion came from footprint cuts and a 3.2% reduction in net cash costs. O-I is mid-program: $53M of benefit booked in Q2 against roughly $700M of cumulative charges. Two furnaces also went down in Europe during the quarter — a one-off the company disclosed and our own red team dropped from its list of causes.

When we do the math on the company's own guidance midpoint (about $1.05B of full-year EBITDA at 5.5x, minus $4.65B of net debt, across 153.7M shares), we get **$7.34 a share**. The stock is $6.94. That is not a big margin of safety, and we say so.

So why own it at all? Because the only surviving reason to stay flat was "we don't disagree with anyone." Our fair value sits *between* RBC's $8 target and BofA's $11 target — below the lowest of them. There is no named, load-bearing number where we differ from the street. Under our consensus-kill rule (ratified today), a kill that amounts to "everyone already knows this and it's roughly fairly priced" is not a reason to hold zero — it is a reason to hold the smallest position that keeps us honest and keeps the name on the clock. A starter costs us a quarter of a percent of the book and buys a graded track record into a dated event. Staying flat would have cost us nothing and taught us nothing.

The one fact that makes this ownable rather than merely cheap: **the option does not expire**. The $1.11B revolver is undrawn, and the only leverage covenant is a *secured* leverage test at 2.50x against debt that is mostly unsecured. There is no covenant cliff forcing a bad outcome, and equity has no carrying cost. We can wait for October 27.

## What the market believes

The market believes Europe is structurally broken and the equity is a thin sliver on top of a lot of debt.

The specific case, and it is a real one:
- Management wrote **all** of Europe's goodwill off — $873M — and on top of that booked a **$96M valuation allowance** against European deferred tax assets. That second act has a technical meaning: management is asserting it is more likely than not that European taxable income will *not* show up. That is the company signing its name to "structural," not "cyclical."
- $608M of goodwill remains, all of it in the Americas, against only $539M of book equity. A second write-off is available.
- Analysts are still cutting. BofA is at Underperform with an $11 target; RBC moved to Sector Perform with an **$8** target, citing competitor oversupply and free cash flow "barely breakeven" in 2027. Citi cut. Short interest is 5.62% of float and rising (+1.93% month over month).
- The company cut its own Fit-to-Win savings target from $750M to $650M, withdrew EPS guidance, and is spending $450M of capex funded by negative free cash flow. Even hitting guidance means full-year free cash flow is *negative* — the business does not pay down debt this year.
- The stock is 1% off its low three weeks after the print. Nobody is stepping in.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We corrected a comparison the bearish case got wrong, and the correction is checkable.** The bear case's headline was that O-I's European margin (0.9%) sits against Verallia's 20.7% — a 23x gap. Those are two different measures at two different perimeters: O-I's is segment operating profit (after depreciation); Verallia's is group adjusted EBITDA (before it, and including non-European business). Corrected to the same measure and the same region, it is roughly **8.0% vs 15.8%** — about a 2x gap. A 2x gap that a live restructuring program is closing is a completely different investment than a 23x structural chasm.

2. **We read the peer prints one quarter deeper than the headline.** The half-year numbers say the peers are fine. The *quarter* says otherwise: Verallia's Q2 standalone adjusted EBITDA fell 5.6% to €192M, with margin down 116bps; Vidrala's organic revenue fell 3.8% on both weaker price and weaker volume. There *is* an industry price event. O-I is the worst-positioned participant in it, not the only participant.

3. **We have a dated resolution, and most people are not waiting for it.** October 27 prints European segment operating profit. Above $40M and the lag-closing thesis has evidence. Below, and it doesn't. That is a clean, cheap test.

4. **The downside was mispriced by our own bench.** Corrected, the bad case is about $1.97 a share, not zero.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: it mostly is. This is the weakest part of the pitch and we are not going to dress it up.

- Our own fair value ($7.34) is **below the lowest published target** on the street ($8). Where we differ from consensus at all, we are *more* bearish. Being quietly below the street on a heavily levered cyclical is not an edge; it is a rounding error with leverage attached.
- +6% to our own fair value is not enough to justify a normal position in something this volatile. That is precisely why the size is 0.25% and not 2%.
- The restructuring-lag story is public. Verallia said out loud where its margin came from. Anyone comparing the two companies can see it.
- The one genuinely differentiated check available here — using satellite CO2 plume data to see which European furnaces are actually running versus idled — **we did not run**, because we do not have the site coordinates. That is the check that would have given us something the street doesn't have, and we skipped it.
- We are buying into visible selling pressure: downgrades still landing, short interest still building, price pinned near the low with no absorption. We normally treat that as a reason to wait for the flow to finish.
