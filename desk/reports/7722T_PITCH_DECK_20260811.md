## What this company is

Kokusai Keisokki builds build-to-order testing machines — the equipment tire, EV-battery, and materials makers use to torture-test their products. Founded family still controls ~43% of the shares. Listed on the Tokyo Standard market since 2001. Nobody on the sell side covers it; foreigners own 6%.

## What happened, and why the machine flagged it

On August 7 it reported a first quarter that looked terrible on the surface: revenue −25.5%, operating profit −60%, only 16% of the full-year plan done. The stock gapped −7.0% on the next session on 6.5× normal volume — from ¥815 to ¥758 — landing 6.6% above a 52-week low it had already visited in May. Our new international dislocation sweep fired on it that afternoon; the court ran the same evening.

The company's explanation, and the reason full-year guidance was left **unchanged**: the miss is *acceptance timing*, not lost demand — parts shortages stretched manufacturing periods, so machines that are built and ordered haven't been formally accepted (and therefore not booked as revenue) yet. Backlog is +10.3% year over year.

## The three gates (why we are not buying today)

The kill-side bench moved every quantitative leg *against* the thesis under verification — none fatally, but enough that three numbers must be read before a single lot:

1. **Whose cash is it?** As a build-to-order manufacturer, some of that ¥8.5bn may be customer advances (deposits for machines not yet delivered). Every Japanese primary source that carries the line — TDnet, JPX, minkabu, ullet — returned 403s all session. If advances are ¥3–4bn, EV roughly doubles and the multiple is ~5–6× mid-cycle, not 2×. **Gate: advances ≤ ~¥1.5bn in the annual report.**
2. **Timing or demand?** The bull's "backlog +10.3%" is arithmetically contaminated: backlog rises *by identity* when acceptances stall, even if new orders fell. Only the order-intake figure answers timing-vs-demand, and it was never quoted. **Gate: order intake flat-or-up.** (This critique was strong enough that it's now a desk-wide detector: `backlog_masks_order_intake`.)
3. **The US tax trap.** Cash and deposits are 41% of total assets against the 50% PFIC asset-test line — and the ratio *worsens* in a downcycle exactly when we'd want to hold. A trip makes the dividend non-qualified and forces mark-to-market ordinary treatment in our tax-loss-harvesting book. **Gate: annual PFIC re-test.**
