## What we believe

QXO is a roll-up: it bought Beacon (roofing distribution, closed 4/29/25), Kodiak (lumber and building materials), and TopBuild (insulation, cash closed 7/1/26). The stock is down 55% per share from its February 18, 2026 high of $27.61 and trades at $12.365 in the pack tape.

Three things we think are true:

1. **The drawdown is real holder damage, not a bookkeeping illusion.** A shareholder from February is down 55%. The fact that the share count grew from ~565M to ~1.04B does not undo that.
2. **There is real margin compression, but it is smaller than the headline.** Adjusted EBITDA margin went from 10.7% (Q2-25) to 8.4% (Q2-26) — a 230bp fall. Strip out Kodiak's lower-margin $595M of revenue at a plausible 6% margin and about **180bp still survives**. The mix excuse does not close the gap: to restore 10.7% you would need the rest of the business to have earned $283.7M of EBITDA against a reported total of $272M, meaning Kodiak would have had to lose $11.7M. That is not credible.
3. **But we cannot show the stock is expensive.** On our pro-forma numbers the tape implies roughly **11.2x EV/EBITDA** (≈$12.9B equity + ~$7.0B net debt + ~$3.0B senior preferred ÷ ~$2.05B pro-forma EBITDA), versus the **14.9x QXO itself paid for TopBuild**. Neither bench could establish a fair value below the current price. Avoiding a name because risks are visible is not an edge.

Net belief: fairly priced, wide outcomes, no directional edge yet. The one number that would create an edge — the street's Q3 adjusted-EBITDA estimate — **neither bench retrieved.**

## What the market believes

- Q3-26 revenue ≈ **$4.99B**; FY26 revenue ≈ **$13.5B**; FY26 EPS ≈ **−$0.538** (already cut from −$0.452). A loss year is consensus, not a discovery.
- Last print, adjusted EBITDA **beat** by roughly $19M ($272M vs ~$253M) and adjusted EPS of $0.08 was in line, while GAAP was **−$0.14** (a $55M net loss). The market is willing to look at adjusted numbers during integration.
- The short base is crowded: one source puts QXO as the **most-shorted industrial at >18% of float**; another shows 34.0M shares / 5.44% at a stale $19.98 price. The two disagree and we have not reconciled them.
- Building products is soft, leverage is high, dilution is ongoing. All three are inside a −$0.54 consensus EPS and a −55% tape. **They are priced.**

## Why we might have edge

- **The mix defence is arithmetically dead.** Anyone reading "it was just Kodiak" is wrong by ~180bp. That is our only differentiated fundamental fact.
- **A second, independent corroboration nobody in the debate leaned on:** adjusted gross margin fell to **24.7% from 25.3%** (−60bp), which is closer to price/mix than to portfolio mix.
- **The screen that surfaced this name is broken in a knowable way.** Per-share drawdown on a serial acquirer overstates equity-value change (−55% per share vs ~−31% market cap). Knowing *why* the screen fires stops us from paying up for a fake dislocation — and stops us from re-buying the same artifact next month.
- **Supply is documented, not guessed.** A 424B7 registers **95,876,547 Kodiak consideration shares** for resale — about 9% of shares outstanding — into a falling tape ([primary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1236275/000110465926051639/tm2612770-1_424b7.htm)). Most narratives about "flowback" are hand-waving; this one has a document.
- **Cheap-vs-what.** The company paid 14.9x for TopBuild and its own stock is at ~11.2x. If integration works at all, that gap is the trade.

## Why it might be priced in

- Every bear leg we hold is consensus: dilution, leverage, soft demand, negative GAAP EPS.
- The revenue bar we would have used ($4.8–5.0B) **is** the street number to the decimal. We reproduced consensus and nearly called it research.
