# PITCH — IMRX — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — human-gated (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Immuneering (CIK 1790340) is a pre-revenue, one-asset cancer-drug developer whose stock is half off its 52-week high ($5.04 vs $10.08) and sitting on a lot of cash relative to its size. Cash was $182.7M at 6/30/26 against a market value of about $326M (Class A only) to $338M (adding the small Class B block) — so more than half the price is cash in the bank. Primary source for the cash figure: the 8/5/26 earnings 8-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001790340/000179034026000088/imrx-20260805xexx991.htm, and the Q2 10-Q, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1790340/000179034026000091/.

What we actually believe, stated narrowly:

1. The real burn is about $17.15M per quarter, not $20M. That is filed arithmetic: cash went $217.0M (12/31/25) → $182.7M (6/30/26), i.e. $34.3M in six months. At that rate the money lasts roughly 10.6 quarters — into the first half of 2029 — which matches the company's own "into 2029" language.
2. The business value net of cash is therefore about $156M–$168M. That is a modest price for a Phase 3 pancreatic-cancer program, but it is **not** a giveaway.
3. The "cash floor" people lean on is softer than it looks. Founders control the votes through Class B, the mandate is one drug, and management will spend to the readout. Outside holders have no lever to force cash back. The floor that is actually enforceable is the *leftover* cash at the readout — roughly $25M–$45M — plus scrap value, not today's $2.5–2.8 per share.
4. A stock sale before the readout is probable, not certain, and it will likely be priced off a depressed tape.
5. Net of all that, our own scenario math gives about $4.23 per share of expected value against a $5.04 tape. That is a fair-to-slightly-rich price. We are not owners here yet.

## What the market believes

The market has voted twice on this company's own data — roughly −21% in early January and −23% around June 1–2 (both event dates are **unverified** in our pack; we have no price series, only the 52-week extremes). The read implied by those moves is that 17.3-month median overall survival in 55 selected, fitter patients, with a confidence interval whose top end is not yet reached, is not clearly better than the decade-old standard-of-care benchmark. The market also appears to be pricing a dilution event: the stock has retraced only 19% of its peak-to-trough range ((5.04−3.86)/(10.08−3.86)), so it is trading nearer the panic than the recovery.

Honest caveat: neither bench could produce a single published outside estimate — no probability-of-success number, no peak-sales range, no coverage count. "The market believes X" is, for this name, an inference from two price moves, not a measured consensus. An uncovered $326M microcap can be wrong for a long time in either direction.

## Why we might have edge

- **Arithmetic edge over the loudest bear version of this story.** The bear case circulating internally used a $20M/quarter burn that appears in no filing; it is our own stress input. At the filed $17.15M, runway extends about three quarters further and the "raise into a blackout with one quarter of cash left" story loses its teeth. Anyone trading the $20M number is trading a number the company never printed.
- **Gross-to-net reconciliation nobody did.** Q2 gross operating expense was $14.0M R&D + $5.0M G&A = $19.0M, yet net cash only fell about $17.15M. The ~$1.85M/quarter gap is non-cash stock comp (roughly $1.7–2.0M/quarter) plus interest earned on ~$180M of cash (roughly $1.8M/quarter). That wedge is exactly why "into 2029" is a true statement rather than promotional.
- **Balance sheet inside the payoff table.** The bear prize table valued the drug and forgot the cash. Adding $182.7M back moves the worst realistic funded cell from about $0.90 to about $3.58 per share and puts three of nine cells above +50%. The downside tail was understated as badly as the upside.
- **A dated, cheap test.** The 11/11 print (date unconfirmed) settles three things at once for free: share count, cash, and whether R&D is ramping. We can be flat and still be first to know.

## Why it might be priced in

- Two derates on the company's own data is the market doing the work. There is no obvious new fact in our hands that the tape has not seen.
- More than half the market value is cash. That is a well-known screen; net-cash biotechs are not an undiscovered category, and the reason they trade there is precisely the dilution and no-forcing-mechanism problem we describe.
- Every number in our payoff table — the 1.9× peak-sales multiple, the launch year, the success probabilities — is analyst-constructed and unverified. We are not disagreeing with a measured consensus; we are substituting our guesses for guesses we never read.
- The entry is +30.6% off the low. If the dislocation was the trade, we missed the good part of it.
- Nothing value-bearing is scheduled to print for roughly 21 months. Being right early is indistinguishable from being wrong.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — Dilution at the drawdown low (has a tripwire).** The company raises $100–150M in 2027 at a 10–25% discount because it must fund enrollment, and there is no good news to raise into. Share count goes ~67M → ~90M+, every per-share cell in the payoff table compresses by 15–25%, and the offering resets the tape lower for months. Tripwires exist and are cheap: an S-3/424B5, an ATM prospectus supplement, an 8-K, or a jump in the 10-Q cover-page share count.

**Failure narrative 2 — Silent clinical degradation, no tripwire at all (unknown-unknown class: CLINICAL OPACITY / silent-state failure).** Enrollment slows, a safety-monitoring committee quietly narrows or re-randomizes, sites drop, or the internal view of the survival curve deteriorates — and none of it is disclosed because none of it is individually material. There is **no monitorable signal** between now and the readout. Registry text and site counts are a weak proxy at best and sponsors update them late. This is the class of failure where our sensor list cannot help by construction: the state of the asset is private for 21 months, and the first public datum is also the terminal one. We should size as if we are blind here, because we are.

**Failure narrative 3 — The floor was never a floor.** The readout misses, the company arrives with $25–45M, and dual-class control means no wind-down, no return of capital, no activist path. The stock does not stop at cash-per-share; it goes to scrap-plus-a-story, call it $0.60. Partial tripwires only: 13D/G changes, Form 4 sale codes, any governance or capital-return announcement — none of which is reliable.

**Reflexive and structural leg (stated plainly, including what we don't know).** We do not have average daily volume, short interest, or borrow — that is a real coverage gap, not a rounding error. Base-rate estimate for a $5 stock with a ~$330M cap: 400k–900k shares/day, i.e. $2–4.5M notional. A quarter-unit (0.25% of book) starter is small in absolute terms but plausibly 10–20% of one day's volume, and the exit is into the same order book the company's own at-the-market program will be selling into. In a stock where the issuer is a structural seller, our size is not the reflexive problem — *their* size is, and we are junior to it.

Predicted flow, in order of likelihood: issuer at-the-market selling into any strength; December tax-loss selling in a name already down 50%; offering-related hedging pressure if a deal is marketed; nothing supportive from index or ETF demand at this cap.

Doctrine interaction — this is the part that should worry us most. Two of our own patterns fire on this name simultaneously and in *opposite* directions: "buy the net-cash floor" says support at $2.5–2.8, while "cash floor without a forcing mechanism" says the enforceable floor is roughly 7× lower. If we hold several dual-class, single-asset, cash-heavy names sized off the first doctrine, they will all fail together for the same structural reason, and our book-level diversification is an illusion. Related: the "financing squeeze inside an evidence blackout" pattern and the "spend-to-readout with no forcing mechanism" pattern are the same trade seen from two ends; do not count them as two independent confirmations.

**Expression math, priced off the table itself (undiscounted, to the readout):**

Conditional recommendation only: **sell only if the premium is ≥$0.15 and the net assignment basis is ≤$3.85** (inside reopen band 1), and only after the 11/11 gates pass.
- **Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated on a single mid-2028 event — two clusters at ~$0.60 and ~$7–9, almost nothing in between at that date. A single-vol lognormal model prices drift, not a fork; near-dated options therefore misprice the event and long-dated options embed a smile we cannot see without the chain. Treat any single implied-vol number as unusable for the 2028 expiry.
- **Instrument output of the above arithmetic: STOCK, limit orders only, at the reopen bands.** Long calls fail on price (2× our fair value at the money, ~5× out of the money). Long-dated premium selling fails because it shorts our own tail. Short-dated cash-secured puts are the only options route with positive arithmetic, and they are **gated and unpriceable today** because no chain data exists. Covered calls are moot with no position.

**Tax overlay.** 2026 is a harvest year. The loss branch of this trade resolves at the mid-2028 readout, so any long-dated expression pushes the loss into 2028 or later — deferred out of the intended tax year and therefore **excluded** unless the edge numerically dominates. It does not: EV is 16% *below* the tape. The short-dated December-2026 put is the only expression whose loss branch lands in the current tax year, which is a second, independent reason it is the only options structure on the table.
