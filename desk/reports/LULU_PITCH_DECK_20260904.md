# PITCH — LULU — ADJUDICATED  — WATCH / NO ENTRY: the stock is cheap but the earnings decline is real and still going, so we wait for the Americas sales trend to turn before we buy anything (conviction 5/10).

*Price used throughout: ~$100.3 (desk feed, 2026-09-04 21:12Z) / $100.61 vendor close, after a −17.4% single-day fall from $121.77. New 52-week low ($98.00 intraday). High was $225.98. We hold nothing and have no orders working.*

---

## What we believe

Lululemon has gone from a growth stock to a broken one in about nine months, and the market has finally repriced it: down 56% from the high, down 17% in a day on the second-quarter report. At roughly $100 the shares trade at about 11.5× the company's own guided full-year earnings excluding a one-time tariff refund (about $8.60–8.90 a share), against a business with no debt, roughly $1.39 billion of net cash, and gross margins in the mid-50s.

We believe three things, in order of confidence:

1. **The cheapness is real but it is earned, not accidental.** North American same-store sales are negative and getting worse, not stabilizing. The company just cut its full-year earnings guidance by about 27% versus what analysts were carrying. Nothing in the filings says the decline has bottomed.
2. **The setup is not the usual retail death spiral.** Inventory is down 7% in units. That matters: the thing that normally turns a struggling apparel retailer from "cheap" into "value trap" is a pile of unsold goods that has to be marked down. That pile is not there. Cash generation and the balance sheet can absorb a bad year.
3. **There is a real, dated change of leadership.** Heidi O'Neill — Nike's former President of Consumer, Product and Brand — was named CEO on 2026-04-22 and starts 2026-09-08, one trading day from now. New CEOs inheriting bad numbers typically clear the decks: write-downs, store closures, a rebased guide. That has *not* happened yet, and it is the single cheapest thing she can do.

What we do **not** believe: that we can currently explain the company's own forecast. Management guides third-quarter earnings to $0.93–0.98 against $2.59 a year ago — an operating margin of about 5.6% versus 17.0%. But the full-year guide requires the fourth quarter to earn $3.96–4.16, which is only about 4.4 percentage points of margin below last year's fourth quarter, on the same revenue decline. Something is assumed to get much better, fast. Nobody has said what. Until someone does, we are not buying.

## What the market believes

The market's view split in half on print day and has not re-formed:

- **Before the print**, 31 analysts carried $13.26 of full-year earnings on $11.10 billion of revenue. The company guided $9.48–9.73 on $10.35–10.50 billion. That is a −6% revenue and −27% earnings reset in a single morning.
- **After the print**, there is no consensus to disagree with. The published price target is $111.68 with a range of **$44 to $280** across 34 analysts — a 6.4× spread. Next-year consensus ($10.93) still sits *above* this year's guide, which is arithmetically stale. The same vendor page shows a forward P/E of 14.9, implying about $6.75 of earnings, which contradicts its own consensus.

So the honest statement is: the market believes lululemon is broken and is arguing about how broken, with no shared anchor. That is not the same as a market that has priced this correctly. It is a market that hasn't finished measuring.

One data point on what the company itself believed: it bought back 1.0 million shares for $118.8 million at an average of about $118.80 between 8/3 and 8/28 — after the quarter had closed and days before it told the world the year was 27% worse. That is roughly $180 million of shareholder money spent 18% above today's price by people who already had the numbers. $712.5 million of buyback authorization remains.

## Why we might have edge

- **The inventory fact is under-weighted.** Most people reading a −17% print assume markdowns are coming. Units are down 7%. The mechanical bridge from "slowing demand" to "gross margin collapse" is missing here, and it is in the filing rather than the headline.
- **The competitive story that everyone tells is wrong in our data.** The standard narrative is that Alo Yoga and Vuori are taking lululemon's share. Our consumer-heat detector reads lululemon FLAT at 40.9 and **Alo Yoga DECAYING at 27.5** — the supposed share-taker is fading faster. That reads as the whole category cooling, not a share transfer. (Caveat, stated plainly: Alo is private, so this score has no revenue to check it against; it is directional only.)
- **The activist situation is settled and most commentary hasn't updated.** Chip Wilson (8.7%) signed a cooperation agreement on 2026-05-27: two new directors, a third apparel-brand director due by 2026-10-01, and roughly an 18-month standstill. The "founder is going to dump stock / fight the board" story is no longer live.
- **A dated leadership catalyst is one trading day away** and nobody is positioned around it, because the company said nothing about what she'll do.

Being honest about the size of this edge: it is an edge about *facts*, not about *value*. We can see the situation more clearly than the average holder. We cannot yet say what the shares are worth, because we can't explain the fourth-quarter assumption.

## Why it might be priced in

- **11.5× is not obviously cheap for a business whose earnings are still falling.** On next year's likely $7–8 of earnings, the same price is 12.5–14.3×. That is a normal multiple for a decelerating retailer, not a bargain.
- **34 analysts cover this.** Coverage means it is *measured*, not measured *correctly* — that distinction is desk doctrine and we're applying it — but it does mean there is no informational secret here. Every fact in this deck came out of public filings within the last four months.
- **The balance sheet strength is well known.** Net cash and no debt are in every model.
- **The leadership change was announced in April.** It has had four and a half months to be priced. Anyone arguing "new CEO catalyst" is arguing that the market failed to price a widely reported appointment, which is a weak claim on its own.
- **If we adjust the enterprise value for leases** ($366.6 million current + $1,774.5 million long-term = $2,141.1 million), the capital structure is heavier than the "net cash, no debt" line suggests — about $11.97 billion of lease-adjusted enterprise value versus $9.83 billion unadjusted. (Our own review overstated this as a multiple hit; see the table below.)

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the multiple was never the floor.** We buy at 11.5× because it "can't get cheaper," Americas comps go from −6% to −10%, next year's earnings come in at $6.50 instead of $7.50, and the market pays 10× for a shrinking brand. That is $65, a 35% loss from here. *Tripwire: the Americas comparable-sales line in each quarterly release, plus our app-review cohort score as a between-prints proxy.* Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — the reset is a rebuild, not a haircut.** O'Neill takes the job, looks at the brand, and concludes it needs two to three years of reinvestment: heavier marketing, product overhaul, store closures, a rebased forecast for next year, and the buyback stopped to pay for it. The stock is cheap on earnings that management is deliberately choosing not to make. *Tripwire: an 8-K restructuring or impairment charge; capital-allocation language on the next call; buyback pace against the $712.5 million remaining, visible in the next 10-Q.* Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 3 — the category itself is over, and we have no sensor for that.** Athleisure ends the way logo tees and premium denim ended: not with a competitor winning, but with the wardrobe cycle turning over. In that world lululemon *and* Alo *and* Vuori all decay together, our "category softness, not share loss" finding — which we currently treat as exculpatory — is actually the tell, and every multiple we compute is anchored to a demand level that never comes back. **There is no monitorable tripwire for this.** No filing announces it. No 8-K discloses it. Our consumer-heat score is a *level* (40.9), not a slope; the app-review cohort delta lags the consumer's wardrobe decision by several quarters; and by the time comparable sales confirm it, the re-rating has happened. The unknown-unknown class is: **a taste-regime change in a latent variable we do not instrument — a "category clock" unknown.** We should say plainly that our detector library has no channel for it and does not pretend to.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own flow, our own rules:**

- *Our size is not the risk.* A 0.5% starter against a roughly $5 million deployable book is about $25,000, or ~250 shares, against 37.4 million shares of average daily volume — about 0.0007% of one day. We cannot move this stock and never will. The real distortion runs the other way: we are doing institutional-grade work on a position too small to matter, which creates pressure to size up to justify the effort. That pressure is the trap, not market impact.
- *We predicted flow that does not exist.* Our first pass built its entire entry trigger on Chip Wilson's collared forward, treating $94.17 as a level where forced selling would appear. The hedge is 1,274,318 shares — **3.4% of one day's volume**, spread over a two-year term — and the dealer's net hedge between $100 and $94 is flat-to-falling, not growing. We would have sat waiting for a wave that was never coming. The desk rule "never bid into flow you predict" failed here in the opposite direction: we predicted flow badly and it cost us a band, not a fill.
- *Tax and sleeve interactions.* Two live ones. (a) The Parametric direct-indexing sleeve almost certainly holds lululemon as an S&P 500 constituent and is actively harvesting losses in it. Buying lululemon in the desk account within 30 days either side of a Parametric sale disallows that harvested loss — an invisible tax cost attached to a position too small to earn it back. This must be checked before any order, not after. (b) 2026 is a harvest year with a $1.855 million reserve against realized gains; a new losing position carried into 2027 defers its own loss out of the year we need it.
- *A thesis conflict the adjudicator flagged.* If the book is long On Holding on a share-taker thesis, long lululemon is a bet against our own position: both cannot be right about who owns the category. Notably, our own detector split (Alo decaying faster than lululemon) argues the share-taker story is weaker than assumed — which weakens the On thesis too. This needs reconciling at the book level, not the name level.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a cheap multiple on an earnings base that had not stopped falling — Americas went from −5% to −6% and kept going, and we told ourselves 11× was a floor when it was just a waypoint.

That is the whole argument for staying flat, in one number. A 3.6% expected return over roughly three and a half months, with a 20% chance of losing a third, does not clear a starter — and it is well inside the error bar of a table built on an unexplained margin assumption. The verdict is the arithmetic's output, not a mood.

**Instrument recommendation: NONE.** The reasoning, shown: our table's implied volatility (52.6%) and the estimated chain implied volatility (~52%) are the same number, so there is no volatility edge to harvest in either direction. The apparent ~$0.80–1.30 of value in the calls comes almost entirely from the 15%-weighted $160 branch, which is the least-supported cell in the table — and it is smaller than the error on an implied volatility we could not observe because the market-data rail was denied (open item #7). **These are terminal values, not touch probabilities; a touch estimate would be roughly double and is not what a European-style option pays.** The table is genuinely bimodal — 35% of probability sits in the $65 and $160 tails, forked on a single December print — which is exactly the shape that makes a directional call look attractive and is exactly the shape where paying for a fork you cannot handicap is how money is lost.

**Volatility risk premium:** estimated IV ~52% minus estimated realized ~45–55% ≈ **0 to +7 points**, and both terms are estimates rather than measurements. That is not a premium worth selling into. Separately, this is a taxable account: premium selling is barred outside three named templates, and neither the event-driven cash-secured put nor the paid-trim covered call qualifies here — we hold nothing to write against, and we have no conviction in a floor to sell puts to. **Any premium-selling view goes to the shadow ledger, not to the broker.**

**Tax overlay.** 2026 is a harvest year. If a position were ever taken here, a December-2026 expiry lands any loss inside the harvest year and is acceptable; a January-2027-or-later expiry defers the loss out of the year we need it and is excluded unless the edge dominates numerically — and a +3.6% expected value does not dominate anything. Prior to that, the Parametric wash-sale check (open item #9) is a hard gate: buying lululemon within 30 days of a sleeve harvest in the same name silently voids that harvested loss, which on a $25,000 starter is a certain tax cost incurred to chase an uncertain 3.6%.
