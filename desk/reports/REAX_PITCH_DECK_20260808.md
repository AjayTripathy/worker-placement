# PITCH — REAX — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: FLAT — no position into the Aug-14 merger vote or through deal close; re-entry becomes studyable only post-close, and only if three specific conditions are met. Both benches concur (red 7/10, blue 8/10 conviction on the FLAT stance; idea quality cut from 7 to 5 out of 10).**

## What we believe

Real Brokerage is a genuinely fast-growing market-share gainer — agents up ~26% and transaction sides up ~27% year over year — in a housing market that is shrinking around it. That growth refutes the "the sector is killing this company" story. But the growth is bought with stock: trailing-twelve-month stock compensation is **$76.45M**, which is roughly equal to the company's entire trailing operating cash flow of **$79.5M**. Charged honestly for the shares it hands out, the business currently earns approximately nothing per existing shareholder. It is a breakeven variable-cost machine, not a cheap compounder.

The pending merger with RE/MAX (each RMAX share converts into 5.15 REAX shares, with a 15% cash-election pool at $13.80) is effectively certain to close in the market's eyes, and it brings ~$490M of secured debt at a coupon we have not been able to confirm, plus roughly **148 million new REAX shares (~68% of the current share count)**. Around 65M of those go to insiders who likely can't sell immediately; the remaining ~100M can reach the free float as merger-arbitrage and index holders exit. That is a structural wall of selling in the weeks after close.

So we believe: don't own it now, don't own it through the vote, and only look again after the close and the planned 10-for-1 reverse split, on strict conditions listed below.

## What the market believes

The stock closed at **$2.14 on Aug 7** (up 7.5% the day after earnings), down 60% from its 52-week high of $5.41, market cap ~$469M on 219.2M shares. Six analysts cover it, all bullish — consensus "Strong Buy," average price target $4.95, implying 131% upside. On the merger: RMAX trades at $11.37 against a blended deal value of $11.44 (0.85 × 5.15 × $2.14 stock leg + 0.15 × $13.80 cash leg), meaning the market assigns **≥95% probability the deal closes**. In short: the market believes the deal is done and the stock is deeply undervalued — the drawdown is a visible, analyst-tracked deal-risk discount, not neglect.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: our edge here is mostly in **not** being long, and in having done the arithmetic the bull case skips.

- The stock-compensation math ($76.5M SBC vs. $79.5M operating cash flow — a wash) closes the standard bull escape hatch of "ignore the accounting loss, look at the cash flow." We have not seen this framing in the published coverage.
- We have mapped the post-close share supply (~100M free-float shares of potential flowback) and the insider-locked remainder before the event, so we know what we're waiting for while others may buy into it.
- We identified that the "cheap at 5.5x EBITDA" anchor rests on an unaudited, derived number (pro-forma $157M minus RE/MAX's $94M ≈ $63M standalone), which no one should underwrite.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost everything else is. The company is covered by six bullish analysts, so the discount is a known deal-risk and dilution discount, not an overlooked situation. The merger close is priced to near-certainty, so there is no payoff for holding through the vote — the "deal breaks and the stock pops" branch is dead weight the market has already discarded. And the growth story (agent count, transaction sides) is the headline every bull already cites; what the price does *not* yet reflect, in our view, is the post-close flowback supply and the unknown debt coupon — which is precisely why we wait rather than buy the "cheapness."

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry. Position through the vote and close: zero.** The failed-vote lottery ticket is not worth holding for (≥95% priced to close), and nothing at the vote resolves the two open underwriting questions (debt coupon, flowback). Since the courts say no-entry, the **reopen bands** are:

- **Timing gate:** only after (a) the merger closes and (b) the planned 10-for-1 reverse split — today's $2.14 becomes ~$21.40-equivalent. All post-close levels below are in post-split units.
- **Reopen study trigger:** a flowback-driven decline **below the ~$21.40-equivalent** (i.e., below today's price, split-adjusted) during the post-close arb-exit window.
- **Three hard conditions, all required before any buy order exists:** (1) the valuation is re-underwritten charging the full ~$76M/yr of stock compensation against whatever earnings figure is used — audited numbers, not the derived $63M; (2) the ~$490M term debt's coupon is disclosed and ≤ roughly 9% (red's own stated reversal threshold); (3) the ~100M-share free-float flowback is either demonstrably absorbed or mapped against daily volume so we know how long the supply lasts.
- **Pre-close watch level (not a buy):** a break below **$1.55** (the 52-week low) before close would signal vote stress or forced selling and warrants a fresh look — examination only.
- **Sizing:** none proposed. Any future position must be sized at a fresh court after the conditions above are met; no number is invented here.
- **Catalyst dates:** RMAX special meeting **2026-08-14** (five trading days out; date taken from the benches, primary proxy unreachable — see unverified); deal close shortly after a yes vote; Q3 print **2026-10-29** (yfinance-derived, unconfirmed by company PR).
