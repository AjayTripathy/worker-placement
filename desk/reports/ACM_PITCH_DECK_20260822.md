# PITCH — ACM — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position today (0% of book); both benches land flat with a dated re-open, and the one finding that survives cross-examination is a data problem, not a price problem.

---

## What we believe

AECOM is an engineering and program-management firm — it designs and manages infrastructure projects rather than building most of them itself. The stock is $65.18, about 51.5% below its 52-week high of ~$134–135 and only 8% above its 52-week low of $60.35.

The bull case we brought into court was: the company took a single $336.8M loss on one construction-management contract, guidance excluding that loss is intact at $5.90–$6.10 of adjusted earnings per share, backlog is at a record (+13%, with 1.6x more work booked than burned), and the shares therefore trade near 10x a normalized number after a fall the sector did not share.

**After both benches, here is what we actually believe:**

The valuation is genuinely low. Using the company's own balance sheet, the enterprise is worth roughly $10.3B, which is about 10.7–11.0x this year's guided EBITDA and about 7.9–8.1x EBITDA excluding the charge. That is cheap against peers trading at 18–22x forward earnings.

But we no longer believe the charge is one-time. The same 10-Q that reports the loss also discloses that *disputed* amounts the company has booked as revenue and expects to collect — "significant claims recorded in contract assets" — grew from $400M to $620M in nine months, a 55% increase. The filing adds that clients may file material counter-claims and that the company "cannot provide assurances that it will be successful." A fixed-price contractor whose disputed-recovery balance is compounding while it books a large loss as non-recurring has not finished taking losses; it has staged them.

We also believe our own bull write-up got a fact backwards. It claimed the underlying margin target was raised. The company's own release says the opposite: excluding the charge, adjusted operating margin and EBITDA margin **fell 60 basis points**, and in the Americas segment the ex-charge margin on net service revenue **fell 250 basis points**. Our brief cited that 250bp-lower number as supporting evidence.

**Net: cheap, but on numbers we cannot yet trust. We wait for one dated document.**

## What the market believes

Two markets disagree with each other, and that is the most interesting fact in the file.

The **sell-side** is bullish: a "Strong Buy" consensus from 13 rating analysts and an average price target of $86.92, about 34% above the current price. On the vendor's own forward multiple (10.58x at a $64.79 close), the street's next-twelve-month earnings estimate is roughly $6.12 — *above* the $6.00 our bull case used.

The **tape** is not bullish. The stock fell about 28 points between September 2025 and February 2026 while published guidance was rising, then the company disclosed a large loss and cut its free-cash-flow guide from $400M to $300M. Peers did not fall: Jacobs is 10.9% off its high, Tetra Tech 14.2% off. AECOM is 51.5% off. Whatever the sell-side thinks, price-setting money has been marking down company-specific contract risk ahead of disclosure, and has now been right twice.

So the market's operating belief is: *the reported earnings basis is not the real earnings basis.* That is exactly the belief our surviving bear finding supports.

## Why we might have edge

1. **Nobody has priced the ex-charge cash economics.** At ~8x EBITDA excluding the loss, with record backlog, this is a cheap professional-services business if the loss is genuinely behind it. Neither bench found that number in any published note.
2. **The published "consensus" is nearly meaningless and could mislead the market as easily as it misled our own bench.** The $4.49 fiscal-2026 mean comes from **three** analysts, and its range ($3.95–$6.03) is literally the company's two different guidance bases side by side — the low end is the headline guide's floor, the high end is the ex-charge guide's ceiling. Averaging numbers computed on two different accounting bases produces a figure that anchors nobody correctly. Whoever resolves the basis first sees the real number first.
3. **The re-rating trigger is a specific, dated, public document.** The fiscal-2026 10-K, expected around November 2026, will show both the claims balance and the year's net revisions to contract estimates. That is an unusually clean, binary, calendar-known test of the load-bearing question.
4. **Fiscal-year labeling is creating a fake expensive-vs-cheap argument.** Fiscal 2026 ends 2026-09-30 — about five weeks away and ~90% determined, contaminated by the charge. Anyone quoting "14x fiscal 2026" is quoting a nearly-finished, charge-polluted year. The forward year is where the argument actually lives, and there the market pays about 10.6x the street's own number.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, most of it probably is, and we should say so plainly.

- **The cheapness is not a secret.** Thirteen analysts cover it, the rating is Strong Buy, and the target implies 34% upside. We are not early, contrarian, or differentiated on the valuation view — we agree with a consensus that has been wrong all year.
- **There is no estimate divergence, only a multiple bet.** Our number sits *below* the street's forward estimate. So we are not saying earnings will be higher than expected; we are saying the multiple should be higher than 10.6x. We have no named mechanism that forces the multiple to move.
- **The tape's discount looks earned, not accidental.** The market front-ran two negative disclosures. Assuming the third discount is the mistake requires evidence we don't have.
- **The obvious catalyst may be worth nothing.** A strategic review of the construction-management business could produce a sale. The last time AECOM exited a construction business, it kept 18.7% of the buyer, Shimmick — 7,745,000 shares now worth about $29M against a 52-week range of $1.95–$6.76. (Caveat below: our bench over-read this.)
- **Cash is being spent on buybacks while the balance sheet absorbs the damage.** Shares are still being retired while free cash flow was cut by $400M→$300M, roughly $315M of project cash burn remains, and true net debt is about $2.0B.
- **Revenue is shrinking while backlog grows.** The street models fiscal 2026 revenue of $15.3B, down 5.2% year over year, against "record backlog +13%." Rising backlog with falling revenue is a conversion problem, and neither bench explained it.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry. Position today: FLAT, 0% of book.** The named reason is not the price — at roughly 8x ex-charge EBITDA the price is not the problem. The named reason is that the "one-time" premise is contradicted by a growing disputed-claims balance in the company's own filing. A cheaper price does not fix a data problem, so we do not set a buy level. These are the conditions that would re-open the case.

**Re-open condition A — the primary one, dated.**
Fiscal-2026 annual report (10-K), expected around November 2026. Both of the following, from the filing itself:
- "Significant claims recorded in contract assets" **below $500M** (down from $620M — i.e. the disputed balance converting to cash, not compounding), **and**
- net loss from revisions to contract estimates **approximately zero** for the fourth quarter.

If both hold → **starter position, 0.5% of book**, bought at whatever the market is then, with no price condition attached. This is deliberately price-blind: what we are buying is the resolution of the data question. If only one holds → stay flat, re-court in 90 days.

**Re-open condition B — independent of A.**
An 8-K announcing resolution of the construction-management strategic review, with **cash proceeds and a capped indemnity**. Stock consideration, an earnout, or an uncapped indemnity is worth zero to us and does not re-open anything. Cash-with-cap → re-court immediately, starter 0.5%.

**Re-open condition C — the margin reversal.**
Fourth-quarter segment adjusted EBITDA margin at or above **17.4% on an ex-charge basis**, reversing the 60bp decline. That would show the margin deterioration was mechanical to the charge rather than structural. Reported with the fiscal-2026 print.

## What remains unverified

- **Whether the two disclosed design-build P3 contracts are the only fixed-price contracts of that type.** This is management's assertion, uncorroborated by any filing either bench could reach. Both benches flagged it and both excluded it from their verdicts — correctly, but it is the largest unknown in the file.
- **The identity and location of the loss-making construction-management project.** It is unnamed in the 10-Q. One unnamed job drove $336.8M of a $344.6M nine-month revision. Because it is unnamed, every location-based cross-check we own — permits, site records — is unrunnable. **That non-disclosure is itself a finding**, not an absence of one.
- **The dated peer comparison.** We know where peers trade today. We do not know where they traded on the two dates that matter.
- **What AECOM's own multiple did across its 2021 exit from self-perform construction.** This is the correct precedent for valuing a divestiture catalyst, and no price series was pullable in either session. Our bench substituted the wrong precedent (the buyer's stock) and drew a conclusion from it.
- **Why backlog is up 13% while modelled revenue is down 5.2%.** The gap is confirmed; the mechanism is not. It could be slower conversion, mix, or contract-type shift — each has a different meaning for margins.
- **How much of the $1,012.9M cash is genuinely available.** We now know $250.9M sits in joint ventures. We do not know whether the remaining ~$762M carries any further restriction.
- **All peer and consensus prices**, which are vendor closes, not broker-verified.
- **Bench conviction:** the bearish bench put its kill case at 8/10; the reviewing bench put "stay flat with a dated trigger" at 7/10. Both named the same residual doubt: roughly 8x EBITDA excluding the charge, with record backlog, is genuinely cheap, and the one check that would settle the sector question has not been run.
