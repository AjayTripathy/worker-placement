**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Stay out (no position, no resting orders, nothing into the August 14 report). Re-open only if helium production is physically confirmed by a third party AND the next 10-Q shows at least $150M of unrestricted cash at the parent company.**

## What we believe
ASP Isotopes at $4.43 is not the bargain the original write-up claimed. The write-up's core idea was that ASPI is the one company in its beaten-down peer group with signed customer contracts and first production less than 60 days away, unfairly marked down alongside companies that have nothing. Both of our internal review teams — one assigned to attack the idea, one assigned to defend it — concluded that premise is wrong on the facts:

- **The helium plant is not producing helium today.** The company's own August 4 shareholder letter says helium production is expected "to commence… prior to September 30, 2026" — future tense, with pipeline tie-in work still incomplete ([primary source: the letter](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/04/3338261/0/en/ASP-Isotopes-Issues-Letter-to-Shareholders.html)). This same plant has a five-year history of missing this exact milestone: targeted 2021, first produced liquid helium January 2023 (a cold-box leak shut it down), restarted April 2024 (down again), and still nothing commercial in August 2026. It has been commissioned and broken twice — which is arguably worse evidence than never having run.
- **The customer contracts cover only about 15% of the plant's claimed capacity.** The letter itself says so. Roughly 85% of the touted ~$27M/year helium revenue is aspiration, not contract.
- **More shares are likely coming.** About $110.7M of 8% convertible notes remain outstanding after a first exchange that printed at roughly $4.71/share. The same treatment on the residual would add ~23.5M shares — about 16% more dilution — and the company filed a shelf registration the day after the first exchange.

What survives on the bull side: the company raised ~$290M, owns ~89% of the helium subsidiary, completed Phase-1 drilling four months ahead of schedule, and the historical failures happened under a prior, capital-starved owner. That caps how bearish we should be, but it doesn't make the stock ownable today.

## What the market believes
The stock is down 69% from its 52-week high of $14.49 but 26% above its 52-week low of $3.51. The market is treating ASPI like the rest of its pre-revenue cohort: a story stock whose production dates have slipped repeatedly, with a large convertible-note overhang and an active shelf registration. Our attacking team's conclusion, which the defending team largely accepted, is that the market has this roughly right — the price reflects the realistic base rate for a plant that has missed this milestone for five years, not an overreaction. Note this is a covered name (Canaccord and Citi conferences, published estimates), so "the market just hasn't looked" is not available as an argument.

## Why we might have edge
Honestly: not much, and not yet. The one genuine asymmetry is that the decisive questions are *checkable* before the market fully re-prices them:

1. **Physical confirmation of helium production** by September 30 — verifiable through satellite/thermal observation of the plant (once we have surveyed coordinates) or customs bills of lading once product ships to the Asian buyer. Confirmation ahead of a press release would be real, tradeable information.
2. **Where the cash actually sits.** The claimed ~$121M net cash has never been verified as parent-level and unrestricted; some sits in South African rand under exchange control, and $20M is committed to the subsidiary spin-off. The next 10-Q can settle this.
3. **The regime change is real.** New owner, ~$290M raised, drilling finished early. If the plant's past failures were a funding problem rather than an engineering problem, the base rate everyone (including our own attacking team) is using overstates the risk.

## Why it might be priced in
The market's skepticism is grounded in the company's own disclosures: the future-tense production language, the 15% contract coverage, the convertible overhang and shelf registration are all public. A stock 26% off its low already embeds some probability of eventual success. And "reaffirmed guidance" from a management chain that has missed the same date repeatedly since 2021 is not new information — the August 14 report is likely to be noise (presentation changes, a stale share count, a probable accounting reclassification of the helium unit as held-for-sale) rather than resolution. The real resolvers come later: the September 8 London capital-markets day and the September 30 production deadline.
