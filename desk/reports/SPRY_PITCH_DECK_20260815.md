# PITCH — SPRY — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: DO NOT BUY at $5.65. Move to WATCH with specific reopen triggers; the designated entry event is the expected equity raise (likely first half of 2027), or a Q3 report that proves the margin question in the company's favor.**

## What we believe

ARS Pharmaceuticals sells neffy, the first needle-free (nasal spray) epinephrine for severe allergic reactions. The demand story is real: product sales roughly doubled year over year, grew about 50% quarter over quarter to $26.2M in Q2 2026, and about 16,000 doctors are now prescribing it. This is not a broken drug.

The problem is the money. The company spent $101M of cash in the first half of 2026 and has $143.8M left, against a $96.7M term loan and guided second-half operating spend of $100–110M. Even crediting gross profit against that burn, cash lands around $81–94M at year-end 2026 and roughly $25–55M by mid-2027 — thin enough that a stock sale before profitability is probable (though, importantly, not mathematically forced — our first bench overstated this). The company already has an active "shelf" registration on file with the SEC (filed January 2025), meaning it can sell new shares quickly whenever it chooses, and its Q2 press release said nothing about how it plans to fund itself.

Two more things we believe: nobody outside the company actually knows neffy's true gross margin (the honest answer is "somewhere between 51% and roughly 78%" — see the bench-error table below), and no new insurance coverage can arrive before the January 2027 formulary window, so the next two quarters of growth must come entirely from existing coverage while the sales organization is being cut back. A securities class action is also pending (class period March 9 – June 24, 2026, over statements about CVS Caremark coverage).

Net: real product, unresolved unit economics, a probable share sale ahead, and no coverage catalyst until January. The right trade is to wait for the raise to price — that event, not today's tape, is where we want to be a buyer.

## What the market believes

The stock closed at $5.65 on 8/14, down 62.6% from its 52-week high of $15.09 and only 15% above its 52-week low of $4.91. It fell 23.9% on June 24 when the company disclosed zero new formulary wins and cut costs, and fell another 7.4% the day after a Q2 report that showed 50% sequential product growth. A stock that falls on a strong sales print is telling you the market is pricing the financing, not the drug: consensus already believes dilution is coming and coverage has stalled. The market is not wrong about either.

## Why we might have edge

- **We corrected the record in both directions, so our downside picture is more accurate than the public bear case.** The widely-repeated idea that SPRY has negative net cash is wrong: the $74.9M "ALK financing liability" on the balance sheet is an accounting artifact of a European licensing deal (ARS already *received* $155M; the liability has no maturity date and no repayment requirement — cash only leaves if ARS voluntarily buys the rights back). True net cash is about **+$47M**, not −$28M. If the stock craters on "negative net cash" headlines, we know that's a mispricing.
- **We know the margin question is open, not answered — and we know exactly when it resolves.** The "62% gross margin" floating around doesn't appear in any company document; equally, the bearish "51%" counter mixes mismatched revenue and cost lines. The Q3 report (~Nov 10) should resolve it, because the free pre-approval inventory that currently flatters costs is nearly used up ($6.7M → $3.1M remaining).
- **We know the calendar**: class-action lead-plaintiff deadline Oct 5, 2026; Q3 print ~Nov 10, 2026; formulary window January 2027; and a live shelf that makes any raise fast. We can position for the entry event rather than guess at it.
- The demand signal itself (+105% y/y, 16,000 prescribers) means the equity is worth owning at some price — this is a timing kill, not a business kill.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands we say it: "buy after the biotech raise prices" is one of the most consensus playbooks in the market, and a 63% drawdown means much of the bad news is already in the price. If Q3 prints a high product margin, or the company monetizes something non-dilutively (an 8-K deal covering the 2027 gap), or January brings formulary wins, the stock re-rates from $5–6 without ever offering us the raise-price entry. Our own first bench put its conviction at 8/10 *against* entry and the review bench at 7/10 — meaning both benches accept a real chance the cautious path costs us the upside. We accept that cost knowingly: with an undisclosed margin, unread loan covenants, and a live shelf, the asymmetry still favors waiting.
