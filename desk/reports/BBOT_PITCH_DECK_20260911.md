## What we believe

BridgeBio Oncology Therapeutics is a pre-revenue KRAS/PI3Kα drug developer whose stock has fallen about 64% from its 52-week high of $14.87 to roughly $5.40, sitting essentially on its 52-week low. Our original thesis was "damage-absent": the company was not financially broken — it reaffirmed cash and runway inside the very press release that crashed the stock, filed no impairment item, and its next big data readout lands *inside* funded cash. That narrow claim survived both benches.

But surviving the balance-sheet test is not the same as being a good trade, and we now believe it is not one at $5.40. Three things changed our mind:

1. **The company moved its own goalposts by nine months.** Earlier guidance promised combination and expansion data in H2 2026. The September 8, 2026 release now points to mid-2027. That is a guidance breach, not a re-ordering of slides.
2. **It walked away from its biggest market.** The lead drug's best public number — a 63% response rate (26 of 41 patients) in patients who had never been treated with a KRAS G12C inhibitor — sits in the *large* US funnel (~21,000 patients). The company de-prioritized that setting and pivoted to second-line-and-later patients who already failed a G12C drug, where the entire dataset is 17 patients (the headline "75%" is a dose subgroup inside those 17). Choosing to compete where the data is thinnest, against three approved or late-stage rivals, reads as a verdict on the asset rather than a focusing decision.
3. **The prize is roughly fair, not free.** On the benches' shared math (risk-adjusted value = probability of success × peak sales × 3.0 × a 12%/6-year discount factor of 0.507, i.e. $1.52 of value per $1 of peak sales), today's enterprise value of about $134M is what you'd pay for a 5–8% success rate on $1.0–1.5B of peak sales. A 50% move up requires roughly $350M of pipeline value — about $1.5B peak at 15% odds, or ~$2.9B at the 8% base rate. Those are not silly numbers, but they are not free-option numbers either.

So: **not broken, not cheap enough, and the clock just got nine months longer.** We want this name, at a lower price, with two specific facts in hand.

## What the market believes

The market is split into two camps that never speak to each other.

The sell side still believes the story. After the September 8 release, JPMorgan kept an Overweight with a $25 target (trimmed from $27) and Canaccord kept a Buy at $20 (from $23) — roughly four times the tape, and cuts of only 8–13% against a ~24% one-day drop. There is no revenue consensus to anchor against, because there is no revenue.

The tape believes the opposite, and has believed it for nine months. Using the company's own filing covers as price stamps, the stock was $9.20 on August 11, 2026 and $8.80 on August 31, 2026 against that $14.87 high. About 41% of the decline was already gone *before a single new data point printed*. A director option struck around $7.73 in early July, so the grind actually bottomed mid-summer and then rose about 19% into mid-August before the September event took it to the low.

Net: the market is not arguing about the science on a given day. It is steadily marking down duration, dilution risk, and the credibility of guidance — and the sell side has not followed it down.

## Why we might have edge

- **We know precisely what the crash release did and did not say.** The 8-K is Item 8.01 only, attaching the press release and a revised corporate deck — no impairment, no going-concern, no restatement ([SEC 8-K, 2026-09-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1869105/000119312526385225/d97546d8k.htm)). Anyone trading "the company blew up" is trading a headline the filing does not support.
- **We correctly de-bunked the scariest structural story — our own bench's story.** The idea that a 63.05M-share resale shelf (≈79% of shares out) being re-registered on September 1 caused the collapse does not survive the lock-up documents: those shares were contractually frozen until roughly August 11, 2026 under a lock-up written into the bylaws ([S-1, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1869105/000119312525192760/d67813ds1.htm)). Registration is not release. And the actual unlock was *absorbed* — the stock rose into it.
- **We resolved the largest holder correctly.** The 4,528,186 shares that moved from the sponsor vehicle were distributed for no consideration to three funds inside the same complex, all of which signed a joinder to the same lock-up; the group total is unchanged at 17,878,594 shares / 22.32% ([13D/A primary doc](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001869105/000121390026075546/primary_doc.xml)). Zero shares became sellable. Most desks will read "sponsor distribution" as a sell signal. It isn't.
- **We have a clean, cheap, dated way to be wrong or right:** the Q3 10-Q tells us whether the $200M at-the-market program was actually used. That single fact separates "quiet company husbanding cash" from "issuer selling stock at its low."

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: most of it probably is.

- Our best insight — "the balance sheet is fine" — is *already the consensus long*. JPMorgan and Canaccord re-underwrote after the event and still carry $20–25 targets. We are not the variant view; we're the crowded one, four times below the crowd's price.
- The narrowing that we call the real problem is in the **title of the press release**. It is not a discovery; it is a headline, and both target cuts post-date it.
- The at-the-market program's existence is public (S-3 effective in the same week), so the dilution overhang is in every screen.
- The only genuinely non-public facts left are (a) whether the ATM has been tapped, (b) total liabilities behind the cash, and (c) the date and patient count of the next update. Two of those three resolve on a scheduled filing that anyone can read.

The honest framing: the mispricing, if any, is not informational. It is a *patience* premium — being willing to wait for a mid-2027 readout in a name that burns ~$45M a quarter and has a live ATM. That premium is real but small, and it is why we're proposing bands rather than a buy.

## What could go wrong  (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

**Failure narrative 1 — "The ATM eats the rally." (Monitorable.)**
Cash was $344.1M at June 30, 2026, burning ~$44.8M per quarter → about $299M at September 30 and about $165M ($2.06/share) by a mid-2027 readout. The company has a $200M at-the-market facility live at the 52-week low. The reflexive trap: every bounce we participate in is precisely the price level at which the issuer is most likely to sell stock. We bid, they print. In this branch we're not wrong about the science — we're wrong about who is on the other side of our own strength. *Tripwire: Q3/Q4 10-Q share count and any 424B5; price gate below $4.10.*

**Failure narrative 2 — "Guidance slips again." (Monitorable, weakly.)**
H2-2026 already became mid-2027. A company that moved a dataset nine months once can move it again, and the second slip costs far more because the cash floor has dropped to roughly $2.06–2.30/share by then. *Tripwire: any 8-K/PR that re-words the mid-2027 timing, or a Q4 "update" that arrives without a dated combination readout attached.*

**Failure narrative 3 — "The drug is quietly worse than the slides." (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)**
The de-prioritization of the treatment-naïve setting may not be strategy; it may be that internal, undisclosed data — dose-limiting toxicity, early discontinuations, short duration of response, a competitor's head-to-head we haven't seen — made that fight unwinnable. Nothing in EDGAR, no price gate, and no 13D/A will show this. It surfaces only at the readout, by which time the stock has already moved. **Unknown-unknown class: silent-science risk** — non-disclosable in-trial signals (tolerability, durability, enrollment quality) that are legally invisible between data cuts, compounded by **competitor-set blindness** (neither bench could pull the clinical trial registry, so we do not actually know what the rival G12C salvage programs are enrolling). We cannot arm a sensor for this. We can only size for it.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size vs. liquidity:* we do not have average daily volume in the pack — that is a drain item, not an assumption. At a proposed ≤0.25% starter on a ~$430M market cap, our clip is small in dollars but this is a de-SPAC with a 79%-registered float and thin natural sponsorship; in a stressed tape we would likely be a meaningful share of the day's volume and we should assume we cannot exit in one session.
- *Predicted flow:* the dominant predicted flow is the issuer itself. An ATM is a price-elastic seller — it converts our buying into supply. Secondarily, the now-unlocked legacy holders are *able* to sell (lock-up expired ~August 11, 2026) even though they have not yet done so in size.
- *Doctrine interactions:* this deck leans on **DIVERGENCE-NOT-COVERAGE (2026-08-17)** to treat the 4x street/tape gap as an anchor rather than a kill — but that doctrine cuts against us here, because once the supply mechanism was refuted the gap must be *fundamental*, and the tape is the better underwriter. It also leans on **registration-is-not-release** (our own new mechanism) to dismiss the supply kill — if that doctrine is ever wrong in a specific case (e.g., a waiver or early release we never saw), our entire rebuttal of the supply story collapses and Red's original kill is restored at full strength. And the **anchor-and-adjust** discipline in the prize table means our scenario prices are anchored to visible tape levels ($2.06 cash floor, $8.80 pre-event, $14.87 high), which systematically under-imagines a true blue-sky partnering outcome.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we bought a nine-month wait in a pre-revenue company with a live at-the-market program at its 52-week low — and the issuer, not the science, supplied the shares that capped every rally while the real readout kept moving right.

**EV = $6.05 vs. $5.40 spot = +12.0%.**

That is the whole case in one number: a positive but *thin* expected return, against a standard deviation of **$4.09 (75.8% of spot)**. Twelve percent of edge does not pay for that much dispersion. This is exactly why the verdict is FLAT-with-gates rather than a buy. The bands above exist to buy the same distribution at $3.60–4.10, where the same table returns roughly +50% to +68%.

**Bimodality — and it's fork-dated.** The distribution has two distinct fork dates (an undated Q4-2026 monotherapy update and an undated mid-2027 combination readout) and two separated masses: 50% of the probability sits at $2.50–4.00, while 25% sits at $9.00+. There is very little mass at the middle. A single-volatility model will systematically **overprice** near-the-money exposure and **underprice** the right tail. Our table's lognormal-equivalent volatility is **61.4%**, materially below its arithmetic dispersion of 75.8% — that spread *is* the bimodality.

**The problem: there is no options chain in the evidence pack, and we will not substitute an underlying-level aggregate volatility feed for chain-level quotes.** That is the TSSI lesson — the aggregate feed printed 71 while the chain printed 101, and trading off the wrong one is how you buy "cheap" volatility that isn't. We therefore cannot compute IV-vs-RV, an IV percentile, or the volatility risk premium (IV − RV) today. Both inputs are drain items (#6 and #7 above), and our *estimate* — chain IV 85–120% against realized volatility of 80–110% — implies a **volatility risk premium of roughly +5 to +10 points, i.e. modestly rich**, which is typical for a post-catalyst small-cap biotech.

Conclusion: **no options expression is authorized in a harvest year at current EV; if the value band fills, take it in common stock.** (We currently hold no position, so there is nothing to harvest against — this constraint binds only new expressions.)
