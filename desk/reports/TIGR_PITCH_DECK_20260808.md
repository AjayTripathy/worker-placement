# PITCH — TIGR — PROPOSED VERDICT: No purchase today — stay flat into the ~Aug 27 earnings report, then buy a starter position only if the report clears three specific checks. **(PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)**

## What we believe

UP Fintech ("Tiger Brokers," ticker TIGR) is an online brokerage whose stock has fallen 65% from its 52-week high of $13.55 to $4.74, after Chinese regulators cracked down on brokers serving mainland-China customers. The bull case the court examined: the punishment priced into the stock is far larger than the actual damage. Specifically:

- Stripping out the one-time regulatory fine, the company earned roughly $173M over the last twelve months, which at today's price is about **4.9x earnings** — a number the defense confirmed arithmetically ($173M ÷ 178M shares, at $4.74).
- Management says mainland-China customers are only ~10% of customer accounts and 20–25% of revenue, and that over 75% of new accounts come from Singapore and Hong Kong, where business grew ~27% and can plausibly refill the lost mainland revenue within 4–6 quarters.
- Its closest peer, FUTU, took the same regulatory hit but trades at roughly **twice TIGR's multiple** on a consistent, fine-inclusive basis (7.67x vs 12.05x) — and FUTU's stock actually *rose* 15.9% when it booked its penalty, suggesting the market treats quantified damage as relief. TIGR has received no such relief.

The court's net position: the cheapness is real but dented, the key risk items are data gaps rather than proven kills, and the correct response to data gaps is to **gate and size, not reject**.

## What the market believes

The market is pricing TIGR as if the crackdown does lasting damage well beyond the disclosed mainland exposure. At $4.74 the stock sits at roughly 1.0x book value and, on the prosecution's math, is not cheap at all: if the mainland business (which the prosecution argues drove 45–60% of operating income at bull-market incremental margins) runs off, the "real" earnings multiple is ~11–15x — ordinary, not bargain-priced. The market may also be discounting three specific worries the court could not resolve: the mainland exposure figure is self-reported by management; ~$195M of cash left the balance sheet in Q1 for reasons no one has publicly explained; and the "cushion" cash largely sits inside regulated broker subsidiaries where it cannot simply be paid out.

## Why we might have edge

- **The relative-value gap survived a genuine attack.** The prosecution tried to kill the TIGR-vs-FUTU comparison and got its own numbers wrong (details in the table below). When the defense corrected both sides to the same basis, TIGR still trades at about half FUTU's multiple whether you include the fines or exclude them. A 25-point profit-margin gap explains part of that discount — but not obviously all of it.
- **The market rewarded FUTU for certainty and hasn't extended that to TIGR** ([FUTU rose 15.9% after booking its penalty](https://www.sahmcapital.com/news/content/why-futu-holdings-futu-is-up-159-after-booking-a-major-regulatory-penalty-and-profit-drop-2026-05-31)), and the litigation overhang turns out to be industry-wide, not TIGR-specific ([class actions now name FUTU as well](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/13/3326301/3080/en/lost-money-on-futu-holdings-limited-futu-join-class-action-suit-seeking-recovery-contact-levi-korsinsky.html)).
- **The buyback is real and accretive.** A US$50M repurchase was approved in June and the share count fell 5.3% quarter-over-quarter — buying back stock at ~1.0x book adds value per share.
- **Concession (stated plainly):** this edge rests on the ex-mainland growth continuing and on management's exposure figures being roughly honest. Neither is independently verified yet.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The trailing earnings are a peak.** FY2025 profit grew +181% on +63% revenue in a bull tape for brokerages, *including* the mainland business. Cheap multiples on peak earnings are a classic trap, and both benches agreed this concern survives.
- **The decisive number is self-reported.** The 20–25% mainland-revenue figure comes solely from management, the party with maximum incentive to minimize it, and no external tool can check customer nationality. (The defense noted this cuts both ways — FUTU's figures are equally self-reported — but it caps how much confidence any long deserves.)
- **The cash cushion is triple-impaired**: the Q1 decline of ~$195M is unexplained (debt repayment doesn't account for it — total debt is only $66M), roughly $110M of known post-quarter obligations (the fine plus the buyback) draw against it, and much of the remainder is regulatory capital held at broker subsidiaries, not free cash.
- **The upcoming earnings report may not settle anything.** Forced selling by mainland customers *generates* commissions near-term, so a resilient quarter could be a false all-clear — and, symmetrically, that churn could inflate the mainland revenue share and make a decent quarter look bad. True run-off speed may not be observable until 2027.
