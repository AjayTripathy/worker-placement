## What we believe

Builders FirstSource sells and manufactures building products to homebuilders. Its profitability moves with US single-family housing starts. The stock is at $70.21, down 53.5% from its 52-week high of $151.03 and 7.8% above its 52-week low of $65.10. Enterprise value — market value of the shares plus debt, minus cash — is $12.12B (107,596,457 shares; $4,632.4M total debt; $65.7M cash; net debt $4,566.7M).

The belief is narrow and mechanical:

1. **Gross margin is tracking housing volume on a straight line, not eroding independently.** Three consecutive years fall almost exactly on it: 1,011,000 single-family starts → 32.8% gross margin (2024); 943,000 → 30.4% (2025); ~875,000 → ~28.0% (2026 guidance). That is about 2.4 points of margin per 70,000 starts, three years running. If margin were being competed away, the line would break. It hasn't.
2. **Therefore the margin loss is under-absorbed fixed cost, which reverses when volume does** — not price given away in a share war, which would not.
3. **At a normal 1.0–1.1M starts, mid-cycle earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) is roughly $1.8–2.1B.** That is our own number, not management's. Management says $2.1–2.4B; we think management is about 15% too rich. At $12.12B enterprise value, we are paying 5.8–6.7x that.
4. **What the market pays today implies about $1.5B** ($12.12B ÷ 8x). The gap between $1.5B and $1.8–2.1B is the entire idea. Nothing else.

At 8x a $1.95B mid-cycle number, the shares are worth about $102 — roughly +45%. At the challenge team's $1.5B, they are worth about $69 — i.e. today's price. At $1.3B, about $54, or −23%.

## What the market believes

That the trough is real and the recovery is uncertain, and it is paying a fair price for a mid-cycle number around $1.5B.

- Analysts (25 covering) cut 2026 earnings per share from $4.37 to $3.71; Q3 2026 consensus is $1.08. **We do not disagree with the street about 2026.** Our own guide arithmetic lands at $0.95–1.05 for Q3. There is no dispute about the near term.
- The market is not pricing a permanent trough. It is pricing roughly 29–30% gross margin recovery already. The original brief's framing — "5.0–5.7x versus a 10–12x peer group" — overstated the gap by comparing today's price to *management's own forecast* rather than to any independent anchor. That framing is struck.
- Free cash flow is falling from $874M (2025) to $0.4–0.5B guided, and net leverage has gone from 2.7x to 3.6x. The share buyback, which shrank the count from 138M (2023) to 108M today, is switched off. The market is right that this engine is idle for a while.

## Why we might have edge

- **We pulled the year that decides it, and one of our own teams hadn't.** FY2025 gross margin was 30.4% ($4,615,777k gross profit ÷ $15,190,638k revenue) — *inside* management's 30–33% band — at 943,000 starts, which is **below** the 1.0–1.1M volume management calls normal. The challenge team's central claim was that the 30–33% band only ever existed in 2022–2024, the distorted post-COVID lumber years. It didn't check 2025. 2025 clears the floor, one year ago, at sub-normal volume, outside the distorted regime.
- **We caught a base-effect error that would otherwise have killed the idea.** See the next two sections — it was our own error.
- **We removed a fake risk.** The brief carried a debt-covenant tripwire as an open risk. The 10-Q says the springing test triggers only if borrowing availability falls below $180.1M; actual availability is $1,559.8M — 8.7x the trigger — and the company states it is "not in violation of any covenants." That risk is closed, not deferred.
- **The margin-versus-volume curve is a reusable test**, not a BLDR story: before accepting a "price war" diagnosis for any distributor, fit margin against unit volume across three or more years. On the line means volume and cost absorption, which reverses. Off the line means price, which doesn't.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated straight, because several of these are strong:

- **The discount is narrow and the downside is real.** Buying at $70.21 puts us about 2% above the challenge team's fair value of $69, and about 30% above its low case of $54. This is not a wide-margin-of-safety purchase. It is a skewed bet with a live losing side.
- **The $2.1–2.4B anchor cannot be found in a vetted document.** Both teams searched. It appears in the Q2 earnings call transcript (management, Beckmann) and nowhere in the Q2 press release, which contains no mid-cycle figure at all. We are relying on a spoken number that management has not put in writing with a bridge.
- **Neither team obtained the margin bridge.** Nobody produced the management discussion in the filings that splits the 470bp margin decline from 2024 into commodity, mix, and *price*. Our volume-curve argument is a good statistical fit, not a disclosure-level proof. The challenge team's price-war mechanism is unsupported, not disproven.
- **The pre-2021 history is not clean.** The BMC merger changed the product mix materially (46.2% of sales are now manufactured value-add), so comparing today to 2019 is not apples to apples in either direction.
- **Management's recent capital allocation was poor and the same people author the forecast.** $303.5M of stock was repurchased year-to-date at an average $81.26 — above today's $70.21 — and then halted to $3.5M in Q2 while leverage rose from 2.3x to 3.6x. The rebuttal team's reading (textbook deleveraging at 3.6x) is reasonable; it does not erase the $81.26.
- **Two senior departures in three months.** The 8-K filed 2026-08-07 separates Gayatri Narayan, President of Technology and Digital Solutions, effective 2026-08-14, mid-rollout of an ERP program burning $41.0M per quarter ($68.5M year-to-date). The filing states it was not the result of any disagreement over operations, policies or practices. A second Item 5.02 was filed 2026-06-05. ([primary source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1316835/000119312526340381/0001193125-26-340381-index.htm))
- **The mid-cycle earnings-per-share drag nobody modelled.** With buybacks off for multiple years, the share count stops falling. Our $102 assumes the multiple does the work; it does not assume any further count shrinkage, but neither team modelled what a multi-year buyback pause does to per-share compounding.

### What could go wrong (adjudication pre-mortem, quoted verbatim)

A genuine share war — a levered competitor buying share, or builder-direct procurement bypassing distribution — produces the *same* three points on the way down and only reveals itself when starts recover and margin doesn't follow the curve back up. No feed watches competitor pricing; the truth arrives with the FY27 10-K, roughly a year after we would have added.
> 2. **Starts leg-down before the proof.** Affordability pushes SF starts toward ~800k; the curve itself implies ~26.5% GM, sub-$1.0B EBITDA, leverage >4.6x — the equity de-rates on credit fear before the margin mechanism can be tested. Partially tripwired (monthly Census starts) but the gap is faster than the sensor.
> 3. **Reflexive/structural leg.** The add-gate leans on management restating its anchor at an Investor Day that is itself unscheduled; if it slips or restates lower, the venue-divergence kill fires *after* entry. The ERP program burns $41M/qtr with its executive gone mid-rollout — an operational-integrity mode with no external sensor. And our only true edge is one audited data point (FY2025 on the curve) that every one of 25 covering analysts can read.
