# PITCH — TME — PROPOSED VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION:** No position today. Tencent Music is a confirmed decelerating subscription business trading at roughly fair value (~6.0–6.5× enterprise value to earnings with ~30% of the market cap in net cash), not at the distressed price our original brief claimed — reopen only on the price and evidence gates below.

## What we believe
Tencent Music's core engine — paid music subscriptions — is slowing down, and the slowdown is worse than the headline numbers show. The company reported membership revenue up 8.1% in the second quarter of 2026, but it closed the Ximalaya (podcast/audiobook) acquisition mid-quarter and blended RMB 407 million of acquired revenue into its lines without saying which line it landed in. Using Ximalaya's own pre-deal Hong Kong prospectus — which shows subscriptions were 51.7% of its revenue — roughly RMB 210 million of that lands in membership, which means underlying membership growth was about **+3.4%** (plausible range +2.6% to +4.3%). The trend is now 17% → 13.2% → 8% → ~3.4%, falling every quarter with no sign of a floor. The company has also stopped disclosing paying-user counts and per-user revenue, so the one metric that would settle the question is only reported annually.

We also believe the balance sheet is genuinely strong but weaker than our own brief claimed: about **$4.08 billion of net cash (~30% of the $13.7 billion market cap)** after counting all borrowings and notes payable, with the cash held onshore in China and Tencent holding voting control — so minority holders cannot force it out.

## What the market believes
The stock is $8.66, down 67.6% from its 52-week high of $26.70 and sitting 9.1% above its 52-week low. The market has already repriced this from a growth story to a fading one. At the current price, the business trades at roughly 6.0–6.5× enterprise value to annualized after-tax profit (about $1.58 billion a year on the latest quarter). That is a reasonable price for a profitable company whose growth is sliding toward zero — the market is treating it as a fair fade, and after the court's corrections, we agree. The $400 million buyback (average price paid ~$9.20, above today's price) signals management sees value, but at ~2.9% of the market cap per year it is support, not a floor.

## Why we might have edge
- **The organic-growth split is knowable and we did the work.** The company didn't disclose how much acquired revenue sits in membership; we resolved it from the target's own prospectus. Anyone reading only the press release sees +8.1%; the real number is ~+3.4%. That's a genuine informational step ahead of the headline — it just happens to support *avoiding* the stock, not buying it.
- **We know exactly what would change the picture and when.** The Hong Kong interim report (due by September 30, 2026) must include a business-combination note that can reveal the actual Ximalaya allocation and any purchase-accounting haircut to deferred revenue. If Ximalaya contributed less than RMB 100 million to membership, organic growth was actually ≥+5.6% and the deceleration story breaks — a specific, dated, checkable trigger.
- **A reusable pattern came out of this:** when a mid-quarter acquisition blends into a growth line whose unit metrics were already withdrawn, the target's own pre-deal filings can pin down the organic number. Both benches endorsed adding this to our detector library.

## Why it might be priced in
Because it largely is — that's the court's central ruling. Our original brief argued the market was pricing "terminal decline" at ~5× earnings with 41% of the market cap in net cash. Both of those cushion numbers were wrong: debt was understated (we said ~RMB 5.8 billion; the actual total including notes payable is RMB 16.5 billion), so net cash is ~30% not 41%, and the multiple is ~6.0–6.5× not ~5×. A 67.6% drawdown on a company whose organic growth just printed ~+3.4% and is still falling is not obviously an overreaction; it may simply be accurate. There is no near-term catalyst that forces a repricing in our favor, the adjudicating metric (paying users) only prints annually, and the stock sits in the new-low band where our own doctrine requires review, never a reflex buy.
