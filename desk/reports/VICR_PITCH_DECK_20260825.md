# PITCH — VICR (Vicor Corp) — PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION

**Verdict:** No position at $194.62 — the operating business is genuinely inflecting, but the price already embeds more than we can defend; open a limit-only ladder at $120 / $100 and let the October print decide.

---

## What we believe

Vicor makes power-conversion modules that sit next to AI accelerators. Underneath a reported quarter that looks spectacular, there is a real, improving business — and a large amount of accounting noise on top of it.

Stripping the noise out of the June 2026 quarter ([10-Q, filed 2026-07-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/751978/000119312526322462/R4.htm)):

- Total revenue was $143.4M. Of that, **$30.4M was royalties** and $2.0M was engineering fees. The actual product business did **$110.9M**, up **32.5%** from $85.7M a year ago ([revenue detail](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/751978/000119312526322462/R42.htm)).
- Gross margin **on product** was 46.7%, up from 42.9% a year ago — nearly 4 points of improvement.
- Excluding royalties entirely, the operating business made **+$4.5M** this quarter versus **−$9.9M** a year ago. It crossed from losing money to making money.
- Reported EPS was $1.04. But net income ($49.8M) was *larger* than pre-tax income ($38.9M), because of a **$10.9M tax benefit** driven by employees exercising options while the stock was near $250. Take the royalties out and tax the rest at a normal 21%, and the quarter is about **$0.14 a share**.
- Capital spending is accelerating: $23.6M in the first half versus $10.8M a year earlier, and the plant is measurably running hotter year over year — consistent with rising utilization.

So: a real franchise, genuinely inflecting, with a temporarily flattering income statement. The problem is entirely the price.

## What the market believes

Wall Street models **$911.9M of revenue in 2027** (four estimates) against $581.3M this year, with an average price target of $282.50 ([consensus](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/vicr/forecast/)).

Back the royalties out of that 2027 number — roughly $70–90M — and the street is implicitly forecasting **$825–840M of product revenue**, versus something like $490M this year. That is product revenue nearly doubling. The company's disclosed one-year backlog is $379.7M, which covers **43–45%** of it.

At $194.62 the equity is worth about $9.0B; net of $453.6M of cash and essentially no debt, the enterprise is **$8.5B**, or roughly **16–19× product sales** (the range depends on which product base you use — see "unverified").

The stock is down 49% from its 52-week high of $382.65 but still **4.1× its 52-week low of $47.56**. Both facts are true at once, and together they describe a crowded position unwinding, not a bargain appearing.

## Why we might have edge

Three things, and we should be precise about which are real.

1. **The royalty line is not what the earnings call said it was.** On the call, management quantified "$15M" from a new licensing agreement. The filed document shows the royalty *category* was $30.4M — meaning there is a **second, legacy ~$15M/quarter royalty stream** that the call never mentioned. Anyone modeling from the transcript is working with half the number. That is a real gap between what was said in one venue and what was filed in another, and it is checkable in one document.

2. **The tax benefit is stock-price-dependent and nobody is adjusting for it.** The $10.9M benefit exists because options were exercised around $250. It shrinks as the stock falls. Models that annualize this quarter's net margin are annualizing something that is already gone.

3. **The prior-year comparison is contaminated.** A **$45M litigation settlement was paid to the company in May 2025** and credited inside operating income — you can prove it arithmetically, because reported Q2'25 operating income ($45.4M) is larger than that quarter's entire gross profit ($47.1M), which would require operating expenses of ~$1.7M for a company that spends ~$47M a quarter ([commitments note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/751978/000119312526322462/R21.htm)). Ex-settlement, last year's operating income was about $0.4M. Both the bull comparison and the bear comparison are wrong unless you make this adjustment.

Honest limit on all three: this edge tells us the reported numbers are misleading in **both directions**. It does not, by itself, tell us the stock is cheap. It tells us where the fair value band is — and the band is below the price.

## Why it might be priced in

- **We do not disagree with the street. We agree with it from below.** Our own best forecast for 2027 product revenue (~$686M, +40%) is *lower* than the street's implied $825–840M. When your differentiated work lands you underneath consensus on a stock trading at 16–19× sales, you do not have a long. You have a valuation objection.
- **The lawsuit is public knowledge.** *In re Vicor Securities Litigation* (N.D. Cal., filed 7/11/2024) alleges misleading earnings-call statements about customer relationships. It is disclosed in every 10-Q and carried by every law-firm wire. It is not our finding.
- **The insider-selling angle is dead.** We checked it and killed it ourselves: recent Form 4 sales total roughly $1.4M, one of them under a pre-set plan adopted in November 2025, and one officer exercised options on 8/20 and **held**. There is no insider overhang.
- **The capacity story is directionally visible to anyone.** Rising capital spending and a hotter plant are not secrets.
- **Our fair-value band is a multiple assumption.** $94–141 comes from ~$3.13 of 2027 EPS at 30–45×. Anyone willing to pay 60× gets a different answer, and in this tape people have been.

## What could go wrong

**The pre-mortem. Top three failure narratives.**

**1. We never own it, and that is the loss.** The ladder sits at $120 and $100. The October print shows product revenue at $150M+, the growth story validates, the stock re-rates from $195 toward $280, and we hold nothing while having done all the work. This is the most likely bad outcome, and it is a *real* cost, not a consolation. **Tripwire exists:** Q3 product revenue (total minus royalties minus engineering fees) ≥ $150M, and upward revisions to the FY27 consensus. If both fire, the file closes as a miss and we do not chase.

**2. We get filled at $120 for exactly the wrong reason.** The price reaches our level *because* the Q3 royalty line steps down — the new agreement rolls off, the legacy stream turns out to be finite after all — at the same time the tax benefit disappears (it disappears mechanically as the price falls) and product growth decelerates from 32% toward the mid-teens. We buy a "cheap" 25× on numbers that are still falling. This is an adverse-selection fill: the gate opens because the thesis broke. **Tripwire exists:** the Q3 10-Q royalty disaggregation, split legacy versus new. It arrives with the filing, not with the press release — one to two weeks *after* our fill would happen.

**3. The failure with NO monitorable tripwire: the customer we cannot name.** The load-bearing demand story rests on a single unnamed hyperscaler-adjacent customer, asserted by analysts at 25–30% of the book, which management declined to confirm. We cannot monitor an entity we cannot name. Two live vectors, neither one watchable:
   - **A design socket gets reassigned.** The industry is moving to 800V DC power architecture, and NVIDIA publicly named Delta as a partner on 8/11/2026 — *after* Vicor's last call. If Vicor's topology loses the next-generation socket, no Vicor filing announces it. It shows up as a guidance cut, one to two quarters late, after the tape has already moved.
   - **An export-control action touches the customer chain.** The asserted customer's largest backer is a UAE entity with prior US scrutiny. A designation anywhere on that chain is a cliff on a quarter of the revenue base, and we have no named counterparty to screen against the restricted-party lists.
   
   **Unknown-unknown class, named: unnamed-counterparty concentration.** The generalization is that when the entity carrying a quarter of revenue is unresolvable to a name, *every* monitoring instrument we own is inoperative for that risk — screening lists, docket watches, competitor watches all require a name as the key. The secondary class is **standard-body architecture shift**: a specification change decided outside the issuer that silently reassigns the socket, with no filing obligation on the issuer at all. We have no connector in the atlas for either. Both are gaps, not oversights.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**

- **Our own size is not the problem.** At 0.5–1.0% of book on a $9B company, we do not move this tape. But we should say plainly that we **could not measure daily volume this session** — the broker snapshot was permission-denied to both benches — so we are asserting immateriality from market cap, not from measured liquidity. Confirm before the first fill.
- **The reflexivity that matters is inside the income statement, not the order book.** The $10.9M tax benefit is a function of the share price. As the stock falls toward our $120 entry, that benefit shrinks and reported EPS gets *worse* — so the headline numbers will look their ugliest precisely when our limit fills. We must not talk ourselves out of the fill using a metric we already know is price-reflexive.
- **We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict.** Our own read is that this is a momentum position unwinding, not a completed dislocation. Placing a resting bid at $120 is bidding into selling we expect to continue. That argues for splitting rung one into two tranches rather than one lump, and for not treating $120 as a floor.
- **A resting limit order across an earnings print is an accidental bet against volatility.** If the 10/20 date is real, the $120 order must be cancelled before the print and re-placed after, or it becomes a short-volatility position we never chose.
- **Do not let the "clean court, take a starter" reflex fire here.** That default exists for courts that produce no named kill. This court produced one: we forecast *below* consensus on a 16–19× sales stock. Flat at spot is the correct answer, not timidity.

*If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that our limit filled during a momentum unwind that had further to run, because the legacy royalty stream and the price-driven tax benefit both faded faster than the product business grew — leaving us long a good franchise at 25× sales on numbers that were still coming down.*
