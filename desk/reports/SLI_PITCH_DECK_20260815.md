# PITCH — SLI — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY at $2.38.** Even a fully funded project is only worth about today's share price at today's lithium price, so the financing news everyone is waiting for cannot, by itself, create upside. Stay out; reopen only on the joint trigger in the entry-bands section.

## What we believe

Standard Lithium is a real company with a real project. It plans to extract lithium from brine in southwest Arkansas with Equinor as a 45% partner, has a completed definitive feasibility study (a bankable engineering estimate: $1.45 billion to build, including a 12.3% contingency cushion, producing 22,500 tonnes per year for 20 years at a cash cost of $5,924 per tonne), a $225 million U.S. Department of Energy grant, and one signed customer (Trafigura). None of that is in dispute.

What we believe, after two adversarial reviews, is that the stock is a trap dressed as a turnaround, for three reasons:

1. **The balance sheet is fully spent, not comfortable.** The company holds $137.3 million in cash, but that cash is budgeted to fund roughly $36 million of spending per half-year through June 30, 2027. By the time the final go-ahead decision ("FID") arrives — targeted for year-end 2026 — about $100 million remains free. The equity check due at that decision is roughly $69 million in the base case, but any shortfall in the planned $1.1 billion of project debt lands on the company, and the only backstop is selling new shares.
2. **The company is already selling those shares.** On August 11, 2026 — one day after reporting the quarter in which the FID timeline slipped — it registered a new $50 million at-the-market share-sale program while $13.99 million of the previous program was still unused ([prospectus supplement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1537137/000110465926093563/tm2622588d1_suppl.htm)). Selling the full ~$64 million near the current price means issuing about 27.5 million new shares — 11.2% dilution on the 246.4 million outstanding. Last year the same program sold shares at an average of $4.01–$4.12; the same dollars now cost roughly 1.7× as many shares.
3. **Lithium, not financing, caps the upside.** This is the decisive finding. Pricing the project as if fully funded, across lithium prices (blue bench's table, using the feasibility study's stated sensitivity, an estimated ~25% tax haircut, the ~$100 million of cash, and 246.4 million shares):

| Lithium carbonate price | Fully-funded value per share | vs. today's $2.38 |
|---|---|---|
| Spot, ~$18,160/t (falling) | ~$2.03 | **below the current price** |
| Study's assumption, $22,400/t | ~$3.25 | +37% |
| $26,000/t | ~$4.35 | +83% |

At today's lithium price, a signed debt package would fund a project worth *less than the current market cap*. The stock only offers a worthwhile (+50%) prize above roughly $23,500 per tonne — and lithium has been falling since February, sitting at a six-month low.

## What the market believes

The stock is down 63% from its 52-week high of $6.40 and has bounced 25% off its $1.91 low. The market is pricing genuine doubt about whether the project gets financed and built: the final investment decision has slipped within 2026, no second customer agreement has been filed since March 9, and the debt package exists only as "indications of interest" mentioned on a conference call — the audited management discussion filed with the SEC says only that financing must still be secured ([the filed version](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1537137/000119312526341303/ck0001537137-ex99_2.htm)). Bulls are waiting for a debt announcement or a second offtake customer to re-rate the stock.

## Why we might have edge

- **The prize-table insight.** Most observers — including our own first-draft analysis — frame this as "financing risk: if the debt closes, the stock re-rates." The math says otherwise: at spot lithium, closing the debt funds a project worth about the current price. The event everyone is watching is not an entry signal. Knowing which catalyst *doesn't* matter is the edge here.
- **The share-sale tell.** Filing a new $50 million share-sale program before exhausting the old one, one day after disclosing a timeline slip, is a pattern worth cataloging: management is choosing continuous dilution near the lows rather than waiting for the repair catalyst it is guiding investors toward. Both benches endorsed this as a reusable warning sign.
- **The venue check.** The bullish specifics (an "~80% offtake target," debt "indications of interest") live only in the call transcript. The filed documents are silent. When the vetted document and the spoken pitch diverge, the filed document wins.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, largely because it is. At $2.38 the market is paying roughly the fully-funded value at spot lithium — which is a coherent price, not an obvious mistake. Our contribution is defensive: we are not claiming the stock is wildly overvalued (red bench explicitly found no fatal flaw and put conviction at 7/10 on the corrections, not on a short case). The Equinor terms are public and mildly supportive — $30 million already paid, a $33 million spending carry, and up to $70 million more payable to Standard Lithium if final investment decisions are taken ([Equinor announcement](https://www.equinor.com/news/20240508-partnership-standard-lithium)). If lithium recovers and the debt closes, the upside table above is real. We simply are not paid to wait for both while a disclosed share-seller leans on every rally.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry at any price today.** The courts converged on flat, so these are the **reopen bands** — conditions under which the case gets re-heard, not standing buy orders:

- **Joint reopen trigger (both required):** (a) a signed, committed project-debt package of at least $1.0 billion disclosed in an actual SEC filing — not a transcript mention; **and** (b) lithium carbonate sustained at roughly **$23,000/t or above**. Either alone is insufficient: debt without the lithium price funds a project worth about today's stock price; a lithium rally without committed debt still faces the $69M+ equity check and the active share-sale program.
- **Alternative single triggers (either reopens on its own):** a second offtake agreement disclosed by filing, or filed confirmation that the up-to-$70M Equinor milestone payment is tied to this specific project. Both improve the prize rather than just the funding path.
- **Reference values if reopened:** fully-funded value ≈ $3.25/share at $22,400/t lithium and ≈ $4.35 at $26,000/t. A reopened court should demand entry at a meaningful discount to the funded value at the *then-current* lithium price, net of whatever dilution the share program has realized by then.
- **Explicitly not a buy level:** $1.91 (the 52-week low). A break below it while the share-sale program is active means dilution math is worsening — it is a warning, not a bargain.
- **Sizing:** 0% now. If reopened, sizing is set by the re-court; nothing here pre-commits capital.
- **Catalyst dates:** ~2026-09-30 (company-guided window for additional offtake agreements — transcript guidance, unconfirmed); ~2026-11-05 (next earnings, data-vendor-derived, unconfirmed); 2026-12-31 (year-end target for the final investment decision — passing without one starts binding the June 2027 cash runway).
