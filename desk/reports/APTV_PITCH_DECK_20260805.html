02

What actually broke: an honestly-built beat, then a cut that deleted the upside case

The Q2 beat is real, and we tested it for cosmetics. Adjusted EPS $1.63 against a $1.30–1.50 guide; adjusted EBITDA margin 18.7%, +160bps. The obvious suspicion — a $44M gain on debt extinguishment sitting in Other income — checks out clean: the reconciliation explicitly removes it from adjusted net income, and residual other income is $14M vs $15M a year ago. The beat is operating margin plus a lower tax rate, not below-the-line inflation.

Alongside the cut: the Q3 guide of $1.25–1.35 came in reportedly below street expectations (street consensus not independently sourced — unverified), and first-half free cash flow printed −$196M against +$264M a year earlier — a $460M deterioration that the full-year guide assumes largely reverses in the back half.

The shape of the cut matters more than its size. The February and May guides were identical, line by line — so this is the year's first cut, and small. But it was taken almost entirely off the top of every range: the high end of sales fell 2× the low end, EPS 3×, free cash flow 5× (the FCF range narrowed from $200M wide to $100M wide, all off the top). That is a management team withdrawing confidence, not re-forecasting a level. And GAAP guidance moved up (+2.7% EPS) on below-the-line items while the operating measure fell — the two guides went in opposite directions, and only the adjusted one reflects the business.
03

Credibility first — the review corrected itself, and we print it

We charged management with misdirection. The transcript proved us wrong.

Working from the press release and 10-Q alone, the review's first pass ruled that Aptiv was blaming a recovering China while the real damage sat in Europe — the segment tables show Intelligent Systems EMEA revenue down 15.4% in Q2 while Asia Pacific rose 15.8%. That reading was a category error, and the earnings call refuted it two ways:

  • The CFO bridged the cut, precisely. Of the $300M sales cut: ~$150M China domestic schedule weakness, ~$100M delayed China program ramps, ~$50M software timing — ~83% of the cut attributed to China, quantified line by line. That is granular disclosure, the opposite of evasion.
  • The geography lines don't mean what they look like. Aptiv reports geographic revenue on a manufacturing-location basis (10-Q Note 19). European luxury OEMs building cars for export into China book into the EMEA line. The "EMEA collapse" and the China story are one story seen from the factory side, not two competing explanations.
Why this slide exists. The correction moved the ruling toward the bull — the company's disclosure of the cut is candid, not misdirecting — and it stays in the record because the correction is the discipline. A review that only publishes the charges it sustained isn't a review.

The real story: the segment that justifies the premium multiple is the broken one

Aptiv markets itself as a re-accelerating technology company. One quarter as a standalone, the technology is the part that isn't growing:

  • Inside Intelligent Systems the mix is worse than the total. "Sensors and Compute" — $2.67B in H1, 42% of the entire company — is shrinking (−0.9% H1). The genuinely growing "Software and Services" line is $297M in H1: 4.7% of revenue, being used to characterize the whole.
  • Every headline claim in the release is individually true — but the marketed takeaway (a re-accelerating technology company) diverges from the company's own segment data, where all the profit growth is connectors and cabling. Consequence: valuation, not integrity — you should not pay a technology multiple for this business.
The market's same-day verdict, through the price channel: Versigent — Aptiv's own spun-off commodity wiring-harness unit — rose +9.4% the day the parent fell 16.6%, and it trades at a higher EV/EBITDA than the parent (6.66× vs 6.22×). The market is paying up for the boring wire business and de-rating the technology business. That corroborates the segment finding from a completely independent channel.

How much that leg is

Intelligent Systems Asia Pacific, $278M/quarter — the documented threat hits Aptiv's smallest China pocket

The insourcing mechanism is documented, not theoretical: BYD is itself a top-3 ADAS supplier in China at 9.1% share (behind Bosch 10.4% and DENSO 9.4%), with its own 4nm driving chip already in mass production across five wafer fabs. Cockpit controllers are locally owned. Aptiv appears on none of the leader boards. But the 2025 damage lived entirely in that one leg — the connector business grew +11.4%, at market, in the same period.

The bigger unknown — and the honest gap: Engineered Components carries ~70% of Aptiv's Asia-Pacific dollars ($602M vs $278M per quarter). Whether Chinese OEMs start insourcing connectors is unanswered — no evidence found in either direction — and it matters more than the ADAS share loss that is documented. The a-priori case is weaker (high-mix, low unit value, qualification-heavy), but that is reasoning, not evidence. Carried as unverifiable and load-bearing.

Bounding note: exact China revenue is never disclosed — only "Asia Pacific" (26.4% of revenue, an upper bound that includes India/Korea/Japan), on a manufacturing-location basis. Peer filings corroborate the 2026 weakness as China-specific, not global: Magna cut its China units assumption while raising North America and Europe.

The honest current number, computed two independent ways from the filings, is +2 to +4pp. Nothing said today is false; the burden of proof has been shifted onto the analyst, who cannot discharge it from primary sources. That alone justifies a lower multiple.
07

What you'd own: the post-spin company, with a clean balance sheet — and a corrected drawdown number

  • The spin is done. Versigent (the wiring-harness/EDS business) separated on April 1, 2026. Aptiv received a $1.9B dividend and used it to redeem $1,847M of senior notes — the harness business most exposed to Chinese vertical integration left with the spin.
  • Capital return is running: $325M of buybacks in H1 against a $1.8B remaining authorization. The pace, on management's own framing (~half of free cash flow), is a multi-year program, not near-term price support.
  • Leverage ~2.1× net debt / EBITDA (1.96× on the cut forward guide), a 3.5× covenant, a fully undrawn $2B revolver. Cap structure clean: zero converts, zero preferred, zero warrants.
Correcting the number in circulation: the "−46% off the 52-week high" figure compares today's post-spin stock to the pre-spin company that still owned the harness business — apples to oranges. The post-spin high is $78.49 (June 3, 2026), so the true drawdown at $47.72 is −39.2%. Still severe; not −46%.

Also for the record: the segments were renamed in Q1-26 (Advanced Safety & User Experience → Intelligent Systems; Engineered Components Group → Engineered Components) — composition unchanged. Top-10 customer concentration fell from 56% to 44% with the spin.

The two load-bearing unknowns, stated plainly

  • The "Other, net −$331M" working-capital line.
