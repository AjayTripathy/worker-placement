## What we believe

SoFi is a real, profitable, deposit-funded lender that got cheap for a reason that has nothing to do with the reason our original brief argued about. The brief said "the market is pricing a credit event, and credit is fine." Half of that is right: on the primary filings, credit is fine-to-improving. The other half is wrong: the market is not pricing a credit event.

What we actually believe, stated plainly:

1. **Credit is not deteriorating on a like-for-like basis.** Personal-loan charge-off dollars fell sequentially (~$186M → ~$171M) even as the book grew, the unscrubbed "all-in" personal-loan loss rate fell 70 basis points in Q2, and the vintage loss curves — matched at like amounts of loan balance remaining, which is the seasoning control that matters — showed 2024/25 cohorts beating older ones by a *widening* gap (4.68% vs 6.43% in Q2, wider than Q1's 4.64% vs 6.32%).
2. **The deposit-flight story is dead.** Deposits $45.5B, up $5.3B in the quarter; net interest margin 5.98%; net interest income +52%.
3. **The book value is real and mostly earned, not printed.** Share count grew only ~0.7% quarter-over-quarter (stock comp only). The +16% year-over-year count is one discrete $1.5B underwritten stock sale in 2025 — done, in the past, and priced above book, which is why book value per share still rose ~56% to $7.34.
4. **But what is actually pressuring the stock is the rate path and balance-sheet intensity, not losses.** The guide is re-based on one-to-two Fed hikes; the fair-value loan markdowns were driven by a higher discount rate; a fresh sell-side Hold initiation (Loop Capital, 2026-09-09) cited rate-hike risk and a mix shift toward capital-heavy lending. The stock fell 8.9% on a print with revenue +43% and net income +61%.
5. **Therefore we have no differentiated view worth paying 2.45x book for.** Our best refutation refutes an argument nobody is making. At $18.01 that is 2.45x tangible book and 30x the $0.60 earnings guide, after a ~+17% rally off the post-print low and ~+21% off the 52-week low. The prize table below prices the next three months at an expected $16.72 — about 7% *below* the tape.

**Position: flat. Buy only lower, and only after we run the one dataset neither bench ran.**

## What the market believes

- **Consensus for Q3-26:** roughly $0.17 of earnings on ~$1.26B of revenue (single-vendor; a second vendor still shows a stale $0.11 / $1.14B, so even the consensus number is soft). Revisions over 90 days: 5 up, 9 down.
- **Analyst price targets sit above the tape:** ~$21.41 (31 analysts), ~$20.26 (25 analysts), ~$22.19 (16 analysts). So the sell side is mildly bullish, not bearish.
- **The bearish anchors below the tape are one vendor's model at two vintages** ($13.62 and $16.40) — not two independent marks, which our red bench initially mis-sold as two.
- **The marginal seller's stated reason is rates and capital intensity**, plus a cap on incremental profit: the revenue guide went up to $4.75–4.85B while the EBITDA guide stayed at ~$1.6B — the raise carried no extra profit.
- **The market also believes the full-year math needs a big Q4:** actual Q2 $0.12 + street Q3 $0.17 means Q4 must be roughly $0.23 to hit $0.60.
- **The short base is meaningful but not the story:** last-known short interest 133.3M shares, ~11.4% of float, versus ~30.4M shares (~$550M) of average daily volume, so about 4.4 days to cover. That data is a February-2026 vintage and is stale.

Net: consensus is "good company, capped profit, rate risk, fairly valued." That is our view too. Same view, no edge.

## Why we might have edge

Honest list, weakest to strongest:

1. **One clean dataset nobody has pulled.** SoFi Bank, N.A. (RSSD 962966; total assets ~$49.67B, deposits ~$42.32B) files regulatory call reports with *unadjusted* past-due, charge-off and reserve figures. That series is immune to the loan-sale scrub described below. Both benches called it unchecked-but-retrievable, and both said the earlier decision to dismiss it was wrong. If bank-level past-dues are flat-to-falling, the loudest bear claim dies with public data, before the print.
2. **A repeatable mechanism we now own.** The company sells delinquent loans *before* they charge off and headlines a loss ratio that explicitly excludes them (Q2: $94.3M of unpaid balance sold for $13.6M, an $80.7M realized loss, kept out of the charge-off numerator). That is a genuine detection pattern for other consumer lenders. Here it does not change the direction of the numbers — the unscrubbed rate fell too — but the mask *shrank* year over year (sale balances were flat at ~$94M on a 42%-larger book), which is itself mildly good news that nobody is publishing.
3. **A possible sequential inflection the year-over-year comparisons hide.** The technology-platform segment looks terrible year over year (revenue $84.5M, −23%; contribution profit $11.8M, −65%; enabled accounts 135M vs 160M) but two of three series turned up sequentially (+13% revenue, +2M accounts), and the ugly comparison lapses in Q4 because the lost client finished exiting before end-2025.
4. **Almost nobody is modelling the capital-light mix as the checkable axis.** Only $3.1B of $14.8B of originations went through the loan-platform/fee route this quarter. That fee-versus-balance-sheet mix, not credit, is the number that decides the multiple. It is disclosed quarterly and it is not in most models' foreground.

## Why it might be priced in

- **Our conclusion equals the published conclusion.** "De-rating, not impairment" is consensus. There is no gap to be paid for.
- **The re-rate is already partly delivered.** The stock closed 15.25 the day after the 7/29 print and has drifted +17% in the 33 days since, range 15.26–19.50. We would be buying the recovery, not the dislocation.
- **The valuation is not cheap on the metric that matters.** 2.45x tangible book, 30x the guide, versus a business whose own guide says extra revenue brings no extra profit.
- **The improving credit numbers are structurally flattered even where they are honest.** A book originating ~$10.7B of personal loans a quarter is younger than its own loss curve; and $29.7B of loans carried at fair value means credit stress can show up as marks in non-interest income rather than charge-offs at all. So a clean charge-off series cannot *certify* health — a point our red bench made well and our blue bench upheld.
- **The rate risk is genuinely un-edgeable by us.** If the guide's one-to-two hikes become three, the multiple compresses and nothing in our research kit sees it coming first.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy a re-rate that is already 17–21% delivered, and the tape simply mean-reverts to the vendor model.** Tripwire exists and is monitorable: a close below $16.50 (the pre-drift level, 2.25x book) invalidates the "the drift is real" premise; a close below $15.25 (post-print close) says the print reaction was right and the drift was noise. Monitorable via price gates below. This is the most likely, least dangerous failure: it costs money slowly.

**Failure narrative 2 — Rates, not credit, break it.** The guide assumes one-to-two hikes and a 22% tax rate that management called ~700 basis points above the original plan (a transcript number we never diffed against the 8-K — flagged as unchecked by red and still unchecked). Three hikes, or a funding-cost step-up, and both the earnings bridge (Q4 needing ~$0.23) and the multiple go at the same time. Partly monitorable — Fed calendar, forward curve, the fair-value note's discount-rate language — but our tripwires are lagging, because the fair-value note only lands with the 10-Q ~a week after the print.

**Failure narrative 3 (NO monitorable tripwire) — a private, contract-level or supervisory event.** Warehouse-facility covenant renegotiation, a whole-loan buyer stepping away, a repurchase-obligation dispute, a bank-charter condition or consent order from the primary regulator, or a Technology-Platform client exit we cannot see. The 10-Q's own tagging shows facilities, trusts and repurchase-obligation concepts; the size and terms of those private arrangements are not in any feed we own, and supervisory actions are typically disclosed *after* they bind. We flagged this in the detector sweep: the customs/import instrument that normally identifies a concentrated customer **cannot see a software client** — a real coverage gap on the exited Tech-Platform client (−25M enabled accounts) with no substitute instrument.
**Unknown-unknown class named: PRIVATE-CONTRACT / SUPERVISORY-ACTION CLASS — information that exists only inside a bilateral contract or a regulator's confidential supervisory file, and is therefore unobservable until it is announced.** There is no sensor we can arm for this. The only defence is size.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size versus liquidity:* trivial. Average daily volume ~30.4M shares (~$550M). A 1/4-size starter at 0.5% of book is a rounding error against that — well under a minute of tape. **We create no impact and we can exit in one print. Liquidity is not the risk; that also means liquidity is not an excuse for a bigger size.**
- *Predicted flow that is not ours:* short interest 133.3M shares / ~11.4% of float / ~4.4 days to cover on a stock that just rallied 17%. That flow works *against* our reopen bands — a squeeze can hold the price above $16.50 indefinitely and we simply never get filled, or we get filled only on a real fundamental break, which is the fill we least want. This asymmetry is the main reason a limit-only reopen plan can quietly become "long at a bad price or never long."
- *Doctrine interactions:* two of our own doctrines collide here. The "damage-absent" doctrine says a clean, primary-sourced refuting metric upgrades a name. The "misaimed refutation" pattern says a valid refutation of a narrative the marginal seller is not trading has *zero* price consequence. SOFI is precisely the case where doctrine 1 would have sized us in and doctrine 2 correctly stops us. Separately, the pre-charge-off delinquency-sale mechanism means our standard credit screen can be satisfied by a *constructed* metric — if we let that screen alone authorize entry on the next consumer lender, we will be wrong there and blame SOFI.
- *Reflexive risk in our own process:* the evidence pack's quarterly revenue block was wrong (it showed $151.89M where consolidated revenue is ~$1.22B). Both benches caught it and refused to compute on it. If a future deck runs share-count, stock-comp or cash-flow math off that block, the error propagates into sizing.

The blank, filled: not credit, but the rate path plus the profit cap — we pay ~2.45x book for a lender whose own guide adds revenue without adding profit, then a third hike re-rates it to 2x while our credit work stays perfectly correct and perfectly irrelevant.
