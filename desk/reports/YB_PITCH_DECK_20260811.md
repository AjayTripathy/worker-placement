# PITCH — YB — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY. Stay flat through the ~Aug-26 earnings report. The case is tabled as "missing data + wrong timing," not dead — reopen it on the Q2 segment detail, on finally reading the annual report, or on the first quarter reported under the new marketing rule (~March 2027).**

## What we believe

Yuanbao Inc. (NASDAQ: YB) is a Chinese online insurance distributor whose business is, on the numbers it publishes, growing fast and profitable. The stock has been cut in half — $13.84 against a 52-week high of $30.50, and only 15% above its low of $12.01, which itself sits about 20% below the IPO price. The market is pricing a regulatory threat: in April 2026 eight Chinese regulators issued sweeping new rules on online financial marketing, and separately China has been enforcing a rule that commissions insurers pay to distributors must match the rates they filed with the regulator (the "fees must match filings" regime).

Our review reached a two-part conclusion:

1. **The threat is real and the company's revenue is moving toward it, not away from it.** In Q1 2026, "system services" fees — the category regulators have publicly named as a common disguise for excess commission — were 68.8% of revenue and grew faster (+39.8%) than the plain-vanilla distribution line (+27.8%). The faster-growing two-thirds of the business is exactly the part under the microscope. Source: the company's own Q1 release, https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/10/3309527/0/en/Yuanbao-Inc-Announces-First-Quarter-2026-Unaudited-Financial-Results.html
2. **The question cannot be answered on the timeline the stock is trading on.** The new marketing rules were *issued* April 24, 2026 but do not take *effect* until September 30, 2026 (confirmed via Caixin: https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-04-28/china-imposes-sweeping-curbs-on-online-financial-marketing-102438783.html). So the upcoming ~Aug-26 report can partially test the commission-fee question, but the marketing-rule question stays open until the October–December quarter reports, around March 2027. A clean report this month would not clear the cloud.

On top of that, the stock is structurally hard to own: insiders hold ~96.8%, the tradeable float is roughly $20M, daily volume is about $196k, and the company announced a $15M buyback — about 75% of that float. Even if we were right, getting out would be slow and expensive.

## What the market believes

The 54% drawdown says the market believes the regulatory changes will materially damage the economics — either the fee category gets reclassified/compressed, or the short-video and livestream marketing funnel (how the company acquires customers) gets restricted. A secondary belief, common to controlled Chinese ADRs: that minority shareholders may never see the cash. That second belief took a real hit in our review — the company paid a $58M dividend that actually cleared China's capital controls and reached ADS holders' accounts. The money can get out. That is a genuine point against the deepest bear case, even though ~$56M of the $58M went to insiders simply because they own ~96.8%.

## Why we might have edge

- **We dated the rule correctly and most commentary doesn't.** Knowing the effective date is 9/30/26 tells you what each earnings report can and cannot prove. Anyone buying a "clean" Aug-26 report as vindication is buying an untested quarter.
- **We identified which leg is testable now.** The fee-matching enforcement is already live on the insurer side; the Aug-26 report's segment split (system-services growth and implied take-rate, plus sales-and-marketing spend ratio) is a real, immediate test of the larger threat.
- **The rule may cut both ways.** The marketing rules confine online insurance marketing to licensed institutions and their contracted platforms. YB *is* a licensed distributor. The same rule that threatens its funnel could eliminate unlicensed competitors' funnels and consolidate share toward it. Nobody in the public discussion we found is pricing that branch.
- **The cash-actually-moves proof.** The dividend clearing to ADS holders, plus a buyback done below roughly 3.5× earnings (which mechanically increases remaining holders' share of profits), refutes the laziest version of the bear case.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the symmetric list, and it is heavier:

- **The discount may simply be correct.** If "system services" fees are premium-linked commission in disguise, reclassification compresses two-thirds of revenue, and the current price could still be too high. We could not verify the fee contracts — see below.
- **There is no way to be proven right soon.** The decisive quarter reports around March 2027. Until then, every report can hurt the stock but none can clear it. Money parked here is dead for two-plus quarters even in the good case.
- **A clean Aug-26 report is biased upward anyway.** Marketing spend was likely pulled forward ahead of the September 30 deadline, so even the partially-testable leg reads better than the future run-rate.
- **The exit door is tiny and shrinking.** ~$20M float, ~$196k/day volume, and a buyback consuming up to 75% of the float. A controlled company shrinking a micro-float is also the classic setup for a take-private at a modest premium, which would cap the upside regardless of business value. (Our cross-examination correctly noted there is *no evidence* of a take-private on the record — this is a structural possibility, not a prediction.)
- **Latent insider supply.** Insider selling so far is clean (only option exercises at $0.15/share and tax withholding), but that means enormous near-zero-cost stock sits behind the float, un-sold *so far*.
