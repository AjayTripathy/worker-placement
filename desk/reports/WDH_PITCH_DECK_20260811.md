**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Stay flat today. ## What we believe

Waterdrop (WDH), a Chinese insurance broker listed in the US since 2021, trades at $1.00 — down 54% from its 52-week high of $2.18 and just 4% off its low of $0.96. Our original working thesis was that the stock is a value trap: that the advertised RMB 2,880.7M "cash position" was mostly restricted customer money, that unrestricted liquidity had collapsed ~46%, and that investment income had been killed. **The court refuted all three of those claims against the primary source.** What survives is a different, narrower bear case: the company's growth is rented. Selling and marketing expense tripled year-over-year (RMB 541.1M vs 172.4M, of which RMB 361.4M was paid to third-party traffic channels), and for every incremental RMB of revenue, only 0.8 cents reached operating profit. The company remains profitable (17 consecutive profitable quarters), is buying back stock and paying a dividend, and its balance sheet is honestly presented — but the growth engine is expensive and the deep-value case rests on an unread disclosure.

## What the market believes

At $1.00 the market prices Waterdrop at roughly 0.49× book value and ~5.9× enterprise value to operating profit — near-liquidation pricing typical of small Chinese ADRs. The market is implicitly saying some combination of: the cash isn't real or isn't reachable, the growth spend won't earn a return, and/or China-listing risk deserves a permanent discount. A widely-circulated data-vendor series showing "unrestricted liquidity down 46%" supports the bearish framing — but the court traced it and found it wrong (it ignored RMB 2.08 billion of long-term investments; actual cash and equivalents *rose* from 483.0 to 751.9 in Q1). The finance chief's resignation in July (effective 7/31, personal reasons, explicit no-disagreement language, an internal successor-signer in place) adds texture to the bear mood without substance.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the actual balance-sheet table; much of the market apparently hasn't.** The Q1 2026 release ([primary source, PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/waterdrop-inc-announces-first-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial-results-302801217.html)) states verbatim that the "cash position" *excludes* restricted cash — the RMB 935.4M restricted balance sits separately and tracks insurance premium payables (RMB 1,009.0M), i.e., properly segregated customer float, not hidden inside the headline. The most damaging bear claim is disprovable in public print.
- **We closed the "mystery income decline" the vendors couldn't.** The ~RMB 16M below-the-line swing decomposes into three small items (other-income swing −11.8M, FX −2.1M, a new equity-investee loss −2.2M) — none is a distressed-liquidation signature.
- **We know exactly which single fact decides the trade.** Not sentiment, not the print: the annual-report note describing what the RMB ~1,985M of long-term debt investments actually are. That is a one-document gate with a binary outcome.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the symmetric view: 0.49× book for a Chinese broker with tripling customer-acquisition costs may simply be *fair*, not cheap. The correct "hard cash" comparison is cash plus short-term investments of ~US$130M — not the US$417.6M headline — against a company that just added RMB 227.7M of new short-term borrowings while sitting on that supposed cash pile (a classic tell worth respecting until explained). The buyback support is weaker than it first appears: the US$120.1M figure is cumulative program-to-date since inception, not recent activity; the live shareholder-return proof is only the US$10.8M dividend. And 69% of the cash floor is in instruments whose nature we have not been able to read (SEC access blocked repeatedly this session). If that note reveals reach-for-yield paper, the market's discount was right and we were slow.
