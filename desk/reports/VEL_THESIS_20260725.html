Internal Thesis · Velocity Financial (VEL) · 25 July 2026 · independent court

Velocity Financial: a genuinely cheap, spread-widening non-QM lender — but the 17.5% ROE is flattered by a related-party sale, and the float is 84% sponsor-owned and being sold monthly

A non-QM / investor-real-estate lender the tape treats as a melting late-cycle credit book near its 52-week low. On the primary filings the business is better than the price implies — book value covers the stock, the spread engine widens with rates, and defaulted loans recover above par. But an independent pass finds two things the finder soft-pedaled: ~18% of last year's earnings came from selling bad loans to an affiliate, and three private funds own 84% of the shares and are trimming into a $1.6M-a-day tape every month. Real value, modest edge, real overhang — a smaller starter than staged, at a lower price.

What the market believes vs. what we believe

What the market believes

A thin, near-52-week-low non-QM lender, down 17% YTD — a deteriorating late-cycle real-estate credit book, fairly priced at ~1× book / ~6× earnings because the ROE is about to mean-revert toward ~10% as housing and CRE soften.

What we believe

The filings show the opposite direction: ROE roughly doubled off the 2020-21 trough, net interest income compounded 2.4× at a rising ~3.6% NIM, book grew $433M→$693M, and defaulted loans recover 102–109% of principal. But the headline 17.5% ROE is flattered by a one-time related-party loan sale; the clean run-rate is ~13%. You are paid to own a spread-widening lender at a trough-ROE (~0.92× book at our limit) price — in small size, because the float is sponsor-controlled and being distributed.

The decisive finding — the ~18% one-timer is a sale of bad loans to an affiliate

The FY25 headline (net income $105.0M, 17.5% ROE) is flattered by a $19.3M gain the company booked selling non-performing loans to a related party in December 2025 — a channel the finder did not surface. The 10-K reports a line, brand-new to 2025 ($0 in 2023–24), called "Gain on Sale of Loans to a Related Party" of $19,344K, from selling $128.9M of UPB of non-performing loans to an affiliate. Three private funds own 84% of the company, so this counterparty is not arm's-length, and it is the kind of year-end transaction that can manage earnings. The mitigant is real, though: the loans changed hands at ~102% of unpaid principal — the same recovery rate at which VEL's organically-resolved defaults have cleared every year (102.4–102.9% across 2023-25). So it is genuine high-LTV recovery economics crystallized through a related party, not an inflated markup. The correct response is to underwrite the normalized ~13–14% ROE, not the 17.5% headline — and the freshest quarter does exactly that.

That gap — a low-teens-ROE spread lender trading at book — is the value edge. It is modest, not a table-pound, precisely because the overhang below caps how fast it can close.

Where the finder was right — verified against the primary filings

The two things the whole long rests on both survive independent verification off the audited data. (1) The spread engine widens with rates. Net interest margin expanded 3.34% → 3.56% → 3.61% (2023→24→25) even as the cost of funds rose 5.58% → 6.24% — assets reprice up faster than the fixed securitized debt. The "benign-rate peak about to compress" bear is refuted by the company's own MD&A. (2) Defaults recover above par, and it is not a housing-bubble artifact. Resolved non-performing loans recovered 102–109% of unpaid principal in every disclosed cohort from 2023 through Q1-2026 — i.e., it held through the post-2022 cooling in home-price appreciation, not just during the boom. The ~65% origination LTV is the cushion, and it is doing its job.

The credit channel-shift is real but marked and expensed — not a hidden hole

The one genuine credit catch: non-performing loans are being resolved partly by foreclosing them into a growing pile of real-estate-owned (REO): $68M (2024) → $118M (2025) → $131.8M (259 properties) at Q1-26, foreclosing ~$120M/yr against ~$54M/yr of disposals. But this REO is fair-value-marked every quarter through the P&L, and the company earned its returns after absorbing the write-downs (REO valuation adjustments −$3.9M / −$6.1M / −$17.5M for 2023/24/25). Critically, the acceleration did not continue: the Q1-26 REO valuation adjustment was only ~−$3.2M (annualizing ~−$12.8M, below the −$8M/quarter line that would signal timing-friction turning into real loss). And REO still sells at a net gain to carrying value every year (+$1.4M in 2025). Disclosed, marked, expensed — a throughput friction (slow judicial-state foreclosure), not a solvency problem. The honest gross problem-loan figure is larger than the finder's framing, though: $554.5M of UPB is 90+ days past due or non-accrual = 8.5% of the book — a real late-cycle number to watch, even if well-marked.

Funding — the way non-QM lenders usually die — is de-risked, not fragile

The balance sheet is termed out: non-recourse securitizations ($4.4B, VEL holds only the residual) plus a new $500M syndicated term-note issue completed January 30, 2026 and $75M of 2029 notes. The 10-Q states VEL was "in compliance with all covenants" as of March 31, 2026; there is no going-concern language. The genuine fragility is the full-recourse warehouse repurchase lines, whose capacity shrinks with delinquency — and two of them mature (roll over) in May and September 2026, a routine but real near-term watch. A shutdown of the securitization market would stall origination — the true tail risk — but that is a sector event, not a company-specific weakness here.

The catch the finder buried — an 84% sponsor float being sold every month

The "$1.6M thin ADV" is not a neglected micro-cap; it is a private-equity-controlled float being distributed into. Three funds own ~84% of the shares — Snow Phipps / TruArc 34.0%, PIMCO 32.2%, Beach Point 17.7% — leaving only ~16% genuine public float. And an affiliate broker has filed a Form 144 (notice of proposed sale) nearly every month from July 2025 through July 2026 — a steady sponsor sell-down. That is a persistent supply overhang that pins the stock and caps the re-rating: even if the value gap is right, a controlled seller distributing into $1.6M/day of volume is a headwind to it closing. Separately, insider open-market activity is net selling (CFO, chief legal officer, EVP-capital-markets, chief accounting officer all sold small clips in the $18–20 zone in 2026; zero open-market purchases) — no management conviction at $17. Neither is disqualifying, but both argue for a smaller position and a lower entry than the staged starter.

Tripwires — what would change the verdict

Kill / re-underwrite: a larger related-party NPL sale, or the affiliate recovery mark diverging materially above the ~102% organic rate → the one-timer becoming a recurring earnings crutch rather than genuine recovery.

Kill / re-underwrite: a sponsor block-exit or SC 13D amendment signalling accelerated distribution → supply overwhelming the $1.6M-ADV tape and derating the stock (the monthly Form-144 drip is the standing tell).

Kill: REO valuation adjustments accelerating past ~−$8M/quarter (Q1-26 was ~−$3.2M), or resolution recovery breaking below 100% of UPB as the aged $132M REO inventory clears → the channel-shift turning into slow-motion loss.

Kill: normalized ROE (ex the related-party gain) falling below ~12%, NIM slipping under ~3.6%, or warehouse-covenant / borrowing-base stress (or a failed May/Sep-26 rollover) in a 10-Q.

Add (only) on: a broad credit / small-cap risk-off overshoot to ~$14.75 (~0.83× book), with the recovery rate and NIM still intact.
