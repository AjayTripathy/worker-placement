How the hype is measured — a standing daily instrument, in two phases

Phase 1, demand (today → 13-Oct): Planet Zoo 2's position on Steam's most-wishlisted chart, read automatically every day — the industry's standard pre-launch demand gauge, and the one Frontier's own launches have historically tracked. The thresholds were fixed in advance, so no reading can be argued with after the fact: breaking into the top-60 confirms the demand leg — the first tranche may then fund, but only once the ~10-Sep audited accounts also print clean (two independent signals, both required, either can arrive first); slipping below #200, or sitting outside the top-100 a week before launch (6-Oct), is a pre-registered warning — no money moves pre-launch on a warning state. Baseline at registration: #158–159. Secondary reads: trailer-view velocity and announcement cadence, checked by hand.

Phase 2, verdict (13-Oct →): the instrument switches to the launch review positive-rate ladder, read daily and graded against Frontier's own four-point history — Planet Coaster 1 94.6% · Planet Zoo 1 87.8% · RollerCoaster Tycoon realised 67.1% · Planet Coaster 2 59.3%. The bars: ≥85% bull · 78–85% base · 70–78% warning · ≤67% flop confirmed, with the formal read on 15-Oct (PC2's verdict landed within 48 hours). Review volume is deliberately secondary: PC2 posted 4,499 reviews in 14 days and still flopped — pace measures attention, the positive rate measures the verdict.

The discipline line: pre-launch heat predicts the launch window; it cannot predict the review-driven tail — and the FY27 guide needs the tail. Planet Coaster 2 is the in-sample proof: fine pre-launch signal, 600k units, and still the flop. Which is why the next slide exists. And what the instrument honestly cannot see: Steam publishes rank, not wishlist counts, so a rank move can reflect other titles' motion; community-channel and follower data sit behind gates and are checked manually, not scripted. The instrument bounds the hype question — it does not settle execution quality, which is unknowable before 13-Oct by construction.

The Warhammer sidebar — an honest paragraph

The Warhammer franchise surge is real — three licensed titles sit in Steam's global wishlist top-30 — and Frontier does hold live Games Workshop economics as publisher of the undated Chaos Gate — Deathwatch (wishlist #379). But the precedent title's arithmetic caps the prize: at Daemonhunters' scale, Frontier's publisher take is roughly £1M a year against £105M of revenue — a sliver, not a thesis. We reviewed Games Workshop itself as the pure-play carrier and passed on price — 3 of 10 on the same conviction scale (the royalty option is real but trades at 34× core earnings with every pipeline title undated; our buy levels sit far below the market). Nothing in this FDEV underwrite leans on Warhammer.

08 / 16The autopsy — what actually killed Planet Coaster 2, coded

3,712 negative reviews of the last flop, ranked — then held against this launch's build

The bear isn't "sequels are risky"; it's "PZ2 repeats PC2." So we pulled PC2's negative-review corpus to launch day (2,359 of 3,712 landed in the launch window; 1,466 English reviews coded), weighted by community helpfulness, and ranked what actually killed it. Then we graded each cause against Planet Zoo 2's disclosed pre-launch build. PC2's score was 61.8% positive on day zero — the verdict landed within 48 hours.

09 / 16The confession — two of our own instruments, withdrawn

The autopsy killed our evidence before it touched the bear's

The most valuable output of the second adversarial pass wasn't about Frontier — it was about us. Two instruments we were leaning on were tested against the reference flop and failed in-sample. We publish the failures because the corrections are the credibility.

What we believedThe test that killed itStatus
"The PZ1 fanbase is warm on the sequel — 9 of 9 recent review mentions positive" (cited as bull evidence in the telemetry pass) The control: Planet Coaster 1's fanbase in PC2's own pre-launch window ran 60-of-64 positive = 93.8%, against a 93.1% base rate — zero information. They weren't wise; they were blindsided. Pre-launch fanbase warmth does not discriminate. Preview-embargo tone failed the same test — PC2's hands-on previews glowed ("Frontier's best park sim yet?"). Withdrawn
"≥3,500–4,500 Steam reviews at the two-week mark = launch on pace" (our original tranche-2 volume gate) PC2 had 4,499 reviews at day 14. The pace bar would have passed the reference flop — because PC2's problem was never sales volume (600k units); it was what the reviews said. A volume gate cannot catch a quality failure. Withdrawn
The replacement — the launch positive-rate ladder, calibrated on Frontier's own four-point history: PC1 94.6% · PZ1 87.8% (the successes) vs Realms of Ruin 67.1% · PC2 59.3% (the failures). Bands: ≥85% = bull confirmed · 78–85% = base · 70–78% = warning · ≤67% = bear confirmed. Readable from the public Steam page by 15-Oct — PC2's verdict landed within 48 hours of launch. Volume (units ≈ 50 × reviews) demoted to the secondary check. Adopted

Minor errata also logged: the earlier telemetry note understated PC2's pre-launch campaign (it ran 22 Steam announcements, not 3) — campaign breadth is non-discriminating too. Every instrument that survives this slide has been tested against a known failure, not just a known success.

12 / 16The floor — what a flop actually costs

Three floors under a flop — and a structure that never rides to them

The structural point outranks the floor: only tranche-1 (0.375%, ~$12.4k) is ever exposed to the flop — tranche-2 cannot fund in the flop state because its gate is the launch evidence itself. And the rate ladder reads the verdict by 15-Oct (PC2's landed in 48 hours), so the exit happens into the verdict, not the drift after it. Realized worst case ≈ the gap-day move on 0.375%: roughly $3.6k at settled-bear, ~$5.4k at the panic print — one to one-and-a-half basis points of the book. Against that, the base case pays ~+$3k on T1 and opens a 0.75% position into a 530p+ path. The asymmetry, not the floor, is why a hit-driven binary earned an entry at all.
