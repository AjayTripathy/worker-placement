# PITCH — ZG — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Buy a half-sized initial position (~0.5% of the book) near $35, with all additions gated on events — the FTC verdict, a stabilization in visitor counts, or any concrete sizing of the CoStar lawsuit — and explicitly no additions on a trial-headline pop. (Blue bench 7/10; red bench 5/10 wanted to stay flat until the verdict.)**

## What we believe

Zillow at $35.16 — down 61% from its 52-week high and 21% off its low — is a roughly breakeven, cash-rich marketplace being priced as if its core business is collapsing, when the actual damage evidence is thinner than the tape implies. Corrected numbers: 224.9M shares → ~$7.9B market cap, $682M cash, no convertible debt (settled May 2025), an undrawn $500M revolver, and the scary-looking $465M "repo" is just collateralized mortgage warehouse borrowing. On full-year guidance of $730–760M adjusted EBITDA minus a ~$300M stock-compensation run rate, the enterprise trades at roughly **16x a conservatively-defined real cash earnings number** — not the 40x+ the bear case claimed. The company bought back $200M of stock in Q2 at an average of ~$35.4–35.7 (essentially today's price) and has **$1.1B of authorization remaining, about 14% of the market cap** — a real, funded floor under the price. Revenue grew through a quarter in which the consumer-disintermediation story says it shouldn't have: Rentals +31% and Mortgages +75%, which monetize property managers and borrowers, businesses that do not grow like that through a collapsing funnel.

## What the market believes

The market believes three stacked negatives: (1) the FTC's case over the Zillow–Redfin rentals deal — bench trial starting **August 24, 2026**, six trading days out — threatens the fastest-growing segment (Rentals is 42% of incremental growth and is the business on trial); (2) CoStar's copyright suit over 53,000 photos is an unbounded tail, made worse because the infringement count *grew* after the complaint (a willfulness accelerant) and the motion to dismiss was **denied** in May; and (3) the consumer funnel is genuinely eroding — unique users fell 3% and then 2%, the leading indicator, while the flagship Residential segment grew only +7%. Layer on ten insider Form 144 sale filings clustered August 11–14 into the pre-trial window (10b5-1 status unverified), a "26% margin" guide built on an adjusted metric that excludes both stock compensation (~10% of revenue) and — remarkably — **the FTC litigation's own defense costs**, and a Q2 margin of 23% that requires a second-half lift to hit guidance. The market's view is coherent, not irrational.

## Why we might have edge

The court process itself generated the edge: **both directions' headline numbers were wrong, and the corrections net out in favor of a small long.**

- The bear case's central valuation kill used a stale stock-compensation ratio (~15% of revenue, a 2022-era figure). The actual Q2 number is **$75M on $772M revenue = 9.7%** ([Q2-26 shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001617640/000161764026000050/exhibit992.htm)). That single correction moves the "real" multiple from 40x+ to ~16x — the kill was itself the artifact.
- The "growth is fake mix" claim degraded on inspection: property-manager and mortgage revenue growing 31%/75% is delivered *through* the consumer funnel; that's evidence the funnel still converts, not an accounting trick.
- The downside "has no floor" claim is directly rebutted by a live buyback executing at exactly this price with $1.1B remaining.
- The market is pricing the trial *start* as a catalyst; the bench verdict will come weeks later, the FTC already lost its quick-look and partial summary-judgment motions on July 8, and it now carries the full rule-of-reason burden ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/judge-denies-ftc-request-to-presume-zillow-redfin-deal-unlawful/)).

## Why it might be priced in

Honest counter-case, and it's substantial. The CoStar tail is *worse than the bear bench knew* — the motion to dismiss was already denied when the bear wrote "pending," the 10-Q says the loss is "not estimable," and per-work statutory damages theoretically exceed the market cap. Nobody on either bench can size it; a stock down 61% may simply be pricing an unsizable legal tail correctly. The visitor decline is real and leads revenue; if monetization follows traffic down with a lag, the +7% Residential growth is the past, not the future. The 26% margin guide needs a second-half acceleration that Q2's 23% doesn't yet show, into an earnings date that isn't even company-confirmed — and management excluding its own live litigation costs from that headline margin is a genuine earnings-quality red flag. Insiders sold into the pre-trial window. At ~16x cash earnings, this is cheap-for-a-reason, not free money.
