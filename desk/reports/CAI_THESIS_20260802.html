1 · The four-idea frame

Market believes

A June-2025 IPO that broke. Down 63% from its high, still on a triple-digit forward multiple, with a

44%-owner founder overhanging the register and post-IPO supply that never got absorbed. High-growth

diagnostics is out of favour; sell it and move on.

We believe

The thing that would actually have killed this company **has been resolved, and the resolution is

audited.** The S-1 carried "substantial doubt as to going concern"; the post-IPO filings retire it — the

Q1-2026 10-Q contains zero occurrences of "going concern," "substantial doubt," or "ability to

continue as a going." That is not an interpretation; it is a text search of the primary document.

And the operating turn is real on the reported numbers: revenue $216.2M, +78.8%, molecular profiling

+85%, gross margin 65% (from 47%), income from operations +$5.3M against −$58.0M a year

earlier, adjusted EBITDA +$26.2M, free cash flow +$22.5M, cash and marketable securities

$825.7M against a $400M term loan. The FY2026 guide — $1.00–1.02B, +23–26% — was issued

2026-02-26 and reaffirmed 2026-05-07, unchanged since.

So the −63% is a post-IPO valuation de-rate, not a business break. At $15.67 the stock trades at

roughly 4.0x FY2026E EV/sales — which the record identifies as the bear-case multiple. The

courted claim is narrow and correct: this is fundamentals-de-risked, not valuation-cheap.

What this pass adds, and it is not comfortable. Three things the record does not carry:

1. The controls. The Q1-2026 10-Q states verbatim that disclosure controls and procedures **"were not

effective at the reasonable assurance level as of March 31, 2026 due to the previously reported

material weakness… lack of sufficient qualified accounting resources, including those necessary to

account for and disclose accounting transactions that require complex calculations."** Remediation is

ongoing, not complete. Context from the proxy: EY declined to stand for re-election in July 2024,

the disclosed reason being that same weakness — *"ineffective control activities related to the

incorrect application of GAAP."* Deloitte was engaged September 2024 and ratified 2026-06-04.

2. The real forced-sale channel is a margin loan, not a secondary. **25,000,000 shares held by Halbert

Family Capital, LLC are pledged as collateral for Mr. Halbert's personal indebtedness** — ~8.8% of

shares outstanding, ~$392M at the 7/31 close, on a stock down 63% from its high. The record frames the

overhang as "control"; the mechanism is a pledge.

3. The supply overhang the record worries about is empirically absent. Since 2026-06-01 there has been

no S-1, no S-3, no S-3ASR, no follow-on and no Form 144. All three Form 4s are acquisitions —

including **Jon S. Halbert (director) buying 68,000 shares on the open market at a weighted-average

$14.56, $990,080**. David Dean Halbert filed nothing. The IPO lockup expired ~mid-December 2025 and is

already in the tape. Caris also authorised a $100M share repurchase on 2026-06-08. The flow runs

the opposite way to the overhang hypothesis.

Why we might have edge

The record is explicit and this doc keeps it: "DILIGENCE not EDGE." The going-concern flag that

looked decisive was a stale-corpus artifact, fixed by reading the post-IPO 10-K and 10-Q rather than

the S-1. Caris discloses everything — the lumpy quarter, the controls, the pledge, the auditor change — so

on takeaway-versus-data grading it is CLEAN. There is no liar here to exclude, and re-detecting

honestly disclosed bad news is not alpha.

What is left is a genuine but modest structural claim: a broken-IPO small-cap with 50% insider ownership,

thin coverage and a de-rating that overshot the business gets sold by mechanisms that never open the 10-Q.

Carry-vs-edge label: **risk-premium, and the premium is mostly warranted — the record's own words are

"paid_for: Halbert control overhang + lumpy/decelerating Q1-26 + post-IPO supply — mostly WARRANTED, not a

cushion."**

The one place this doc claims an information advantage: **the pledge and the control-effectiveness

language are in the primary documents and in essentially no screen.**

Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

1. The operating turn is one quarter thin and the quarter was lumpy. Revenue fell ~26% quarter-over-

quarter into Q1-26. Income from operations went −$18M → +$33M → +$88M → +$5M across four quarters.

A +$5.3M operating profit on $216M of revenue is a 2.4% margin — that is a crossing, not a trend.

2. The multiple is still very high. Even after −63%, this is ~4x forward sales on a business that just

printed a 2.4% operating margin. There is no earnings support; the entire valuation is a

growth-durability bet.

3. The bear case in the record is not a floor. It is a multiple bear (4x sales) computed on the

guided sales base. If the growth decelerates below guide, both the multiple and the base fall

together. "0% downside to bear" is true only if the guide holds — which is precisely what the Aug-5

print tests.

4. The controls cut directly at the thesis. Every number that de-risked this position — the operating

crossover, the adjusted EBITDA, the free cash flow — was produced under a control environment

management describes as ineffective for "transactions that require complex calculations." That is not

an accusation; it is the company's own conclusion, and it means the de-risking evidence carries more

uncertainty than its precision suggests.

5. The one shrinking segment is the one with outside validation. Pharma R&D services fell −21.4%

to $5.4M. It is small, but it is the line where pharmaceutical partners vote with their budgets.

Mechanism falsifier

Q2 growth decelerating below the 23–26% FY guide, OR income from operations reverting to a loss — the

record's "one number" tripwire. Secondary: cash burn resuming; a Halbert control event, which after this

pass should be read specifically as a margin call on the 25M pledged shares, not as a voluntary sale.

Edge type

RISK_PREMIUM (mostly warranted). Not EDGE. Sizing reads this label and the gate reads the date.
