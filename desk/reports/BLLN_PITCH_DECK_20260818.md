# PITCH — BLLN — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-08 — DECLINE at $93. No position, no order. The 39% drop was earned, not a mistake: the company missed on profit, broke its own pattern of raising guidance, and its main business stopped growing in the same quarter the biggest competitor set records. This is a watchlist name, not a dead one — 70% gross margins, positive free cash flow, roughly $460M of net cash. It reopens at ~$78 or on hard evidence the core business re-accelerates.**

*Staleness note up front: this verdict was set on 2026-08-08 at a $93 tape. As of 2026-08-18 the stock is $87.99 — down another 5.7%, and now 12.8% above the reopen price. Nothing else in the case has changed, but the gap to a live decision is narrower than the verdict date implies.*

---

## What we believe

BillionToOne sells genetic tests. Two businesses: prenatal testing (the big one, ~88% of revenue) and cancer testing (small, growing fast). In the June quarter it did $109.4M of revenue — up 64% versus a year ago, which sounds excellent, and up **0.9% versus the March quarter**, which is the number that matters.

Our belief, plainly: **the market read this correctly and we do not have a better read.** The stock fell because the company stopped compounding, not because anyone panicked. Specifically:

1. **The core business went flat.** Prenatal revenue was roughly $95.0M in Q1 and $95.8M in Q2 — call it flat, and possibly slightly down depending on which company release you anchor to. Every dollar of sequential growth came from the small cancer segment ($10.7M → $13.7M).
2. **Price per test is falling.** Reported average selling price went $571 → $551. Gross margin went 73% → 70%. Both moved the wrong way in the same quarter.
3. **The guidance the company reiterated now requires an acceleration it hasn't shown.** Full-year guide is $450–465M. The first half delivered $217.8M. That means the second half has to do $232–247M, which is 6.6–13.5% growth half-over-half — coming out of a quarter that grew 0.9%.
4. **A same-day competitor print removes the "it's the whole industry" excuse.** Natera reported the same day: +38% year over year, a record 1.044 million tests, and a raised full-year outlook. Its stock rose 16% while BLLN fell 39%. Whatever went wrong is specific to this company.

What we also believe, and it is why this is a watch and not a tombstone: **the balance sheet and the business quality are genuinely fine.** ~$548.6M cash, $91.0M debt, no maturity until 2031, positive free cash flow, 70% gross margins. There is no solvency story here. This is a growth-rate question, not a survival question.

## What the market believes

The market moved from "this is a compounder that raises guidance every quarter" to "this is a company whose main product line has stopped growing and whose pricing is eroding." That is a repricing of the growth rate, and it happened in one day.

At $87.99, with 47.19M shares and ~$458M of net cash, the enterprise is valued at ~$3.69B — about **8.1× this year's guided revenue**. That is not a distressed price. It is the price of a good business growing 40–50% with a live question mark over it. The market is not saying the company is broken; it is saying it will no longer pay a premium for growth it can't verify.

The market has *not* concluded the company is losing. If it had, the stock would be nearer the $61.96 52-week low. It is pricing uncertainty, not failure.

## Why we might have edge

Three honest candidates, in descending order of strength:

1. **The valuation comparison is being done wrong by most people, including our own red bench.** Natera trades at roughly 15.3× revenue (143.22M shares × ~$308 ≈ $44B against a $2.85–2.91B guide) growing 31%. BLLN trades at ~8.1× enterprise value to sales growing 40–50%. On the crude growth-adjusted math, BLLN is the *cheaper* one. If the flat quarter turns out to be a stumble rather than a trend, the multiple gap closes toward the leader.
2. **Two dated product launches sit inside the second half, and neither is in anyone's model yet.** The Unity 130-gene panel (launched 2026-08-17) and Northstar Origin (2026-09-01) are both higher-content tests, meaning they should raise revenue per test. The bear case says price per test is structurally eroding. If the erosion was mix — cheaper cancer tests diluting the average — and the new launches lift content, the ASP line inverts. We do not know which it is, and neither does anyone else, because the segment-level price split has not been read.
3. **The company has ~$300M of newly contracted covered lives from payer wins.** If those convert, volume re-accelerates mechanically. Nobody can prove they will, but nobody has proven they won't either.

Note what all three have in common: they are reasons the stock could work *later*, on evidence that does not exist yet. That is not an edge you buy today. That is a reason to keep the file open.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly, most of it is.

- **The launches are public and dated.** Sell-side has the same August 17 and September 1 dates we do.
- **8.1× sales is not a distressed multiple.** If we are right and the company re-accelerates, we make money on a re-rating from cheap-ish to expensive. If we are wrong, 8× has a long way to fall — the 52-week low at $61.96 is 30% below here, inside the range this stock has actually traded in the last twelve months. The payoff is not obviously asymmetric in our favor.
- **The competitive read is the real risk and it is not priced as a certainty.** If Natera is taking share in prenatal — the exact segment that went flat — then this is not a stumble, it's the start of a trend, and 8× becomes 5×. Nothing in the current price assumes that.
- **Insider supply is open-ended.** The IPO lockup expired 2026-05-05. The Form 144 filings in the pack are a steady drumbeat (six in the eight trading days before the pack was built). There is no scarcity of stock.
- **There is no catalyst to pay us for waiting.** The next scheduled report is early November. Holding an unproven re-acceleration thesis for eleven weeks with no dated event is paying rent for nothing.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these are load-bearing.

1. **We never read the 10-Q ourselves.** The evidence pack's document excerpt is XBRL scaffolding — element tags and date contexts, no financial values. The XBRL quarterly block came back **completely empty**: revenue, stock compensation, diluted shares, and operating cash flow are all blank. Every income-statement and cash-flow figure in this deck traces to company press releases, filing aggregators, or news coverage. One bench hit a 403 from sec.gov and could not fetch the segment detail at all.
2. **Consequently: the cash, debt, and free-cash-flow figures are secondary-sourced.** The "$548.6M cash / $91.0M debt / FCF positive / no maturity until 2031" line — which is the entire reason this is a watch rather than a pass — comes from a filing aggregator's summary, not from the filing. It is probably right. It is not verified.
3. **Stock-based compensation and share dilution are completely unmeasured.** Zero data. This is a company that IPO'd nine months ago; dilution could be meaningfully changing the per-share math and we would not know. Any valuation in this deck that uses 47.19M shares is using a share count from the 10-Q cover page, which is a point-in-time count, not a diluted average.
4. **We do not know whether the reported ASP is company-wide or prenatal-only.** As noted above, this determines whether the story is "volumes are fine, price is falling" or "both are stalling." Those are very different investments and we cannot currently distinguish them.
5. **The Q3 print date is unconfirmed** and our own two internal records disagree by one day.
6. **The competitive question — is Natera actually taking prenatal share from BillionToOne? — is unanswered.** We established that Natera did well while BillionToOne did not. We did not establish causation. That is the difference between a stumble and a decline, and it is the whole investment case.
7. **Crowding is unknown.** No short interest, no ownership concentration, no read on how consensus the bear case already is.
8. **The $300M of contracted covered lives is a company claim we have not independently checked** against payer policy documents.
