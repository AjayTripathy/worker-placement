## What we believe

Finance of America is a reverse-mortgage lender. It originates loans against retirees' homes, pools them into government-guaranteed securities, keeps the servicing rights, and carries almost the whole balance sheet — about $37 billion of assets — at estimated fair value rather than cost. That accounting choice is the entire company: when the estimates move, the reported equity moves.

The idea that brought the name to us was simple. The stock is down 38% from its 52-week high and trades below its stated book value, while management has reaffirmed a full-year adjusted profit of $4.50–$5.00 per share and just bought a $5.2 billion servicing portfolio from Onity. Cheap asset, working business, temporary drawdown.

**After the court, we no longer believe that.** What we believe now is narrower and mostly negative:

1. The discount to book is real but smaller than briefed — roughly **0.83x**, not 0.79x — and it is **shrinking on its own**, because book value per share fell about 7% during the most recent quarter.
2. The company owes far more at the parent level than anyone in our process originally thought: **about $347 million of debt that the corporate entity itself is on the hook for**, against about **$85 million of cash**, from which another **$24.9 million is already committed to Onity this quarter**.
3. Management has told investors a piece of that debt gets retired in November 2026. **No filing we could reach states a maturity date, and no filing shows where the money comes from.** There is no going-concern discussion, no liquidity discussion, and — in the August 3 8-K that restructured the share classes — no Item 2.03 disclosure, which is the box a company checks when it takes on new financing. The absence is itself evidence that no new financing had been arranged as of that date.
4. Because of point 3, **the price is not the variable that decides this name.** A missing disclosure is. Until it appears, the correct size is zero at any price.

We also believe the name is worth keeping on a leash rather than throwing away. The business generates revenue, the Onity deal is real and arm's-length, and a single 8-K could convert this from un-underwritable to underwritable in an afternoon.

## What the market believes

The market's load-bearing number is the company's own adjusted earnings guidance: **$2.8–3.1 billion of origination volume and $4.50–5.00 of adjusted earnings per share for 2026**. At $18.35, the stock trades at roughly 4x the midpoint of that. That looks absurdly cheap, which is presumably why anyone owns it.

Two things about how that number reaches the market matter:

- The guidance does **not** appear in the furnished 8-K press-release exhibit. It lives in the slide deck and the earnings call ([Seeking Alpha's summary](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4625613-finance-of-america-reaffirms-2026-adjusted-eps-of-4_50-5_00-as-onity-5_2b-hecm-msr-deal)). The reaffirmation is genuine — we are not alleging anything improper — but it lives in the less-formal venue.
- The first half delivered **$1.94** of adjusted EPS. Getting to the low end of the full-year guide requires the back half to produce **$67–80 million of adjusted net income against $51 million in the first half — a 31% to 57% increase.**

The market also appears to be reading the headline earnings-per-share line as positive. In the second quarter, basic earnings per share was **+$0.10**. Diluted was **$(1.28)**. A buyer at $18.35 is economically the diluted holder, not the basic one.

Short interest is **11.07% of the float** at 4.6 days to cover, so a meaningful cohort disagrees with the cheap-stock read. But nothing we found in sell-side or short-seller commentary carries the specific findings below.

## Why we might have edge

Four findings, all from the primary documents, none of which we could locate in any outside commentary:

1. **The recourse debt stack.** Pulling the debt footnote out of the 10-Q and adding it up gives Senior Secured Notes $133.8M + Exchangeable Secured Notes $132.1M + a $20.0M promissory note + Convertible Notes carried at $61.2M = **~$347 million**. Our own initial brief, and our own first bench, both thought the figure was around $150 million. Anyone anchoring on the senior notes alone is understating the corporate obligation by more than 2x.
2. **Basic-versus-diluted basis mixing.** The name's optical profitability in Q2 comes from the parent company recognizing a one-time **$42 million tax benefit** (release of a valuation allowance against deferred tax assets) that sits at the parent rather than at the operating LLC. That pushed the reported loss disproportionately onto the non-controlling interest (**−$30.9M**) versus the controlling interest (**+$2.1M**). The +$0.10 basic figure is an artifact of where the tax benefit sits. Strip the benefit and pre-tax was roughly **−$71 million**.
3. **The missing maturity date.** Both benches independently tried and failed to find the senior notes' maturity date in any extractable filing. Management's "retired in November" is, as far as our documents go, an unsourced statement. Screens that treat it as a scheduled deleveraging catalyst are booking a forecast as a fact.
4. **The share count nobody reconciles.** The company's adjusted-EPS denominator is **26.3 million shares**. GAAP fully-exchanged shares are **17,306,890** (9,217,956 Class A + 8,088,934 Class B, per the 10-Q cover as of 2026-08-06). Our second bench closed the ~9.3 million share gap by attributing it to the exchangeable and convertible notes ($132M + $40M ÷ ~9.0M shares ≈ **$19.10/share blended**, roughly at the money). That is arithmetic, not a read of the exchange price, so we label it plausible rather than confirmed — but if it is right, fully-exchanged book is about **$22.0/share, 0.83x**, and the notes are the dilution, not the charter.

The general edge is process, not information: we read the equity roll-forward and the debt footnote rather than the press release, and we insisted on separating what management said from what a document says.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: a good deal of it probably is.

- The stock is already down 38% from its high and 11% of the float is short. A 4x adjusted-earnings multiple on a lender is not a market saying "this is fine."
- The **$347 million recourse stack is disclosed**, in a public 10-Q footnote, to anyone who opens it. It is not hidden. Our edge is that most participants in a $2.2 million-a-day stock have no economic reason to open it — that is a *neglect* edge, which is fragile and which we cannot size into anyway.
- The convertible-note fair-value mark cuts against us as much as for us. Our first bench's strongest kill — "the quarter destroyed $31.4 million of book with no buyback" — turned out to be roughly 40% an accounting reflex: **$21.2 million of that was a markup on the company's own convertible notes, which are carried at fair value based on FOA's closing stock price.** The stock rose into June 30, so the liability got written up. At $18.35, that markup mechanically unwinds. Ex-that-mark, Q2 destroyed about $10 million of book, not $31.4 million. Q3 should recapture most of the $21 million and will look better for a reason that has nothing to do with the business.
- Our sharpest structural finding — that the reported loss allocation is skewed — was, on audit, **fully explained by ordinary parent-level tax accounting**, not a governance gimmick. That is a real concession: the scandalous reading did not survive.

And the deepest problem is that our own thesis is internally inconsistent unless we pick a side. We cannot simultaneously argue that **0.79x (really 0.83x) book is cheap** and that **the fair-value marks moving that book are non-economic add-backs to be ignored**. If the marks are noise, book is noise and the valuation floor disappears. If book is real, adjusted EPS is fiction. The original thesis needed both. We can only have one. Choosing "book is real" — the more conservative branch — leaves us with an anchor that fell 7% in the operative quarter.

## What could go wrong

Assume we buy this and lose money. The three most likely stories, in order.

**Failure narrative 1 — The refinancing arrives, and it arrives as equity.** The senior notes come due, there is no funded source, and the company solves it the way a small-cap lender at 0.83x book with $85 million of cash usually solves it: a convertible or an equity raise, priced at a discount, into a 4.89-million-share float. Our discount-to-book premise is then not merely wrong but inverted — we bought the thing that gets diluted. *Tripwire exists:* any S-1, S-3, 424B5, or 8-K announcing a financing. This is monitorable, and it is a hard exit, not a dip to buy.

**Failure narrative 2 — The back half misses and the multiple was never the point.** The guide needs +31% to +57% half-over-half. A rate move, a slowdown in reverse-mortgage origination, or integration friction on the $5.2 billion Onity portfolio takes 2026 adjusted EPS to, say, $3.50, and the stock re-rates on the *earnings* axis at the same time book keeps drifting down on the *asset* axis. Both legs of the valuation fail together, which is what usually happens when a thesis has two supposedly independent supports that are actually the same balance sheet. *Tripwire exists (partially):* the Q3 print, plus a loan-officer hiring baseline we have not yet recorded and should.

**Failure narrative 3 — We lose money to something inside documents we could not read. THIS ONE HAS NO TRIPWIRE.** Two specific holes:

- The August 3 8-K carries an Item 1.01 exhibit — a **68-page agreement** filed alongside the charter and bylaw amendments. Our proxy fetch returned only the filing index. This is precisely where the **tax receivable agreement** would be restruck. A tax receivable agreement is a contractual obligation to pay pre-IPO owners a share of future tax savings in cash. It ranks ahead of ordinary shareholders and it is not in anyone's cash-need arithmetic — not ours, not either bench's, not, as far as we can tell, the market's $347 million-plus-$85 million-of-cash picture. We do not know its size. We cannot set an alert on a number we have never seen.
- The company's assets are loans secured by homes across the United States, and **the 10-Q discloses no state or county concentration table.** Hazard-driven collateral impairment feeds directly into the fair-value marks that this whole analysis turns on. There is no observable input.

**The unknown-unknown class here is: private contractual claims and undisclosed asset perimeters — obligations that are legally senior to us, economically material, and disclosed only in exhibits or aggregations that a filing-level monitor cannot parse.** We have no detector for this class. Naming it does not fix it; the honest statement is that a slice of this balance sheet is dark to us and would stay dark after we bought.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own size, our own flow, our own rules.**

- **We cannot get out.** Average daily volume is 119,051 shares, about **$2.18 million a day**. Our publishing rule caps us at 1% of that: roughly **1,190 shares, or $21,800 per day.** Building even a $500,000 position takes about 23 trading sessions. So does unwinding it. If the funding gate resolves badly on day 11 of a build, we are holding a half-built position in a stock with 11% short interest and 4.6 days to cover, trying to sell into exactly the tape that just repriced. The illiquidity is not a kill of the idea — it is a cap on how much of the idea we are allowed to own, and it makes the idea's maximum contribution to the book small enough to ask whether it is worth the attention at all.
- **We would be laddering into someone else's supply.** Bloom Retirement Holdings has a Goldman 10b5-1 program for up to **650,000 Class A shares**, begun 2026-06-08 and amended by the 2026-08-06 SCHEDULE 13D/A, with only about 95,204 disposed. The residual ~555,000 shares is **about 11% of the 4.89 million float** — materially bigger, relative to float, than the "~7% of Class A" framing our brief used. Programmatic selling is not a reason to refuse to buy, but on a 1,190-share-a-day ladder we would be the marginal bid absorbing it, and we should not tell ourselves we are being paid a dislocation premium when we are simply on the other side of a scheduled seller.
- **Doctrine interaction:** there is a November 3 earnings date on the calendar. Our rule against resting orders into a print applies — leaving a good-till-cancelled bid out over that date is an unintentional bet that nothing happens. Any re-entry must be a price alert that pages us plus a same-session worked order, never a resting order.
- **Doctrine interaction, the other way:** our default says a FLAT verdict on a clean court requires a *named* kill, and we should grade our own silences. We have named kills — the ~$347 million recourse stack and the undisclosed, unfunded November obligation. FLAT is licensed here, not lazy.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___** that the November senior-note obligation gets refinanced with equity or an equity-linked instrument priced at a discount to a 4.89-million-share float, so the book-value discount we bought is transferred to new paper rather than to us.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry. Below are the reopen conditions, not entry levels.** Nothing here authorizes an order today.

**Hard prerequisite — non-negotiable, and it is not a price.** Size is **zero at any price** until a primary document discloses the senior secured notes' **maturity date and a funded repayment source**. Acceptable evidence: an 8-K with Item 2.03 (or an Item 1.01 credit agreement), a 10-Q subsequent-event note, or a filed refinancing commitment. Management statements on a call do not clear this gate. Both benches converged on this independently.

**Path 1 — funding gate clears favorably (refinanced or extended on disclosed terms).**
- Reference value: **fully-exchanged book ≈ $22.00/share** ($406.7M equity + $172M of exchangeable/convertible debt ÷ 26.3M shares). This is the conservative anchor; the simpler 17.3M-share figure of $23.50 is the generous one, and we use the conservative one because the more-diluted count is what a buyer at today's price actually becomes.

- **Add band: at or below $15.40** (0.70x anchor), and **only after** the Q3 10-Q shows total equity of **at least ~$425 million excluding buybacks and dividends**, with the convertible-note fair-value mark disclosed separately so the earnings-to-book test is not contaminated by the share price again. **Path 2 — funding gate does not clear by the November 3 print.** Size stays zero. In particular, **a break of the 52-week low at $15.84 with the gate still open is confirmation of the bear case, not an entry** — that is the market pricing a refinancing problem, and buying it would be the classic error of treating our own unresolved question as someone else's overreaction.
