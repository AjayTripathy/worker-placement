# PITCH — FNF — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Hold the existing 210 shares (break-even, avg $48.005 vs ~$48 tape) as an *unclassified* position — the "fairly-paid carry" label is REJECTED by both benches until the Q2 10-Q is read as a primary source; no adds, no buy band, no forced exit.**

## What we believe

Fidelity National Financial is the largest US title insurer, with a majority stake (~72%) in the publicly traded annuity company F&G. The original thesis held that the stock, down about 16–19% from its 52-week high after a soft Q2 report, was a fairly priced dividend carry: a ~4.3% yield described as "covered at ~38% of adjusted earnings," a title business trading at a sum-of-the-parts stub of roughly 9× earnings versus rival First American at 10×, with a September Fed cut as the volume catalyst and an October FHFA title-waiver update as the dated risk event.

After the adversarial review, we no longer believe the "fairly priced" part is *established*. We still believe the franchise and dividend are real — the market's consensus is Buy with an average target of $61.40, F&G upstreams roughly $94M/year of actual cash dividends to the parent, and GAAP payout is 48% — but every load-bearing valuation leg turned out to be unverified, and each unverified leg happened to lean in the thesis's favor. What survives is a hold, not a conviction.

## What the market believes

The market is not pricing the bear case. Sell-side consensus is Buy with an average price target of $61.40, about 28% above the current price. The stock sits mid-band — 16% off its 52-week high but 13% above its 52-week low — which is drift after a disappointing quarter, not a dislocation. The Q2 report ([issuer release, primary](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fnf-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-302843960.html)) showed GAAP revenue of $4.051B and GAAP EPS of $1.08, and contained only positive forward language ("exceptionally well positioned…"). The prevailing view appears to be: rate cuts eventually revive transaction volumes, the dividend is safe, and the FHFA title-waiver pilot (which runs to November 30, 2027) is a contained experiment on refinance loans.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: after this court, less than we thought. What genuinely remains:

- **The dividend-coverage framing is misleading in a way the street repeats.** "38% of adjusted EPS" counts 72% of F&G's earnings that mostly do not reach the parent as cash. Real parent-level arithmetic: ~$552–555M/year of dividends against $457M of holding-company cash (under 10 months of cover) plus ~$94M/year of actual F&G dividends. If the title subsidiaries' regulator-approved dividend capacity (in the unread 10-Q) is tight, the market's "safe yield" assumption is softer than it looks. This cuts both ways — it is currently an argument for caution, but a clean 10-Q reading would *upgrade* the position.
- **The rate-catalyst skepticism.** The precedent is documented: the Fed cut 100bps between September and December 2024 and the 30-year mortgage rate *rose* from 6.08% to 7.01% by January 2025, because mortgage rates key off the 10-year Treasury and MBS spreads, not the federal funds rate. If the street is holding this name for a September-cut volume recovery, that catalyst can fire and deliver nothing.
- **The blue bench found something both original benches missed:** F&G has a 6.875% Series A *mandatory* convertible preferred outstanding ([F&G dividend press release, primary](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fg-annuities--life-declares-dividends-on-common-and-preferred-stock-302844332.html)). Mandatory conversion will mechanically dilute FNF's stake below 72%, which shrinks both the sum-of-the-parts math and the future dividend stream from F&G. Terms and timing are in the unread filings. We may be early to this detail.

## Why it might be priced in

- The stock already fell ~16% from its high; the market has repriced the soft quarter once.
- Consensus is Buy at $61.40 — meaning the professional coverage that has read the 10-Q we haven't apparently doesn't see a solvency or coverage problem.
- The FHFA waiver threat is publicly reported ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fannie-title-waiver-pilot/), [National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/pulte-says-title-waiver-pilot-expanding-new-study-warns-of-risk)) and has been in the price for months; it is an open-ended tail everyone can see, not private information.
- The F&G cash-versus-consolidated-earnings distinction is standard insurance-holding-company analysis; any careful reader of the statutory filings gets there.

1. **Read the Q2 10-Q (filed 2026-08-06) as primary.** Reopen a buy band only if all three hold: regulator-approved dividend capacity of the title subsidiaries ≥ ~$600M/yr; debt at the holding company confirmed separable from F&G's; claims-reserve development flat or favorable.
2. **Rebuild the sum-of-the-parts on a seasonality-weighted full-year 2026 title estimate** (not a single-quarter annualization). Reopen only if the title stub still comes out at or below ~10× the First American equivalent.
