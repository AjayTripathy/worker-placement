# PITCH — JMIA — PROPOSED VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY at $6.23. Reject the buy case at the current price; keep Jumia on an unarmed watch and re-run the court only when one of four reopen triggers fires (red bench kill-at-price sustained at 8/10; blue bench overturned the "never own this" posture, 7/10).**

## What we believe
Jumia (Jumia Technologies AG, the pan-African e-commerce marketplace, NYSE: JMIA) is a genuinely improving business at a price we cannot justify paying today. Orders grew 28% and quarterly active customers 23% on an adjusted basis in Q2 2026, gross profit improved on both measures the company reports (14.2% of merchandise volume vs 13.3% a year ago; 59% of revenue), and the quarterly loss shrank 36% year over year. On August 12 the company raised $50 million at $5.52 per ADS — half from the International Finance Corporation (a World Bank Group institution), half from Axian (already one of its largest shareholders) and other investors — leaving roughly $96 million of pro-forma liquidity ([company 6-K, August 12, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756708/000092963826003081/0000929638-26-003081-index.htm) is the neighboring filing; the raise terms are in 6-K 0001756708-26-000025, quoted in our evidence pack).

But the same evidence cuts the other way. Management cut full-year merchandise-volume growth guidance from 27–32% to 20–30% — the damage is arriving now, not receding. Liquidity fell from $77.8M to $48.3M in six months, this is the second capital raise in twelve months, and on delivered cash consumption another raise before breakeven is the base case. Our conclusion: real operational progress, wrong price, and a resolvable set of unknowns that a later court can settle.

## What the market believes
The stock closed at $6.23 — down 58% from its 52-week high of $14.72 and only 9.5% above its 52-week low of $5.69, so the market is already treating this as a distressed, serially-dilutive name. Yet the stock rose about 7.7% on the Q2 report and raise announcement, meaning the marginal buyer read "IFC validation plus funded runway" as good news. The market price sits 12.9% above the $5.52 placement price. This is a thinly covered stock; we should not assume the current price reflects careful analysis in either direction.

## Why we might have edge
1. **We read the actual filings, not the headlines.** The unregistered nature of the new shares (sold under exemptions from registration, per the 6-K) means the raise closing in late August adds *no* selling pressure to the open market — a fact the original thesis got backwards and that casual readers will also get backwards, in both directions.
2. **We ran the cash-flow bridge nobody quotes.** Q2 operating cash outflow was $11.8M including a $3.0M working-capital swing; excluding working capital it was ~$8.8M, nearly matching the $8.7M adjusted-EBITDA loss. The scary-looking gap between liquidity burn and reported losses is mostly timing, not a structural hole — a nuance that separates us from both the bulls (who ignore burn) and the bears (who overstate it; our own red bench did exactly this).
3. **We identified the specific seasonal risk.** The full-year guide only works if Q4 repeats last year's strong sequential improvement — and Q4 is the electronics-heavy Black Friday quarter, while the company itself discloses that smartphone supply disruption from memory-chip price inflation "persists into the early third quarter" with no company-controlled end date. That linkage (guide rescue depends on the exact quarter the shortage impairs) appears nowhere in public commentary we've seen.
4. **We know what $5.52 is and isn't.** It is a conventional ~5% discount to the pre-announcement price of ~$5.81, negotiated with a development-finance buyer that also bought an ESG policy package. It is not an informed-investor verdict on fair value, in either direction.

## Why it might be priced in
Symmetry requires saying this plainly: at 9.5% above the 52-week low, the market may already have done our work. The guidance cut is public. The dilution (~7.3%, verified from the 13G/A share count) is public. The second-raise-in-a-year pattern is visible to anyone. IFC's participation — whatever discount it came at — is a real signal that a diligence-capable institution with NDA access to the Q2 numbers committed $25 million. Axian, the largest-shareholder class, added rather than sold. And the operational acceleration (orders, customers, gross profit) is real and disclosed; if Q4 supply normalizes, the current price could look cheap in hindsight. Our rejection is about the price *today* against unresolved risks, not a claim that the market is wrong to see improvement.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS
**The courts say no entry at any price today.** No buy order, no resting limit order. Specifically rejected: a standing order at $5.52 — the benches showed the only realistic path to that fill is bad news (a Q3 miss, a naira/Egyptian-pound devaluation, the supply shortage extending), at which point $5.52 would no longer be the right price. Buying there blindly means buying precisely when the thesis has gotten worse.

**Reopen bands — any ONE of these reopens the court; none of them is itself a buy signal:**
1. **Price at or below ~$5.50**, *only if* we can establish the decline was not caused by thesis-worsening news (no Q3 miss, no currency devaluation, no shortage extension driving it). A general-market selloff dragging JMIA down qualifies; a Jumia-specific bad headline does not.
2. **A registration statement is filed** for the new shares (watch for F-1/F-3 or a 6-K announcing one). This converts the undated future-selling overhang into a dated, analyzable supply event.
3. **Q3 results show operating cash flow (excluding working capital) converging with the adjusted-EBITDA loss** — confirming the burn picture blue bench sketched and red bench named as its own mind-changer.
4. **A new 13G/13D shows accumulation above $6 dated after August 12** — an informed buyer paying up post-results, which the August filing turned out not to be.

**Sizing:** 0% of book now and at any reopen until a fresh court passes. **Catalyst dates:** raise expected to close second half of August 2026 (no exact date disclosed; no market-supply effect expected). Next earnings ~November 11, 2026 — that date is derived from data-vendor patterns and is NOT company-confirmed; a company press release naming it is itself reopen-relevant.
