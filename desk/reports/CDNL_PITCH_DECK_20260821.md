# PITCH — CDNL — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position and no order at $43.50; both benches land FLAT, and the only entry either would defend is a gated starter at or below the insiders' own buy price (≤$36, red's looser version ≤$38), conditioned on the Q3 print.

---

## What we believe

Cardinal Infrastructure Group (CDNL) is a civil site-work and paving contractor built by acquisition — it grades land, lays pipe, and paves for homebuilders, developers and municipalities, mostly in North Carolina and Georgia. It went public on 2025-12-11, sold more stock on 2026-06-24 at $73.00 raising $319.0M, and seven weeks later cut its profit-margin outlook. The stock is $43.50, down 54.9% from its 52-week high of $96.40 and up 97.9% from its low of $21.98.

Our belief, after both benches finished, is narrower and less dramatic than where we started:

1. **The margin damage is real but smaller than the crash implies, and it is concentrated in the legacy business, not the acquisitions.** The company raised its revenue outlook from $665–678M to $880–900M while cutting its margin outlook from "20%+" to 16–18% ([Q2 PR, 2026-08-11](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cardinal-infrastructure-group-inc-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-and-updates-2026-outlook-announces-acquisition-of-allied-paving-302847652.html)). Do the subtraction: the *added* revenue is being guided to carry only about 3–13% profit margin (~7.8% at the midpoints), against management's own statement that the newly-bought Allied Paving runs at 20.3%. If the acquisitions really are that profitable, then arithmetic forces the entire cut onto the original business — implying roughly 16.4% there versus the "20%+" claimed, a ~360bp cut to the core.
2. **The "roll-ups dilute margin" defence does not hold up against this company's own record.** FY2024 was 17.9% and FY2025 was 17.9% — identical — on revenue that grew 45% through heavy acquisitions ([FY25 PR, 2026-03-19](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cardinal-infrastructure-group-inc-announces-full-year-2025-results-and-affirms-2026-guidance-302718146.html), $456.0M revenue / $81.5M adjusted EBITDA). They absorbed a year of deals with zero dilution. So "mix" is not a sufficient explanation for what happened this year.
3. **Cash generation is being flattered by paying suppliers late.** First-half operating cash flow was +$22.0M, but the change in accounts payable alone contributed +$39.6M. Strip that out and operating cash flow was **−$17.7M**, versus +$9.3M on the same basis a year ago. After $34.0M of equipment spending, free cash flow was **−$12.0M**. Site-work contractors stretch their subcontractors before they admit to a schedule problem.
4. **The cash on the balance sheet is not free cash — it is acquisition currency.** $339.1M of cash sits against $133.4M already spent on deals in the first half and roughly $120M committed to Allied Paving in October ($62M cash + $58M stock).
5. **The company raised equity at $73.00 six days before the quarter closed, then cut guidance seven weeks later.** That is now under plaintiff-firm investigation ([Hagens Berman, 2026-08-14](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/14/3345060/32716/en/hagens-berman-national-securities-law-firm-announces-investigation-into-cardinal-infrastructure-group-inc-nasdaq-cdnl-following-post-offering-stock-plunge.html)). Our standing rule is that a name with an unresolved integrity question can never be paid full fair value for.
6. **There is a large, structural supply of stock waiting.** 27,044,449 Class B units — 57% of the company — are held by pre-IPO owners and convertible into ordinary shares. The IPO lockup expired and the follow-on priced 195 days after the IPO, immediately afterwards. Separately, each conversion creates a new payment obligation to those same holders under a tax-sharing agreement; that obligation has already grown from $39.4M to $47.2M in six months and is left out of most published valuations of the company.

**Net: this is a cheaper-than-it-looks business with a real, quantified profit problem in its core, a stretched working-capital position, and an unresolved question about what management knew when it sold stock. We do not think it is uninvestable. We think it is not investable *at this price*.**

## What the market believes

The market cut the stock roughly 26% on the guidance day and 55% from the high. The prevailing narrative is: a hot infrastructure IPO oversold its margins, the roll-up is buying lower-quality revenue, the raise was opportunistic, and litigation risk is now attached. Insiders bought 34,000 shares on 2026-08-14 at $36.59 and $37.73 — genuine open-market purchases, not pre-scheduled — and the stock has since traded up to $43.50, which suggests some buyers read that cluster as a floor.

**We could not find a published analyst estimate for second-half or full-year 2026 profit with a range attached.** Both benches tried and failed. CDNL IPO'd with an underwriting syndicate, so coverage almost certainly exists; this is a retrieval failure on our side, not proof of an absent consensus. Until we close it, **neither we nor anyone here may claim "the market is wrong"** — we have no external number to be different from.

## Why we might have edge

- **The incremental-margin subtraction.** Nobody in our evidence record — not the company's presentation, not the plaintiff release — computes the profit margin on the *added* revenue. It is one subtraction, and it converts a vague "mix" story into a specific ~360bp cut to the original business. This is the strongest original piece of work in the whole court.
- **The two-year flat-17.9% record as a rebuttal.** The mix defence is refutable from the company's own prior filings, and we found the refutation.
- **The supplier-payment stretch, correctly located.** Our first attempt blamed the wrong line item (unbilled work) — and when we scaled it to revenue, that tell vanished (8.3% of revenue vs 7.6%, essentially unchanged). The real stretch is on the payables side (10.1% of revenue vs 3.8%). Getting the mechanism right matters, because it tells us what to watch next quarter.
- **Counting the tax-sharing obligation in the valuation.** $47.2M of it, compounding, excluded from every enterprise value in the record except ours.
- **Sizing the insider buy honestly.** $8.5M sounds like conviction until you note it is ~0.7% of the same insiders' ~$1.18B of untraded stock.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The stock already fell 55%.** Two of our surviving objections — "roll-ups dilute margin" and "they raised money before bad news" — are exactly what the tape has been pricing for two weeks. Those are consensus observations, not edge.
- **At the company's own guidance the multiple is not demanding.** Enterprise value $1.982B against guided profit of $140.8–162.0M is **12.2–14.1x**. Cheap-ish for infrastructure services, if you believe the guide.
- **The 16–18% guide may not be a real cut at all.** "20%+" was never an achieved number — the company printed 17.9% in both public years. The new range brackets what it actually does. The market may have punished the loss of an aspiration.
- **The second-half hurdle is smaller than the headline suggests** (see the table — our own bench got this wrong first). Against last year's second half at 17.6%, the low end of the guide requires roughly no year-over-year improvement at all.
- **Insiders bought, and they did not sell into the June offering** — that deal was primary, i.e. new shares from the company, not insider stock.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry here. These are the reopen conditions, taken from the benches' own arithmetic.**

**No buy at $43.50.** You would be paying 15–19% above the only informed bid in the record (the insiders at $36.59–$37.73), five sessions after they bid.

**Reopen band — primary (blue bench, the stricter):** limit **≤$36.00**, i.e. at or beneath the insider basis rather than at the top of it, **and both** of the following true at the Q3 report:
- Q3 adjusted profit margin **≥15%**
- Accounts payable **back under 6% of revenue**

**Reopen band — looser (red bench):** **≤$38.00** on valuation alone. We flag the disagreement rather than splitting it: red's is a price-only gate, blue's adds the two operating conditions. **We propose blue's.** Red's own conviction that FLAT beats a gated starter was only 4/10, so it is not a strong dissent.

For scale, at those levels the enterprise value falls to roughly **$1.63B at $36** and **$1.72B at $38** (our arithmetic, from the bench inputs), i.e. ~10.8x and ~11.4x the midpoint of company guidance, or ~14.8x and ~15.6x on our lower organic estimate of ~$110M.

**Do-not-enter conditions (either bench would abandon the name, not just the band):**
- Q3 adjusted margin **below 15%** — that breaks the 17.6% prior-year second-half baseline and makes the problem structural rather than seasonal
- A second quarter of payables above 9% of revenue *with* receivables above 8% — that turns a data point into a liquidity concern
- Any 8-K disclosing a covenant amendment on the October 2025 credit facility
- The ALGC purchase-accounting adjustment (10-Q Note 3, the restatement context dated 2026-02-18 — **still unread**) turning out to have written *down* acquired backlog or customer relationships, which would mean the acquired-margin story was mismarked at closing

**Toward the bull side:** a Charlotte project-restart disclosure with dollars attached, plus Q3 gross margin ≥13.9%.

**Sizing.** Starter only, no adds before the Q3 print. **Neither bench put a percentage on "starter" — that is a gap.** Our proposal, stated as our assumption and not theirs: **0.5% of book at first fill, 1.0% maximum in the name.** The standing rule for a name with an unresolved integrity question caps what we may pay at 0.85× expected fair value — **but neither bench published a fair-value point estimate, so that cap cannot currently be applied arithmetically.** The price gates above are doing the work the cap is supposed to do. Closing the fair-value number is an open assignment.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **2026-11-12 — Q3 report. UNCONFIRMED.** Derived from a data vendor; no company release names it and the investor-relations page timed out for both benches. ~57 trading days out. This is the decisive date for both reopen conditions.
- **October 2026 — Allied Paving expected to close** (~$120M; $62M cash leaves the balance sheet, ~1.3M shares issued). Exact date not disclosed in our record.
- **2026-10-15 — our own hiring re-scan**, comparing *open requisitions* (baseline: Raleigh 50 open reqs as of 2026-08-21), not monthly posting counts, which we now know are an artifact.
- **Undated but live:** conversion of any part of the 27.04M pre-IPO units into ordinary shares — new supply, and a new increment to the tax-sharing obligation each time.

## What remains unverified

1. **No external estimate to compare ourselves against.** We could not retrieve a published second-half or full-year 2026 profit estimate with a range. Our lower number (~$110–124M) therefore has nothing to be measured against. This caps confidence in the whole exercise and forbids any "the market has this wrong" framing.
2. **The 2025 quarterly split is not public.** We know the second half of 2025 averaged 17.6% but not whether that was a strong Q3 and weak Q4 or the reverse. So the claim "Q3 only has to avoid repeating Q2" is **unproven** — it may be an easy comparison or a hard one.
3. **The payables stretch is real but unsized.** On revenue that roughly doubled, the correct measure is days-payable on cost of sales, not payables as a share of revenue. Neither bench computed it. Acquired companies' payables also enter the cash-flow statement only after closing, which muddies the comparison.
4. **The insider purchase attribution** ($8.54M total, $38.54 average) comes from an aggregator. The primary filing we read shows an attorney-in-fact signature; we verified the transactions, not the person.
5. **The Q3 report date is a vendor guess.**
6. **The management explanation — severe Georgia weather — is ungraded.** We have no site-level rainfall or job-site data and no tool in our kit that reads it. The one connector that matched on "weather-dependent demand" reads snowpack at ski resorts. Reported as a coverage gap.
7. **No job-site or asphalt-plant location is disclosed**, so every satellite-imagery, thermal, and workplace-safety check is unrunnable. The asphalt plant is the largest unquantified item in management's second-half bridge and we cannot independently confirm it exists as an operating site.
8. **No permit data exists in our kit for Wake or Mecklenburg County, NC** — which is exactly the feed that would test the Charlotte restart question directly.
9. **Two assignments remain unopened:** the ALGC purchase-accounting adjustment note, and the pre-IPO payout question.
10. **No fair-value estimate has been published by either bench**, so the 0.85× cap on what we may pay is currently unenforceable as arithmetic.
