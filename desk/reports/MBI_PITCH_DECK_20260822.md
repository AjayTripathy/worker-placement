# PITCH — MBI — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — a 0.25% starter in MBIA Inc. at ≤$5.10 (tape $4.90), sized as a cheap option on National's capital converting to cash faster than a decade; no order stages until FABLE adjudicates, because the red bench's REJECT (8/10 conviction) was wrong on structure but right that nothing dated resolves this year.

## What we believe

MBIA Inc. is not an insurance company anymore. It is a holding company whose only real asset is a subsidiary — National Public Finance Guarantee — that stopped writing new business and is now just collecting premiums on old municipal bond policies and paying the occasional claim. National holds **$968M of regulatory (statutory) capital**. The parent has **$228M of its own bonds** outstanding and burns roughly **$40M a year** keeping the lights on.

Subtract the parent's debt from National's capital and you get **$740M, or $14.51 per share**, against a stock at **$4.90**. That is the whole idea: the market is paying 34 cents for a dollar of capital sitting in a subsidiary.

We do *not* believe that dollar is worth a dollar today. Getting it out is slow. New York insurance law caps what National can pay up to the parent each year, and our own bench put that number at roughly **$50–97M/year** — call it a decade. Discount the $740M over five years at 12% and net out the parent's annual burn and you get about **$275M, or $5.39/share**.

So the honest statement of the belief is narrower than "70% upside": **the stock at $4.90 is priced for a five-plus-year grind to full value, and we think there is a real, non-trivial chance it happens in two to three instead** — via a sale of National, an extraordinary dividend approved by the New York regulator, or a Puerto Rico recovery that adds cash on top. Any one of those re-rates the stock toward $7.35+. None of them has a date.

## What the market believes

Two things, and they are consistent with each other.

**First, that the clock is long and uncertain.** At $4.90 the market is implicitly agreeing with the five-year-at-12% math almost exactly. That is not despair; it is patience priced correctly.

**Second, that the balance sheet is scary.** MBIA reports **negative book value of −$45.52 per share** and **$3.6 billion of debt** against a $250M market value. Most screens stop there. The reason for the negative book is a separate, older subsidiary — MBIA Insurance Corp — whose "surplus notes" have been accruing interest at roughly SOFR + 11.5% since the regulator blocked all payments on them in January 2013. Roughly **$953M of principal and ~$1.6B of unpaid interest** now sit on the consolidated balance sheet ([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/814585/000119312526076834/mbi-20251231.htm)).

The two sell-side analysts who still cover the name both have **$7 price targets** (KBW Buy, cut from $7.50; Roth MKM Hold), with forecast losses of −$0.50 and −$0.45 per share in 2026 and 2027. So the professional view is "worth more, earns nothing, no timeline."

## Why we might have edge

**1. The scary number is in the wrong pocket.** MBIA Insurance Corp — where the $2.6B of accruing notes live — is a *sister* company to National, not its parent. National has been a direct subsidiary of MBIA Inc. through National Holdings since the [2009 restructuring](https://investor.mbia.com/investor-relations/press-releases/press-release-details/2009/MBIA-Establishes-Separate-Public-Finance-Bond-Insurance-Company-and-Advances-Transformation-Strategy/default.aspx). The Q2 filing says it plainly: *"the absence of any cross defaults between the entities and the lack of reliance by MBIA Inc. on MBIA Corp. for dividends."* The notes are also subordinated and legally unpayable without regulatory approval that has not come in thirteen years. They cannot reach into National's $968M. A screen that reads consolidated debt of $3.6B against a $250M market cap sees a zombie; the actual claim standing between us and National's capital is $228M, not $2.8B.

**2. The number that scares people compounds mechanically, which makes it look like deterioration when nothing is happening.** Consolidated long-term debt rose from $2,843M to $2,915M in the first half of 2026 with **zero new borrowing**. That is just unpaid interest accruing in a ring-fenced box. Anyone running a "rising debt" filter gets a false positive every single quarter, forever.

**3. There is a second, quieter channel around the dividend cap.** The 10-K discloses an **Advances Agreement** under which National may lend to MBIA Inc. at SOFR + 0.512%. Neither bench sized it. If it is material, the "ten-year dividend ladder" is not the only route cash takes upstairs.

**4. A price is about to become observable where none existed.** In July 2026, roughly **$30M of National's Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority bankruptcy claims were transferred to a custodian in exchange for tradeable receipts**. That is ~8% of the remaining claim. When those receipts print, we get an outside, arms-length mark on what PREPA claims are worth — a number nobody currently has.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: mostly, it is.

- **The discount is not egregious.** $4.90 against a $5.39 five-year-conversion value is a 9% discount. That is not a mispricing; that is a fair price for a slow asset. The upside case requires the *timeline* to compress, and we have no evidence it will.
- **Two analysts at $7 is a +43% gap to the tape, but two analysts is not a market.** Our blue bench called red's "we agree with the street" a coverage argument rather than a pricing argument, and that is fair — but it cuts both ways. Thin coverage means neither the $7 nor the $4.90 is a well-tested number.
- **Management is not buying.** There is a **$71M buyback authorization and zero shares repurchased**, at a price 3.8% off the 52-week low and roughly a quarter of National's statutory capital. If the people who read National's statutory statements every month thought $4.90 was 34 cents on the dollar, this is the cheapest possible way to say so. They aren't saying it. (Partial defense: they paid **$46M** of PREPA claims on 7/1/26 and have a **$95M** bond maturing in 2027.)
- **Nothing dated resolves.** See below — the one calendar entry everyone points at is not a decision date.
- **The stock is 40.7% off its 52-week high and 3.8% off its low.** Being cheap is not a catalyst, and the tape says the market has been getting *less* interested, not more.

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — The clock wins (most likely)

Nothing bad happens and we still lose. No sale of National, no extraordinary dividend, ordinary dividends of $50–97M/year that roughly cover the parent's $40M annual burn and the 2027/2028 bond maturities and nothing else. Parent liquidity has already gone from **$357M to $337M**. Five years from now the stock is $5.20 and we've earned nothing while the market compounded. This is the boring, high-probability outcome and it is what the current price says.

**Tripwire (monitorable):** parent-company liquidity line in each 10-Q. Below $250M, the "patient" thesis becomes a "forced" thesis. Also: National statutory capital declining two consecutive quarters.

### Failure narrative 2 — Puerto Rico goes the other way

The Title III bankruptcy court **denied** the bondholders' administrative-expense claim motion on **3/16/26**. National is the party appealing a loss. If the First Circuit affirms, the PREPA recovery assumption — worth on the order of **$150M**, or roughly $3/share of the upside case — comes out. And it may come out slowly: partial adverse rulings, remands, another two years. Meanwhile National paid out **$11M (1/1/26) and $46M (7/1/26)** in claims this year — cash leaving while the recovery argument is unresolved.

**Tripwire (monitorable):** First Circuit docket for case activity after the 9/15/26 argument; the custodial receipt prints. If the receipts trade well below ~$0.60 on the dollar, the recovery leg is impaired and we should hear it from the tape before we hear it from the court.

### Failure narrative 3 — The regulator simply never says yes (NO TRIPWIRE)

The New York Department of Financial Services decides — for reasons of institutional caution, a change of superintendent, or a policy view about municipal-insurance capital that never appears in writing — that National's capital stays where it is. No extraordinary dividend, no approval of a sale, no order, no filing, no press release. The stock drifts down for years as the market slowly concludes the capital is trapped, and there is no single day on which we could have known.

**We cannot monitor this.** There is no docket, no form, no disclosure obligation attached to a regulator *declining* to act. The precedent is sitting in this very company: NYSDFS has withheld approval of surplus-note interest payments **since January 2013** — thirteen years — and no filing ever announced that decision; it is inferable only from the absence of payments.

**Unknown-unknown class: discretionary regulatory posture with no disclosure obligation.** Sub-classes we would also be blind to: a change in the NY superintendent or the state's political posture toward insured-muni capital; a confidential supervisory letter restricting National; a private determination that National's single-risk-limit non-compliance (real, and confirmed in the filing) matters more than the filing's stated consequence implies.

### The reflexive / structural leg

- **Our size is irrelevant to the market and that is a warning, not a comfort.** At the desk's current NAV, 0.25% is roughly **$2,600 — about 530 shares**. Against MBI's daily volume that is a rounding error. We cannot move this stock and we cannot exit into a gap either way; whatever happens to the price is entirely other people's flow. **Caveat: MBI's average daily volume is UNVERIFIED — IBKR market data was permission-denied to both benches, so this is an inference from a $250M market cap, not a measurement.**
- **We are not bidding into our own predicted flow.** Nothing about this position creates the flow it depends on.
- **We may be over-exposed to Puerto Rico across sleeves.** The desk runs a municipal sleeve. If that sleeve holds Puerto Rico paper or insured municipal bonds, MBI is not a diversifying position — it is the *same* First Circuit ruling, levered. **This has not been checked** and must be before any order stages.
- **Our own doctrine may be manufacturing this position.** The desk's "clean-court minimum-starter" rule says that walking away requires a *named damage kill*, and the blue bench explicitly invoked it: no bench named damage, therefore starter. But red's case was never "this company is impaired" — it was "the conversion takes a decade and nothing dated resolves." Our rule has no clean category for *time* as a kill, so timing objections get converted into "size it smaller" rather than "don't." A rule that can only ever output a starter is not a filter. This deck should be adjudicated with that pressure named out loud.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that National's capital never converts on a tradeable timeline — the regulator quietly never approves, no buyer emerges, and we spend years holding a correctly-priced slow asset while the discount we thought we were buying turns out to have been the fair cost of waiting.**
