# PITCH — TRIP — VERDICT

ADJUDICATED 2026-08-08 — **DECLINE: no long, no short, no position.** The cheapness that made this interesting was an arithmetic error; the profit that remains sits inside a business the company itself says is being taken apart by AI search results, and the "growth" business is fed by that same shrinking traffic.

---

## What we believe

Tripadvisor looks statistically cheap and is not. Three things are true at once, and together they close the case.

**1. The profit number that made it look cheap was roughly 58% too high.** The idea that arrived on our desk was: about $500M of enterprise value against about $300M a year of profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — under two times. That $300M came from taking the June quarter ($76.4M) and multiplying by four. But the June quarter is the seasonal peak. The company's own first-half number for continuing operations was $92.8M, which means the March quarter was $16.4M — the June quarter was 4.7x the March quarter. Using the company's real historical seasonal split (first half = 48.1% of the year), the honest full-year 2026 figure is about **$193M**, not $305M. The "under two times" multiple does not exist; it is closer to three times, and on cash that actually belongs to shareholders it is seven to eight times.

**2. That $193M is falling fast, and the fall is accelerating.** First half 2026 was $92.8M against $143.5M a year earlier — down 35%. The Hotels and Other segment (the old core Tripadvisor site) went from $315M of segment profit in 2023 to $254M in 2024 to $207M in 2025, and is down 32% in the first half of 2026. Revenue there fell 21% in the June quarter. No sub-line inside it is growing: hotels −23%, advertising −12%, other −20%.

**3. The company tells you why, in writing.** From the 10-Q filed 2026-08-06 and the 2025 annual report: traffic loss from "**AI overviews displacing top ranked links, reduced click-through rates and a shift towards platform based non-traditional search**," and separately that Google "frequently promotes its own competing products in its search results, which has negatively impacted placement of references to our company." This is a distribution channel being withdrawn by a counterparty who is replacing it with their own answers. It is not a cycle.

**The part that turns a cheap melting asset into a decline:** the Experiences business (Viator) is not an independent growth engine sitting next to the melting one. It runs on the melting one's traffic. Free search traffic used to feed it; that traffic is disappearing, so the demand now has to be bought. You can watch it happen in the numbers: Experiences marketing spend went from 56.2% of that segment's revenue in Q2 2025 to 61.3% in Q2 2026 (first half: 60.6% → 64.8%), while revenue growth collapsed from 13% to 3%. In the June quarter, an extra $18.6M of marketing bought an extra $8.1M of revenue — **$2.30 spent for every $1 gained**. That is not investment; it is paying to stand still.

There is one genuinely good decision here: selling TheFork to American Express Travel for $700M cash. TheFork made $118.5M of revenue and $2.0M of operating income in the first half — effectively nothing. Selling a non-earner for roughly three times revenue is a good trade, with a $35M fee payable to Tripadvisor if the deal dies on antitrust grounds. We just don't think it changes the answer.

## What the market believes

The market is not confused; it is pricing this roughly correctly, which is the problem. At about $10.23 the stock is down 49% from its 52-week high of $20.16 and 13.5% off its low of $9.01. The bull case that other people hold is:

- $700M of cash arriving into a roughly $1.2B company by year-end, which "must" be returned.
- The dual-class share structure and the Liberty TripAdvisor overhang are gone — a single class of shares, no controlling holder, and the last two Liberty-affiliated directors stepped off the board in March 2026.
- An activist investor is on the register, and management says $85M of annual cost savings are landing in 2026.
- Experiences is the "largest, most durable growth category in travel" (the CEO's words) and the company is reshaping around it.

The market is not wrong that all of those facts are facts. It is that none of them survive contact with the profit trajectory.

## Why we might have edge

Four places where careful reading beats the screen:

1. **The seasonality trap.** Anyone annualizing the June quarter gets a number 58% too high. This is the single most common way this name gets mispriced *upward* in a screen, and it is exactly how it reached us.
2. **The segment redefinition.** In late 2025 Tripadvisor changed how it reports segments. On the old basis, Viator was charged internally for the Tripadvisor traffic it consumed (about $134–135M a year). On the new basis that charge is gone. Same $840M of 2024 revenue, but the reported margin goes from 3.9% to 9.4%, and 2023 goes from 0.0% to positive. Total company profit is unchanged — $334M old basis, $333M new basis. Profit moved between boxes; none was created. Very few people re-derive this.
3. **What management does versus what it says.** Tripadvisor paid about $16.27 a share to buy in Liberty TripAdvisor's stake in April 2025 and $14.22 for a further $40M in the open market. It has bought **zero** shares in 2026, in a window where the stock traded to $9.01. Meanwhile the share count went from 114.76M (February) to 117.20M (July) — up 2.1% in six months on stock compensation with nothing bought back. And the remaining buyback authorization is $110M, not $700M; a large buyback would need a new board approval that does not exist today.
4. **The cost savings are already in the bad number.** The $85M savings program is being realized *during* the first half we just looked at. Profit fell $50.7M anyway. Strip the savings out and underlying deterioration is around $90M in one half-year, and there is no second $85M program waiting for 2027.

## Why it might be priced in

We should be honest that most of this is visible to anyone who reads the filings, and the stock is already down by half.

- **The structural-fix catalyst has already been run as a live experiment, and it failed.** "Collapse the share structure, remove the controlling holder, and the multiple re-rates" was tested in April 2025 at $16.27. The stock is around $10 now. That is the strongest evidence that the remaining catalysts are also priced.
- **The $700M is real and the break fee floors the downside on the deal itself.** If you believe the cash gets returned, the math changes. We don't — but that is a judgment, not a fact.
- **The valuation is genuinely low on the reported measure.** Roughly three times a falling profit number is not an obviously expensive stock. Our case is that the denominator keeps falling, not that the price is high.
- **We are also declining the short**, and for good reasons: 60% implied volatility, $41M average daily dollar volume, an activist present, a $700M cash inflow pending, and a company that discloses its own bad news honestly. Cheap-and-melting with a large cash event is a bad short.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry. No long, no short, no order staged, no size.** Position today: none. Size if this were approved: 0.0% of book. There is no price at which we buy this on current information — the objection is to the direction of the profit line, not to the price, so a lower price does not fix it. Buying it cheaper is buying the same melt at a discount, and we already watched the last structural catalyst fail from $16.27.

These are the reopen gates, exactly as adjudicated:

- **Q3 10-Q Note 13 (~early Nov): Experiences marketing <= 58% of segment revenue -> re-look**
- **>=$300M buyback authorized at TheFork close -> secondary reversal trigger**

Reading the first gate plainly: Experiences marketing was 61.3% of that segment's revenue in the June quarter and 64.8% for the first half. If the November filing shows 58% or lower, the "we have to buy back the traffic we used to get free" mechanism is not compounding, and the whole case deserves a fresh look. If it is higher, the case is confirmed and we do nothing.

Reading the second gate plainly: a *new* board authorization of at least $300M — not the $110M left on the old one, and not a press-release intention — landing at or around the TheFork closing would mean the cash is actually coming to shareholders rather than sitting on the balance sheet while the share count drifts up 4% a year.

- **2026-11-05** — expected Q3 earnings release. Vendor-derived, **not confirmed by the company**. Treat proximity accordingly.
