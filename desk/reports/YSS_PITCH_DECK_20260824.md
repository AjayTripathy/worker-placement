# PITCH — YSS — VERDICT

ADJUDICATED  — NO ENTRY. Stay flat. The adjudicated buy zone ($12–15) was written on a pre-cut information set that the 8/13–8/14 filings destroyed; the price has fallen *through* that zone to $9.76 for reasons the zone did not contemplate, so the flush is not a discount — it is a repricing we cannot yet value. Reopen is evidence-gated, not price-gated.

## What we believe

York Space Systems builds satellites and satellite buses, mostly for one U.S. government customer. It went public on 2026-01-29 at a growth multiple, and it has since lost about 78% of its value from the high.

Our honest belief, after both benches worked the filings, is narrower than the original idea:

1. **The company is not about to fail.** It holds roughly $379M of cash against $148.1M of term debt and an undrawn $150M revolver. Gross margin is around 24% and improving. This is not a solvency story.
2. **The growth story that justified the IPO price was never supported by arithmetic.** First-half 2026 revenue was $208.9M, up 9.9% from $190.1M. Management's original full-year guide of $545–595M required the second half to grow 71–97%. They reaffirmed that guide on 2026-05-12, after a quarter that showed no acceleration at all. On 2026-08-13 they cut to $375–405M, which implies a second half that *shrinks* 0–15% year over year.
3. **The cash burn is a delayed-shipment signature, not a demand collapse — but that distinction is smaller than it sounds.** Operating cash flow was −$186.6M in the first half. The biggest year-over-year deterioration came from inventory (up 123%, from $18.7M to $41.8M) and receivables, which is what a company looks like when hardware is built but not yet delivered and billed. That was the original test the idea set for itself, and it partly passes.
4. **What actually blocks us is three things that all resolve at one future observation:** a revenue covenant with 0.7% of headroom at year-end; a CFO change announced eight days before the guide cut; and an unresolved integrity allegation neither bench examined.
5. **We do not currently have a defensible fair value.** The adjudicated $16–20 was set before the guide cut. We cannot reproduce it on post-cut numbers, and we will not pretend otherwise.

## What the market believes

The market has moved from "high-growth space prime" to "show me." Specifically, the price at $9.76 embeds:

- An enterprise value of roughly **$1.11B** ($1.34B market value at 137.36M shares, less $379M cash, plus $148.1M term debt). That is **2.7–3.0x** the new, cut revenue guide.
- No credit for the $592M backlog or the $1.07B contract ceiling the company markets. (Both figures are from an investor deck, not from the 10-Q — we have not verified either.)
- Continued distrust of management forecasts, which is rational given the May reaffirmation.
- Ongoing insider and institutional supply: the IPO lock-up expired in late July, there was a cluster of insider sale filings on 7/31–8/10, and a large institutional holder filed a full exit on 8/14.

Sell-side price targets sit around $17–18 — roughly 80% above the tape — while ratings are mostly "Hold." That combination is target-lag, the least informative kind of anchor, and we are not building anything on it.

The one thing the market may *not* have fully absorbed: the year-end revenue covenant. We found no evidence it is being discussed publicly, and the low end of the company's own new guide clears it by $2.5M.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the covenant schedule.** Note 7 of the 10-Q discloses a trailing-twelve-month minimum revenue test: $264.4M at 6/30/26, $319.2M at 9/30/26, and **$372,510,143 at 12/31/26**, escalating to $758.5M by late 2028. Against a guide low end of $375M, that is 0.7% of room. Most people stop reading at "compliant as of the balance sheet date."
- **We did the quarter arithmetic on that covenant, and it corrects one of our own benches.** The 9/30 test is essentially free: the trailing-twelve-month base already contains $105.3M from Q4-2025, so the company needs only about $5M of Q3 revenue to pass it. The *binding* test is 12/31. First-half revenue was $208.9M, so the second half must total **$163.6M — about $81.8M per quarter versus $92.5M in Q2** — with no margin for a slip. That is a specific, dated, checkable number that our own blue bench's proposed gate ("trailing revenue ≥ $340M at 9/30") would have waved through.
- **We separated the seasonal from the structural in the cash flow.** Our red bench's headline finding — that customer prepayments had been "exhausted" — turned out to be a calendar artifact. The 10-Q discloses both years: $108.1M of the opening prepayment balance was consumed in H1-2026 versus $114.1M in H1-2025, a 5% decline, and the balance refilled to $110.3M by year-end last year. The company draws down advances every first half. What survives is smaller and real: *new* advances collected fell from roughly $41M to roughly $12M.
- **We noticed the sequencing of the CFO exit.** The 8-K dated 2026-08-05 announced the CFO stepping down with an interim effective 8/14 — eight days before the guide cut and effective the day the 10-Q was filed.
- **We know what we cannot see, and we say so.** This is an ITAR-controlled defense prime with one customer. Our physical-flow detectors returned nothing: zero customs bill-of-lading records, no satellite thermal or construction reads run. There is no outside-the-filings channel here. Most stories give us one; this one does not.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, most of it probably is.

- **2.7–3.0x cut revenue** is not a growth multiple. The market has already marked the forecast down.
- **The single-customer concentration (96% of first-half revenue) is a risk factor in every initiation report.** It is a size cap for us, not a discovery. Our red bench tagged it as a novel find; that tag is wrong.
- **The stock is already 9.8% off its 52-week low.** Whatever forced selling happened around the lock-up has begun to clear. There is no panic tape to buy into right now.
- **The covenant may be defused by a phone call.** The credit agreement contains a "Leverage Covenant Toggle" — a one-sided written election by the company that swaps the revenue test for a 4.50x first-lien net leverage test. With $148.1M of term debt against $379M of cash, first-lien net leverage is negative. Management can almost certainly step out of the trap. We have not seen them elect it, but the escape hatch exists and sophisticated credit investors will know that.
- **The insider supply is a known, dated, mechanical event**, not a secret.

Our genuine edge, if any, is narrow: the exact 12/31 covenant number, the per-quarter revenue it implies, and whether the toggle election ever gets disclosed. That is a monitoring edge, not a valuation edge.

## What could go wrong

*(Pre-mortem, v1.7. We hold no position. This is the failure analysis for the 0.25% starter we would take if the reopen conditions were met.)*

**Failure narrative 1 — The covenant traps them and the fix costs equity holders. (Monitorable.)**
Q3 revenue prints near $60–70M instead of $82M. Year-end trailing revenue lands under $372.5M. Management either did not elect the toggle or the lenders extract a price for the amendment — fees, a higher coupon, a springing test, or an equity issue at a distressed price. Our starter is diluted or the stock re-rates to a credit story. *Tripwires:* Q3 revenue below $80M; absence of an 8-K disclosing the toggle election by 12/31; any 8-K under Item 1.01 or 2.04; any S-1 or 424B filing indicating a follow-on. All observable in advance of the loss.

**Failure narrative 2 — The one customer re-scopes, and the backlog turns out to be a ceiling, not a commitment. (Monitorable, with a lag.)**
The adjudication records that the Tranche 3 Transport program was zeroed for FY27 with a sole-source award going elsewhere (Starshield). Neither bench independently verified this — it is the single most important forward-revenue fact in the file and it sits outside both benches' work. If it is right, the $592M backlog and $1.07B ceiling are largely unfundable, and the 2027 revenue base is far below anything the current multiple assumes. Second-quarter bookings ran at roughly 0.46x of revenue, which is consistent with this. *Tripwires:* contract liabilities failing to rebuild above ~$60M by year-end; bookings staying below 1.0x; an 8-K naming a program termination or descope; appropriations language in the FY27 budget. The lag is the problem — we may learn this two quarters after the customer decided it.

**Failure narrative 3 — The revenue was never as earned as reported. (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)**
A short-seller report (Wolfpack) alleges integrity problems; the adjudication records it as partially corroborated — a software disappointment on the customer's program and a $5.5M estimate-at-completion reserve. This company recognizes revenue on long-term contracts using percentage-of-completion, which means reported revenue is a *management estimate* of progress toward completion. If those estimates were optimistic, the correction arrives as a restatement or a large catch-up reserve, and the loss is instantaneous and un-hedgeable.

There is no tripwire for this, and I want to be explicit about why rather than pretend a sensor exists. **Unknown-unknown class: unobservable-by-construction — management-discretion accounting on a classified, single-buyer program.** Every external channel we would normally use is structurally closed here. Customs records return zero shipments (expected for an ITAR prime, and therefore uninformative in both directions). There are no consumer reviews, no permits that matter, no second customer to triangulate against, and no public program-level appropriation detail. The first observation of this failure *is* the disclosure of it. Under our own tainted-integrity rule, this alone means the name can never be treated as fairly-priced-and-ownable at full size, and caps any fair value we assign at 0.85x.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own footprint and our own rules.**

- *Size versus liquidity: unmeasured, and that is a real gap.* Our live-quote tool was permission-denied for both benches this session, so we have no live bid/ask, no volume, and **no average daily volume figure at all**. Our published rule is that ladders stay under 1% of average daily volume, and right now we cannot demonstrate compliance with our own rule. A 0.25% starter is small in dollars, but "small in dollars" is not the test — the test is share count against a tape we have not seen.
- *We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict.* Our lock-up detector fired: insider sales cluster 7/31–8/10, institutional exit 8/14, share count up 8.04M between 6/30 and 8/12 from restricted-stock settlement. Our own flow-gate rule says do not put a resting bid underneath supply we have already forecast. Any limit order in the $8–10 area is, in effect, offering liquidity to a seller we identified in advance.
- *Doctrine collisions, stated plainly.* Our "clean court gets a minimum starter" default would normally push us to take a small position rather than sit at zero. It does not apply here, because that default requires a court with **no named kill**, and this court has two named kills (0.7% covenant headroom; unresolved integrity overhang). Meanwhile the tainted-integrity rule forbids treating this as fairly-paid risk at all, and the pay-up allowance does not apply because fairness is unverified rather than verified-and-modestly-rich. The rules are consistent here; they all point the same direction, which is flat.
- *The symmetric failure of doing nothing.* If Q3 shows inventory converting to deliveries and the toggle election is disclosed, this becomes a clean timing dislocation at 2.7x trough revenue and it will not be at $9.76 when we get there. Staying flat has a cost and we are choosing to pay it.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a delayed-shipment story that was actually an accounting story — that the percentage-of-completion estimates on the single customer's programs were optimistic, and the write-down arrived as a disclosure we had no way to see coming, on a name where every external verification channel is closed by ITAR and single-buyer concentration.
