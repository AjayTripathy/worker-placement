# PITCH — ABR — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: DO NOT BUY at $5.36. Both benches converge on no position (kill conviction 6–7/10). Re-hear the case at the Q3 earnings report (~Oct 30); specific reopen conditions and bands below.**

## What we believe

Arbor Realty Trust is a real-estate lender with two businesses: a $12.1B balance-sheet loan book (mostly floating-rate bridge loans on 2021-vintage apartment buildings) and a $36.7B agency loan-servicing business. The stock has fallen 57% from its 52-week high to $5.36, about 46% of the company's stated $11.59 per-share book value.

The original long thesis was that the market is pricing the whole company as if the troubled loan book infects everything, while the servicing business is a credit-insensitive cash machine that alone justifies much of the price. **The court killed that thesis.** The company's own Q2 2026 quarterly filing shows Arbor shares losses with Fannie Mae on the servicing book too: it carries a $118.9M loss-sharing reserve against it and took $18.0M of new loss provisions on it in the first half of 2026 alone ([SEC 10-Q, filed 2026-07-31](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001253986/000125398626000048/abr-20260630.htm)). Both engines are exposed to the same apartment-loan credit cycle. What we now believe: this is a damaged but functioning franchise, trading at a discount that is roughly fair for the risks, with a probable dividend cut ahead of it.

## What the market believes

The market is treating ABR as a contested battleground, not a neglected bargain. Roughly 47M shares (~29% of the float, as of stale March 2026 data) are sold short. Short sellers and a Bloomberg-reported DOJ/FBI inquiry (2024-vintage, [Bisnow coverage](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/arbor-realty-trust-investigated-by-doj-following-short-seller-charges-125078)) argue the loan book is marked too optimistically: the loss reserve is only 1.35% of loans while ~8.8% of the book is non-performing. The dividend was already cut to $0.17/quarter, and the stock's price says the market expects more cuts and more credit losses. Notably, the market also just showed it will still lend to Arbor: in July the company placed $375M of unsecured 3-year convertible notes at 6.25% — funding access is intact, but expensive.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: less than the original pitch claimed. What survives:

- **Funding access is proven, not assumed.** The July convert placement refutes the "terminal funding spiral" bear case. The red bench never engaged this leg.
- **The servicing business is damaged, not worthless.** The loss-sharing reserve is ~0.32% of the $36.7B servicing portfolio, and the ~$36M/year provision run-rate compares to ~$195M/year of servicing and escrow revenue. There is still a real earnings stream here.
- **The heavy short interest cuts both ways.** A 29%-of-float short base is fuel for a squeeze if credit news improves — it argues against being short, even as it kills the "nobody's looking at this" framing.
- **A defined, near-term test exists.** Management has a December target of reducing foreclosed real estate to ≤$350M. If hit with genuine cash sales, it is checkable evidence the workout is progressing.

## Why it might be priced in

- The core dispersion argument — clean servicing engine vs. dirty loan book — is factually wrong per the company's own filing. Both engines carry the same credit.
- The $0.17 dividend is being paid out of $0.10 of distributable earnings, with management guiding $20–30M per quarter of realized losses. A second cut is the most likely dated event, and the decision lands at the ~Oct 30 report.
- The loss reserve ($163.4M) covers only ~15% of the ~$1.07B of non-performing loans and would be consumed by guided losses in ~6 quarters. Either reserves rise (book value falls) or the book is optimistic. $11.59 is not a conservative anchor.
- The July converts convert at ~$6.10/share — 61.5M potential new shares on a ~209M share base. Above $6.10, dilution kicks in and pro-forma book value per share falls to roughly **$10.34**, so the upside doesn't stop there but it decelerates meaningfully. Management effectively sold part of the recovery upside to fund the buyback.
- At +13% off the 52-week low with an active buyback underneath, the tape has already bounced; this is not a fresh dislocation.
