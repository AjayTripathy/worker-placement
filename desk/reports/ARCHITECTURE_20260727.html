The Desk OS — System Architecture

Every layer, rule, and detector, in plain English, as wired on 2026-07-27. Naming, precisely: SignalOS is the verification core — the machinery that checks claims against primary sources. The Desk is the watcher — it monitors everything and routes what matters. The rest is the trading operation built around both. The page reads top to bottom because that is how information actually flows: raw sources become verified facts, facts become judged investment cases, cases become written-down predictions, and only then does money move — always behind a human's approval.

The product is the accumulated knowledge — a growing map of how information travels into prices: which mechanisms move which companies, through which channels, on what delay. Trades and diligence reports are byproducts. A strategy that tests out worthless but teaches a mechanism is a success. The one rule underneath everything: whoever generates an idea never grades it.

LAYER 1

Data connectors

SIGNALOS CORE primary sources only — claims get checked against the world, not against news coverage
Filings & regulators (the audited spine)
EDGAR / XBRLEvery US company's filings, straight from the SEC. When we quote a number it comes from the audited tables, never from the company's press release.
EDINETJapan's filing system. We read roughly a thousand Japanese companies directly — including the accounting quirks that make some cheap-looking ones fake-cheap.
EMMAThe municipal-bond disclosure system. The fastest way to see whether a city or school district files its financials honestly and on time.
SEC Form DChecks a private fund manager's marketed fund sizes against what they actually registered — "unverifiable" is itself an answer.
ClinicalTrials.govThe drug-trial registry, with edit history — catches companies quietly changing what a trial was supposed to prove after the fact.
Government & courts
USASpendingEvery federal contract dollar. Shows when a company's government money actually arrives — including money routed through subsidiaries the company doesn't highlight.
CourtListenerFederal court dockets. Screens any executive or company for lawsuits that matter — fraud and disputes, not routine employment claims.
openFDA / MAUDEFDA product registrations and device-complaint reports. Used to verify claims — not to predict failures, since complaint spikes measure paperwork, not defects.
FEMA risk mapsFederal disaster-risk data joined to school-district boundaries: which municipal borrowers sit on land that burns or floods, and whether insurers are leaving.
State gaming dataCasino and slot-machine revenue that states publish monthly — hard numbers to check company stories against.
Physical truth (things that are hard to fake)
Carbon MapperSatellites that see methane plumes. When a company says "no leaks," we can check from orbit.
Sentinel-2Free satellite imagery. Did the factory actually get built? Is the farmland actually healthy? Pixel changes answer both.
Customs recordsUS import manifests, shipment by shipment. Often reveals who a company's undisclosed biggest customer really is.
Job postingsHiring at a specific plant ramps before production does — a free early read on utilization.
Appointment calendarsHow full the booking systems of lab-test companies are, weeks before they report earnings. Fed our best-graded prediction to date.
Virality radarTracks which beauty products are going viral, then finds the public company that makes them — social buzz shows up in revenue one or two quarters later.
Maps dataDo the stores a company claims actually exist, and do they have customers? Google Places and OpenStreetMap answer store-by-store.
Ships & stormsTanker traffic through chokepoints like Hormuz, and hurricane tracks in season — for the positions those actually touch.
Markets & crowds
Interactive BrokersThe broker. Live prices, what we actually hold, what orders are working. Always the final word. As of 2026-07-28 the connection can STAGE untransmitted orders (they activate only when the operator clicks Transmit); live transmission from code remains forbidden until the sizing gate is earned.
Option pricesOptions embed the market's own odds on where a stock goes. We compute those odds and use them as the honest baseline for our predictions.
Betting marketsPolymarket and Kalshi price elections and policy events in real time — a genuine crowd number for political questions.
Short-sale dataDaily short-selling volume shows who's betting against a stock and when they're being squeezed out; free delayed prices serve as backup.
Fresh price first — get a live quote from the broker before valuing anything; never reuse this morning's number. Look like a real browser when a government site blocks the scraper, before falling back to archives. Read the pictures — if a filing puts its numbers in a chart instead of a table, fetch the image and read it. Nothing found ≠ nothing there — an empty search means try a second phrasing before concluding absence.

Watchers & detectors

THE DESK watches everything, decides nothing — it routes what matters to the verifier

scanner suiteTen screens that go looking for candidates: unusually cheap companies, expiring share lockups, odd borrowing costs, betting-market disagreements, and so on.
Many small detectors beat one big score — each fires rarely and means it when it does; blended master-scores looked great and failed blind tests. Every detector declares what it applies to, so the right checks run on the right kind of company — and a pre-flight check catches coverage gaps. New watchers must be switched on explicitly — one wasn't, and sat silently dead for months. Now it's checked. Expected a cover-up and found none? That's a finding too — record it, don't discard the sector.
LAYER 3

The diligence courts

SIGNALOS CORE the best findings come from trying to kill the idea, not from checking its boxes
pin down who we're actually investigating→ pull their filings up to today→ verify every claim at the source+ independently try to disprove the thesis→ prosecution vs defense, then a ruling→ verdict recorded with sizing and kill-conditions
Two-pass diligenceEvery pitch gets both: verify what it claims, and separately attack it from first principles. The attack pass has flipped more verdicts than the checklist ever has.
The courtOne agent prosecutes the thesis, another defends it, on the strongest model available — and any single finding big enough to decide the case gets re-verified before anyone acts on it.
The honesty lensWe flag companies whose story diverges from their own numbers. Re-discovering bad news a company disclosed honestly is not a finding — the value is excluding the ones who shade the truth.
Count all the sharesWarrants, convertibles, and pre-funded instruments come out of the fine print before any per-share math — or the valuation is fiction.
Same claim, every venueWhat a company tells investors is compared against what it told regulators, courts, and its own past self.
Right entity firstThe name on the pitch deck is rarely the right thing to search — permits live under the contractor, filings under the shell company, patents under the founder.
No shortcut diligence — everything routes through the pipeline; a quick ad-hoc look skips detectors and calls gaps "clean." Trust the cleans, re-check the exclusions — our error pattern runs one direction: we wrongly condemn more often than we wrongly acquit. Check the tape — before repeating "the stock crashed on X," verify the crash actually happened. One famous one hadn't. When an agent suggests a rule improvement, wire it in — a validated lesson left as a note is a lesson lost.
LAYER 4

Predictions & the scoreboard

TRADING OPERATION every belief is written down before the event and graded after — our own honesty test, applied to ourselves
write the prediction down before the event→ pre-write exactly how it will be judged→ the sentinel grades it when the event lands→ the scoreboard updates→ any pre-agreed action fires within 30 minutes
How a prediction gets made (the enforced path)
Fixed event typesEvery prediction is one of a fixed list of event kinds, chosen before results existed — so the scoreboard's categories can't be reshuffled after the fact to look better.
The four questionsEvery prediction must answer: what do we believe, what does the market believe, why might we know better, and why might it already be in the price?
Two calls per earningsThe business result and the stock's reaction are predicted separately — being right about the business and wrong about the stock are different mistakes, and a good quarter never excuses a bad stock call.
Check the crowd firstBefore a prediction freezes, the system checks whether a betting market already prices the same event — and records the check even when none exists.
New this week — defer to the market where we haven't earned the right to disagree
Options-implied oddsFor every "will the stock be up a week later" call, we now compute the odds the options market implies and store them beside ours — with a source stamp, so a real market number can never be confused with one we derived ourselves.
Name the driverA stock-reaction prediction must say what the stock will actually trade on — the guidance, a policy decision, the backlog. "The quarter" is not an answer; that mistake cost us our worst grade.
The ±5-point ruleIn categories where we've shown no skill, our number defaults to the market's. Disagreeing by more than five points requires naming what we know that the market doesn't.
Kill the story firstA confident story-based prediction requires one documented attempt to disprove it — plus the growth arithmetic: "growth continues at 20%" against a year that grew 38% is a long-shot, and now the system says so.

The sizing gate: our predictions may influence position sizes only once they beat the market's own odds across 20+ events with real market prices. Currently 0 of 20 — not earned. Odds we derived ourselves don't count as "the market" — only real external prices (options, betting venues) qualify; four old unlabeled numbers are excluded until traced. Frozen means frozen — a prediction is never revised after the fact; a wrong date gets moved, never regraded. Grades speak plainly — a 55% call that misses "leaned slightly wrong, little information"; only high-conviction calls can meaningfully fail. No ambiguous grading — if two predictions could match the same grade command, the system refuses rather than guessing.
LAYER 5

From idea to position

TRADING OPERATION courts before capital; being paid fairly for a risk needs no crystal ball
scanners propose candidates→ an independent court judges them→ a written thesis anyone could read→ order prepared with limits→ the human submits it
Three reasons to own anythingWe know something the market doesn't (rare, must be proven) · we're being paid fairly for a bounded risk (the default) · it survived deep diligence. Fair pay is enough — no forecast needed.
The problem decides the responseToo expensive → wait for the price. Business risk → own less. Timing uncertainty → buy in steps. Bad data → kill it. Each objection type maps to exactly one lever.
What's already priced in?Before claiming an edge, we back out what the market already believes from the current price. Bullish-but-priced is fair, not attractive.
Thesis travels with the eventFor fast-moving names the court and the written thesis are finished before the catalyst — so nothing gets decided in the heat of the reaction window.
Every idea needs a dateEach position names the future event that could prove it right, with odds and tripwires. No event on the calendar = dead money.
Before every earnings reportTwo questions, answered in advance: what do the public numbers already show, and what has the stock already priced in?
Sizing rules
Sizes are set against the target $1M book, not today's balance — a first position is about 1% (~$10k); only thin trading volume shrinks that. Sector limits are checked before the court sits, counting everything held, pending, and staged together. No option-selling in the taxable account — the tax treatment eats the premium; patient limit orders do the same job better. 2026 is a tax-loss-harvesting year — no muni bonds here (the household already owns them elsewhere); every ballast dollar stays harvest-capable. Limits, never market orders — and in thin stocks, extra patience; some exchanges quote in cents, so units are verified before any order exists. A net saver doesn't buy crash insurance — with cash still flowing in, a drawdown is a discount, not a disaster.
LAYER 6

Execution rails

TRADING OPERATION the machine proposes; the human disposes
The software cannot tradeSessions prepare orders — ticker, size, limit price — staged UNTRANSMITTED at the broker (as of 2026-07-28); nothing goes live until a human clicks Transmit. Live transmission from code stays forbidden until the sizing gate is earned — the guarantee is now discipline + review, no longer the config switch.
One writer at a timeOnly one session may write to shared state, named in a lock file. Two sessions once staged the same orders with different numbers — never again.
The broker outranks the notesApprovals happen overnight without any session knowing. When our notes and the broker's records disagree, the broker is right.
Autonomy is earned, not grantedDirect order placement unlocks only on measured performance: prediction skill beating the market (20+ events), execution costs verified low, and a full month of clean operations — then still with hard limits.
Check holdings before claiming holdings — plans and resting orders are not positions. The misses get graded too — every "we'll buy at X" that never filled is scored later: was the discipline right, or did we just miss it?
LAYER 7

Portfolio, risk & operations

TRADING OPERATION the book is measured like a fund; the machine is maintained like a fleet
rotation_fundMeasures what the whole book is really exposed to — which sectors, which risk factors — stress-tests it against past market rotations, and routes new money toward whatever is underweight.
regional_factorsForeign holdings are measured against their own home markets, so the claimed diversification is verified, not assumed.
The household pictureThe full family balance sheet — outside accounts, the concentrated stock position, incoming cash — so this account never duplicates or collides with the rest (tax rules included).
BallastCash plus a small gold position as the book's stable floor; the cash reserve doubles as ammunition for buying dislocations.
The dashboardOne local web app: the scoreboard, the strategy book, every thesis, system health, and daily briefs — refreshed continuously.
Memory & handoffA ~140-entry memory of hard-won lessons plus a living handoff brief, refreshed whenever doctrine changes. Sessions are temporary; the files are the truth.
Every red light gets investigated the day it appears — root cause found, fixed or formally retired, never ignored. Perishable facts carry dates — memory supplies questions to re-check, never stale premises to build on. Merge, never overwrite shared records — clobbering a shared file is the system's oldest recurring bug. Reports keep the discipline, drop the jargon — every published page is written for a reader who has never seen the codebase. (This page included.) Published research carries a disclaimer and our positions — and once positions are published, their exits get published too, forever.
LAYER 8

Household resilience & uncorrelated streams

HOUSEHOLD LAYER what pays or protects when the book doesn't — deliberately outside its correlation structure
Disaster PlannerRuns the entire family balance sheet through tail scenarios: how big the drawdown would be, which sleeves bleed versus hedge, and how much cash could be raised without touching illiquid assets or triggering the tax bomb.
Harvest engineTracks the $2.4M tax-loss goal across all accounts, projects the year-end total at the current pace, and says out loud — in time to act — if we're tracking to miss. Losses must land by December 31; they carry forward, not back.
The built-in hedgeA large incoming gain plus a harvesting engine makes the tax bill itself anti-correlated with the market: a crash produces more harvested losses, which shrinks the tax owed. The reserve gets cheaper exactly when everything else gets worse.
Crash insurance: priced and declinedA full options-based insurance program was designed, priced, and rejected — correctly: a household still adding money has no forced seller to protect. The design stays on the shelf and reactivates only if the book ever uses leverage.
The research-as-product threadPublishing our verified research is itself an income stream uncorrelated with markets — replicating what active managers charge ~1% a year for, with the transparency (including our own confessed errors) as the brand.
Dry powderThe cash reserve doubles as dip-buying ammunition, a weekly gold accumulation provides the stable floor, and the large September inflow is standing optionality — not a deadline.
A net saver doesn't buy index insurance — self-insurance is the default; the market's crashes are a buyer's discounts. Tax rules wall off the hedges — no derivatives that overlap the professionally-managed accounts, ever, without counsel signing off first. Harvest losses from stocks, not bonds — bond discounts count as ordinary income and can't be harvested. The concentrated position is a HOLD — no harvesting against it (owner's explicit call); restructuring it is a lawyer conversation, not a trade. One model for planning and panic — the disaster scenarios and the daily dashboard read the same balance sheet, so they can never drift apart.
