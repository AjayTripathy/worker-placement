02

How the ladders are built — three rules learned the hard way

  • 1 · Every rung maps to the scenario math, not to round numbers. Each investigation priced a bear, base, and bull case from primary filings. The top rung sits below spot at a technical or ownership anchor; the bottom rung sits at or below the bear-case fair value — so the deepest fill is bought at a price that works even if the pessimists are right.
  • 2 · The wrong-side test. A limit order that fills only when the thesis dies is a trap dressed as discipline (this docket's Bruker case: a $36 limit fills almost exclusively on the guidance cut that drops fair value to $38 — so Bruker got alerts, not orders). Each of the three ladders below passes: the rung prices can be reached on drift, sector pressure, or the pending sell-side downgrade wave — paths that do not, by themselves, break the thesis.
  • 3 · No ladder runs through its own verdict date unexamined. Every ladder carries an expiry or a cancel-before-earnings rule, because price and evidence are negatively correlated: if the next print confirms the business, the stock won't be at our rungs — and if it breaks the business, we don't want yesterday's rungs live.

Context that keeps us honest: this same sweep graded the dislocation template 0-for-7 at the entry bar — on an earnings break, the headline usually is the cash-flow news. These three survived as ladder candidates precisely because their sell-offs overshot a quantified, bounded problem; the ladders monetize further overshoot, never the first drop.

Ladder 1 · Viemed Healthcare (VMD) · fell −15.9% on Aug 4

A net-cash home-ventilator specialist, punished 10× the size of its actual guidance change

Q2 was a record revenue quarter (+23.9%); the full-year EBITDA guide moved down all of $1M (−1.5%) while the revenue guide was raised at the low end — and the capex guide fell more than the EBITDA give-up, so the revision is actually free-cash-flow accretive. Balance sheet: net cash. Trailing free cash flow ≈ 9% of the market cap. Management bought back stock at $9.65, above where our ladder starts.

Why it isn't a buy at market — the honest side: the unchanged guide quietly requires an H2 margin step (+497bp) with no precedent in the company's own history (last year's was +104bp); the census the release headlines as a record has been flat for three quarters; and the real regulatory event is fresh — CMS added VMD's core billing codes to the required face-to-face documentation list effective Oct 28, days after the company lost an appeal on a ventilator-billing audit it disclosed only in the 10-Q, not the press release. Ladder 2 · Centuri Holdings (CTRI) · fell −21.5% on Aug 4

A utility-infrastructure contractor that beat, raised, grew backlog 21% — and got sold like it missed

Record revenue $962M (+33%, $126M above consensus), EPS beat, full-year guidance raised, $6.4B backlog +21%, and roughly $450M of master-service-agreement renewals in the quarter's bookings. The de-rate has a real cause — operating income was flat on a third more revenue, and 77% of contracts carry annually-reset, capped cost escalators, so this year's fuel and labor inflation is genuinely unrecoverable until reset. But the franchise (mandate-driven gas-pipe replacement, 59% of revenue) did not break. Carl Icahn holds 14.4% with a board seat, at a verified $19.74 average cost — our entire ladder sits at or below the activist's own basis.

Full size 0.75% (~$25k) across both branches.

Ladder 3 · Aptiv (APTV) · fell −16.6% on Aug 4

A −3.4% guidance cut, a −16.6% price: overshoot on a fairly-priced franchise — so the ladder starts 12% lower still

Q2 beat cleanly (EPS $1.63 vs $1.30–1.50 guided; margins +160bps, honestly built — the debt-extinguishment gain is excluded). The cut is real but small, and ~83% of it is China, bridged explicitly by the CFO. What the market is actually repricing: the premium-multiple segment (ADAS/software) is flat with profit falling, while 91% of profit growth comes from the unglamorous connectors-and-cabling side — the market's same-day verdict was Aptiv's own spun-off harness unit rising 9.4% while the parent fell 16.6%. This is a fairly-paid quality cyclical, not a mispricing: at spot it trades in the upper half of its peer set. The ladder therefore starts below the peer-median multiple and ends at bear fair value.

Rules on the order: GTC through the Q3 print (~Oct 29), then re-examined, never rolled blind. Do not buy the gap — day 0 of a 52-week low, negative drift regime.

Summary — what would actually happen if you approve

Nine GTC limit orders across three names, ~$75k total (2.2% of deployable capital), every rung below market, every bottom rung at or below its bear-case value, every ladder with an expiry before its next earnings verdict. Fill probability is deliberately modest — these orders monetize a further overshoot, panic, or the pending downgrade waves; if none comes, nothing fills and nothing is lost. One pre-condition outstanding: the independent re-check of the CMS documentation rule before Viemed's rungs rest.
