# PITCH — STDN — VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — minimum starter, gated, 0.40% of book at ~$13.27; no adds until the authorization and DOE-allocation gates clear.

---

## What we believe

Standard Nuclear turns government-owned enriched uranium into TRISO reactor fuel pellets. It IPO'd on 7/16/2026 at $15 and trades at **$13.272** (evidence pack tape; 52-week range $7.05–$17.34, so it is down 23.5% from the high but still up 88% from the low).

Our belief, stated plainly:

1. **This is a toll processor, not a fuel company.** The uranium (HALEU) is allotted by the Department of Energy to STDN's *customers* — Radiant and Antares — and STDN gets paid to convert it. That means the celebrated ~67% gross margin is a conversion fee on free raw material, and it means STDN cannot ship if DOE does not allocate to its customers, regardless of whether STDN's plants are authorized to operate.
2. **The funded business is small.** The $576.9M "backlog" is a through-2035 number in which one metric ton is firm and seven are options. Decomposed, the firm piece is roughly **$13M a year**. The Q2 revenue of $4.7M was a single 50-kg shipment to one customer, not a run rate.
3. **The stock is priced for the scale-up, not for the funded case.** At the near-term 2.5 metric-ton throughput the company itself guides to, the whole company is worth roughly **$4–6/share**. You only get to today's price if you believe the 7.5-tonne world (the two plants plus the Framatome joint venture) arrives, and you only get meaningfully paid above $20 if you believe the 10-tonne world arrives and is funded without brutal dilution.
4. **Therefore: a small, gated starter, not a position.** We buy a lottery ticket on a real industrial asset with two named customers and a government-blessed feedstock path, sized so that the January share-unlock cliff and a possible stock offering cannot hurt us, and we add only on evidence, not on price.

## What the market believes

The market believes the headline. UBS raised its target to $20 on 9/1 — after the 8/20 customer agreement — and Stifel is at Buy. Those targets sit ~50% above the tape and above the funded value of the company as either of our benches calculated it. The street appears to have taken the $576.9M backlog and the "93% growth" at face value without splitting firm orders from options, and it appears to be treating "targeted Q4 2026 authorization" as a schedule rather than an aspiration.

The market also believes — reasonably — that the US government wants a domestic TRISO supply chain badly enough to keep feeding it. That belief is not stupid. It is just not underwritten by anything we can observe.

Honest caveat: **we could not retrieve a single consensus revenue number.** Neither bench could. So we cannot say precisely what the street's numbers are, only what its price targets are. A price target is the weakest possible read on consensus, and we say so rather than pretend.

## Why we might have edge

- **Backlog decomposition.** We split the $576.9M into ~$13M/year of firm revenue plus a nine-year option tail. The street headline number was published undecomposed and the sell-side raise came after it. If that is right, it is a real informational gap and it is *bearish* — which is why our position is small rather than absent.
- **The toll-processing insight.** Recognizing that the 67% margin sits on customer-supplied, government-allocated uranium reframes the whole risk stack: the binding gate is a DOE allocation decision to a third party, not STDN's own plant authorization. We have not seen this framed anywhere in public commentary.
- **The capacity arithmetic is public but scattered.** The 2.5-tonne near-term number, the 2-tonne Framatome JV, and the ~40-tonne end-of-decade ambition live in three different documents. Putting them in one table is not genius, but it is work most holders have not done.

## Why it might be priced in

- Everything above is drawn from public filings, public trade press, and a public earnings call. There is no private data here. A careful reader of the 10-Q and the Q2 call can reach the same place.
- The bearish leg (option-inflated backlog, tolled margin) is more likely to be understood by the shorts than by us, and the stock is already 23.5% off its high.
- The bullish leg (40-tonne ambition, government support) is exactly what a $2B market cap on $4.7M of quarterly revenue is already paying for. We are not discovering the story; we are arguing about the discount rate on it.
- **Net:** our expected value is roughly **+12%** over ~15 months against a stock that can plausibly halve. That is a starter, not a thesis.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative #1 — DOE quietly reprioritizes the uranium. *No monitorable tripwire.***
DOE allots HALEU to customers using internal prioritization criteria, including material controlled by NNSA. If Radiant's or Antares' allocation is delayed, shrunk, or redirected to a competing reactor developer, STDN has nothing to convert — its plants can be fully authorized and still idle. There is **no public docket, no filing trigger, and no third-party observable** for an allocation decision. Customs data does not help: this uranium is domestic and never crosses a border, so it never appears on an import manifest. We would learn about it from a revenue miss, one to three quarters late.
**Unknown-unknown class: DISCRETIONARY STATE-ALLOCATED INPUT** — a business whose principal raw material is distributed by administrative discretion to a third party, with no published register, no appeal record, and no leading indicator. We cannot build a sensor for this. We can only size for it.

**Failure narrative #2 — the customers are the cohort.**
100% of revenue and essentially all funded backlog is owed by private, pre-revenue microreactor developers who fund themselves by selling equity. If rates back up or the AI-datacenter power trade cools, those two companies raise less, order less, and stretch payables. STDN's receivables *are* the advanced-nuclear cohort; it is not decoupled from it. Partial tripwire exists (funding-round press, an 8-K on any contract modification), but it lags the underlying stress by months.

**Failure narrative #3 (the reflexive / structural leg) — the float breaks before the story pays.**
The IPO floated ~10M shares against ~154–161M total outstanding. Roughly **94% of the shares unlock around mid-January 2027** — before or on top of any authorization payoff. Separately, the charter authorizes 175M Class A shares against ~143M issued: about **32M shares of unissued headroom**, which is a far more likely dilution instrument than the lockup itself, because the 40-tonne plan is entirely unfunded and the remaining scale capex is on the order of $150M+. A single follow-on into a 10M-share float would be a 3x float expansion.
*Our own size vs. liquidity:* we cannot pull ADV in this sandbox. Estimating **0.8–2.5M shares/day** (~$11–33M notional) from comparable hot small-float industrial IPOs, a 0.40%-of-book starter is a few thousand shares — under 0.5% of a day's volume, immaterial on the way in. **The asymmetry is on the way out:** if the January unlock plus a follow-on triples the float while the story is unresolved, the exit is against a tape where every insider is a seller and we are one of very few natural buyers. Doctrine interaction: our own "add on weakness" rule and the lockup-flush add band point in the same direction at the same time — we would be adding into precisely the supply event most likely to be permanent rather than mechanical. We have hard-separated those two cases in the entry bands below, and any Class A shelf or ATM filing voids the add entirely.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the uranium never showed up — DOE allocated HALEU to someone else, or slowly, and STDN's authorized plants sat idle while ~94% of the shares unlocked into a 10-million-share float, an outcome for which we have no sensor, no docket, and no lead time.

**Vol read — and the arithmetic behind the instrument choice.**

**No options chain is present in the evidence pack, and no chain-level quote feed was reachable by either bench.** So the required IV-vs-RV and IV-percentile comparison **cannot be completed today** — that is open item 7, not an omission. What we *can* do is pre-compute the decision rule, so that when a real chain is pulled the answer is arithmetic rather than argument.

Pricing candidate strikes off the table itself, C_ours = Σ pᵢ × max(Sᵢ − K, 0), on a ~June-2027 expiry (S = $13.272, T = 1.25y, r = 4%):

| Strike | C_ours from the table | Implied vol that makes the chain agree with us |
|---|---|---|
| **K = $15** | 0.25(0) + 0.40(0) + 0.25(\$4.00) + 0.10(\$15.00) = **$2.50** | **49.0%** |
| **K = $20** | 0.25(0) + 0.40(0) + 0.25(0) + 0.10(\$10.00) = **$1.00** | **43.2%** |

**Decision rule, output of that math:**
- If chain-level **midpoint IV on those specific strikes** prints **above ~49% (K=15) / ~43% (K=20)** — which our prior of 75–100% for this name strongly suggests it will — then **the calls are expensive against our own table and we do not buy them.** Rich IV routes to the premium-selling gates: a cash-secured put at $10 (below the funded-case midpoint, so we are happy to be assigned) and covered calls at $20 against any assigned stock.
- Only if chain IV prints **below ~45%** do gated adds get expressed as calls.
- **Use chain-level midpoint IV on the actual strikes, never the aggregate underlying IV feed.** These diverge badly on thin small-float names — the TSSI lesson, where the aggregate feed read 71 against a chain that was really 101. On a 10-million-share float with an eight-week-old chain, that error will be larger, not smaller.
- **σ_table (41% annualized) vs. IV × √T:** at an assumed 85% IV, IV × √1.25 = 95% versus our 46% table dispersion. Our own distribution is roughly **half as wide as the options market's**. Taken at face value that is a large variance risk premium (IV minus RV cannot be computed — the stock has under 8 weeks of price history, so any RV estimate is statistically meaningless and we will not manufacture one). Read it as: the market is paying a lot for a distribution wider than the one we can defend.
- **Bimodality warning.** Our table is fork-dated — the authorization decision (Q4 2026) and the unlock (Jan 2027) are two separate, discrete forks inside a single expiry. The distribution is two-humped, with mass at $6 and mass at $19 and comparatively little in between. A single lognormal IV number will systematically misprice both the at-the-money (too rich) and the wings (too cheap). Do not read a single IV print as a verdict; price the wings separately against the table.

**TAX OVERLAY.** Every option expression above resolves after **December 31, 2026** — the June-2027 calls, the Jan/Feb-2027 cash-secured put, all of it. If 2026 is a harvest year, **the loss branch lands in FY2027 and is therefore excluded.** Our edge here is +11.9% expected value against a table our own benches disagreed about by 40% on the roof — that does **not** numerically dominate, so the exception does not apply.

**Instrument recommendation: COMMON STOCK.** Not because we prefer it, but because it is the only expression whose loss branch can be realized inside the intended tax year by selling before December 31, and because our own table says the calls are probably expensive. Revisit the premium-selling gates in January 2027 once (a) a real chain is pulled and (b) the harvest-year constraint lifts.
