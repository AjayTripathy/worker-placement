# PITCH — ONT — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no order; do not buy at $15.33; arm a band at $14.45 that only becomes live after the Q3 print clears two named gates.

## What we believe

Onterris (formerly Montrose Environmental Group; NYSE: ONT) cut its 2026 forecast on August 5th and the stock fell about 17.5% that day. It is now roughly 51% below its 52-week high of $31.21.

We believe the market priced the *revenue* cut and largely ignored what the company said the revenue cut was **made of**. From the company's own release:

| Driver of the revenue cut | Amount | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Pass-through revenue | −$35M to −$55M | Costs billed straight through to customers at near-zero margin |
| Emergency response revenue | −$35M to −$45M | Cleanup work after environmental accidents — a lottery, not a demand curve |
| "Other" revenue | −$15M to −$25M | Unlabeled |

Source: [Onterris Q2 2026 results release](https://ir.onterris.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/245/onterris-reports-second-quarter-results-updates-full-year-2026-guidance-and-announces-board-led-strategic-review-process) (mirrors 8-K exhibit, accession [0001193125-26-335022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001643615/000119312526335022/ont-ex99_1.htm)).

Two things follow, and they are the thesis:

**1. The profit cut is small and the revenue cut is mostly not about profit.** Revenue guidance went from $840–900M to $740–790M — a $105M cut at the midpoint, −12.1%. Profit guidance (adjusted EBITDA) went from $125–130M to $117–120M — a $9M cut, **−7.1%**. That means only about 8.6 cents of profit was lost per dollar of revenue lost. That is the arithmetic signature of removing revenue that was never earning much. About $85M of the $105M cut — 81% — sits in the two buckets the company *named*, and one of those buckets (pass-through) carries almost no profit at all.

**2. The emergency-response decline is a base effect, and the company said so.** The release states the quarter had "historically low environmental emergency response and related recovery service revenues," against "an abnormally high prior-year quarter driven by a single environmental event." That is one big accident in mid-2025 not repeating in mid-2026. It is weather and industrial-accident frequency, not customers leaving.

The operating evidence supports this. In Q2, the Consulting & Treatment segment lost 27% of its revenue ($171.7M → $125.6M) and its **profit margin went up**, 21.9% → 22.2%. Company-wide margin also rose, 16.9% → 17.1%. A business genuinely losing demand does not expand margin while shrinking that fast; a business shedding pass-through revenue does exactly that.

Meanwhile the market marked the equity down 17.5% on a 7.1% profit cut. At a constant multiple, a $9M profit cut is worth about $1.88 per share, roughly 10%. The extra ~7 points is the market re-rating the multiple and pricing a governance discount. That gap is what we would be buying — *if* the price ever gets to our band, and if the fourth-quarter question below gets answered.

**But we are not proposing to buy.** The verdict is flat, for the reasons in the next three sections.

## What the market believes

- **A roll-up whose model just broke.** The company bought its way to ~$830M of revenue and is now guiding revenue *down* 7.9% while still acquiring — which means the underlying, non-acquired business is shrinking faster than 7.9%. That reading is fair, and neither of our benches refuted it.
- **Management can't forecast.** The company **reiterated** full-year guidance on May 6th and cut it $105M on August 5th — nine weeks later. In between, it spent $29.987M buying back its own stock at roughly $18.75 a share, funded by drawing $67.213M on its credit line, while operating cash flow for the half was **negative $5.481M**. The stock is now $15.33. Management bought high with borrowed money, weeks before telling the market the year was worse than it had just confirmed.
- **The board is playing defense.** On the same night as the cut, the board announced a "comprehensive Board-led strategic review" with no deadline, and adopted a shareholder rights plan (a "poison pill") in response to what it described as undisclosed accumulation of shares and derivatives. A review with no end date plus a takeover defense is a combination the market reads as entrenchment.
- **"Adjusted" profit isn't real profit.** Roughly $38M a year of the "adjusted EBITDA" is stock issued to employees, added back. That is about 32% of the $118.5M guide. Add ~$25M of capital spending and the cash the business actually generates for owners is far smaller than the headline.
- **The balance sheet has no book value.** Goodwill and intangibles total $579.4M against total equity of $430.3M — tangible equity is roughly **negative $149M**. The market capitalization ($538M) is now below the carrying value of what the roll-up bought. A write-down is likely.

## Why we might have edge

Stated honestly, and ranked by how much we actually trust each one.

1. **We read the bridge; the tape read the headline.** The distinction between "revenue fell" and "near-zero-margin revenue fell" is in the release, in plain English, with dollar amounts. It requires no modeling — only reading past the first paragraph. Sell-side notes on print day led with the revenue miss and the 17.5% drop. This is a *small, real, and short-lived* edge.
2. **The emergency-response line is a lottery draw, and lotteries revert.** If the driver is "no single large environmental event this year," then the correct forecast for next year is the long-run event frequency, not zero. Both of our benches initially mis-framed this as a demand or a regulatory problem. It is neither.
3. **The third-quarter margin is guided, not guessed.** The company published Q3 guidance of $190–210M revenue at **17.0–18.0%** margin. At the midpoint that is $200M × 17.5% = **$35.0M** — versus $33.7M in Q3 2025 on $224.9M. Profit up ~4% on revenue down ~11%. If that prints, the mix story is confirmed on the tape rather than argued in a memo. Our red bench asserted "no source" for the second-half profit step while this published guide sat in the document it was quoting.
4. **A possible classification artifact.** ONT carries SIC code 8742 (management consulting), which routes it into consumer-discretionary screens despite being an environmental testing and remediation business. Both benches independently noticed this misrouted eight of our own detectors. If it also misroutes index and quantitative flows, part of the 51% drawdown may be a classification accident rather than a fundamental judgment. **We have not verified this** — it is a hypothesis, not a finding.
5. **The takeover overhang has a hard expiry we can date.** The rights plan is *limited-duration* and expires **August 4, 2027** (confirmed in the release). Our red bench called it entrenchment without disclosing this. A one-year limited-duration pill adopted in response to accumulation is the governance-acceptable form, not the abusive form. It caps the accumulator at 14.9% for twelve months — a real constraint, but a dated one.

## Why it might be priced in

This section is longer than the one above, which is itself informative.

- **Our own record on this exact setup is 0 for 7.** We have run the "market overreacted to a headline at a post-print dislocation" template seven times and lost or gone nowhere every time. The lesson we recorded was: *the headline is the news*. This pitch is that template. That prior should carry more weight than any single argument above.
- **The fourth quarter is unearned.** Full-year guidance is $117–120M. Subtract the first half (approximately $53M) and the guided third quarter ($35.0M) and roughly **$29–32M** must arrive in Q4 — against about $24M in Q4 2025, on a lower revenue base. That implies roughly 230–320 basis points of year-over-year margin expansion with no named source, worth about **$5–8M of profit, or $1.30–$2.05 per share at 9x**. This is the one objection that survived both benches, and it is real. It is also *smaller* than either bench claimed (see the table below).
- **A second cut is live.** The team that reiterated in May and cut in August is the same team standing behind the Q4 number. Their demonstrated forecast half-life is under one quarter.
- **On cash, not "adjusted profit," it is not cheap.** Enterprise value is roughly $874.7M ($538.4M market cap + ~$336.3M net debt). Against guided profit of $118.5M that is 7.4x. But subtract the ~$38M of stock compensation and ~$25M of capital spending that the adjusted number adds back, and you are paying about **15.8x** actual owner cash flow for a business with declining revenue. Our red bench's version of this (17x) used an inflated enterprise value, but the *direction* is correct and blue never rebutted it. This is red's best surviving point.
- **The cash statement is worse than the profit statement.** First-half operating cash flow was −$5.481M versus +$27.398M a year earlier. And that was *helped* by receivables, which were a $15.0M source of cash this year versus a $27.4M use last year. Strip that out and underlying cash generation deteriorated by roughly $59M year over year against a net loss deterioration of only $10.3M. **Nobody has explained the difference.**
- **Leverage limits the room to be wrong.** Net leverage is 3.20x against a 3.75x covenant — about 0.55 turns of headroom, or roughly a 15% shortfall in covenant profit before a breach. That is not imminent, but it is why any position here must be small.
- **A write-down is probably coming.** Market cap below the carrying value of acquired assets, after a 51% drawdown and a guidance cut, is a textbook trigger. Non-cash, but it will produce a headline and it is an admission the roll-up overpaid.

## What could go wrong

*(Pre-mortem. Three failure narratives, worst first. The second has no sensor we can build.)*

**Failure 1 — The mix story is true and the fourth quarter still misses.**
Everything in "What we believe" can be correct — pass-through is low-margin, emergency response is a base effect, segment margins held — and the year still comes in at $108–112M instead of $117–120M, because the Q4 margin step was always aspirational. Management then cuts again in November or guides 2027 below $120M. The stock goes to $12 and change; at $110M profit and 7x, fair value is about $12.35. We are down 15% from a $14.45 entry.
*Monitorable.* Tripwires: Q3 margin below 17.0%; Q3 revenue below $190M; any 8-K revising guidance. This is the failure we can see coming.

**Failure 2 — The unexplained cash drain repeats, and we never learn what it was. (NO TRIPWIRE.)**
About $59M of first-half cash deterioration is unaccounted for by receivables and unaccounted for by the widening loss. Nobody — not the filing's own discussion, not our red bench, not our blue bench — has named it. Our best guess is earnout payments to the owners of acquired businesses: the filing tags contingent-consideration liabilities in both current and long-term buckets, and those are cash obligations that never appear in "adjusted EBITDA." But that is a guess. If it is a recurring drag, the company is levered, cash-negative, and buying back stock into it, and the equity is worth far less than any multiple of adjusted profit suggests.

**We cannot arm a sensor on this, because we cannot name the line item.** You can build a tripwire on a number; you cannot build one on a residual. The unknown-unknown class is **undiagnosed cash residual — an unmodeled obligation sitting outside the metric the entire valuation is built on.** This class has a specific nasty property: it is invisible in every quarter until it is the only thing that matters, and adjusted-profit-based valuation is structurally blind to it by construction. The nearest thing to a partial sensor is reading the cash flow statement's working-capital detail in the Q3 10-Q — but if the drain is spread across "other," that read comes back empty too.

**Failure 3 — The structural and reflexive leg: our own order design, and a process we don't control.**

*Our size is not the risk.* At 0.25% of book the position is on the order of 1,500–3,000 shares against average daily volume of ~734,000 shares — under half a percent of one day's volume. We move nothing going in or out. Anyone citing liquidity as the risk here is wrong; we checked.

*Our order placement is the risk.* The band is $14.45. The tape is $15.33. The next earnings report is around November 4th. A resting good-till-cancelled buy at $14.45 would, in practice, fill **only** if bad news gaps the stock through it — we would be handing out free downside insurance and buying precisely the outcome we said kills the thesis. This is the accidental-short-volatility trap, and it is the single most likely way this specific proposal turns into a loss through pure mechanics. **The band must not be a resting order.** It arms manually, after the Q3 print, with the gates read.

*We would also be bidding into our own forecast.* We are simultaneously saying "a Q3 miss takes this below $14.45" and "buy at $14.45." Those are the same event. Bidding into flow you predicted is not a discount, it is adverse selection.

*Our upside is capped by other people's process.* The strategic review and the pill work together against us. The pill holds the accumulator at 14.9% until August 2027, removing the bidder who might otherwise force a price. The review, with no deadline, can land as a take-private struck off a depressed tape — we could be entirely right that fair value is $19–21 and get cashed out at $17. And the discount we are applying for management's disclosure quality (buying back stock into a guidance they were about to cut) is the same discount that makes our entry band unreachable at any price the market is likely to offer while the story is still working. The doctrine that protects us from a dishonest management is the doctrine that guarantees we mostly don't own it — that is the trade-off, and it is working as designed here.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we correctly identified a low-margin revenue mix effect the market ignored, and it turned out not to be the reason the stock fell — the market was pricing a levered roll-up with negative operating cash flow, an unexplained cash drain, a board on defense, and a management team that reiterated guidance nine weeks before cutting it, and every one of those was still true after our mix argument was proven right.**
