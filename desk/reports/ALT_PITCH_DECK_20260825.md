# PITCH — ALT — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Do not buy here at $3.145. Place a single 0.25%-of-book starter only if the stock trades below $2.70, or if one of two named de-overhang events happens first.

---

## What we believe

Altimmune is a small drug developer with one asset that matters: pemvidutide, an injectable now in a Phase 3 trial (PERFORMA) for MASH — fatty liver disease with inflammation and scarring. It has a second asset, the same drug repurposed for alcohol use disorder, which produced a clean mid-stage result and has an FDA meeting pending.

Three things we think are true and worth stating plainly:

1. **The company is funded for the trial it started.** $519M of cash and investments as of June 30, against a Phase 3 that reads out in 2029. That is real and we verified it from the primary source ([Q2 2026 results, 8/12/26](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/12/3343554/0/en/altimmune-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-business-update.html)).
2. **The prize, if the drug works, is far larger than today's price.** At a $645M market value, the stock roughly doubles even if the drug only ever reaches $500M of peak annual sales. It would be a 5x at what Wall Street currently models ($2.5B). The size of the win is not the problem here.
3. **The only real question is the odds and the wait.** Today's price implies roughly a 14–20% chance the drug succeeds. Our honest range for that probability is 15–25%. That makes the stock *fair to slightly cheap* — not the bargain our first pass claimed, and not expensive either.

We do not have a differentiated view on the science. We have a differentiated view on the *arithmetic* — specifically that both the bull case and the bear case circulating on this name are built on mis-marked numbers, and correcting them lands you in the middle.

## What the market believes

Wall Street is uniformly bullish and completely disbelieved by the tape. Consensus price targets sit at roughly $16.00–16.60; one broker (JMP) is at $25. Peak-sales models run $2.5B (consensus) to over $5B. The stock is $3.145 — down 51% from its 52-week high of $6.44, and only 23% above its 52-week low of $2.56.

So the market is not "unaware." The market has read the same research and marked the stock at roughly one-fifth of the average target. Translated: analysts are implicitly assuming something like a 40% chance of success; the tape is paying for about 15%. Everyone is looking at the same drug and disagreeing about the odds, not the facts.

There is also a structural reason the price is pinned. In April the company raised $225M by selling stock at $3.00 *with a warrant attached to every share* — 75M warrants struck at $3.00. Anyone who wants exposure to this drug bought it four months ago at a price one cent below today's, and got a free three-to-five-year call option on top. New buyers of common stock are buying the worse half of that deal.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly, less than we first thought. Here is what actually survives:

- **The warrants are mispriced by both camps, in opposite directions.** The warrants expire at the *earlier* of 2031 or **45 days after a successful Phase 3 readout** ([offering pricing PR, 4/23/26](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/23/3279527/0/en/altimmune-announces-pricing-of-225-million-oversubscribed-public-offering-of-securities.html)). That clause matters enormously and almost nobody prices it. Bulls value the warrants at zero. Bears value them as ordinary five-year options. Both are wrong: the good outcome kills the option 45 days after it happens, so the option is worth less than a five-year vanilla — but it also means the overhang *self-liquidates*, converting into $225M of cash for the company and then disappearing, rather than capping the stock for three years. Our mark: ~$101–124M, versus a bear mark of $149–174M and a bull mark of zero.
- **The 24-week / 52-week mismatch.** The Phase 2b biopsy result everybody quotes was measured at **24 weeks** (the peer-reviewed paper is literally titled "IMPACT: 24-week results," [PubMed 41237796](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41237796/)). The Phase 3 measures at **52 weeks**. Placebo patients in these trials get better over time on their own — a comparable 52-week trial (ESSENCE) showed 34.3% of placebo patients improving, versus the 19.1% placebo rate in the 24-week data. The margin that looks large at 24 weeks compresses on the endpoint that will actually decide approval. We searched published coverage for this specific argument and did not find it. This is the one genuinely non-obvious thing in the file, and it cuts *against* the stock.
- **The alcohol-use-disorder asset is unpriced by everyone, including us.** It hit its mid-stage endpoint (−1.45 heavy drinking days per week, p=0.0014; 42.2% of patients reached zero heavy drinking days versus 17.4% on placebo) and has an FDA end-of-Phase-2 meeting pending. Bears treat it purely as a future cash drain (the trial isn't funded). Bulls call it free. It is neither — it is a partnerable asset with a dated regulatory checkpoint, and it is the only near-term event in a stock whose central flaw is a 2029 timeline.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is.

- **The endpoint design we thought was insight is in a press release.** The Phase 3's "and/or" composite endpoint is the same construct used by the two approved/late-stage competitors. It's printed in the [trial initiation PR of 8/3/26](https://ir.altimmune.com/news-releases/news-release-details/altimmune-initiates-performa-phase-3-trial-pemvidutide-patients). Anyone who read one press release knows it. That was our claimed edge and it does not exist.
- **The cash is not a cushion, it's a budget.** The company guides runway "through" the 2029 readout — meaning it ends at roughly zero, not with money left over. Quarterly cash burn was about $17M when the Phase 3 was barely started; $519M over 3.4 years implies ~$153M/year. Burn roughly triples from here. And that budget *excludes* the confirmatory trial required for full approval, the unfunded alcohol Phase 3, and any sales force. More share issuance is structural, not optional.
- **Competitive position is fourth-in-line.** Resmetirom is approved. Semaglutide hit *both* co-primary endpoints in MASH. Pemvidutide has never shown biopsy-confirmed fibrosis improvement at any timepoint — the 48-week disclosure was based on non-invasive markers, not biopsy. Fibrosis is what drives labels and insurance coverage.
- **Zero commercial track record.** No approvals, no marketed products, ever. That is a legitimate discount for a company that would be fourth to market.

## What could go wrong

Three ways we lose money, ranked.

**1. The drug simply doesn't clear the bar at 52 weeks. (Most likely; no useful tripwire.)**
The Phase 2b win was at 24 weeks against a 19.1% placebo rate. At 52 weeks the placebo rate roughly doubles, and the endpoint that matters most — fibrosis improvement on biopsy — has never been demonstrated by this drug at any timepoint. The stock in that scenario is worth cash-minus-burn, call it under $1.50.

**This is the failure with no monitorable tripwire, and that is the honest problem with the whole position.** Between today and the 2029 readout there is essentially no disclosure plane. Enrollment quality, site selection, dropout rates, placebo-response drift, protocol amendments — all of the things that determine the answer are invisible from outside until the print. Quarterly filings will tell us cash and headcount, not whether the trial is working. **Unknown-unknown class: silent-process risk on a multi-year single-binary asset** — we would be holding an unobservable for three years and calling the interval "research." We cannot instrument our way out of this one, and we should not pretend a sensor list fixes it.

**2. Dilution arrives before the answer does. (Monitorable.)**
Runway ends *at* the readout, not after it. The confirmatory trial, the alcohol Phase 3, and any commercial build are all unfunded. The company has used an at-the-market program repeatedly (Leerink; earlier Evercore/JMP/B. Riley). Any bad tape gets met with more shares at a lower price. Tripwire: new S-3/424B5 filings, ATM usage disclosed in the 10-Q, share count creeping above 194.5M common.

**3. Reflexive and structural: we would be buying the exact dip we predicted, in the exact zone we said supply lives. (Partly monitorable — and one leg is unverified.)**
Our proposed entry is "below $2.70." The entire reason we picked that number is a belief that holders of the April warrants hedge by shorting stock, creating mechanical supply that pushes the price down there. **Neither bench verified that premise.** Nobody pulled short interest, borrow cost, or the composition of the April book. The pack shows four 13G filings clustered 5/01–5/15/26, which is the signature of long-only institutional buyers — and long-only buyers do *not* delta-hedge. If the April book is long-only, there is no mechanical supply, $2.70 never prints, and our gate is simply a way of never owning the stock while telling ourselves we're disciplined. Conversely, if it *is* hedged supply, we would be stepping in front of a flow we ourselves forecast — which is exactly the mistake we have a rule against.

On our own footprint: a 0.25% starter in a $645M-market-cap name is small in absolute terms, but we have **not** verified average daily volume for this ticker, so we cannot yet state our order as a fraction of it. That check is owed before any order is staged, not after.

Doctrine interactions worth naming: we cannot treat this as a "fairly-paid risk" starter, because fairness here rests on a success-probability estimate neither bench actually sourced — it is an assumption, not a verified input. And the "clean court gets a minimum starter" default does not apply, because the bear bench produced a specific, named, decisive objection (the 24-week/52-week basis shift). Separately, a position bought today and held to a 2029 readout is a three-year commitment; averaging down turns one small mistake into a large slow one, which is why the proposal is a single rung and not a ladder.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that pemvidutide's 24-week efficacy margin does not survive to the 52-week biopsy endpoint against a placebo arm that heals on its own — and we will have no way to see it coming, because there is no disclosure between now and the 2029 print.**
