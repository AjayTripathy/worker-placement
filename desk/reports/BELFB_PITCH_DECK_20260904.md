# PITCH — BELFB — VERDICT

## What we believe

Bel Fuse makes unglamorous electrical parts — power conversion units, magnetics, connectors — and one of its product lines has been pulled into the AI data-center build. The business is genuinely accelerating, and I can show that from the company's own tagged filing data rather than from a press release:

|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 2025 | $168.299M | $103.216M | 38.67% |
| Q2 2026 | $210.685M | $126.718M | 39.85% |

(Source: SEC XBRL company-concept API, [`RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax`](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0000729580/us-gaap/RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax.json) and [`CostOfGoodsAndServicesSold`](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0000729580/us-gaap/CostOfGoodsAndServicesSold.json), CIK 729580.)

Revenue up 25.2% year over year with gross margin up 1.2 points. First half revenue $389.2M against $320.5M, up 21.4%. The balance sheet went from $197.5M of long-term debt to zero and from $57.8M of cash to $306.1M. The company is guiding the September quarter to $205–225M of revenue at 39–41% gross margin, which would be another step up.

We believe this is a real operating improvement that the stock has not been given full credit for, and that the stock is roughly 20% cheaper than the recognized bellwether of the same theme (Vertiv) on the same measuring stick.

We also believe — and this is the part the courts did not settle — that 20% is *not* 20% of edge. Most of it is payment for real, permanent differences between Bel and Vertiv. What is left over is maybe five to eight points of price. That is a starter-sized idea bought on a pullback, not a position bought at market.

## What the market believes

The market believes the ramp is real and has been raising its numbers. Consensus 2026 earnings per share went from $8.42 to $8.94 over ninety days, across seven analysts, with a range of $7.44 to $10.04. At $247 the stock is 27.6x that consensus.

The market also believes Bel deserves less than Vertiv, which trades at 34.3x forward. It is pricing in, plausibly: that a large share of the growth was bought (Enercon, acquired 80% in November 2024) rather than built; that three of eight manufacturing plants are in China and one is in Israel; that the company is a fraction of Vertiv's size and far less liquid; that the founding family controls it through a two-class share structure; and that in May the company sold 1,725,000 new shares at $266, increasing the share count roughly 14%, and the stock has traded below that price ever since.

The stock is 26% below its 52-week high of $335.29 and 90% above its 52-week low of $129.85. This is not a forgotten company. It is a violently re-rated one that has given some back.

## Why we might have edge

**1. The screens are literally wrong on this ticker.** Bel has two share classes and the data vendors compute them off inconsistent share counts. On the same vendor, the same day, the same single stream of earnings shows a forward P/E of 37.33 on the B shares and 28.01 on the A shares — a 33% gap on a 19% price gap. Anyone running a screen off vendor multiples gets a wrong answer here in either direction. That is a small, real, structural reason the price could be off.

**2. The minority-interest line is quietly marking the acquired business up.** The redeemable minority interest — the accounting home of the 20% of Enercon that Bel does not own — stepped from $93.2M to $102.6M in six months. That number moves with Enercon's own results. It is telling us the acquired business is performing, and management has said in the 10-Q that it currently intends to buy the rest by early 2027. Buying out your minority partner before a re-rating is the behaviour of people who think it is worth more than the carrying value. That is a filing-visible signal that almost no screen reads.

**3. Inventory is not bloating — now confirmed on tagged data, not estimated.** This mattered because a components maker whose inventory outruns its sales is usually about to disappoint. Days of inventory: 145.2 days in Q2 2025 versus 143.8 days in Q2 2026. Inventory grew 21.6% while revenue grew 25.2%. The blue bench reached this conclusion using an *estimated* cost-of-sales figure; I checked whether the estimate was load-bearing (it was — the conclusion flips if Q2 2025 cost of sales exceeded $104.1M) and pulled the tagged number ($103.216M). It holds, with about half a point of margin to spare.

**4. The auditor was upgraded, cleanly, and nobody noticed.** Neither bench looked. Bel dismissed Grant Thornton and engaged Deloitte & Touche, announced December 2025 and effective 24 February 2026 after Grant Thornton finished the 2025 audit. No adverse or qualified opinions, no disagreements, no reportable events ([8-K/A, 2026-02-24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/729580/000143774926005358/belfa20260213_8ka.htm)). Going up an audit tier, in the orderly sequence, right after a $442M raise, is a mild positive that no price screen captures.

## Why it might be priced in

**1. The original claim was arithmetic that did not survive contact.** The brief said Bel trades at 22x against Vertiv's 34.3x. It got 22x by taking one quarter of *adjusted* earnings and multiplying by four. That implies full-year earnings of $10.83 per share — above the single highest analyst estimate of $10.04. On consensus, the number is 27.6x. The 40% gap that put this name in court does not exist. It is about 20%, and the red bench's kill on this point stands.

**2. The strong balance sheet and the overhang are the same event.** The cash did not come from operations; it came from selling stock at $266 to buyers who are now down 7%. And $102.6M of the $306.1M cash is already claimed by the Enercon minority buyout, plus up to $10M of earnout. Real enterprise value is about $3.37B, not $3.16B. That is a 3.4% adjustment — small, but it moves in the wrong direction and it is definite.

**3. We would be buying the expensive share class, and neither bench drew the obvious conclusion.** BELFB (non-voting) trades around $247. BELFA (voting) trades around $207. Same company, same earnings, and the non-voting share is 19% *more* expensive because it holds the float and the index eligibility. The dividend does not explain this: the filing's per-share dividend tags show $0.06 on Class A and $0.07 on Class B per quarter — four cents a year of difference, which cannot be worth forty dollars of price. So here is the part the court missed entirely: **BELFA at $207 is 23.2x consensus, which is a 32% discount to Vertiv — and that satisfies the red bench's own stated condition for reopening the case (a same-basis gap of 30% or more).** The cheap expression of this thesis is the share class we are not proposing to buy. I am flagging this rather than recommending it, because BELFA has never been courted: I do not know its daily volume, its index status, or whether the discount is a permanent feature. Buying A instead of B swaps a 20% peer-discount bet for a 32% peer-discount bet *plus* an uncourted bet that a 19% intra-company spread narrows. That deserves its own short court, not a footnote in this one.

**4. The thesis is relative and the trade is absolute.** The whole argument is "Bel is cheap *versus Vertiv*." The proposed expression is simply owning Bel. If the entire AI-capital-spending cohort de-rates 30%, the discount can close perfectly and we still lose 30%. Neither bench raised this. It is the single biggest structural problem with the trade as constructed.

**5. Consensus has already moved 6% in ninety days.** We are not early to the earnings revision. We would be arguing about the multiple, which is a slower and less reliable thing to be right about.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — we were right and lost money anyway (the cohort de-rates).** *This is the reflexive/structural leg.* The position is a relative view worn as an outright long. Vertiv's 34.3x is the anchor for every price in this deck; if that anchor moves, every target moves with it and nothing in our monitoring would flag the thesis as broken — the discount would look fine right up to the drawdown. It compounds with our own doctrine: our value-ladder rule says never bid into flow we ourselves predict, and we *do* predict flow here (the underwater $266 buyers, roughly 14% of the float, against $30M of daily volume). So our $225 limit is a bid into a decline we are forecasting. We handle that below by refusing to arm the first tranche until the supply goes quiet, but the honest description is that our entry price and our flow forecast are in tension. On size: 0.35% of the book against $30M average daily volume is nothing — at a 1% participation cap that is a one-day fill. **We are not the flow. We are downstream of it.** The same is emphatically *not* true of the BELFA sidecar, where our clip could be a meaningful share of a day.

**Failure narrative 2 — the disclosure we are waiting for never arrives, and the discount just persists.** The bull case leans on management volunteering a backlog dollar figure or a segment margin at the 10 September fireside or the October print. They have chosen not to disclose either for six quarters while repeating an unquantified "bookings above sales" claim on calls. A company that has kept a number private for six quarters is more likely to keep it private for a seventh. Then we own a fairly-priced components maker with no re-rating catalyst and a live equity overhang, and we are simply wrong about the timetable rather than the facts. This has a tripwire (the calendar) but no fix.

**Failure narrative 3 — an end customer we cannot name stops buying. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** Bel does not disclose customers. The data-center ramp almost certainly runs through a small number of programs at one or two large buyers. If one of those buyers dual-sources the part, re-specs the design, or pauses a build, we find out in an earnings print, after the price has moved. There is no instrument that sees this coming: the company files nothing about it; the three satellite/site detectors the court consulted all came back unusable (every Chinese location in our data is a city or province centre point, not a plant boundary, and the one plant the matcher did resolve — Dongguan — makes magnetics, not the product in question); the workplace-safety database covers only the two US sites, which are the wrong segment. **Unknown-unknown class: undisclosed customer concentration and design-socket loss — the private commercial-relationship plane, for which this desk has no observation channel at all.** A close relative sits on the same plane: what share of cost of goods originates in the Chinese plants, which determines the tariff exposure. Also unobservable, also only visible after the fact.

**Other named risks, monitorable:** a second equity raise at a lower price; Deloitte's first-year audit (fiscal 2026, opinion due around February 2027) turning up a restatement — a non-reliance filing would be close to fatal to the position; the Enercon buyout pricing well above the $102.6M carried value and consuming the cash; four separate Item 5.02 officer/director filings in seven months, which we have not read.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ that the entire AI-capital-spending cohort de-rated, so Bel closed its discount to Vertiv exactly as we predicted and we still lost money, because we expressed a relative view as an outright long.
