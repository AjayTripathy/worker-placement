What the market believes vs. what we believe

What the market believes

A structurally declining UK specialist-media publisher whose organic revenue is falling and whose audience is being eroded by AI search — an ex-growth business. Down ~62% from its 2024 high (785p → 298p).

What we believe

A real cash machine — 109% cash conversion, no goodwill impairment — with an identifiable value-closer: the company has retired 22% of its shares since 2023 and a fifth buyback is live. The AI-search bear is overstated (only ~16% of revenue is website-session-correlated; Go.Compare price-comparison, magazines and subscriptions are session-independent). But organic revenue is still −6% and hasn't inflected, so the margin of safety is thin at market and only real below.

The decisive finding — a discount with a closer, not a value trap

The buyback satisfies the "no-actor" test: someone is actively closing this discount. Future has bought back 22% of its shares since 2023 and is ~25% of the way through a fifth (£30m) programme, returning £52.9m to holders in the most recent half. The share count fell from ~114m (end-2024) toward ~91m (mid-2026). Free cash flow is real — adjusted operating cash inflow £101.5m less £10.4m capex = £91.1m adjusted free cash flow, a 109% conversion to adjusted EBITDA — and the statutory-vs-adjusted gap is non-cash amortisation of acquired intangibles (~£25.8m), not an impairment. On the 270p limit this is ~3.5–4× after-tax free cash flow with a live buyback tailwind and a price-comparison floor. Unlike a value trap, this discount has a company-shaped agent working to close it.

The honest catches

Catch 1 — the order size is wrong, and it is load-bearing. The plan reads "BUY 185 shares @ 270p GTC ~$5k / 0.5%." Those numbers do not reconcile: 185 shares × 270p (£2.70) = £499.50 ≈ $670, which is ~0.07% of a $1M base — roughly 7.5× too small for the stated $5k / 0.5% half-starter. The price unit is unambiguously pence (the 52-week range of 271–785 is Future's real pence trajectory; the ordinary shares are £0.15 nominal), so 270p = £2.70/share is correct and the share count is the error. A genuine ~$5k / 0.5% half-starter at 270p needs ~1,400 shares (still trivial — ~0.24% of the £2.1M daily volume). Which was intended — the ~$5k size (→ ~1,400 shares) or a token ~$670 position (→ keep 185 and relabel) — is a principal decision on a real order, not something to change silently.
Catch 2 — the buyback is funded into rising leverage, so the tailwind is capacity-constrained. In the most recent half, adjusted free cash flow was £91.1m, yet the company returned £52.9m to holders and did a £39.9m SheerLuxe bolt-on, drawing £54m of new financing — so net debt rose from £276.4m to £314.1m and leverage went 1.3× → 1.6× in a single half. The £52.9m return did not come out of surplus cash after distributions; it leaned on the balance sheet alongside the acquisition. Management has explicitly pivoted to reducing net debt in H2 and managing toward 1×, and the slow buyback pace (only ~25% through the fifth programme) is consistent with that deleveraging priority. This does not break the thesis — the cash generation and the buyback closer are both real — but the "returns machine" is currently competing with an M&A appetite, and there is only ~0.2× of headroom to the 1.8× kill line. Separately, >100% conversion is not fully sustainable: FY26 is guided to ~90% conversion, and the 109% half benefited from favourable working-capital phasing.

Why this is a below-market watch, not a buy-at-market

Valuation frame. Bear 200p (35%) / base 360p (45%) / bull 520p (20%) → probability-weighted ~330p, only about +11% versus the ~298p tape. That is insufficient margin of safety at market. At the 270p limit it becomes favourable — ~3.5–4× after-tax free cash flow plus the buyback tailwind and a price-comparison floor. This is a risk-premium-plus-buyback-optionality position, not a hard mispricing until organic revenue inflects — hence a patient below-market bid, not a market order.

Tripwires

  • The swing factor: the Q3 trading update and the FY26 preliminary results (~December 2026) — organic-revenue inflection is the whole question. The Q1→Q2 improvement (−7% → −5%) is the early tell to watch.
  • Kill conditions: a goodwill / consumer-division (B2C-CGU) impairment; the buyback paused or leverage above 1.8×; the organic decline widening past mid-single-digit; Go.Compare car-insurance revenue re-accelerating downward.
  • The leverage watch (from Catch 2): H2 net-debt reduction toward the 1× goal is the confirmation that the buyback tailwind stays open; continued leverage creep while buying back is the warning.
