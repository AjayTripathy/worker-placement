Say the awkward part first. This is the smallest kind of yes. Four of the five kills we overturned failed the same way — the bear case read one side of a two-sided disclosure — and a pattern like that is what breaks an 8/10 rejection, not brilliance on our part. What survived is real and we are carrying it: the second-half margin promise is untested until November, and the company has almost no contracted backlog. That is why the position is one third of normal size rather than sized to the conviction.

We are buying a business whose reason for falling has already reversed, at a price that has not noticed. We are not buying a bargain.

Pane 1 · What we believe

The break has a date, the cause has reversed, the price hasn't

Bentley broke on November 5, 2025: third-quarter recurring-revenue growth printed 10.5% in constant currency, down from 11.5%, and the stock fell 8.1% then 7.6% in two weeks against a software index down 5.2% and 1.9%. That was genuinely Bentley's own problem — conceded without argument.

Since then the same metric has printed 11.5%, 11.5%, and now 12.0% — the highest figure in the disclosed series, 150 basis points above the number that broke the stock. Account retention held at 99% and net retention at 109% the entire way through. The stock is still 32% below its pre-break level.

That is the whole thesis in one sentence: a dated, identifiable cause of a drawdown, which has since gone away, on a price that still reflects it. Everything else in this document defends that sentence or bounds what happens if it is wrong.

Pane 2 · What the market believes

AI takes the engineers, the meter takes the hit

That software companies selling seats to engineers are about to lose those seats to AI — and that Bentley, infrastructure design software priced on how much engineers use it, sits directly in the path.

The sharp version deserves stating properly: Bentley's largest product line is not a seat licence at all. It is a meter that charges on how many distinct users open the applications each day. A seat licence survives to renewal; a daily meter falls immediately. On that reading Bentley is more headcount-exposed than a normal software company, not less. The mechanism is right. The reading of it, we think, is wrong — and the market's belief is coherent, widely held, and is what is keeping the price here.

Pane 3 · Why we might have edge

The sensor for the feared damage is published quarterly — and nobody reads it

The metric everyone watches — 109% net retention — is a twelve-month backward average, structurally incapable of detecting a hazard customers are only "beginning to apply." But the usage meter itself is recognised on actual daily access and disclosed every quarter with at most one quarter of lag. It reads +12.4% year-over-year in the June quarter, against total revenue +12.8%, on a line that is 46% of recurring revenue.

Four-plus quarters into the AI-displacement story, the metric that would collapse first is growing in line with the company. The gap between what the market watches (twelve months of lag) and what actually measures the risk (one quarter) is roughly three quarters of lead time. That is the edge — and the exit: the same line tells us first if we are wrong.

Pane 4 · Why it might be priced in

A fair price for a compounder, not a distressed asset

At $35.36 the enterprise is worth $11.74B against free cash flow guided to $500–570M — about 22× the midpoint, a 5.0% cash yield, roughly 26× adjusted earnings. Against 12% growth, 99% retention, 93% recurring revenue and a 31% cash-flow margin, that is cheap relative to Bentley's own history and to the reversal in its growth — and not cheap in absolute terms. If the reversal turns out to be priced after all, we own a fairly-valued business at a fair price. The two live problems — the untested second-half margin promise and the near-absence of contracted backlog — are handled in the plan below.

The finding the pro-stock bench filed against its own side

Bentley has almost no contracted backlog. Committed future revenue is $280.6M — about 19% of one year's revenue, 94% of it inside twelve months, and it declined sequentially while revenue grew. Durability here is behavioural (99% account retention, 109% net retention, a $496M prepaid balance), not contractual. That is a legitimate reason to own less of this than of a comparably-growing company with a committed book — and it is the single clearest reason the position is capped at one third.
