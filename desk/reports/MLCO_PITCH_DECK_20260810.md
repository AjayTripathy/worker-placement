# PITCH — MLCO — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY before the August 13 earnings report. Stay flat through the print, then re-screen August 14 with a pre-staged buy plan near $4.40 that only activates if specific conditions (listed below) are met.** Both the prosecution and defense benches independently landed on flat-before-the-print; they disagree on why, and the why matters for what we do afterward.

## What we believe

Melco Resorts (MLCO) is a Macau casino operator whose US-listed shares have fallen 46% from their 52-week high to $5.46, sitting 7.7% above the 52-week low. We believe the stock is not obviously mispriced today, but it is set up to *become* mispriced within days, in a way we can prepare for now:

1. **The recent Macau revenue weakness is largely explained and largely temporary.** June market-wide gaming revenue fell 12.1% year-over-year, but the World Cup (which ended July 19) pulled Chinese gamblers away from tables, and July was down a milder 8.4%. Year-to-date through July the Macau market is still *up* 4.4%. Wynn's management said on August 5 that volumes recovered in late July and early August. This looks like a dated, explainable dip — not structural decline.
2. **Reported quarterly results in this industry are partly a luck lottery.** Casino "hold" — the percentage of money wagered that the house actually keeps versus the statistical expectation — swings reported earnings violently quarter to quarter even when underlying customer demand is fine. Sands China reported earnings down 24% *despite* customer wagering volumes growing 15%+, purely because luck ran bad; Wynn Macau reported earnings up 17% because luck ran good. MLCO's August 13 report is therefore a coin flip on optics, not a predictable miss.
3. **The genuinely scary thing is the founder's margin loan.** The controlling shareholder has pledged 677.4 million shares — essentially 98.5% of the control block — as collateral for a $1 billion loan facility. At $5.46 the collateral is worth about $1.23 billion, only 1.23x the loan. A ~19% drop in the stock (to roughly $4.40) puts collateral value equal to the loan. A bad-luck earnings print could gap the stock toward exactly that level, potentially triggering forced selling that feeds on itself. That is both the reason not to hold through the print **and** the reason a post-print crash could overshoot into a real buying opportunity.

Net: we don't own it, we shouldn't own it before Thursday's report, and we should be ready to buy weakness *if and only if* the weakness is a luck-driven headline miss with healthy underlying demand and the margin-loan facts check out.

## What the market believes

The market treats MLCO as a fairly priced, leveraged, weaker sibling in a soft Macau cohort. This is not an ignored stock: it is heavily covered by sell-side analysts, and Morgan Stanley's Equal-weight rating carries a $6.00 target — only ~10% above the current price, i.e., the Street sees it as roughly fair. The whole cohort is marked down, not just MLCO: Wynn is 24% off its high and Las Vegas Sands 35.5% off its high (2.8% above its low). MLCO's deeper 46% drawdown is approximately what you'd expect from applying its higher debt load (~4x net leverage; roughly 35–40% equity move per 10% change in operating earnings) to the same industry news. Crucially, the "disappointing quarter" inputs are already public: Sands China reported July 23 and Wynn Macau August 5, so the flat-market, luck-hurt-earnings story has been in the price for two to three weeks. Options are pricing the August 13 report as a major event (implied volatility around its 98th percentile).

## Why we might have edge

- **We decomposed luck from demand where the headline series doesn't.** The government's monthly revenue figure (from Macau's gaming regulator, DICJ) measures casino *winnings*, which luck distorts. The peer reports let us see *wagering volume* — actual customer demand — separately, and demand grew (Sands mass-market wagering +15%, Wynn mass wagering +5.5%). If MLCO prints an ugly headline on bad luck with growing volumes, headline-reading sellers may hand us a mechanical, explainable dislocation.
- **We priced the margin-loan trigger to a specific level.** From the July 10 Schedule 13D/A: 677.36 million pledged shares × $1.48/share ≈ $1.0 billion, i.e., collateral equals the loan at about **$4.40**. Few headline reactions are computed against that line; forced-selling overshoots below fundamental value are exactly the kind of technical seller we like buying from — but only with the facts verified first.
- **We caught our own first-draft error before it cost money.** Our prosecution bench initially argued the weak June revenue made an earnings miss "mechanical." Our defense bench overturned that by showing the revenue series is luck-contaminated — the exact confusion we're counting on other sellers to make on August 14.
- **The pledge status question is resolved, against the optimists.** The loan facility nominally matured in June 2026; the July 10 13D/A describes it in the present tense ("is secured by…"), and 13D filers must promptly amend on material changes. So we treat the pledge as live — no wishful "maybe it expired."

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands we say this plainly:

- **The bad quarter is old news.** Peer results made the flat market and luck problems public weeks ago. $5.46 already reflects them; the August 13 residual is MLCO-specific luck and market share — a symmetric coin flip, not a skewed short or a skewed long.
- **This is not a neglected orphan.** Heavy analyst coverage and a consensus near-fair target mean there is no "nobody's looking" story. Our own defense bench conceded the stock fails as a simple hold-for-the-yield candidate, and the prosecution's demand to strike the "lean real candidate" label was accepted by both benches.
- **The market may be repricing something real.** MLCO's excess drawdown versus peers is roughly what its leverage justifies. If Macau demand disappoints post-World Cup (August revenue is the test), the current price is correct or generous.
- **The margin loan cuts both ways.** It creates the overshoot opportunity, but until the Q2 filing confirms the facility's terms and status, buying a gap-down means buying *in front of* a possible forced seller, not behind one.
