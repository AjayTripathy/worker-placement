## What we believe

BillionToOne runs a blood test that reads fetal DNA out of a pregnant patient's plasma. The core product, Unity, tries to answer a harder question than the standard prenatal blood test: not "does the fetus have an extra chromosome" (settled science, everyone does it) but "did the fetus inherit two copies of a specific broken gene" — cystic fibrosis, spinal muscular atrophy, sickle cell. Doing that from a blood draw, with no sample from the father, is genuinely novel. It is also genuinely hard, and the difficulty is arithmetic: at a typical 8% fetal fraction, the entire signal separating an affected fetus from an unaffected one is a four-percentage-point shift in an allele ratio that sits at 50%.

We believe four things about the business, and they are in tension:

1. **The financial complaint that started this file was wrong.** We originally believed the price per test was rolling over — $571 down to $551. It isn't. The 10-Q shows the company booked $2.8M of prior-period revenue in Q2 and $12.0M in the first half, meaning Q1 carried $9.2M. Strip the timing noise out of both quarters and the price per test *rose* about $15, and revenue on clean, current-period business grew 7.5% sequentially rather than 0.9%. Our own thesis died on its own evidence. ([10-Q, revenue recognition note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2070849/000207084926000050/blln-20260630.htm))
2. **The business underneath is real and better than a typical recent IPO.** 70% gross margin, positive operating income ($5.5M in Q2), positive free cash flow ($5.1M), $548.6M cash against $91.0M debt — $457.6M net cash. First-half revenue grew 74% year over year. This is not a story stock burning money.
3. **There is a lawsuit nobody in the original file had opened.** Illumina sued in Delaware on 2026-05-07 asserting three patents that read on the counting mechanism itself, and it is asking for an injunction against the product line that is 86% of revenue. Illumina has run this play to a conclusion three times before in prenatal testing.
4. **We do not have a differentiated number.** Our corrected view of the second half lands inside the company's own guidance and within half a percent of the Street's $455.6M. There is no forecast here that the market doesn't already have.

Put together: a good company, at a price that already reflects it, with an unpriced legal option attached, and no analytical edge on our side. That is a decline, not a short.

## What the market believes

The market believes the August 5th print was a real deterioration and priced it as one — the stock fell 38.9% in a day, from roughly $150 to the low $90s. Nine analysts carry FY26 revenue at $455.6M against company guidance of $450–465M, and FY27 at $576.3M (+26.5%). The average price target is $119.71; the lowest is $90 — below where it trades now.

What the market is *not* obviously pricing is the Illumina case. So whatever the market repriced on August 6th, it was not the patent risk. The patent risk has been sitting in the tape, unexamined, at $150 and at $88 alike.

The market also appears to be treating a headline price-per-test decline as an operating fact rather than an accounting artifact. That is the one place a real difference of opinion might live — except that management explained the artifact correctly on the call, and the Street's revenue estimate already reflects the corrected picture. Being right about a number everyone else also has is not worth money.

## Why we might have edge

Three candidates, in descending order of honesty:

**The strongest is the legal tail, and it is a *sizing* edge, not a forecasting one.** Two of our own reviews and the original screen all missed the Illumina case entirely; a professional file on this name was written twice without opening the legal proceedings note. If that is how our desk behaved, it is plausible the sell-side models behave the same way — none of the nine estimates has a line for a royalty. We are not claiming to know the outcome. **Second: the pattern-recognition catch on the price band.** Our first reviewer looked at the stock being 59.9% above its 52-week low and concluded it was mid-range, not dislocated. **Third, and weakest: the science.** We read the primary literature harder than a revenue model would. The company's stated limitation says "rare homozygous variants" — F508del is over 60% of US CF variants. That is a durable reason two national payers and the obstetrics society decline to endorse the test as a primary screen, and it is a slow-acting risk no financial model carries. But it is also not a catalyst, and we cannot date it.

**Tax overlay.** 2026 is a harvest year with a large realized gain to offset.
