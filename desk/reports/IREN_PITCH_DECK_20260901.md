# PITCH — IREN — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-16 — HOLD the existing half-starter (116 shares @ $44.41 cost, ~$4,308 at the $37.14 tape, unrealized −$843); NO ADD and NO EXIT: two of the three 8/27 gates passed, the funding gate did not, and on the company's own fully-delivered numbers the fair value lands at roughly today's price.**

*Housekeeping note before anything else: the adjudication is dated 2026-08-16, which is eleven days **before** the FY26 print. Both benches below are post-print and carry facts the adjudicator did not have (the $25–30bn capex guide above all). The 8/16 ruling's **disposition** still governs — nothing licenses more than a half position until the three gates pass — but several of its **numbers** are superseded, and I flag each one rather than quietly restating it.*

## What we believe

IREN builds and rents out data-center capacity for AI computing. It is walking away from bitcoin mining — which was essentially all of its profit — and replacing it with long-term contracts to rent GPU capacity to large customers, Microsoft among them.

Three things we believe, in order of how much weight they carry:

1. **The delivery story is real, not promotional.** The bear narrative going into the print was that the contracts were vapor. They are not. Customer prepayments actually landed in the bank: deferred revenue went from $0.9M to $120.4M, and signed contracts total $2.8bn. The first site was accepted by the customer and started producing revenue — the AI cloud segment more than doubled quarter-over-quarter, from $33.6M to $70.5M. The definition of the headline "annual recurring revenue" figure was *not* quietly loosened at the print, which was the specific thing we were watching for.
2. **But the prize, valued honestly, is roughly the price we are paying.** If you take the company's own target — $4bn of contracted annualized revenue, fully delivered — and value it at margins comparable to its closest public peer, you get something between "today's price" and "modestly above today's price," depending entirely on how you charge for the cost of replacing GPUs and for the shares that will be issued to pay for it all. The two benches landed at $37/share and $50/share respectively from the *same* revenue assumption. The whole gap between them is the capital charge.
3. **The upside that would make this interesting is not funded, not contracted, and structurally capped.** Everything above ~$50 requires the 2027 expansion (roughly 1.2 gigawatts) to be built and rented, and that expansion is neither under contract nor paid for. Meanwhile the shares needed to pay for it are already promised: $2.3bn of convertible bonds convert at $51.40 (15% above the tape) and NVIDIA holds warrants for 30M shares at $70. The two places the stock would have to reach are the two places new supply arrives.

**So: we hold a small position because the company is doing what it said, and we do not add because we would be paying full price for a result that requires a second, unfunded miracle.**

Separately and honestly: on 8/16 the principal elected to run this as a named, graded **event bet** — a large short base (22–32% of the tradable shares) sitting on a story already disproven, meeting a confirmed catalyst. That bet has now been graded. See the table below. It half-won.

## What the market believes

The market believes two contradictory things at once, which is why the stock fell 8% after hours on good delivery news.

- **Seventeen analysts, average price target ~$80, mostly "Buy."** They believe the ramp happens: consensus revenue for the fiscal year ending June 2027 is roughly $2.99bn against $707M actually earned in the year just reported. That is a 4x jump in four quarters, and about 10x on the AI segment specifically.
- **Somewhere between 22% and 32% of the tradable shares are sold short** (about 93.7M shares). That side believes the funding does not clear, the shareholders get diluted into oblivion, or both.

The *revealed* belief — what the tape actually did on 8/27 — is the interesting one. The company reaffirmed $4bn of contracted revenue, dated its next three sites, and announced a new frontier-lab customer. All bullish. The stock closed $40.53 (+2.4%), then fell 8.2% after hours to ~$37.19 once the slides were read. What moved it was not the profit-and-loss statement. It was the capital plan: **$25–30bn of spending guided for the coming year, against a company worth about $14.5bn.**

The market's real position is therefore: *we believe the demand, we do not know who pays for it, and every new contract makes that question bigger, not smaller.* That is a coherent and, in our view, correct reading.

## Why we might have edge

Four candidates, ranked by how much I actually believe them.

1. **We read the primary documents and the loudest bear claim is simply false.** The "contracts are fake / revenue isn't landing" story is refuted by prepayment cash in the balance sheet and by an audited doubling of segment revenue. A large short base is positioned on a thesis that the filings have already disproven. That is a genuine information advantage over the marginal seller — *but see the grading table; it did not pay this time.*
2. **We caught our own arithmetic before it cost us.** Our first pass killed this on a valuation table built with stale cash, and the correction was worth about $3.7bn of enterprise value — the difference between "no upside at all" and "+36%." Being able to overturn our own kill is the edge; see the self-check table.
3. **We are watching the right variable.** Both benches converged, independently, on the same binding constraint: share count, not revenue. Shares outstanding went from 283.5M to 394.1M in nine months. Nobody's price target is expressed per-share-adjusted for that. If we track the denominator on every filing cover, we will know before consensus does whether the upside accrues to us or to the next tranche of buyers.
4. **Weak / honest caveat:** the "we can see the buildout from space" edge does not exist here. Our satellite check returned nothing usable — the coordinates were town centroids rather than the actual parcels, and the most recent usable images are eight to nine months old. We have **no independent ground truth** on construction. The entire delivery schedule rests on company press releases. That is the opposite of edge and I am not going to dress it up.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: mostly, it is.

- **The prize table lands at the tape.** Charge the business for replacing GPUs on a five-year life, for interest on $7.6bn of debt, and for the shares that will be issued to fund the plan, and $4bn of delivered revenue at ~65% margins is worth about today's price. The valuation only clears +50% above roughly 67–68% margins with zero slippage, full delivery, and only modest dilution.
- **Seventeen analysts cover it and the average target is more than double the price.** This is not an undiscovered name. It is a crowded battleground with heavy positioning on both sides. Crowding does not tell us direction, but it does tell us we are not early.
- **We agree with the bulls about the trajectory.** Our disagreement is entirely about financing friction — which is exactly the thing the short base is already paid to worry about. When our disagreement with consensus is the same disagreement the shorts already hold, we are not contributing an independent view.
- **The good news that would re-rate it has already printed** and was met with an 8% decline.

## What could go wrong

*(the pre-mortem)*

**Failure narrative 1 — "The prize was real and it went to the new shareholders." (Most likely. Monitorable.)**
The 2027 expansion gets built and rented, revenue hits $4bn and then $10bn, and the stock still does nothing, because paying for $25–30bn of construction against a $14.5bn company required issuing 40–60% more shares. We are right about the business and wrong about our claim on it. This is the single most probable way we lose. Documented behavior supports it: share count is up 32–39% in nine months (the two sources disagree — see unverified), and the company has visibly issued into strength. **Tripwire: shares-outstanding on every filing cover, plus any prospectus supplement (424B5/424B7). Both are cheap and both currently exist only partially — see missing sensors.**

**Failure narrative 2 — "The AI capital-spending regime broke and took the whole complex with it." (Second most likely. Weakly monitorable.)**
A hyperscaler signals a digestion pause, the entire rent-a-GPU complex re-rates, and IREN — which is a leveraged, unfunded expression of that single factor — falls 50% on news that has nothing to do with IREN. We would be right about the company and lose anyway. The tripwire here is weak: we would see peer tape, but by the time the tape confirms it, the move has happened. This is beta, not analysis, and our position is a pure expression of it.

**Failure narrative 3 — "The contracts were repriced or the customer stepped back, and we found out last." (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)**
The $4bn figure is a private construct: contracted price per megawatt multiplied by 8,760 hours a year, i.e. a 100%-utilization assumption, against $70.5M of revenue actually recognized last quarter. Contracts get renegotiated. Utilization assumptions get missed. A counterparty gets into trouble. **None of that generates a filing obligation until it is already material**, we have no independent channel into it, and the party certifying the technical quality of the platform — NVIDIA, whose "Exemplar Cloud" designation is cited as validation — holds 30M warrants on the stock it is validating. That is an interested certifier, not an audit.

I am naming the unknown-unknown class explicitly: **off-tape commercial-terms risk inside a private contract stack we have no independent channel into, compounded by interested-party validation.** We would learn about this from a press release, on the same day as everyone else, at whatever price the tape gives. There is no sensor I can build for it. The nearest partial mitigations — a working satellite/thermal read on whether the sites are actually energized, and a permit feed for the counties involved — are exactly the two channels we currently do not have.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**
Our own trading is not the problem: 116 shares against a stock where 93.7M shares are held short is not even a rounding error on daily volume. We have no market impact and no exit-liquidity risk. The reflexivity runs the other way, and it is worse:

- **A rally is partly self-defeating for us.** This company funds itself by issuing equity into strength. Our upside case *is* the mechanism that creates the supply that caps it. We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict — the one thing our own doctrine tells us never to do.
- **The two upside destinations are both supply walls.** $51.40 is where $2.3bn of converts turn into shares. $70 is where NVIDIA's 30M warrants strike. Those are not resistance levels drawn on a chart; they are contractual share-creation events sitting on the path.
- **Doctrine interaction — the correlation waiver.** The household book already carries substantial AI exposure. The rule that would have sized this position down for that overlap was **waived for IREN specifically** by the principal on 8/16. That waiver is a deliberate, eyes-open decision, but its consequence is real: in failure narrative 2, this position and the rest of the book fall together, and the mechanism that would have sized for that was switched off. The waiver applies to this name only; the overlay stands everywhere else.
- **Doctrine interaction — do not sell calls against this.** High short interest plus an unresolved catalyst is precisely the setup where the stock runs straight through a covered-call strike. Any "collect premium while we wait" idea here is a bad idea, and I am pre-registering that so nobody proposes it in November.
- **Doctrine interaction — reason drift.** This position was re-labeled on 8/16 from a fundamental starter into an **event bet**. That event has now resolved (mostly against us). If we keep holding, we must be honest that we are holding it as a fundamental position that the fundamental work values at roughly the tape — not as an event bet, because the event is over.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we were right about the business and wrong about our share of it — the company delivers the $4bn, then funds the next $25–30bn by issuing 40%+ more stock into every rally, so the prize arrives on schedule and accrues to the shares issued to pay for it rather than to the ones we own.
