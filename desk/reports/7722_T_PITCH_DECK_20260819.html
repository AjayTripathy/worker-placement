Subject: 7722.T — Kokusai Co., Ltd. (EDINET code E02335), an uncovered, family-controlled Japanese manufacturer of precision dynamic balancing machines, tire uniformity testers, and EV motor testing systems. TSE Standard market, ~¥10.45B ($65.3M USD) market cap. Spot trades at ¥775 (down 32.4% from the February high of ¥1,147; 52-week low is ¥711). We hold no position.

What we believe

This is an unleveraged, deep net-net global niche leader that just reported a terrible Q1 due to supply-chain acceptance delays, offering a 4.52% cash carry while waiting for demand confirmation.

The bull spine, as confirmed across 10+ years of audited EDINET filings:

  • The balance sheet is an unleveraged fortress: ¥8,537M in cash and time deposits against zero financial debt and ¥20,801M in total assets (74% equity ratio). Gross net cash covers 81.7% of the entire market cap.
  • Customer advances are structural float, not hidden debt: Forensic reconciliation of the 102-page Yuho confirms ¥2,190M in customer advances (契約負債) is normal negative working capital (~15–18% of order backlog) paid upfront by global automakers to fund custom fabrication. Even deducting all advances, adjusted net cash is ¥6,347M (60.7% of market cap), leaving an adjusted Enterprise Value of just ¥4,100M ($25.6M USD).
  • Cash generation is real: Despite customer advances falling by ¥486M in FY3/26, Operating Cash Flow was +¥1,821M (+14.1% FCF yield), proving earnings are backed by hard cash receipts rather than working capital float expansion.
  • Paid 4.52% to wait: The company paid a ¥35 dividend last fiscal year (raised three years running: ¥20 → ¥30 → ¥35) and forecasts ¥40. We underwrite the ¥35 actually paid, delivering a 4.52% carry while waiting for the November 6 half-year report.

What the market believes

Zero analyst coverage, ~6% foreign ownership, and thin liquidity. What the tape says:

  • A cyclical entering a brutal downturn: Sellers derated the stock for six months before the bad print (¥1,147 in Feb down to ¥711 in May). The Q1 report (revenue −25.5%, operating profit −60.5%) confirmed the market’s worst fears of a global automotive CAPEX freeze.
  • Severe geographic & end-market concentration: 58.2% of sales and 68.7% of order backlog are tied to the tire industry; 71% of revenue is overseas, heavily exposed to Chinese automotive overcapacity.
  • Servo demand crack: Q1 order intake in the servo motor testing segment plunged −31.2% YoY, indicating that order deceleration is real and not just a parts shortage.
  • Dead equity risk: The controlling family has tolerated sub-book pricing (0.68x P/B) for decades with no activist catalyst.

Why we might have edge

  1. Structural neglect: A $65M uncovered, family-held TSE Standard name is not efficiently priced. The February–May derate already priced a severe downturn; buying near the ¥711 52-week low provides massive asymmetric asset protection.
  2. Advances misunderstanding: The market treats ¥2.19B of customer advances as a debt liability. In reality, it is a revolving, interest-free customer float fulfilled at positive gross profit margins.
  3. Calibrated EV/EBIT multiple: On mid-cycle operating profit of ~¥1.30B, adjusted EV/EBIT is 3.15x (and 1.96x on peak FY3/26 profit). Even in a severe downturn where operating profit troughs at ¥600M, EV/EBIT is only 6.8x against ¥6.3B in hard net cash.
  4. Clean customer concentration: The 102-page Yuho confirms no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, clearing single-client insolvency risk.

Why it might be priced in

  1. Servo order intake is broken: Q1 servo segment intake down −31.2% YoY proves that new order intake softened, even if full-year FY3/26 intake was +9.3% (¥15.29B, book-to-bill 1.02).
  2. Chinese tire CAPEX cliff: If Chinese EV and tire manufacturers enter a multi-year CAPEX freeze, backlog will drain and future advances will contract.
  3. Payment terms stretching: Management explicitly noted “longer payment terms with clients,” a classic leading indicator of customer-side cash strain.
  4. PFIC tax drag: Passive cash and deposits represent 41–43% of total assets, sitting close to the 50% US PFIC threshold and requiring annual monitoring.
