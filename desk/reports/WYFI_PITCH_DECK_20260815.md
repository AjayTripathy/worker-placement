# PITCH — WYFI — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line; label clearly "PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION")

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: No entry. Evidence-gated WATCH — the stock is not buyable on price alone at any level; it reopens only on one of three named proof events (both benches at conviction 8/10, red's outright REJECT partially overturned by blue).**

## What we believe

WhiteFiber (WYFI, ~$29.52, market cap ~$1.14B, enterprise value ~$1.4B including $230M of 2031 convertible notes and ~$85–95M of drawn credit facilities against $56M cash) is a real, aggressively-building AI data-center company whose stock price already pays for success that has not yet been proven.

Three things we hold with confidence after the adversarial review:

1. **The headline quarter was weaker than it looked.** Q2 revenue of $28.8M (+54% year over year) included a $12.3M one-time customer-termination fee booked inside cloud revenue. Strip it and the underlying business did about $16.5M against $18.7M a year ago — down roughly 11%. The cloud segment specifically shrank about 31%; the colocation segment grew about 173% from a small base. The same defaulting customer also cost $2.2M in credit write-offs. All of this is in the company's own 10-Q: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002042022/000121390026088026/ea0300877-10q_whitefiber.htm
2. **Nearly the whole story is one customer.** Of the $1.008B in contracted future revenue, $932.9M is the single 10-year, 40MW colocation contract with Nscale at the NC-1 campus. There is no executed letter of credit, deposit, or guarantee behind it — the company says credit support is being "actively evaluated," and the "investment-grade credit support" language in the earnings release attaches to *future* pipeline deals, not this contract. WhiteFiber just demonstrated on its own books (the $12.3M termination plus write-off) what happens when a big customer walks.
3. **The build is real, but the prize doesn't clear our bar at the current price.** Construction-in-progress on the audited balance sheet went from $157M to $485M in six months on $345M of capital spending, and customer prepayments doubled to $143M — money is genuinely flowing in and into the ground. But price the contract at face value: $865M over 10 years is ~$86.5M/year; at a 65–75% margin for a powered-shell landlord that's $56–65M/year of earnings, worth roughly $0.7–1.0B at 12–15×. A *fully performing* NC-1, plus the ~$66M-a-year underlying business, sits at or below today's ~$1.4B enterprise value. Even if everything works, the upside at contracted scope doesn't reach +50%. Upside requires expansion beyond 40MW or new tenants — which don't exist yet.

## What the market believes

The market paid +16.6% on the Q2 print and has the stock at ~21× underlying (fee-stripped) run-rate revenue, 37% below its 52-week high but +181% above its 52-week low. The implicit belief: the +54% headline growth is real growth, NC-1 energizes on schedule ("full run-rate billing later this month" per management), the Nscale contract is money-good, and the campus expands beyond the first 40MW. Two mechanical caveats on reading that tape: Bit Digital still owns ~69.6% of the company, so the free float is only ~$350M — a thin float that makes one day's pop weak evidence of anything — and the parent stake is a standing source of future share supply (the IPO lockup lapsed around February 2026, though no sale registration is on file).

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the fee-strip arithmetic.** The $12.3M termination fee is disclosed, but the headline "+54%" is what circulates. Anyone who reads the revenue note reaches "down 11% underlying"; the tape's reaction suggests most didn't.
- **We priced the whole capital structure.** The red bench itself initially priced only the equity; adding converts and drawn facilities takes the multiple from ~17× to ~21× underlying revenue and makes the valuation problem worse, not better.
- **We priced the prize, not the vibe.** The face-value math on the Nscale contract (above) is the load-bearing bearish fact, and it survives even if construction and energization go perfectly. That is a sturdier reason for no-entry than any construction skepticism.
- **We know exactly what would change our mind**, and each trigger is a public, dated, checkable event (see reopen conditions below) rather than a feeling.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry requires saying the bear case is partly priced and partly wrong:

- The stock is already 37% off its high; some Nscale-concentration fear is plainly in the price.
- The strongest bearish "physical evidence" died under review. Our red bench claimed satellite/thermal data refuted the NC-1 energization — the blue bench showed the satellite scenes predate the claim window entirely (the "recent" construction scene was from November 2025) and that the temperature downtrend at the site reflects the prior industrial tenant's shutdown, not a failed startup. The audited $157M→$485M construction-in-progress series corroborates a real buildout.
- "The cloud expansion is dead" also died: the Montreal MTL-3 site shows no construction because WhiteFiber exercised its purchase option and *bought the finished building* in May 2026 for CAD $24.7M. A completed, purchased asset is commitment, not retreat.
- Nscale is not the same kind of counterparty as the customer that defaulted: it raised a $1.1B Series B (https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-series-b) plus $433M shortly after, carries ~$790M of debt financing, holds a ~$14B GPU deal anchored by Microsoft (https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/15/nscale-eyes-ipo-amid-fresh-14-billion-deal-with-microsoft-.html), and is on a 2026 IPO track. Concentration is a sizing problem, not a presumption of default.

Where the bull case is *not* priced in — and this cuts against entry, not for it — is the gap between today's enterprise value and what the contracted scope is worth even when it works.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry, at any price, on price alone — so these are reopen conditions, not buy levels.**

Why no price band: WhiteFiber trades with the AI-infrastructure complex, and its one material asset is a contract with an AI-compute counterparty. Any tape that marks this stock down to an "attractive" level almost certainly arrives *because* that complex — and therefore the Nscale contract's value — is impaired. A bare price trigger here would fire precisely when the thesis is worse, so none is armed. For reference only, the benches' own math says a fully performing contracted scope backs roughly $11–19/share (NC-1 worth $0.7–1.0B minus ~$264M net debt, over 38.66M diluted shares) plus whatever the shrinking underlying business is worth — i.e., today's $29.52 already prices success plus expansion.

**Reopen triggers — any ONE reopens the name for a fresh court:**
1. **8-K disclosing an executed Nscale credit enhancement or guarantee** (letter of credit, deposit, parent/customer guarantee) behind the $932.9M contract — converts the concentration risk from unsecured to secured.
2. **8-K disclosing NC-1 secured financing signed on stated terms** — closes the $345M-per-half-year capex vs $60M liquidity gap.
3. **Q3 print (est. 2026-11-12, date unconfirmed): colocation revenue ≥ $14M with receivables roughly flat** — the contracted rate actually billing *and collecting*. This is the benches' pre-committed pass/fail number; below it, the "full run-rate billing in August" claim failed its first test.

**Even after a reopen**, entry requires the prize to clear: disclosed expansion beyond 40MW or a second anchor tenant, because at contracted scope the fully-performing value sits at or below today's enterprise value.

**Sizing:** zero now (we hold no position and no orders). Any future entry after a reopen would carry a single-customer concentration haircut and the parent-overhang (69.6% Bit Digital stake) haircut from the start.
