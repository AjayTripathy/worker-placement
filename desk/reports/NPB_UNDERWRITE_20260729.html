The thesis in plain language

Northpointe runs two engines on wholesale money: a mortgage-warehouse lending business (MPP) with zero charge-offs since inception in 2010 — through COVID and the 2022–23 rate shock — and a national mortgage origination/servicing shop. Returns are excellent (ROTCE 14.7%), credit is pristine (0.03% charge-offs), construction concentration has fallen five straight quarters (34.7%→19.5% of Tier-1, recomputed independently from FDIC data), and the 2023-style run vector is absent: uninsured deposits are just 7.8%. H1 EPS grew 21% while the stock went nowhere — multiple compression on a compounder.

The honest other half: the funding is bought, not gathered. Brokered deposits are 58.6% of the stack (wholesale ratio 63.1% by the company's own measure), the NIM is a thin 2.33% with ~1.0 deposit beta, and the sticky custodial layer the sourcing hoped for verified as real but immaterial (noninterest-bearing is 5.7% of deposits). In a systemic wholesale freeze, the ROE goes high-single-digit fast — which is why the multiple is held at ~1× and why the red team capped size at the low end. The rate lever, verified: cuts squeeze NII first (MPP floats), while a genuine refi wave pays through the gain-on-sale line.

What the market believes vs what we believe

The position
Market believesA 58.6%-brokered, wholesale-funded monoline mortgage bank is the 2007 casualty class and deserves ~1× tangible book, permanently. Growth here is rate-cyclical, not durable. And until this year there was a sponsor block waiting to sell every rally. No coverage, no bid.
We believeThe funding risk is real but structurally milder than the archetype it's priced as: collateral is agency-eligible and short-duration, a first-loss lender-risk account sits in front of NPB, term CDs cannot run intraday, and uninsured deposits are 7.8% — the 2023 vector is absent. Meanwhile ROTCE ~15% compounds tangible book whether or not the multiple ever moves, and the sponsor block is verified gone.
Who's mispricing, and whyNobody grossly — that's the honest read. The market's ceiling on the multiple is right; its floor prices a run-and-fire-sale scenario the structure doesn't support. The neglect is mechanical: a Feb-2025 IPO orphan with thin coverage, a flat chart on +21% EPS, and (until early 2026) a 20% overhang that no longer exists — the market hasn't re-read the proxy. We have.
Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrongIf the multiple truly never re-rates, the return is TBV compounding minus dilution and shocks — good, not spectacular, and fully consistent with spot. In a systemic wholesale freeze the ROE halves and 1.0× TBV offered no crisis discount: we are not being paid a stress premium, which is exactly why the size is 0.5% and not more. The comp set (MBIN at ~1.4× today, 0.6–0.8× in stress) brackets both tails.
Mechanism falsifierThe funding-model claim is checked quarterly and mechanically: wholesale ratio (kill >70%), NIM (kill <2.15%), the 15-year MPP zero-loss streak, and the FDIC call-report watch grading all of it between prints.
Edge typeRISK_PREMIUM — fairly-paid carry via book compounding, with a neglect tilt. This is not a mispricing claim; the calibration gate for sizing on claimed edge remains unearned, and the position is sized accordingly.

What the verification pass changed

  • The Castle Creek overhang is dead — REFUTED, favorably. The court and red team carried a ~20% sponsor block with registration rights as a standing supply cap (the "VEL pattern"), sourced from the FY25 10-K. The proxy, one filing later, says verbatim: "In early 2026, Castle Creek exited all of its positions in our stock… registration rights… no longer in effect." The supply already cleared — without breaking the stock. Two tripwires and a calendar entry died with it (the Jan-2027 Series-B registration date is kept pending holder confirmation, not assumed dead).
  • New datum the record lacked: $20M of 7.50% subordinated notes issued March-2026 — modest holdco leverage, now on the kill list if it stacks without asset growth.
  • Stated plainly now: gain-on-sale is down YoY ($17.0M vs $19.4M) — the growth is balance-sheet-led, exactly as the rate framing predicted.
