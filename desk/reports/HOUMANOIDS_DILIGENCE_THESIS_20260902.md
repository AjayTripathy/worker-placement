## 1. THE BET, DECOMPOSED

The room's own investor memo frames the seed as six bets. Restated as eight falsifiable propositions, each with what would confirm or refute it:

**P1 — Density inverts service economics.** Claim: a 54-unit single-parish deployment runs field cost at ~$370/K9/month (12.2% of the $3,000 floor; the memo charts "~31% year-2 worst case" vs Knightscope's 138–171% of service revenue in each of FY2021–25 and United Rentals' 7.9%).
*Confirm:* the monthly actuals table the seed-bet page pre-commits to (hardware failure rate vs 1 actuator/K9/month planning case; hopper-minutes vs the 48/K9/day staffed ceiling) filled with Rapides data at or under plan. *Refute:* failure rate >1.3/K9/month (their own stated morning-slot saturation point), or parts pricing post-warranty at the $440–735/actuator level without the 3-year warranty extension (their own year-2/3 exposure math: $630k–$1.06M per 60-K9 pod). *Public anchor available now:* Knightscope's service-cost ratio is recomputable from KSCP 10-K XBRL — the base rate is verifiable even though the counter-claim is not yet.

**P2 — Customers pay $3,000+ per K9 per month for the outcome.** Claim (memo Bet 2): willingness-to-pay at the floor is proven by the first paying customer from Sep 1, 2026.
*Confirm:* arm's-length signed agreements at ≥$3,000 with cash collected (RPM Living at $3,000 from October; AirGarage at $4,000 from November). *Refute:* the signed-paper record continuing to sit below the floor — the executed LOI record today prices at $100–$1,000 (Amoriss, the related-party first customer), $1,000 list (RPSO), $2,000 (Eglinton after a 3-month free pilot); no signed instrument in the room prices at the $6,000 list. The proposition is currently carried by two *unexecuted* drafts.

**P3 — US assembly converts a future-banned product into a compliant one before the pipeline decays.** Claim: FCC DA-26-786 (Jul 28, 2026) blocks new authorizations for foreign-produced advanced robotic devices; Houmanoids holds a three-layer staged response (Dimensional exemption coverage today; England Airpark FTZ final assembly Dec 2026; AgiBot-anchored manufacturing at >65% domestic content by Q2 2027).
*Confirm:* the Conditional Approval instrument in hand naming their fleet; an executed England Airpark term sheet/lease; an executed OEM manufacturing agreement. *Refute:* the room's own maturity labels persisting — Dimensional coverage is "the founders' understanding, not a cited fact"; England Airpark is "verbal... nothing is executed"; the AgiBot MOU is "non-binding... in drafting." Time-gated physical test: the December build at England Airpark is satellite- and ground-observable.

**P4 — Sponsorship pays for public deployments.** Claim: the RPSO pilot costs the parish $0 because sponsors (Cleco anchor) fund it; the seed maps a ladder from floor (schools pay all) to ceiling (sponsors bid above cost).
*Confirm:* executed sponsorship agreements with dollar amounts; Cleco countersigning anything. *Refute:* the sponsor rate card itself — the dealer proposal prices sponsorship at $100–$1,000/K9/month while the forecast's mature public-site rate is $3,000/month at 100% fill; the Cleco page is a proposal with no response in the room; the forecast's zero-sponsorship downside case is the company's own quantification ($55,000 MRR at Jun 2028).

**P5 — Related-party proof-of-life generalizes to arm's-length demand.** Claim: Amoriss (first LOI, first paying customer) was "chosen deliberately: the fastest path from LOI to robots-on-property, and the template for the arm's-length deals it feeds."
*Confirm:* RPM Living or AirGarage executing and paying — both are named as the template's first tests. *Refute:* pilots that convert only where the founder controls the counterparty. Note the reference stack has the same shape: of three offered reference calls, one is the RPSO contact and two are Amoriss-side (its ops manager, and the Fern-View asset owner who is also the company's 2,400-share advisor).

**P6 — The China structure adds leverage without adding liability.** Claim: "US investors should expect no financial upside from the China entities"; the China side is priced at zero and adds OEM buying power, learnings, and export logistics.
*Confirm:* the commercial arrangement between the US chain and the Chen Dongyun-owned domestic chain, once signed, containing no US-entity obligations; recovery or conversion of the US balance-sheet advances. *Refute:* the US balance sheet already carries $225,000 advanced to Hou Jiyuan (Shanghai) Technology — an entity in the *domestic* chain the US company does not own — plus $34,091 of equipment advances held in China, against total US assets of $456,351 and cash of $10,553.

**P7 — Two founders plus an AI software factory operate a 75-unit fleet on a $3M team budget.** Claim: 8–9 FTE post-close (two founders, one commission salesperson, two teleoperators, 1–2 technicians, 2 engineers) run 75 units across two states plus pilots.
*Confirm:* the Rapides fleet hitting the LOI's own SLO table (≥90% per-unit uptime, <1 physical intervention/unit/week, <1 teleop intervention/unit/day, ≤72h mean repair). *Refute:* those metrics missing, or headcount/opex materially exceeding the forecast's ~$43k/month mature burn.

**P8 — The Series A prices at $600M+ by ~March 2027.** Claim (memo Bet 6 / round table): density proof plus a manufacturing path jumps the company out of the security-robotics comp bucket (no $100M+ round ever) into the platform/onshoring bucket.
*Confirm/refute:* this is a market-clearing event, not a document test; the falsifiable sub-claims are the comp-bucket facts (Knightscope FY2025 revenue $11.3M; Asylon's $26M Series B as the best-funded security-robotics entrant) — both checkable against public filings and press today.
