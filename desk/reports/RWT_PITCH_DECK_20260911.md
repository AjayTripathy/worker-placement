# PITCH — RWT — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — damage arriving (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

---

## What we believe

Redwood Trust is a mortgage lender/securitizer trading at $3.61, which is **0.52× its last reported book of $6.90/share** and 46.5% below its 52-week high. It pays $0.18 a quarter — a 19.9% yield — and its own core earnings (EAD) covered only $0.15 of that last quarter, down from $0.21 the quarter before.

Our belief in one sentence: **this is a cheap price on a book that is still shrinking, and the thing that has to stop shrinking has not yet stopped.** The company earns less than it pays out, funds the gap in the capital markets, and has grown its share count 16.5% over nine quarters while book value per share fell 18.4%. That is the leak. Price alone does not fix it.

We also believe the September 10 financing was, on the arithmetic, **better for the company than either of our benches concluded** — and that the single most position-relevant fact of the month is that the board bought 5,509,641 shares (4.1% of the company) at $3.630, i.e. 0.53× book, with its own money on the same day it sold converts. Management behaved like the stock is cheap.

So: cheap, insider-validated, and still bleeding. We want it — after one clean print, not before.

## What the market believes

The sell side carries Q3'26 EPS of **$0.26**, FY26 **$1.05**, FY27 **$1.08**, and an average price target of **~$9.56** across nine analysts (cut from $10.33). The mechanism behind that number is roughly 92bp of gain-on-sale on ~$8bn of volume with the Legacy segment's drag marked to about zero.

The tape believes something much worse: at $3.61 the market is pricing a dividend cut, further book erosion, or both. A 19.9% yield is not a yield the market believes in.

**We do not agree with the street, and the disagreement is against the bulls.** Two consecutive prints say Legacy is the swing item (−$14m last quarter) and it has not gone to zero. If the street is wrong about Legacy, $1.05 is not the number.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We read the whole filing.** The originating brief read Exhibit 99.1 (the dividend) of 8-K 0001104659-26-106539 and concluded there was "zero company-specific disclosure." Exhibit 99.2 of the same accession is a $150m convertible launch that priced at **$185m of 7.00% converts due 9/15/2030, conversion price ~$4.90, closing 9/15/26**, with ~$129.29m used to retire ~$123.79m of the 7.75% 2027s and up to $20m for a concurrent buyback: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000930236/000110465926106539/tm2625156d1_ex99-2.htm. Anyone working off headlines missed this.
2. **We did the option math nobody else did.** The convert's real cost is 7.00% cash *plus* the value of the embedded call struck at $4.90. Priced properly — including RWT's actual ~19.9% dividend yield, which crushes call value — the all-in cost is **~8.1%–8.8%**, *cheaper* than the 9.75% straight paper the company issued before. The convert is a genuine improvement in cost and in maturity profile.
3. **We sized the dilution correctly.** Principal is cash-settled, so shares are only issued on value above the strike: **0 shares at $4.90, 10.9m (8.2%) at $6.90, 17.2m at $9.00** — not the 37.75m headline. If-converted book is **$6.49** (post-buyback), so the tape is 0.56× a fully-diluted book, not 0.52× a fantasy book.
4. **We know what the next print has to show**, in numbers, before it prints: +$0.14 BVPS from the buyback, −$0.02 to −$0.04 from the debt extinguishment premium, +$0.84m/quarter of extra cash interest, and whatever the marks do.
5. **A dated, near-term primary catalyst is inside 3 sessions** (convert close 9/15) and a real earnings fork ~7 weeks out. We are early to a fork, not late to a story.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- A 19.9% yield on a 0.52× book multiple is the market *already* saying "the dividend gets cut and book keeps falling." We are not discovering distress; we are pricing it.
- The buyback at $3.63 is public and was in a press release. Every screen saw it.
- 5.0× recourse leverage (up from 2.4× in eighteen months) is disclosed and is exactly why the multiple is what it is.
- Our own scenario table (below) produces an expected price of **$3.64 against a $3.61 tape** — a 1% edge, which is zero. That is the single most important line in this deck.
- The dislocation is live but not fresh: the stock is already +11.8% off its low.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative #1 — We buy a confirmation that turns out to be a one-off (monitorable).**
Q3 prints EAD at exactly $0.18 because a single securitization executed well, we enter a starter at ~$4.10, and Q4 reverts to $0.14 with Legacy still bleeding. *Tripwire:* EAD composition — Core Segments line vs consolidated, and gain-on-sale margin vs the street's 92bp assumption. Fires at the Q3 8-K, same day.

**Failure narrative #2 — The dividend is cut at the Q4 declaration (partially monitorable).**
The Q4 declaration lands in December. Coverage at 83% and a capital-markets-funded distribution make a cut a live possibility; the price impact is a gap, not a slide — our table puts that world at $2.60. *Tripwire:* the declaration 8-K itself, plus EAD trend. But the tripwire and the damage arrive in the same instant, so it protects sizing, not entry price. Second-order and nastier: a cut *raises* the embedded call's value by dropping the dividend yield, lifting convert-arb delta from ~0.15 toward ~0.32 — roughly **6m extra shares of hedge supply arriving exactly when the stock is gapping down**. Fundamental damage and mechanical supply are correlated here, not independent.

**Failure narrative #3 — Funding-channel shock. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
The warehouse lines financing ~$3.1bn of loan inventory at 5.0× recourse leverage reset haircuts or terms, and equity absorbs it. We have no loan tape (no ABS-EE pull), no counterparty note until the 10-Q, and **no visibility at all into private repo terms, advance rates, or haircut triggers — those contracts are not disclosed anywhere**. Nothing in our sensor set can fire before the 8-K announcing the damage. **Unknown-unknown class: undisclosed private-contract terms in the funding channel** — Knightian, not probabilistic: we cannot even write the distribution because the underlying agreements are non-public. Adjacent class: correspondent-seller concentration in the Sequoia flow, which we also cannot see.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our size vs. liquidity:* ADV is an estimate, not a measurement — the IBKR bars rail is down. We estimate **2.2–3.5m shares/day (~$8–13m)** from sibling small-cap mortgage REITs and RWT's own history. A 1.0% starter is comfortably inside 10% of a day's volume; the 2.5% cap is ~1–2 days' participation in normal tape and *unknowable* in a dividend-cut gap, which is precisely when we would want out. Size the position to the gap, not to the median day.
- *Predicted flow:* dividend-capture buying into the 9/23 record date, then supply right after it; convert-arb hedge supply of **~3.7–7.3m share-equivalents** at current yield (37.75m conversion-rate calls × delta 0.10–0.19), which is ~2 days of ADV dribbling out through settlement; and — the one nobody schedules — **tax-loss selling in November–December in a name down 46.5% on the year.** All three point the same way: supply before the print, supply after the print, supply into year-end. Buying the 9/23 record date means paying for $0.18 and standing in front of all of it.
- *Doctrine interactions:* our own two rules point in opposite directions on the same document — "below-book convert recap masks marginal cost" (red) versus "cash-settled convert overhang is overstated" (blue). Whichever a reader meets first will drive their view. The arithmetic says the second is closer on *issuance* and **neither is right on hedge delta**, which runs off the full conversion rate, not the spread shares. A desk that applies either doctrine mechanically gets this wrong.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought the 0.52× book multiple and not the coverage gap — entering on a single confirming print while core earnings stayed below the dividend, and then eating a dividend cut whose gap down, arb re-hedging, and year-end tax-loss supply all arrived at once.
