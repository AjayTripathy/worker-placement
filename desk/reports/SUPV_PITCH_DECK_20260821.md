## What we believe

Grupo Supervielle is an Argentine bank whose U.S.-listed shares have fallen 43.5% from their 52-week high ($13.54) to $7.65, while the bank's own reported condition improved through the June-2026 quarter: bad-loan ratio down to 5.5%, headcount down 17% with about AR$42bn of annualized salary savings, and a return to profit.

We believe the shares are **modestly cheap, not deeply cheap**, and that what we would be buying is **Argentine macro exposure at a fair-to-slightly-discounted price — not a company-specific insight**.

The arithmetic, using the bank's own filed numbers and today's peso rate (~1,490 ARS/USD):

- Shareholders' equity AR$1,181.0bn ≈ **$0.79bn**
- Market value ≈ **$0.67bn** (87.5m ADSs at $7.65; each ADS = 5 ordinary shares)
- So the stock trades at about **0.85x book value** — book value is roughly **$9.05 per ADS**
- Adjusted return on equity, excluding one-off severance but *not* crediting savings that haven't happened yet, is **12.4%** — and because Argentina reports in inflation-adjusted pesos, that is a **real** (after-inflation) return

A bank earning a 12.4% real return against a 12–15% real cost of capital is worth roughly book value. That puts fair value at about **$7.50–$9.35 per ADS**, midpoint ~$8.40. From $7.65 that is roughly **+10%**, plus whatever the cost-cutting actually delivers from the September quarter onward.

That is the honest size of the prize. It is a starter position, not a thesis.

## What the market believes

Two different things, and they don't agree with each other.

**The five covering analysts** publish an average price target of **$12.92** (range $10.60–$15.00, zero sell ratings, two buys) — implying **+69%**. Their load-bearing number is FY27 earnings of ARS 369.65 per ordinary share, which is about **$1.24 per ADS**. At $7.65 the market is paying **6.2x** that estimate; the price target implies about **10.4x**.

**The tape** has declined to pay it. The stock fell to $4.56, has recovered to $7.65 — which is only **34% of the decline retraced**, not most of it.

So the market's revealed belief is: *either the FY27 estimate is too high, or Argentine bank earnings deserve a very low multiple, or both.* We think the second is closer to right, and that the analysts' targets are the weaker anchor here — $12.92 is **1.43x book**, which requires a sustainable real return on equity somewhere near **18–19%**. Nothing in the June quarter supports that. Reported return on equity was **4.4%**.

## Why we might have edge

Three candidates, ranked by how much we actually believe them.

1. **A data-plumbing error in the screening feeds (strongest).** At least one widely-used data vendor publishes SUPV's forward P/E as **39.77x**. That number divides the *ADS* price by *per-ordinary-share* earnings, ignoring the 5:1 ratio. The true forward multiple is roughly **7.9x** on this year and **6.4x** on next. Any quantitative screen inheriting that feed throws SUPV out as expensive. This is a mechanical, checkable reason a cheap stock could stay unscreened — and it is the only edge claim in this file that neither side of the court could break.

2. **A cost cut whose benefit hasn't landed yet.** The 17% headcount reduction was implemented during the quarter; the filing says the full run-rate benefit begins in the September quarter. If the savings survive contact with customers, reported earnings step up mechanically. This is timing, not insight — and it is exactly what the analysts are already forecasting.

3. **The currency setup is better than the bears think.** The peso depreciated about 24% over the year against 33.8% inflation — meaning the peso got *stronger* in real terms, which under Argentina's inflation-adjusted accounting is a translation **tailwind** for the U.S.-listed shares, not a headwind. Our own bear bench got this sign backwards and built its strongest kill on it (see the table below).

## Why it might be priced in

Plainly: it probably mostly is.

- **We agree with the published view.** Five analysts already say the stock is worth $12.92–$14.75. We are not carrying a view the market hasn't heard. Our own bear correctly flagged this, even though its supporting numbers were sloppy.
- **The margin expansion is a timing artifact.** Net interest margin rose to 20.3% from 17.7% in the prior quarter — but the filing itself attributes this to *"funding costs easing faster than asset yields throughout the quarter."* Deposit rates reset; that gap closes. And margin is **down year-over-year** (20.8% → 20.3%), which the company blames on *"narrower loan spreads driven by a lower share of retail lending, together with a higher proportion of dollar-denominated positions."* This was the single best point either bench made, and it survived every challenge.
- **Reserve coverage is deteriorating.** Loan-loss coverage has fallen **129.7% → 103.9% → 98.9%** across three quarters while charge-offs rose to AR$16.9bn, +16.6% quarter-over-quarter. The bank is writing bad loans off faster than it is reserving against them. New bad-loan formation is ~20% below the peak quarter — but that still means it is running at **80% of the worst quarter on record**.
- **Capital is being consumed while earnings are weak.** Core capital fell **15.4% → 14.2%** in one quarter, which the filing attributes to *"higher RWA density, driven by an increase in market risk associated with the expansion of the corporate notes portfolio."* The bank is reducing consumer credit risk and taking on market risk instead. Neither bench flagged this until the second pass.
- **The deposit base is dollarizing.** Foreign-currency deposits are **US$1.2bn, up 30.1% year-over-year, now 31% of all deposits** (27% a year ago). The peso deposit float is what generates a 20% margin; it is structurally shrinking.
- **The stock already bounced.** Entry at $7.65 is not entry at $4.56. The easy part of this trade happened without us.

**Trims and exits**

- **Trim half at $11.30** (1.25x book) — a multiple that requires roughly a 16% sustainable real return we have not yet seen.
- **Full exit at $12.92** (1.43x book, the street's average target) unless by then the company has reported a **real return on equity above 17% for two consecutive quarters**. Absent that, $12.92 is a price we sell into, not a price we forecast.

  - Loan-loss coverage falls **below 95%**
  - Core capital falls **below 13.5%**
  - Foreign-currency deposits exceed **35% of total deposits**
- **Also re-open the case (not automatic exit)** if the central bank re-anchors the band below inflation or reserve accumulation stops — that changes the whole Argentine bank group, not just this name.

**Catalyst dates**

- **2026-11-17 — third-quarter results.** This is the one that matters: it is the first quarter with the full cost saving in the run rate, the first test of whether the margin gain was a repricing lag, and the first read on coverage. **This date is derived from a data vendor and is NOT confirmed by the company.** Confirming it is a prerequisite to any add.

- **No near-term company catalyst before then.** The decisive filing already landed on 2026-08-10.

If the only thing we own is Argentine macro exposure at 0.85x book, then the position should be small enough that being wrong about Argentina costs us a rounding error, and the add ladder should require the price to come to us rather than us reaching for it.
