# PITCH — CCOI — PROPOSED VERDICT
**NO ENTRY — STAY FLAT until the refinancing announcement prints and the new 10-Q is actually read; reopen conditions below. PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION.**

## What we believe

Cogent Communications (CCOI, ~$9.92 as of the Aug-7 close, down 78% from its 52-week high of ~$45.69, market cap ~$475M) is a badly wounded but **not imminently dying** company, and the stock is not buyable until one specific event resolves.

The situation in plain terms: Cogent is burning cash, but far less than a first look suggests. Reported operating cash flow (~$3.2M in Q2) **excludes** the ~$25M/quarter T-Mobile subsidy payments, which the company books under investing activities. All-in, the burn is roughly **$12M per quarter**, and the T-Mobile payments continue until roughly **October–November 2027** — not November 2026, as our own original thesis wrongly claimed three times. The company holds $369.7M of cash, but only **$183.7M is unrestricted** (the other $186.0M is restricted, likely escrowed data-center sale proceeds tied to a debt-repurchase covenant). Run that unrestricted pool against ~$12M/quarter of burn and Cogent comfortably reaches the subsidy cliff with roughly ~$124M still on hand.

So the equity's life-or-death question is not near-term cash exhaustion. It is the **$750M of notes due June 2027** and the terms of the refinancing the company has said is coming. Until that refinancing announcement (an 8-K) prints, the largest unknown in the capital structure is unpriced by us, and all three benches in our internal court — the original thesis, the prosecution, and the defense — independently concluded: stay flat.

We also believe the bullish "growth engine" story is weaker than advertised. IPv4 address leasing, pitched as a "+25% compounding engine," grew **0.5% quarter-over-quarter** in Q2 ($18.1M); the year-over-year number is an artifact of a price increase that has now been lapped. Wavelength revenue is genuinely growing (+9.2% quarter-over-quarter) but is only 6.3% of revenue — too small to offset the legacy off-net business bleeding roughly $17.7M/year.

## What the market believes

This is a fully covered, fully public situation: **12 analysts, consensus Hold, average price target $18.68**. The class action, the refinancing plan, and the T-Mobile payment schedule are all public. The stock fell 9.1% on the August 6 earnings print. The company's own bonds trade around 90 cents on the dollar — the credit market is pricing real recovery risk. In short, the market sees a distressed legacy telecom with a growth story that hasn't shown up in the numbers, waiting on a refinancing — and it is waiting on exactly the same 8-K we are.

One nuance the market may be getting wrong in the equity's favor: the company spent ~$107M in July buying back its own notes at 90–92 cents, which is value-accretive to the equity (a cumulative $13.4M gain so far). Our original thesis mislabeled this as insider buying; it is corporate liability management, but it is still rational, equity-friendly behavior — not the behavior of a management team that believes the equity is worthless.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: less than we thought at the start, but not zero.

1. **The cash picture is genuinely confusing, and confusion is where mispricing lives.** Our own prosecution bench — working from the primary earnings release — got the burn rate wrong by 73% (it claimed −$78M/quarter by double-counting the T-Mobile cash out of operating cash flow; the 10-Q states plainly that those payments sit in investing activities). Our defense bench then found the restricted/unrestricted cash split ($183.7M vs $186.0M), which neither the original thesis nor the prosecution had caught. If two motivated internal benches with the primary documents in hand made material cash errors on a name covered by 12 analysts, it is plausible parts of the market are making the same errors — in both directions.
2. **The decisive analysis is still undone by anyone, including us:** a clean reconstruction of fungible liquidity (which cash pool actually funded the July buybacks), the refinancing terms when they print, and an appraisal of the 14 data centers plus the IPv4 address block against the June 2027 maturity wall. That work is tractable and mostly not in any analyst note we can see.

## Why it might be priced in

The case against edge is strong, and the prosecution scored it 8/10 conviction:

- Twelve analysts cover the name. The refinancing plan, the covenant, and the payment schedule are all disclosed. The catalyst we'd be "waiting cleverly" for — the refi 8-K — is exactly what twelve other desks are waiting for. There is no discovery edge; at best there is an analysis edge on public documents.
- The stock is cheap because the business is genuinely deteriorating: the growth limbs are small or stalled, the legacy business is shrinking, and the terminal question (refinance at what rate, against what collateral) is real. "Down 78%" is distress pricing, not obvious mispricing.
- Our court's one candidate for a bullish variant view — "the market wrongly thinks the subsidy dies in 2026" — turned out to be **our own error**, not the market's. Once corrected, the urgency premise of the original pitch evaporated.
