# PITCH — 7235.T — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED  — OWNABLE** — conviction 8.5/10: the dated special-situation story is dead and fabricated, but what's underneath it is a debt-free, cash-heavy Japanese radiator maker trading at roughly one and a half times the value of one year's operating profit, and that is ownable on its own merits at slow-compounder size.

---

## What we believe

Tokyo Radiator makes heat exchangers — radiators, oil coolers, intercoolers — mostly for trucks and construction machinery, mostly for Isuzu and Nissan, mostly in Japan. It has been listed since 1961.

Strip out the story and here is the arithmetic, all of it from the company's own quarterly report filed 2026-08-07:

- 9,521,383 shares actually outstanding (10,400,000 issued minus 878,617 held in treasury). At ¥1,581 that is a **¥15.05 billion company** — about $100 million.
- It holds **¥9.35 billion of cash and deposits** and **¥2.08 billion of investment securities**. That is ¥11.43 billion, or **¥1,200 per share**, against a ¥1,581 share price.
- It has **no interest-bearing debt at all**. Equity is 73.1% of the balance sheet.
- So the market is paying roughly **¥3.6 billion** for the actual operating business, which the company guides to earn **¥2.45 billion of operating profit this year**. Even after haircutting the securities 30% for the tax you'd owe on selling them, that is **1.5 to 1.7 times one year's operating profit**.
- Book value is ¥2,577 per share, so the stock trades at **0.61 times book**. Net current assets alone are roughly ¥1,520 per share — the stock is at **1.04 times liquidation-ish value**.
- The dividend was just **raised 18.5%, from ¥65 to ¥77**, a **4.87% yield**, and that is only 41% of earnings.

We also believe the business is quietly fine, which is the part our own red bench got wrong. The headline looks bad — first-quarter operating profit down 3.5%, net income down 14.4%, and full-year net income guided down 11.7%. But the quarter it is being compared against was up 81.6%, and on a two-year stack operating profit went from ¥401 million to ¥703 million, up 75%. Japan segment profit was **up 21.5%**. Asia was **up 10.5% on 24.9% revenue growth**. The entire ¥26 million decline came from China, and China's profit is now down to ¥82 million — about 12% of total segment profit, so there isn't much left to lose. And critically: **the company guides operating profit UP 3.9% this year.** The −11.7% is the net line, distorted by last year's unusually favorable below-the-line items.

Finally, the thing neither the original write-up nor the red bench noticed and which we think is the most important fact about this company: **between April 2024 and March 2026, it cancelled 4,000,000 shares — 27.8% of everything issued.** Management has been buying back and destroying more than a quarter of the company while paying a rising dividend, and it still has ¥9.35 billion of cash and 878,617 shares sitting in treasury.

## What the market believes

Honestly, the market may not believe anything in particular, because almost nobody is looking. There is no meaningful analyst coverage on a $100 million Japanese parts supplier. We cannot say "this is priced in" because there is no visible price-setting opinion to point at.

What we can read from the tape: at ¥1,581 the stock is **14.1% below its 52-week high of ¥1,840** but **37.2% above its 52-week low of ¥1,152**. That is not a dislocated, panicked stock. Someone has already re-rated it meaningfully off the lows. The market appears to believe this is an ordinary, slightly-below-average Japanese small-cap industrial: cheap on paper, permanently so, because the cash belongs to management rather than to shareholders and the customers (Isuzu, Nissan) are not growth stories.

To the extent there's a bear view, it's the standard Japanese value-trap view: 0.6x book is normal for this cohort and has been for twenty years; the cash never comes out; the discount never closes; you collect 4.87% and wait forever. That's a serious argument, not a strawman.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The share cancellation is invisible on a screen.** Any data vendor showing "Marelli owns 8.01%" and "Marelli owns 12.11%" makes it look like Marelli bought more shares. It didn't — its holding has been frozen at 1,153,200 shares since April 2024. The percentage rose because *the company shrank the denominator*, from 14,400,000 issued shares to 10,400,000. You only see this by reconciling two separate primary filings against each other. Our blue bench did that; our red bench did not; a screen never will.

2. **The base-effect read on the quarter.** The reported numbers say "deteriorating." The segment detail and the guidance say "Japan and Asia are growing, China is nearly finished shrinking, operating profit is guided up." Anyone reading only the headline — including our own first-pass bench — grades this as a declining business.

3. **No coverage means no anchor.** With no sell-side estimate to converge to, there's no consensus for the price to be efficient against. That cuts both ways, and it's a weaker form of edge than we'd like, but it's real.

4. **The overhang is probably misread in the other direction.** The remaining Marelli block is 1,153,200 shares, ¥1.82 billion, about 12% of the company. The obvious fear is that a bankrupt parent dumps it. But this issuer has already demonstrated the capacity to absorb **3.5 times that size** — it cancelled 4 million shares — and it is sitting on ¥9.35 billion of cash. The likelier absorber of the residual block is the company itself.

## Why it might be priced in

We should be straight about the weaknesses, because there are several and one of them is structural.

- **We have no catalyst and no date.** This is the biggest concession. The original write-up's entire reason to act now was fabricated (see the table). There is no scheduled event that forces the discount to close. A cheap thing with no catalyst can stay cheap for a decade, and Japanese small-cap industrials have a long history of doing exactly that.
- **The stock is up 37% from its low.** Whatever re-rating was available for free has partly happened. We are not buying a crash.
- **The cash may not be ours.** ¥9.35 billion is only worth ¥9.35 billion if it eventually reaches shareholders. Buybacks and a rising dividend are strong evidence that it will — but that's evidence, not a claim on the asset.
- **Customer concentration.** Isuzu and Nissan drive this business. Nissan is not in good shape. We have no independent verification of order books because the US customs rail we normally use returned zero — the entire relationship is domestic-Japan and invisible to US shipping records. That is a **coverage gap, not a clean bill.**
- **Single site.** The main plant is in Fujisawa, Kanagawa — coastal, seismic, ¥6.75 billion of book value, 492 employees, and it carries the Japan segment that is 84% of revenue and all of the profit growth. We could not assess this hazard because our hazard tool is US-only.
- **Liquidity.** At ¥15 billion of market value, we cannot build or exit a large position quickly. Any position here is size-capped by the tape, not by conviction.
