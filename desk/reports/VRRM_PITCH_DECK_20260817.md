# PITCH — VRRM — VERDICT

ADJUDICATED 2026-08-08 — FLAT. No position, no order. The "Avis walked away" story is factually wrong — the issuer says in its own Q2 release that Avis *entered into* a seven-year extension — but our own buy case fails its own tests: the document that settles how much of the contract Avis can pull in-house does not exist in any filing we can read yet, and we never pulled the company's tax-receivable obligation or checked a real broker price.

## What we believe

Verra Mobility runs two businesses: photo-enforcement systems for cities (Government Solutions) and toll/violation processing for rental-car fleets (Commercial Services). The stock fell roughly 82% from its 52-week high ($25.57 to $4.60 on the pack's quote) on a belief that its largest commercial customer, Avis Budget Group, was leaving.

We believe that specific belief is false. On 2026-08-05 the company's own quarterly release says Avis "entered into a seven-year contract extension" and Hertz "entered into a five-year extension" — executed language, from the issuer, about the two customers whose departure the crash was pricing. So the question is not *whether* the book was lost; it is *at what price it was retained*, and how much of the work Avis is allowed to take in-house.

That distinction is the whole investment. A repriced-but-retained contract at 2.4x leverage with an undrawn revolver is a survivable, ordinary bad year. A contract that lets the customer progressively insource the actual processing is a structurally smaller company. As of today we cannot tell those two apart from any document we have read.

## What the market believes

The market believes the 2027 earnings base is unknowable and shrinking. Two things support that: management cut the full-year EBITDA guide by about $45M and — on the earnings call — declined to guide 2027 at all; and the same guide cut Government Solutions margins by 450–500 basis points, which says the damage is not confined to the rental-car contract.

The tape is not pricing a bankruptcy. At $4.60 the stock is already about 35% above its $3.40 low, so the renewal news is at least partly absorbed. But it is still roughly 76% below its 26-week high *with executed renewals in hand* — the market is pricing a permanently smaller Commercial Services business, not a temporary reprice.

## Why we might have edge

The crash narrative and the primary text disagree, and the disagreement is eight days wide. The framework press release of 2026-07-28 said the parties had agreed "on the key commercial terms" while "working collaboratively to finalize the remaining operational terms and conditions." The 2026-08-05 release says "entered into." Anyone still trading off the July headline — including, as it turns out, our own red-team bench — is working from a superseded document. Reading issuer primary text in date order is a real and repeatable edge.

Second, the balance sheet was never the argument. Roughly $993M net debt at about 2.4x with the revolver undrawn means the company has time for a repriced contract to season. Nobody on either bench attacked solvency, because there is no solvency case to attack.

Third, the pessimism has a specific, dated resolution point rather than an open-ended one: the executed Avis and Hertz contracts must be filed as exhibits, and when they are, the insourcing scope is either capped or it is not.

## Why it might be priced in

The bounce off the low already happened without us. We would be buying after a 35% move, on news that is public, in a covered mid-cap where we have pulled zero positioning data — no broker print, no read on whether the bounce is crowded dip-buying. Information asymmetry runs against a buyer here: the counterparty selling to us may have read the contract we cannot.

More pointedly: the July framework release grants Avis "the option to selectively perform certain activities internally." That is not the same thing as the "modulate its fleet volume" language in the August release, and our original thesis blurred the two. If the insourcing option survived into the executed contract uncapped, the extension is a much weaker asset than "seven years" sounds, and the market's discount is correct rather than stale.

Finally, we cannot isolate how bad the reprice actually is. The ~$45M EBITDA cut is shared between the Commercial Services reprice and the Government Solutions margin cut. Two drivers, one number — which means our estimate of the new Commercial Services run-rate is not uniquely identified. That error runs both ways: the reprice could also be *smaller* than we modeled.
