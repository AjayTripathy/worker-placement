# PITCH — PAX — VERDICT: **PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

Patria Investments (PAX, $11.21–11.25) — the "compounding engine the market ignored" story is dead; what survives is a small, honest starter in a fairly-priced business with a dated reason to wait. Recommended action: **0.25% of book now, balance gated on the Q3 print.**

*Plain-language note: PAX is a Cayman-domiciled manager of Latin American private funds. Three terms recur below. **Fee income** (the firm calls it FRE) = management fees minus operating costs, before performance fees. **Cash earnings** (the firm calls it DE) = fee income after tax and financing, the number the Street quotes as EPS. **Performance fees** (PRE) = the firm's cut of fund profits, paid only when a fund actually sells something. **Fee-paying assets** (FEAUM) = the assets that generate management fees.*

---

## What we believe

The fee engine is real and on track. First-half fee income was $107.6M against a full-year company guide of $225–245M — 47.8% of the low end, with the second half needing only about +2.8% sequentially off a base that already includes the acquisition that closed in April.

But we do **not** believe there is a mispricing here, and that is a change from the idea that opened this file. Convert the company's own fee-income guide to cash earnings at the company's own recent conversion rate (0.888) and you get $200–218M, or **$1.26–1.37 per share** on the ~158.4M share count the company uses ($1.24–1.35 on the 161.39M actually outstanding). The Street prints **$1.38**. The market is not disbelieving the guide — it is sitting at or slightly above the top of it, and paying about 8x.

Both of our review teams independently landed on ~8x as the *fair* multiple for this business, because it is a serial acquirer whose headline metric expenses none of the assets it buys. **8x times $1.24–1.37 is $9.92–$10.96. The stock is $11.21.** On our own arithmetic we are being asked to pay 2–13% above the fair band we just derived.

So the belief is narrow and unglamorous: *this is a good, growing fee business, currently priced correctly to slightly rich, with one dated risk (an earnings-estimate cut) and one unpriced asset (accrued performance fees) that we have not been able to read.*

## What the market believes

The market believes the guide, and then some. Consensus FY26 cash EPS is $1.38 (range $1.34–1.42 per one vendor, $1.32–1.42 per another — see the bench-error table). First-half actual cash earnings were $0.59, so consensus requires **$0.79 in the second half**.

Here is the problem our defending team found and our attacking team missed: at the *top* of the company's own guide ($245M fee income, less $107.6M already booked = $137.4M), converted at 0.888, over 158.4M shares, the second half produces **$0.77 with zero performance fees**. Consensus sits above the no-carry ceiling. The Street is quietly assuming performance fees resume in a company that has printed **three consecutive quarters of zero** and cut its cumulative performance-fee target to $80–100M.

The market also believes, correctly, that this is a Brazil-beta instrument into an October national election with policy rates near 15%. The stock is down ~35% from its 52-week high and sits 6.6% off the low.

## Why we might have edge

Three honest candidates, all modest:

1. **The conversion-rate arithmetic.** Nobody publishes the fee-income-to-cash-earnings bridge; we built it from the company's own two quarters (0.844 → 0.888). It is what let us see that consensus is above the no-carry ceiling. That is a real, checkable, non-consensus number — but it points *down*, not up.
2. **The accrued performance-fee asset.** A claimed $1.49/share (~$238M) of accrued-but-unrealized performance fees exists only in an image-only investor deck and in secondary coverage. It appears nowhere in the vetted press release. If it is net of employee carry compensation and the funds hold their marks, it is a genuinely uncredited asset worth 13% of the stock. **We have not read it.** That is our single highest-value undone piece of work, and it is drainable this week.
3. **Direction on the improving conversion rate.** 0.844 → 0.888 in two quarters. If it reaches ~0.95, consensus closes without any performance fees at all and the "estimate cut" risk evaporates. Two data points is not a trend, and we say so.

Note what is *not* on this list: any disagreement with the Street about the number the thesis turns on. There isn't one.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost all of it is. The de-rating story, the roll-up discount, and the carry cut are the *published* bear case — the sell-side price-target-cut narrative says "fairly valued around 8x" in nearly these words. Our attacking team's conclusion was novel in its arithmetic and entirely conventional in its answer.

Specifically:
- **The roll-up discount is priced.** Reported net income attributable to Patria was $10.5M in 2Q26 against $57.1M of fee income — GAAP is 18% of the headline metric. Trailing GAAP EPS is $0.43, a 26x multiple. The gap is acquisition intangible amortization and contingent-consideration remeasurement, and for a firm that bought abrdn PES (2024), Nexus (2024) and WP Global (2026), that amortization *is* the recurring cost of the assets. Margin corroborates: 54.0% against the company's own 58–60% target, with management naming acquisitions as the cause.
- **The carry drought is priced.** Three zero quarters and a cut target are public.
- **The election is priced-ish.** Everyone can see the calendar.

The only thing plausibly *not* priced is the accrued carry asset, and it is unreadable in text form — which is precisely why it might not be priced, and precisely why we can't underwrite it yet.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the estimate cut lands (most likely).** Consensus at $1.38 exceeds the $0.77 no-carry second-half ceiling. Q3 prints zero performance fees again, conversion stalls near 0.89, and the sell-side walks FY26 down toward $1.30. At an unchanged 8x that is **$10.40**; if a cut also compresses the multiple to 7x, **$9.10**. *Tripwire exists:* consensus-revision watch and the Q3 print itself. This is the base case for a loss and it has a date.

**Failure narrative 2 — Brazil beta swamps the fundamentals.** The ~35% drawdown is mostly country risk, not company risk, and neither of our review teams sized it — that is a stated gap in the court, not a resolved question. An adverse October election outcome plus sticky ~15% policy rates takes EM small-caps down 10–20% and PAX more, on a beta we estimate at 1.2–1.6 but have not measured. **$8.80** is a live print. *Tripwire exists but it is a proxy, not a signal:* Brazil ETF/currency/rate watch tells us it is happening, not that it is coming.

**Failure narrative 3 — the carry asset is worth far less than $1.49/share, and we never find out until it doesn't arrive. NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.** The accrued performance-fee balance sits on unlisted Latin American infrastructure and real-estate funds, marked by the manager itself, disclosed in an image-only deck with no quarterly line item, no third-party price, and no realization schedule. If those marks are stale — and this quarter's funds were *marked down* — the asset erodes silently. There is no filing, no cadence, no vendor feed and no alternative dataset that publishes this number. No sensor can be armed against it; only a realization event that may never come would reveal it.
> **Unknown-unknown class: state variable with no publication venue** — a load-bearing valuation input whose true value has no disclosure surface at any frequency. Sibling class present here too: **control-block discretion** — Class B founder control means a governance or related-party action (an affiliate acquisition, a low-multiple take-private) would reach us only as a completed fact.

**The reflexive and structural leg.** Average volume is ~154,644 shares, about **$1.73M a day**. At our ≤1%-of-volume publishing rule that is ~1,546 shares (~$17k) of buying per session. A 0.25% starter clears in one to three sessions at any plausible book size; **a full 1% position is 10–15 sessions in and 10–15 sessions out**, and EM small-cap volume contracts into an election. Our exit is the position.

Three doctrine collisions we are walking into with open eyes:
- **Bidding into our own predicted flow.** We forecast an estimate cut, then set an add at $10.96 — meaning we get filled exactly when our forecast is right, from a seller who may know the cut is worse than we do. Adverse selection is structural here, not incidental.
- **No resting orders through the events.** A good-till-cancelled bid left through the October election and the November print is an accidental short-volatility position with no catalyst underwriting it. All bands below are **manual, post-event decision levels — no resting orders.**
- **Wash-sale versus the gated add.** If the election branch hits, we would want to harvest the starter's loss before Dec 31. But the plan adds after the Nov 11 print. Harvesting in December while holding shares bought inside the prior 30 days disallows the loss. **We can have the add or the harvest, not both in the same 30 days** — the adjudicator must pick, and this is materially more binding than the position size suggests, because 2026 is a harvest year under the current capital plan.
- **The country risk cannot be hedged in the account.** There is no physical index instrument available at the broker for this exposure, so the election leg is unhedged by construction.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the market's earnings number was too high rather than too low — consensus cut its way back down to the company's own guide while Brazil's October election drained the multiple, and we were long a fairly-priced roll-up with $1.7M a day of exit liquidity.**
