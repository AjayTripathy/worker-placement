# PITCH — ARI — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position at $6.90; reopen at $6.35 or below, pre-vote only, starter size, with a written obligation to sell before the shares stop trading.

---

## What we believe

Apollo Commercial Real Estate Finance (NYSE: ARI) is winding itself up. It sold roughly $9 billion of its loan book for cash at 99.7% of commitments, already paid shareholders $3.75 per share in July, and has asked shareholders to approve a formal plan of dissolution at a special meeting on **September 29, 2026**. Management's own estimate of total remaining value is **$7.75 to $8.50 per share**. The stock trades at **$6.90**.

So the surface pitch is: pay $6.90, get roughly $3.85 back in cash about a month after the vote, and hold a stub costing you about **$3.05** against a balance sheet that says the leftover real estate is worth **$3.79 per share** net of its mortgage. That looks like a 20%+ discount.

**We do not believe that.** After running both benches, our actual belief is narrower and more negative:

1. The cash part is real and verifiable. The company holds **$1.24 billion of cash** and has **no corporate debt**. Book value per share reconciles exactly: ($1,254,860k equity − $169,300k preferred) ÷ 128,212,093 shares = **$8.47**. ([10-Q, June 30 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467760/000119312526342628/R2.htm))
2. The leftover — what you own after the cash comes back — is **not a diversified pool**. It is four properties, and **74% of the stub's value is one ground-up apartment development in Brooklyn**, carried at $662 million with $371 million of construction debt against it.
3. That $662 million is **not an appraisal**. The company took the property by deed-in-lieu of foreclosure in August 2022 at a fair value of **$270.1 million** — essentially the land — and has been adding construction spending on top ever since. Real estate held this way is carried at cost and only written down if *undiscounted* future cash flows fall below carrying value, a test so permissive that carrying value can sit far above what the building would actually sell for.
4. The property's own reported economics do not visibly support the carrying value. First-quarter 2026 property-level rental income was **$3.7 million against $3.2 million of expenses** — roughly $0.5 million of quarterly operating income. Supporting $662 million at a 5% capitalization rate would require about **$33 million a year**. The property is still leasing up, so this is directionally damning rather than conclusive, but it is the single most important number in the trade and it points the wrong way.

**Conclusion: the market's ~18% "discount" is mostly not a discount.** It is the ordinary gap between what a half-built building cost and what it is worth, plus a fair charge for the fact that the shares eventually stop trading. We think ARI is roughly **fairly priced** at $6.90 — not a bargain, not a fraud.

## What the market believes

The market has read the same proxy. Management published the $7.75–$8.50 range on **July 14, 2026**. Six weeks later the stock sits **11% below the low end of that range** and only **7.2% above its post-distribution 52-week low of $6.435**.

Four new institutional ownership filings landed *after* the wind-down was announced (two new 13Gs on July 30, amendments on July 31 and August 6). Event-driven and liquidation-focused funds are already in the name. The people who do this for a living have looked and priced it here.

The most likely market view, stated fairly: *"The cash is good. The Brooklyn building is worth meaningfully less than $662 million. The trust interests you end up holding can't be sold. $6.90 is about right."*

We think that view is correct. We are not claiming the market is wrong; we are claiming the price becomes interesting only if it falls further.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly, the edge here is thin, and we should say so.

- **The headline decline is fake and screens will keep showing it.** The stock is down 38.6% from its 52-week high, but $3.75 of that is the dividend already paid. Adjusted, it is down **7.8%** ($11.235 − $3.75 = $7.485; $6.90 ÷ $7.485 − 1 = −7.8%). Anything screening on drawdown will mis-rank this name for months. That is a real, if small, source of forced mispricing at the margin.
- **We did the property-level work.** Very little published research separates "the carrying value is an appraisal" from "the carrying value is accumulated construction cost." We found the 2022 deed-in-lieu basis and the quarterly property income. That is the specific fact that turns a "20% discount" into "fairly priced," and it is not obvious from the deck.
- **We dated the exit.** The proxy says the trust transfer and delisting happen "at a future date to be determined by our board," while the initial cash distribution is dated relative to the vote. The two are separated in time. That means this is a listed, sellable trade with a deadline — not a locked box — provided we actually enforce the deadline.
- **We know the anchor is not an anchor.** The $9 billion sale at 99.7% was to **Athene, Apollo's own insurance arm** — a sale from an Apollo-managed REIT to an Apollo-owned insurer, which is why it required a special committee of independent directors and a separately-retained fairness opinion. Anyone treating that print as third-party validation of the remaining marks is making a mistake. ([8-K exhibit, April 24 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001467760/000119312526177686/d24584dex991.htm))

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is.

- The liquidation is announced, the range is published, the vote is scheduled, the specialists are already shareholders. There is no information asymmetry about *what is happening*.
- Every negative we found is derivable from public filings by anyone willing to read the property note. We were slow to it, not uniquely able.
- The discount to the low end of management's range has persisted for six weeks at high liquidity. That is not neglect; that is a price.
- The one thing that would genuinely move the value — a real appraisal or a signed contract on the Brooklyn asset — is unavailable to us *and* to the market. We have no edge in a fact nobody has.

The correct framing: this is **a fairly-paid risk, not a discount**. Under our own rules, a fairly-paid risk with bounded tails can be owned — but only at a price where the risk is fairly paid, and $6.90 is not obviously that price.

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — The Brooklyn building is worth $400–450 million, not $662 million. (Monitorable, partially.)

If the property clears at $450 million, the equity above its $371 million mortgage is $79 million, or **$0.62 per share** instead of $2.27. Total leftover value drops from $3.79 to roughly **$2.14 per share** — a **30% loss on the $3.05 stub** and about **−19% on the whole $6.90 position** even after the cash comes back. At $400 million the stub loses **43%**.

The leverage is what makes this violent. Because the property carries a $371 million mortgage against a $662 million carrying value, a 44% decline in the property's gross value wipes out the equity entirely, and a 22% decline halves it. The blue bench's original work applied a blended haircut to the whole property portfolio and concluded it took a 59% decline to zero out — **that was wrong**, and the red bench corrected it.

| Brooklyn gross value | Equity above mortgage | Total leftover per share | Return on $3.05 stub |
|---|---|---|---|
| at carrying value ($662M) | $291M | $3.79 | +24% |
| −10% | $225M | $3.27 | +7% |
| −20% | $159M | $2.75 | **−10%** |
| −30% | $92M | $2.24 | **−27%** |
| −44% | $0 | $1.52 | **−50%** |

*Tripwire:* any disclosure of an appraisal, a signed contract, a refinancing, or quarterly property-level operating income. Partially monitorable — the company is not obligated to publish an appraisal, and if it never sells the property while we own it, we never learn the answer.

### Failure narrative 2 — We are still holding when the shares stop trading. (Monitorable.)

The proxy states plainly that trust interests "**will not be transferable** (except by will, intestate succession or operation of law)," "**will not be represented by certificates**," and that the company expects to "**delist our Common Stock from the NYSE and deregister**" — with the contingency reserve's final payout possibly "**not occur[ring] for up to three years**." ([DEFM14A, August 24 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467760/000119312526363807/d119099ddefm14a.htm))

For this account that is close to fatal on its own: no ability to sell, no ability to realize a tax loss, likely no margin value, possibly no ability for our broker to custody the interest at all, and annual pass-through tax reporting on income we cannot access. Our core discipline requires positions we can sell for tax-loss purposes; this instrument destroys that permanently.

The board sets the delisting date. If we are asleep on the day it is announced, we cannot fix it afterward.

*Tripwire:* an 8-K or press release naming a delisting or trust-transfer date. This one is genuinely monitorable, and the trade is only allowable *because* we can monitor it.

### Failure narrative 3 — Something about the residual we cannot see. (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.)

This is the one we cannot instrument, and we should be blunt about it.

We do not know the Brooklyn property's **street address, block and lot, unit count, or bedroom mix**. The 10-Q and the quarterly deck disclose the city only. Without those, we cannot check market rents against the census data for that neighborhood, cannot check flood or storm hazard on a waterfront-adjacent New York site, cannot check the building's certificate-of-occupancy status, and cannot determine whether the "99% of *market* units leased" language implies a separate rent-restricted affordable band under a tax-abatement program that would materially cap the exit value. Every one of those is a live question and every one is unanswerable from the public record as it stands.

Similarly: the Massachusetts healthcare property ($25 million) is a joint venture with other Apollo funds, and the filings **name no operator, disclose no rent coverage, and include no going-concern language**. That is the same collateral class that produced large surprise write-offs at other healthcare landlords. It is small in dollars and the least transparent of the four assets.

And we never read the Form 144 filed August 25, 2026 (accession 0001949846-26-000541). Almost certainly immaterial against 128 million shares — but "almost certainly" is what we are relying on, not "we checked."

**Unknown-unknown class: undisclosed asset-level facts in a self-liquidating vehicle where the seller controls both the disclosure and the sale process.** The failure mode is not that a number moves against us; it is that a fact we never knew existed — an entitlement condition, an environmental finding, a ground lease, a tax-abatement clawback, a JV partner consent right, an operator insolvency — surfaces only when the asset is marketed, at which point the price already reflects it and we cannot sell what we hold. There is no filing to watch for this, because the whole category is defined by not being in the filings.

### The reflexive and structural leg

- **Our own size versus daily volume: unverified.** The evidence pack contains no average daily volume for ARI, and our broker's price-history and contract-search tools returned *permission not granted* in this session, so neither bench could build a volume series. We are therefore proposing a starter-size position with an explicit constraint — **no more than 1% of trailing 20-day average dollar volume per day** — that we cannot currently compute. That constraint must be measured before any order is placed, not after.
- **We would be buying into flow we ourselves predict.** Our rule against bidding into our own predicted selling applies directly here. After the initial distribution, the stub is a sub-$400 million, no-dividend, no-index, delisting-scheduled security. Index funds must sell it. Income funds must sell it. Our reopen band of $6.35 sits essentially *at* the post-distribution 52-week low of $6.435 — which is to say, we would be buying exactly where the forced sellers are, which is right, but it also means our own bid is competing with a wave we expect to be larger than us.
- **Doctrine collision.** This position violates the spirit of the tax-loss-harvesting core discipline the moment it becomes a trust interest, cannot be paired against anything, and generates phantom taxable income. Under our own framework this is at best a small satellite position with a hard exit date — never a core holding, never sized as one.
- **Governance reflexivity.** After delisting there is no market check. An Apollo affiliate would be selling assets — one of which is already an Apollo-fund joint venture — potentially to other Apollo funds, with no shareholder able to exit. The manager waived its termination fee (genuinely good), but the trust agreement renews annually and the only quantified fee (0.50% per year, about $0.027 per share) goes to an Apollo affiliate. The dollars are trivial; the incentive to be slow is not.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the Brooklyn development's $662 million carrying value was accumulated construction cost rather than market value, and the levered equity above its $371 million mortgage — 74% of what we would own after the cash comes back — turned out to be worth a fraction of the $2.27 per share the balance sheet implies, while the shares delisted into a non-transferable trust before we could sell out of the mistake.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry at $6.90.** The red bench recommends outright rejection; the blue bench overturns several of red's reasons but reaches the same practical answer through the property-valuation finding. Under our response framework, a valuation objection produces a **price gate**, not a permanent ban. So:

**No purchase at $6.90. Reopen conditions below.**

**Reopen band — primary**
- **Buy at $6.35 or lower**, pre-vote only.
- Rationale, from the benches' own arithmetic: at $6.35, subtracting the expected $3.85 initial cash distribution leaves a stub cost of **$2.50** against $3.79 per share of stated net property equity — a 52% cushion, which still survives a 30% decline in the Brooklyn property (leftover $2.24 → break-even-ish) and only loses badly at −44% or worse. At $6.90 the same cushion is 24% and turns negative at a 20% Brooklyn decline.
- $6.35 is essentially the post-distribution 52-week low of $6.435. We are explicitly proposing to buy at the low, into forced selling, with a dated exit.

**Size**
- **Starter only: 0.5% of the book.** Hard cap 1.0% under any circumstance.
- **Hard execution constraint: no more than 1% of the trailing 20-day average dollar volume per day.** *This number is currently unmeasured — our broker market-data tools were unavailable this session. Measure before the first order; do not place an order on an unmeasured volume constraint.*

**Adds**
- **None.** No averaging down. If the stub thesis is wrong, it is wrong because of a fact about one building, and buying more of a wrong building is not diversification.
- One exception: if the company publishes a **third-party appraisal or executed contract on Brooklyn within 10% of the $662 million carrying value**, the primary objection is answered and a starter becomes justified at up to **$6.90**, still pre-vote, still capped at 1%.

**Exits — mandatory, in writing, before any purchase**
1. **Sell in full on the first public announcement of a delisting date or a trust-transfer date.** No discretion, no waiting for a better print. We never hold this into the trust.
2. **Sell in full if the initial distribution is announced below $3.50 per share** — that would mean the reserve is being sized for problems we cannot see.
3. **Sell in full if the shareholder vote fails** on September 29. The thesis is the liquidation; without it there is a small, externally managed, unprofitable mortgage REIT with no plan.
4. **Trim half if the stock reaches $7.50** without new information about Brooklyn. At $7.50 the stub cost is $3.65 against $3.79 of stated value — there is no margin left, and the only thing we would be holding is exposure to the one asset we cannot value.

**Catalyst dates**
- **September 29, 2026, 10:00 a.m. ET** — special meeting, virtual. Requires the affirmative vote of a majority of *all* shares entitled to vote, not merely a majority of those voting. Record date was **August 21, 2026**. Confirmed from the definitive proxy.
- **Approximately October 29, 2026** — initial cash distribution of **$3.70–$4.00 per share**, stated as "within approximately 30 days following stockholder approval." Date is derived, not confirmed.
- The October 29 "earnings date" in our evidence pack is a data-vendor artifact, unconfirmed by the company, and not decisive for a company in dissolution.

## What remains unverified

- **The Brooklyn property's market value.** No appraisal, no contract, no broker opinion — public or private. Our conclusion that it is worth materially less than $662 million rests on one lease-up-stage quarter of property income and the accounting basis of the carrying value. Direction confirmed; magnitude is an estimate.
- **Brooklyn's address, block and lot, unit count, and bedroom mix.** Not disclosed. Without them we cannot price the building from market rents, cannot check flood or storm hazard, cannot check its certificate-of-occupancy status, and cannot determine whether an affordable-housing tax-abatement band caps the exit. This is the highest-value missing check in the entire review.
- **Q2 2026 property-level operating income for Brooklyn.** Not obtained. Q1 was $3.7M income against $3.2M expenses.
- **The $55.5 million ($0.43 per share) gap** between the company's presentation ($912M / $541M) and the 10-Q balance sheet ($857M / $485.5M). Plausibly the equity-method joint venture plus property-level assets outside the "real estate owned, net" line, but not reconciled. We use the lower number throughout.
- **Who holds the Series B-1 preferred**, and whether it accrues or participates. If Apollo-affiliated, an affiliate sits ahead of common shareholders in a liquidation an affiliate administers. Neither bench checked.
- **Whether our broker will custody a non-transferable, uncertificated liquidating trust interest at all.** We have no written confirmation. Our exit rule is designed so we never find out — but the rule is the only protection.
- **The Massachusetts healthcare property's operator, rent coverage, and going-concern status.** $25 million, undisclosed, and the hardest of the four assets to sell.
- **Average daily trading volume.** Unavailable this session; our size constraint cannot be computed until it is.
- **Whether the board intends to sell assets and distribute cash before any trust transfer**, which would keep the shares listed to completion and materially improve the trade. Not stated in the proxy.
- **The Form 144 filed August 25, 2026.** Not read.
