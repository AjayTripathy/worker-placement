**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY. Stay flat and place no orders into the August 24 earnings report; this is a deferred decision pending one specific number, not a permanent rejection — reopen the file on August 25 at the gates below.**

## What we believe

PicPay (Nasdaq: PICS) is a large Brazilian digital bank that listed on Nasdaq in January 2026 at $19.00 and now trades at $10.22 — down 48.8% from its high, up 22.8% off its $8.32 low. It is a real, revenue-generating business: it has told the market to expect R$3.6 billion of second-quarter revenue and R$245 million of adjusted net income, up 45% from the prior quarter.

The idea we were testing was that the company's loan losses are forming faster than it is setting money aside to cover them, and that its very fast loan growth hides this. **That idea is currently unproven — and we now believe it is neither confirmed nor refuted.** The first bench declared it dead; the second bench showed the test used to kill it cannot actually tell the difference between a healthy book and a deteriorating one. So we are left with an open question, not an answer.

What we do believe with confidence:

1. **The resolving number arrives in five trading days.** The company said in a filed press release that it will report on August 24 after the close. That is a primary source, not a guess: [SEC 6-K, August 10, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841644/000121390026087277/0001213900-26-087277-index.htm).
2. **Something is selling the stock right now, and we don't know what.** The shares fell 10.5% on August 17 with no company news since an August 12 ownership filing. The lock-up on insider shares from the January IPO expired around July 29. Only about 21.4 million of the 129.6 million shares trade freely (16.5%). We predicted supply would arrive; we should not be the buyer standing in front of it.
3. **The legal overhang is financially trivial but epistemically expensive.** There is a securities claim tied to the IPO. The absolute statutory maximum is 22,857,143 shares × ($19.00 − $10.22) = **$200.7 million**, and typical settlements of 2–10% put the realistic exposure at $4–24 million, roughly 0.3–1.8% of the $1.325 billion market value. The dollars don't matter. What matters is that it makes us distrust management's own disclosures as evidence — which means we need an outside read on the business, and nobody produced one.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a broken IPO. It has cut the price in half in seven months and marked it down another 10.5% going *into* an earnings report — the opposite of buyers positioning for good news. At $10.22 the company is worth about $1.325 billion.

There is also a specific, recent memory in the tape: on June 2 the stock rose 4.79% after hours to $11.60 on the first-quarter release, then traded below $9.00 within 48 hours. The market has already learned once this year that the initial reaction to this company's numbers is not the durable one.

Against that, the company has reaffirmed its guidance twice in August, including in the same filing that announced a CFO change, and it is guiding profit to grow far faster than revenue.

## Why we might have edge

- **The float is tiny and the news is binary.** With 16.5% of shares floating and a definite report date, a result that clears our bar can re-rate the stock hard from a base that is now 10.5% lower than when we first looked. Waiting for the number costs us the first move but buys us the actual answer.
- **We have an independent way to check the business that nobody used.** PicPay is a consumer app. Our app-review velocity tool reads customer sentiment and adoption directly from the app stores — it does not pass through the company's accounting. Because our whole problem here is that we don't trust the company's numbers as evidence, this is the one check that would actually resolve the question. Neither bench ran it. That is our edge if we run it before August 24 and our failure if we don't.
- **One filing answers a big question.** An ownership filing landed on August 12 — accession 0001229262-26-000020. A holder crossing 5% on a 21.4-million-share float five days before earnings is either the most important buy signal or the most important sell signal in this case. Two adversarial reviews left it unread. It is a single fetch.
- **We know what number decides it.** Not "how did the quarter look" but: the 90-days-late loan rate, and the reserve held specifically against already-bad loans. That precision means we can be right or wrong quickly instead of arguing.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The stock has already fallen 49%.** A market that has halved a name is not ignoring credit risk. Our "damage is coming" story may simply be the consensus story.
- **The shares turn over fast.** About 2.04 million shares trade daily against a 21.4-million free float — the entire float changes hands roughly every 10.5 days. This is a heavily-traded, heavily-discussed situation, not a forgotten one. We originally called it structurally illiquid; that was wrong, and being wrong about obscurity usually means being wrong about edge.
- **We have no valuation.** Neither bench produced an estimate of what the company is actually worth. Our reopening rule is "buy at or below 85% of fair value," and we have no number for fair value. Without it we cannot claim the price is wrong — only that it moved.
- **The tautology.** Our headline finding was "bad loans grew 45% while the book grew 17% — 2.6 times faster." That is arithmetic, not insight: 1.236 × 1.17 = 1.446. It restates "the late-payment rate went from 7.2% to 8.9%" by multiplying in the very growth we claimed was hiding it. Both benches agreed. We had less evidence than we thought.
