# PITCH — UAMY — PROPOSED VERDICT
**REJECT — no position at $5.34; reopen only on the evidence gates below. PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION.**

## What we believe

United States Antimony's $57.3 million defense (DLA) order book is real, but it is not the insulation the original thesis hoped for. The 10-Q's contract note says each delivery order is priced when it is placed, at the market rate prevailing that day plus certain fees ([10-Q, sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101538/000110465926094035/uamy-20260630x10q.htm)). Only about $12 million of orders existed as of June 30; roughly $45 million — about 80% of the book — was placed in July and August, after antimony prices collapsed. So the book's dollars are reasonably secure, but they were struck near today's depressed prices, not at the $31.71/lb of the first order. There is no hidden ~$25 million of locked-in gross profit waiting in the second half.

What's left is an expensive commodity price-taker: roughly $690 million of enterprise value against a $60–75 million revenue guide (about 10× sales), a 7% gross margin quarter, an operating-loss run rate near $28 million a year, stock compensation running at 37% of revenue, and management media appearances in the two business days before the guidance cut.

## What the market believes

The stock has already traded the news. The Q2 report and guidance cut landed August 11; by the August 13 close the stock was at $5.34, **up 7.44% on the day** — the market sold the cut hard (the stock is 73% off its $19.71 high) and then bought the dip. The prevailing story appears to be a strategic-metals option: a US-listed antimony producer with government contracts, a $62 million cash pile, and a stake in Larvotto, where the defense backlog is treated as proof the story works regardless of the antimony price. The bounce says a meaningful group of buyers reads the $57.3 million book as insulation.

## Why we might have edge

The one thing we did that the bounce-buyers likely have not: we read the contract mechanics in the 10-Q rather than the headline backlog number. "Pricing determined at the time each delivery order is placed at prevailing market rates" plus the July–August timing of ~80% of the orders means the backlog's economics track the collapsed spot price. The CEO's own forward framing on the earnings call — economics off "$10 a pound" against $4–6 feedstock — is consistent with that, not with $31.71. We also confirmed that none of the premium-priced DLA revenue was in Q2's numbers ($2.6 million slipped to Q3 on invoice approval timing), so the reported 7% gross margin slightly *understates* the go-forward mix — a concession to the bulls that we sized and found insufficient.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetrically: at $5.34 the stock has already given back 73% from its high, and our REJECT is a valuation-and-economics call, not a fraud or solvency call. The delivery orders are fixed-dollar once placed, so the second half and 2027 revenue is more secure than a pure spot-seller's. Q2's margin excluded the DLA pounds. The insider picture is mixed, not one-way — a director *bought* 12,500 shares at about $7.45 in June (the red bench initially misread that filing as selling). If antimony prices recover, new delivery orders re-strike higher automatically. None of that makes 10× sales with negative carry ownable today; all of it caps our conviction that the stock must fall further.
