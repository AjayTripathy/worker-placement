**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — consensus kill (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

Coupang's Korean demand did recover after the 2026 data breach, and that recovery is now measurable in won, not just in company slides: August monthly active users hit 35.95M (up ~510k from July) and monthly card spend crossed ₩5T, a record, after troughing at ₩4.02T in February (Herald/SBS, per Blue Bench). The churn-death-spiral story is, on the demand axis, wrong.

We also believe that this is not the same as a trade. Three things sit between "demand recovered" and "the stock makes money":

1. **The recovery does not convert into dollars.** Q2-26 consolidated revenue grew +4% reported vs +10% constant-currency; product-commerce grew +1% reported vs +8% cc. A USD-listed share earns the reported number. Worse, gross profit *fell* 3% reported and gross margin fell 188bps to 28.2% ([8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-08-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1834584/000183458426000070/cpng-06302026ex991.htm)).
2. **Cash generation is genuinely impaired.** 1H26 operating cash flow $551M vs $899M in 1H25 (−39%). TTM OCF $1.4B is −$484M year over year; TTM FCF is $105M. Against ~$26B of market cap and $6.1B cash / ~$3.7B debt, that is roughly 229x EV/FCF. We tested the charitable explanation — that the ₩624.6B (~$278M) privacy fine caused the cash decline — and it fails: the fine was *announced* 2026-06-10 and accrues to liabilities; an accrual does not reduce operating cash flow.
3. **Q3 has a hole our best instrument cannot see.** The July Incheon fulfillment-center fire destroyed building, inventory and operating capacity, with press estimates in the hundreds of billions of won. MAU and card spend measure whether customers want to buy. They cannot measure whether Coupang can deliver.

So: we believe the bull's *fact* and reject the bull's *trade*. Fair-value-wise, the one independent updated fair value in the record sits near $15.00 — essentially the tape at $14.745. There is no visible discount to harvest.

## What the market believes

The market believes exactly what we believe about demand, and is repricing earnings, not revenue. FY26 revenue consensus is unchanged at $37.8–38.1B; FY26 EPS has been walked from −$0.168 to −$0.196 to −$0.227. Pre-Q1 street was +$0.14 EPS on $38.1B — the whole move is on profitability, not units.

The tape says the same thing. Coupang closed $16.75 on print day (2026-08-04), fell ~7% after hours, and trades $14.745 now: about −12% *after* the 24.7M active-customer rebound printed, and after the August Korean demand data that the bull case calls decisive. Short interest is 27.9M shares, 2.17% of float, 1.39 days to cover — nobody is trapped short. This is real-money selling, not a coiled spring.

Stock is 56.7% off its 52-week high ($34.07) and 1.83% off its 52-week low ($14.48).

## Why we might have edge

Honest inventory — this is short:

- **KRW.** The gap between +10% cc and +4% reported is a currency wedge, and the cost base is also in won. If the won mean-reverts, reported revenue and reported gross profit re-rate with no operational change. Neither bench priced this. It is the cleanest un-owned upside mechanism in the file.
- **The remaining $907M buyback authorization.** At $14.745 that is ~61.5M shares, about **3.1 days of proxy ADV** — a real, non-trivial bid if management uses it. Management's credibility is dented (23M shares bought at ~$19.96 in Q2, now ~−26%, ~$122M of mark destroyed in one quarter), but the residual authorization is materially more accretive at $14.7 than at $20.
- **Gross-profit decomposition.** The −188bps GM print is a fact neither bench initially used and the sell-side commentary in the record barely discusses. If the Q3 8-K shows the contraction is fire/fine-driven rather than structural mix, the market has to re-do work it hasn't done.

That is a *conditional, event-gated* edge, not a live one. There is no edge in the demand story: we agree with the street and the street agrees with us.

- The demand refutation printed publicly on 2026-08-04 and the stock fell 12% afterwards. The market saw the datum and kept selling. (Named mechanism: **post_refutation_tape_decay** — a "market hasn't seen this" thesis where the market has.)
- The cash-flow deterioration is a headline bullet in Coupang's own earnings release and in press coverage — not a discovery.

- Margin contraction of 300–400bps is *guided by the company* into mid-2027. Buying "margins are fine" against management's own guidance is not contrarian; it is inattentive.
- Post-audit kill-stack composition: 2 novel findings vs 5 consensus findings. A majority-consensus kill stack caps sizing on both sides — but the one leg that was genuinely anchored against the street (we-agree-with-consensus-on-demand) is the leg the bull case needed.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — We enter on the reopen gate and the "positive reported PC growth" print is FX luck, not operations.** Reported growth turns +2% because the won firmed, gross margin stays sub-28.2%, and the stock gets one relief day before resuming the earnings downgrade path. *Tripwire exists:* the 2026-11-03 print — reported (not cc) PC growth **and** GM vs the 28.2% base, plus USDKRW at print date. Monitorable; sized-for.

**Failure narrative 2 — The Incheon fire loss lands bigger and slower than the press number, and insurance recovery is partial, contested, or booked in a different period than the loss.** Loss hits Q3-26 operating expense; recovery, if any, arrives in 2027 as a non-operating credit. Ex-item margin never gets cleanly computable, so the 8-K reopen gate never actually trips clean and we sit in a position we entered on a gate that turned out to be un-gradeable. *Tripwire exists but is weak:* an 8-K with quantification is required for the gate, and the gate can fail by *silence* — which looks like "no news" rather than "gate failed." We name that asymmetry explicitly.

**Failure narrative 3 (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE) — Korean regulatory/legislative escalation beyond the PIPC fine.** The ₩624.6B fine is one action by one regulator. What has no tripwire in our stack: a follow-on Fair Trade Commission proceeding, a National Assembly platform-regulation bill, MFDS/labor-ministry action after a fatality or a second fire, or a political decision to make the breach a governance case rather than a fine. **None of these file an 8-K on the day they happen, none appear on EDGAR in real time, and none are in our Korean-language feed — we have no feed.** A single 10% down-day arrives with no prior signal in any sensor we own. **Unknown-unknown class: extra-jurisdictional sovereign/regulatory cascade** — adverse state action in a non-US home jurisdiction, disclosed first in a foreign language to a foreign regulator, reaching us only after the price. A related unnamed sub-class: **physical-plant tail recurrence** (a second node loss), which our FEMA-based hazard detector structurally cannot see because Korean sites are outside its coverage.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our size vs liquidity:* we have no ADV feed in the pack. Derived proxy from the benches' own short-interest math: 27.9M shares ÷ 1.39 days to cover ≈ **20.1M shares/day ≈ $296M/day**. A 1.0% weight per $100M of book = ~67.8k shares = **0.34% of proxy ADV**. We are not the marginal price-setter at any size we would carry. Caveat: days-to-cover is a ~20-day average and is itself unverified (item 5 below).
- *Predicted flow:* the $907M authorization (~3.1 days ADV) is the only large predictable buyer. It is also the flow most likely to be *paused* — a board that bought at $19.96 into a −39% OCF quarter may well be told to conserve cash, especially with a fine payable and a fire loss unquantified. So the flow we would be leaning on is the flow most correlated with the risk we would be carrying. That is the reflexive trap here: **the buyback bid and the cash-flow impairment are the same variable with opposite signs.**
- *Doctrine interactions:* **post_refutation_tape_decay** (our refuting datum is already tape-priced) and **kpi_blind_supply_shock** (our demand instrument is blind to the binding supply constraint) both fire *against* entry, and they compound — the first says our evidence is stale, the second says our evidence is aimed at the wrong axis. Meanwhile **no-squeeze** (2.17% float short) removes the mechanical rebound path, so a wrong entry has no rescue mechanic and must be exited manually into the same distribution that created the low.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ that we bought a confirmed demand recovery that the market had already priced, into a quarter whose damage was on the supply and cash-flow side where our instruments were blind.
