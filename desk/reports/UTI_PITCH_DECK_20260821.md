# PITCH — UTI — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — DECLINE the long at $29.12 on two surviving legs (the ex-investment earnings cut is structural, and management reaffirmed a cash guide it could not hit); not a short; reopen only at $21 or on proof the enrollment miss is recoverable. Tape has since fallen to $23.70 — still above the gate, no entry authorized.**

---

## What we believe

Universal Technical Institute runs trade schools — auto, diesel, welding, HVAC, and (through its Concorde business) healthcare training. On 2026-08-05 it cut its full-year outlook for the first time in three years, and the stock fell 34% in a session. The obvious trade was to buy the panic: enrollment was still growing, management has a good track record, and the cut looked like the cost of opening new campuses.

We looked and concluded the cheap version of that story is wrong.

The company publishes a figure it calls **baseline earnings** — profit with every dollar of new-campus and new-program spending removed, by construction. It exists precisely so investors can see the base business without the expansion drag. That figure was cut from about **$156M to about $135M**, essentially as hard as the headline number. At the same time, spending on new campuses went **down**, from ~$40M to ~$35M. So the cut cannot be "we spent more on growth." On the earnings call the CFO gave his own breakdown of the $20M shortfall: roughly **70% a missed enrollment target in auto/diesel high-school recruiting, roughly 30% a permanent shift in what students choose to study** — and nothing attributed to spending timing. Stripped of expansion costs entirely, the base business is now guided to grow profit **1.7% on 7.3% more revenue**. That is margin going backwards in the part of the business that was supposed to be leveraging.

Second, and separately: on **2026-05-06** management reaffirmed full-year free cash flow of **+$20–25M** when it already knew six-month cash flow was **−$44.2M**. Hitting the reaffirmed number would have required the best second half in company history by a wide margin. Three months later the guide went to **−$20M to $0M**. The CEO also said the recruiting-staff shortfall (about 140 recruiters against a need for ~170) ran "throughout the year" — meaning the cause of the miss was knowable in May. The credibility break is the reaffirmation, not the cut.

Third, the fall in the stock is not a mystery to be arbitraged. A 12.9% cut to earnings times a 24.6% cut to the multiple equals 34.3%; the stock fell 34.2%. The multiple compression is earned: a company that beat and raised for two straight years carried a premium *for that reliability*, and the first cut removes it.

So: real business, real demand at the core brand, honest and complete disclosure — but the marketed takeaway ("timing, demand intact, long-term targets reaffirmed") does not match the company's own segregated numbers. We decline at these prices. We are not short: the balance sheet has net cash, the operators are credible, and the 70% piece is a fixable staffing problem.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a temporary stumble at a good compounder. Six analysts cover it, all rated buy or strong buy, with an average price target of **$41.83** — roughly 76% above today's $23.70. The bull case is the company's own: student demand is strong (new starts grew 10.9% last quarter), the missed students are slipping into next year rather than disappearing, campus-expansion costs are front-loaded and will be absorbed as the new schools fill, and the 2029 targets ($1.2B revenue, ~$270M baseline profit) are intact.

The day after our court, the stock bounced 4.5% — the market re-bidding with no new information. Since then it has done the opposite: **−18.6% from $29.12 to $23.70**, now 11% above its 52-week low. We do not know what caused that drift and we say so below.

Two things the consensus is not arguing with, because it mostly does not discuss them: full-year earnings per share are guided **down 46%** ($1.13 → ~$0.60), and free cash flow **flipped from positive to negative**.

## Why we might have edge

1. **We read the company's own control metric instead of its headline.** The press release leads with the one number that grew — student starts. Baseline earnings, the number designed to answer exactly the question at issue, is mentioned once in the filed release and reconciled only in a separate slide deck. Reading it is what turned "timing" into "structural."
2. **A student start is not a unit of revenue.** Students are migrating from auto/diesel programs (~51 weeks) to skilled-trades programs (~9 months). A start counted at par carries roughly a quarter less revenue. That is why starts grew 10.9% while revenue grew 7.2% and the revenue guide fell. Anyone using starts as the "demand is fine" proof is using a metric that management itself names as a cause of the cut.
3. **We checked the reaffirmation against the cash flow statement.** Almost no one does the arithmetic on what a mid-year reaffirmation actually required.
4. **We read the credit-loss line.** Nine-month provision for student credit losses rose **48.7%** while the receivable book grew 5.8% and revenue grew 7.8%. Some of the enrollment growth is being financed in-house to less creditworthy students. This is disclosed in the cash flow statement and appears in no quote, headline, or slide.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly, most of it probably is, and our own benches said so.

- The 34% drop decomposes arithmetically into the earnings cut times the multiple reset with essentially no residual. Our blue team then pointed out that this decomposition is **a tautology** — price is by definition earnings times multiple, so the multiple leg absorbs whatever is left over and the test can never find an overreaction. It proves nothing. The "no dislocation" conclusion has to rest on the peer comparison and the loss of beat-and-raise status, not on that identity.
- The stock is no longer expensive on a trailing basis. At $23.70 it trades at about **10.3× last year's actual profit — below the 10.9× it carried twelve months ago**. Red's "more expensive than a year ago" point was true at $27.88 and is no longer true at $23.70. That leg has decayed with the tape.
- The base business is guided at **">$135M"** — a floor, not a point. The −12.5% cut is the worst case as management framed it.
- Baseline earnings strip out *investment* phasing, not *enrollment* phasing. If the missed high-school students genuinely enroll next year, part of the 70% comes back and the "structural" label softens. Our red team's "0% timing" over-read the CFO's spoken bridge.
- The company just refinanced into a **$200M revolving credit facility maturing August 2031** with Fifth Third, JPMorgan, Truist, Citi and PNC (8-K filed 2026-08-18, accession 0001193125-26-354799). Five banks underwrote this business two weeks after the cut. That removes a funding tail we would otherwise have to carry against negative free cash flow — and it is evidence *against* the darkest reading. It is not evidence about earnings.

If the December-quarter enrollment recovers and the November guide holds the base business flat or better, the decline will look like a missed 44% move.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry. Position: none. Orders: none. Size authorized today: 0% of book.**

The adjudication is a decline, not a wait-for-a-dip. The reopen conditions below are taken verbatim from the adjudication and are not to be substituted with our own levels:

- **Price ≤ $21 → re-derive.** This is a trigger to redo the work, not a buy order. Reaching $21 does not by itself authorize size.
- **Q1 FY27 starts ≥ +12% → the flip path activates.** This is the test of whether the missed students actually enrolled rather than vanished. If it fires, the "structural" label on the 70% piece fails and the long is genuinely back on the table.
- **LINC Q2 (2026-08-10): if LINC absorbs its (smaller) expansion drag and raises guidance again, the "this is UTI's execution, not the sector" leg strengthens. If LINC cuts too, it is the sector, not execution → soften our case.**

**Where the price sits against the gate.** At $23.70 the stock is **11.4% above the $21 reopen** and 11.3% above its 52-week low of $21.29 — the gate and the low are effectively the same level. That matters: if it gets there, we must establish whether it is a real repricing on new information or just drift, because a reopen that coincides with a technical low invites us to buy our own chart. At $21 the company would be worth about $1.17B, roughly **11.4× guided profit** and **35× guided earnings per share**, still with negative free cash flow this year. At today's $23.70 those are **12.9×** and **39×**.

**No adds, no trims, no exits** — there is nothing to add to or trim. If a re-derive at ≤$21 clears, standing doctrine gives it a minimum starter and the size is set at that re-derive, not pre-committed here.

**Catalyst dates:**
- **2026-08-10 — LINC Q2 (already passed, result not yet pulled into this deck).** This is an open obligation, not a future event.
- **~2026-11-18 (unconfirmed) — FY26 year-end results and the initial FY27 outlook.** This is the real test and the single most important date. Two things resolve here: (a) the FY27 baseline profit guide — does the base business re-accelerate or is 15% margin the new level; (b) the fourth-quarter cash number. The current-year cash guide implies **fourth-quarter operating cash flow of about $79.6M against $57.1M last year — up 39% in a year when nine-month cash flow is down 57%.** That is the most fragile number in the guide.
- **~2027-02-03 (unconfirmed) — Q1 FY27 results,** which carry the ≥ +12% starts test.
