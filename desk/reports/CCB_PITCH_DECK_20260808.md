# PITCH — CCB — PROPOSED VERDICT: NO ENTRY — stay flat, park on named triggers (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

## What we believe

Coastal Financial (CCB) is a small Washington bank whose main business is running deposit and lending programs for ~30 fintech partners ("banking-as-a-service"). In Q2 2026 one large partner — roughly $500 million of underlying loans, about a quarter of the whole partner-lending book — went bad. The company took a $68.8 million hit: $22.8 million of loan losses it does not expect to recover, plus a $46.0 million write-down of money partners owe Coastal to cover credit losses ("credit enhancement" receivables, per the [Q2 press release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/30/3335871/0/en/Coastal-Financial-Corporation-Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.html)). The stock has fallen from $120 to $43.93 — down 63% — and sits 20% above its 52-week low.

We believe the damage is real and still arriving, not finished. After the write-down, partners still owe Coastal $154.3 million — equal to about a third of the company's tangible equity. Some partner programs post no cash collateral at all against what they owe (management said so on the earnings call), and in Q2 the amount newly owed ($70.7 million) grew faster than the amount collected (~$51 million). We also believe the decisive question — *which specific partners owe what, and what stands behind each balance* — cannot currently be answered: both our benches independently read the new [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001437958/000143795826000061/ck1437958-20260630.htm) and confirmed it discloses only aggregate figures. No per-partner breakdown, no reserve rollforward, not even an aggregate pledged-collateral number.

So the honest position is: we cannot underwrite this receivable, and at 1.46× tangible book value we are not being paid to hold an unanswerable question. Stay out; watch for the specific disclosures that would make it answerable.

## What the market believes

The market has already repriced this hard and is not asleep. Two analysts cut on the news — TD Cowen from a $110 target to $47 (kept its Buy), Raymond James from Strong Buy/$100 to Outperform/$50 — yet both remained positive, and the stock at $43.93 trades *below* both cut targets. Fraud investigations into the partner situation have been announced publicly. Three insiders — director Hovde (~$658k across two days), the CEO ($445k at $44.45), and the executive chair (a small $13k purchase) — bought stock in the open market in early August, about $1.1 million combined, which the market can read as management signaling the write-down was the kitchen sink.

In short: the market's view is "big known hit, taken; franchise intact; modest upside from here." That is a coherent view, and the price already reflects a lot of pain.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the filing the market may not have.** The 10-Q dropped August 7. Both benches read it in full. What we learned is negative in a subtle way: the filing is *less* informative than expected — no rollforward, no aggregate reserve figure. If the receivable deteriorates, the market will learn it late and all at once.
- **The collateral gap is understated in public commentary.** The earnings call confirmed some programs post no cash collateral. Part of that $154.3 million is backed by nothing but a fintech partner's promise to pay, and at least one large partner is already in trouble.
- **We built the collections math nobody publishes.** Reconstructing from quarterly balances: Coastal collected roughly $48–51 million per quarter from partners, but Q2's new accruals surged to $70.7 million. The gap widened exactly when a major partner weakened. That's a measurable, gradable deterioration signal for Q3.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry requires saying this plainly: much of our bear case may already be in the price, and parts of our original thesis were wrong.

- A 63% drawdown is not a market that missed the story. The name is covered by at least three sell-side firms; the bad quarter, the partner problem, and the investigations are all public.
- **Our thesis omitted the bull half of its own math.** We presented the receivable's growth without the collection leg. The red bench found ~$51 million was actually collected/settled in Q2 alone — the collection machinery demonstrably works; this isn't a frozen balance.
- The insider cluster is a real, costly signal on the other side — three separate buyers within days of the 10-Q, at prices right around today's.
- The original escalation premise — that the sizing answer was "one derivable number sitting in an unread 10-Q" — was simply refuted. The table does not exist. That was the thesis's fault, not the market's.
