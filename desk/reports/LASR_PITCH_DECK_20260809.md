# PITCH — LASR — PROPOSED VERDICT (one line)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: DO NOT BUY. Stay flat and watch. Both the prosecution and the defense independently reached "no entry" at 7/10 conviction; reopen conditions are defined below.**

## What we believe

nLIGHT (LASR, ~$56.16 at Friday's close) is a real, improving defense-laser business that just got hit by something outside its control: China has begun applying case-by-case export-licensing scrutiny to specialized optics that nLIGHT buys as an input, and the company pulled roughly $17 million of expected product shipments out of its Q3 guidance (new range $63–73M) as a result. Management describes the impact as a deferral — the orders and backlog are retained, and shipments are "expected to be delivered in future quarters" — not lost demand.

But we believe the stock is not buyable here, for two reasons that must *both* be wrong for a purchase to make sense:

1. **Duration is unknowable.** There is no published Chinese regulation, list, or statute that names optics or sets license timelines. The constraint is discretionary and case-by-case, which means its duration is a policy decision we cannot read or model. Management's own range is "months to quarters," and its mitigation (qualifying new suppliers, redesigning products) is itself a multi-quarter project.
2. **Even the "clean" earnings don't support the price.** On the *record* pre-disruption quarter — the most generous possible base — the company trades at roughly 66× enterprise value to adjusted EBITDA. Stock compensation is running about $21.8M in the first half of 2026 (~$10.9M/quarter), which is large enough to consume essentially all of the non-GAAP profit. The pre-crash price near $75–87 was resting on a growth story, not on earnings, and the 26% drop was a mechanically justified multiple correction rather than an overreaction.

## What the market believes

The market has already repriced this substantially: the stock is down about 35% from its 52-week high of $86.95, yet still up roughly 126% from its 52-week low of $24.87. The prevailing read appears to be: the defense business (the record Q2, the laser-weapons program momentum) is intact and valuable; the China-optics problem is a supply-chain timing issue of uncertain length; and some meaningful growth premium is still deserved. In February 2026 the market cleared a $201M follow-on stock offering at $44.00 per share — with the underwriters' overallotment fully exercised — so $44 is a recent, real price at which large institutional buyers were willing to take size. Today's price sits 22% above that cleared level.

## Why we might have edge

- **The defense (blue) bench identified a defined upside catalyst the bear case ignores:** if the deferral resolves, a future quarter's guidance becomes base + the returning $17M + catch-up shipments — an asymmetric, dateable positive surprise.
- **The demand story genuinely survived scrutiny.** The prosecution could not show demand destruction; the damage is on the supply side. Backlog is retained by management's own language.
- **The Q3 guide may be an artificially depressed base.** Anyone valuing the company off an annualized trough quarter (as our own prosecution initially did, at "175×") is making a base-effect error; the honest multiple is 66× off the record quarter. If the market makes the same trough-annualizing mistake into weakness, that could create a real entry.
- **We found a fact the market data vendors had wrong:** our own evidence pack's share count (49.6M, from the last audited filing) was stale; the true count is 56,408,709 shares as of 2026-05-06 after the February offering ([StockTitan on the follow-on](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/LASR/n-light-inc-announces-full-exercise-of-underwriters-option-to-pa2tshytd527.html)). Knowing the correct capitalization — and that the ~$330M cash pile is raise proceeds, not cash generation — sharpens any future valuation work.

## Why it might be priced in

- The 26% post-guidance drop already discounts a meaningful deferral. "The narrative was refuted" is not the same as "the stock is mispriced" — the prosecution showed the derating is arithmetically consistent with a growth-multiple correction.
- Defense lasers are a crowded, well-covered 2026 theme. This is not an undiscovered name; the +126%-off-the-low tape is the signature of a story that has already been found. (Caveat: neither bench actually ran our crowding/attention check — this is pattern-read, not verified.)
- Even a full, fast resolution of the China issue returns the company to a valuation (66× EBITDA, with stock compensation eating the non-GAAP profit) that requires years of flawless execution to grow into. Resolution of the *known* problem doesn't make the price cheap.
