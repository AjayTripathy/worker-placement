# PITCH — 1928.HK (Sands China) — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: DO NOT BUY. Stay flat, no orders. Both review benches independently reached the same conclusion (conviction 8/10 each). Re-examine the name after the September 1 Macau visitor-spending data and the Q3 earnings report — not after the August 14 interim results, which cannot resolve the question that matters.**

## What we believe

Sands China is a real business having a bad year, and the one thing the bulls point to is genuine: the company grew its mass-market gambling volume 15% in a flat Macau market, taking share from Galaxy and MGM and holding the #1 position. The stock is down 36% from its 52-week high.

But everything else in the original bull case failed inspection:

- **Profitability is falling, not stabilizing.** Q2 net income halved to $107 million. Reported EBITDA fell 24% to $430 million. Even after adjusting for unlucky gambling outcomes ("hold"), EBITDA fell 7.5% *despite* the 15% volume growth. The company is spending roughly 26.6 cents of every mass-gambling dollar on promotions and comps to win that share. Volume growth bought with margin is a promotional war, not operating leverage.
- **The dividend doesn't pay you enough to wait.** The quoted 5.28% yield is backward-looking. When we actually computed the after-tax return for a US taxable holder (Sands China is a Cayman-incorporated, non-treaty issuer, so the dividend is likely taxed as ordinary income at roughly 50%), the net yield is about **2.6% — below our 3.5% minimum bar** for holding something purely for income. And with net income halved, even maintaining the current payout doubles the payout ratio.
- **The August 14 dividend decision tells you almost nothing.** Las Vegas Sands owns 74.8% of this company and controls the board. Roughly 75 cents of every dividend dollar flows to a parent that is funding Singapore expansion and a New York casino license bid. The dividend is the parent routing cash to itself, not the local board signaling confidence. (One real wrinkle: because the cash flow is real, a dividend *raise* would mechanically worsen Sands China's ~$5.4 billion net debt.)
- **The deleveraging story has a structural ceiling.** Sands China committed roughly MOP 30 billion (~$4.5 billion restated) of investment in Macau through 2032 as a condition of its gaming concession. "The heavy capex is behind them" is wrong at the concession level even if quarterly margins recover.
- **The share gain invites retaliation.** Taking 15% more volume in a flat market is taken from competitors; the symmetric risk is a sector-wide promotional ratchet that makes everyone's margins worse.

The bet you would actually be making at HK$14.31 is that management's promise to "optimize" second-half margins comes true. That is a promise, not a mechanism, and it cannot be verified until the Q3 report — two events from now.

## What the market believes

The market has already marked this down and is not confused about why. Coverage is dense: Macquarie's price target is HK$15.00 — only 4.8% above the current price — and CICC has been cutting. A $14.9 billion company covered this heavily, trading this close to the consensus target after a widely-read parent-company earnings release (every Q2 number was public via Las Vegas Sands' July 22 filing), is not a neglected or misunderstood situation. The market appears to be pricing: weak Macau (July gaming revenue down 8.4% year-over-year per the government's own data), a margin-eroding promotional environment, heavy leverage, and a controlled-company discount — which is roughly what the evidence supports.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: we found no edge at current prices. The candidates we tested and their outcomes:

1. **The share-gain is real and persistent.** If reinvestment spending normalizes while the volume share sticks, earnings power is meaningfully higher than the current run-rate. This is the one surviving positive, but it is unverifiable until Q3.
2. **July's ugly revenue number has excuses.** July carried named one-off drags (World Cup distraction, typhoons) and year-to-date Macau revenue is still +4.4%. The bear reading of July as proof of a shrinking customer wallet was judged an over-reach by our own second bench — that question is genuinely open, and the September 1 spend-per-visitor data will answer it.
3. **A forced-seller or panic price could appear.** The stock already bounced 12% off its 52-week low, so today is not a fresh dislocation — but a break below the low on no new fundamental news would be worth a fast re-look.

None of these justify buying today; they define the conditions under which we would come back.

## Why it might be priced in

- Every economic figure in the upcoming August 14 report is already public through the parent's July 22 release ([LVS 8-K exhibit, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001300514/000130051426000078/lvs_ex991x06302026.htm)). There is no information asymmetry to exploit at the print.
- Sell-side coverage is dense and already bearish-to-neutral, with targets essentially at the current price.
- The government publishes the sector's revenue monthly — the macro deterioration is the most public fact in the story.
- Our own attention/positioning read (reasoning-level, connector not formally run — noted as a gap) says the name is crowded, not overlooked.
