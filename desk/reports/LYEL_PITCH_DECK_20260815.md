# PITCH — LYEL — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: DO NOT BUY TODAY at $13.95. The drug looks real and the stock looks cheap *if the company were funded* — but it isn't, and every path to the data runs through selling more stock below what the business would be worth funded. Reopen on any of four named triggers below.** (Red bench: reject, conviction 6/10. Blue bench: reject-entry-now survives but as a sizing/timing gate, not a kill, conviction 7/10.)

## What we believe

Lyell Immunopharma is now essentially a one-drug company: ronde-cel, a CAR-T cell therapy for patients with large B-cell lymphoma who have already failed at least two prior treatments. The clinical data presented so far is genuinely better than the two approved competitors on the same measures: 76% of patients had a complete response, and half were still progression-free at 18 months — versus 58% complete response and 5.8 months for Yescarta (the ZUMA-1 trial) and 53% and 6.8 months for Breyanzi (TRANSCEND). Both of our adversarial benches checked this against the primary literature and conceded it holds on its face ([ZUMA-1 five-year follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36821768/)).

The company has $228.0M in cash (per the August 6 earnings 8-K, [primary source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001806952/000162828026054330/lyel-20260806x8kexx991.htm)), enough to operate into the third quarter of 2027. Pivotal trial data is guided for mid-2027 and a regulatory filing in the second half of 2027. At today's price the market values the whole business, net of cash, at roughly $160M.

But we believe the equity today is not yet a clean claim on that drug. The cash runs out *before* approval, the only committed funding is a $150M at-the-market stock-sale program that is 99% undrawn, the share count already grew 66% in thirteen months, a newly launched 400-patient head-to-head Phase 3 trial adds a roughly $200M future cost, and 1.1 million shares (about 4.5% of all shares outstanding) held by a licensing partner became freely sellable on July 21. Until the company either lands a partner or announces a near-term data date it can raise against, each new share sold likely prices below what the drug is worth.

## What the market believes

The stock is down 69% from its 52-week high and sits about 33% above its low — but an important caveat our red bench initially missed: the company did a 1-for-20 reverse split in May 2025, so the $45 "high" is really $2.25 in old-share terms. This has traded like a distressed micro-cap, not a fallen mid-cap. The market's implicit view is: promising single-arm data, but a sub-$400M-market-cap biotech that must sell stock repeatedly before its 2027 catalysts, competing against two entrenched approved products from much larger companies, in a patient pool that is shrinking as those competitors move into earlier lines of treatment and off-the-shelf alternatives grow. Institutional signal is neutral: the founding crossover investor Foresite has held its position essentially flat (more on this below — our red bench initially got it wrong). Short interest is reportedly around 11%, though we could not verify that this session.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The clinical bar is genuinely cleared, on the primary evidence.** Both benches — including the one assigned to kill the idea — confirmed the response-rate and durability advantage against the approved benchmarks from the original trial publications, not from company slides.
2. **The market may be pricing the financing, not the asset.** Our funded-value analysis (the "what would this be worth if money were no object" table both benches were required to build) shows the funded business exceeds today's price under mid-case assumptions: roughly $17/share at $400M peak sales and roughly $29 at $800M, versus $13.95 on the tape. Unlike cases where the asset itself fails that test, here the asset clears — the financing path is the whole problem. Problems that are about *when and how*, rather than *whether*, are the kind that resolve.
3. **The head-to-head Phase 3 is an underpriced option.** Our blue bench caught our own red bench counting this trial's ~$200M cost while ignoring its prize: a superiority win directly against Yescarta/Breyanzi would open the full ~$1.5B+ CAR-T-eligible market, not just the shrinking third-line niche. The desk also has a documented history of setting fair values about 13% too low; adjusting for both, the mid-case clears our +50% hurdle.
4. **The bearish "smart money is leaving" story is false.** We proved it false ourselves — see the table below.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the symmetric case, and it is strong:

1. **The comparison is cross-trial.** 76% vs 58% comes from comparing different studies with possibly different patient populations. We never obtained the baseline patient-characteristics table, so we cannot rule out that Lyell enrolled healthier patients. This is a live, unresolved hole in the bull case.
2. **The addressable pool is shrinking.** Competitors have moved CAR-T into second-line treatment, and off-the-shelf bispecific antibodies are growing — both drain the third-line-and-later pool ronde-cel would launch into in 2028-29. J&J walked away from a partnered asset in this space citing a "shifting landscape." Neither bench could quantify the erosion; both flagged it as plausible and unpriced in our own model.
3. **Dilution is demonstrated, not hypothetical.** Share count went from 14.8M to 24.5M in thirteen months. Blue bench softened this — most of it was a PIPE done at $25.61, nearly twice today's price, so past issuance was actually done *above* value — but the forward-looking version stands: the next dollars come from an at-the-market program selling at $13.95.
4. **Fresh supply overhang.** The 1.1M zero-cost-basis ICT shares registered for resale in July are 4.5% of shares outstanding in a thin float.
5. **Concentration.** Three of six pipeline programs were terminated and one partner trial withdrawn. There is no diversification if ronde-cel disappoints; the remaining second asset carries a 55% rate of moderate-or-worse colitis.

- **Price-only band:** below **~$10.50** (tape at/below the *bear-case* funded value and the 52-week low), re-run the court rather than auto-buy — at that price the market is paying you the worst funded outcome for free, but a break there may also mean new bad facts.
