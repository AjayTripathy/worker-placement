## What we believe

Cognizant is a real, cash-generating $21B-revenue IT services company that is quietly out-growing its own end market. The street's own budget work says IT-services spend grows about 2% in 2026; Cognizant printed 5.8% and then 4.5% growth against that. That gap is not luck — it is vendor consolidation and mega-deal share gain, and it has now happened twice in a row. Management raised adjusted-EPS guidance to $5.70–5.82, kept the margin guide at 16.0–16.2%, doubled the 2026 buyback from $1B to $2B (about $8M of buying per trading day against a $29B market cap), and a director bought stock in the open market in June.

Meanwhile a big chunk of the 32% drawdown from the high was **not** about the business: the stock was removed from the Nasdaq-100 effective 22-Jun-2026, which forces index funds to sell regardless of fundamentals. The worst day of the drawdown was 13-May-2026, six weeks before the guide trim everyone blames.

Net: this is a franchise worth $52–55, worth $59 if Q3 bookings come back clean. At $58.61 it is fully valued. We own it, we do not chase it.

## What the market believes

The market believes IT services is the next thing AI eats. Nifty IT is down 24% year-to-date, Infosys cut its full-year guide on 23-Jul-2026, Morgan Stanley cut Accenture to Equal-weight with a price target move of $240→$177 on a CIO survey showing AI crowding out discretionary project spend, and Accenture's own management blamed Middle East conflict and weak late-quarter discretionary budgets for declining bookings. Cognizant then printed Q2 bookings down 6% year-over-year with book-to-bill sliding from ~1.4x to ~1.3x and trailing-twelve-month bookings ticking down from $29.6B to $29.1B.

So the market's view is: a 10x-earnings, 4%-growth body shop whose headcount is already shrinking (356,700, down 900 sequentially) while attrition rises (12.3%→13.0%) is a value trap, not a value stock.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The bookings scare is a comp artifact and we did the arithmetic.** The year-ago quarter was +18% and contained two separate deals above $1B of total contract value. This year's decline was $0.5B — smaller than one of those deals — and the count of large deals actually rose from 6 to 7. A decline smaller than a single base-quarter mega-deal is a calendar accident, not a demand break.
2. **We separated flow from fundamentals.** The index deletion and the Accenture read-across gap-down (CTSH −5.6% premarket on 18-Jun) explain part of the de-rate that the bear case charges to the business.
3. **We know exactly which bear case we have to beat.** It is not bookings. It is AI substitution of the labor-arbitrage model — headcount down while revenue grows can be efficiency (good) or price deflation (fatal). Anyone re-underwriting has to argue that one, and most of the sell-side is still arguing the bookings line.
4. **Sizing discipline as edge:** the fair-value work says $52–55 and the tape is $58.61. Most of the "edge" here is the willingness to not buy 11% above our own number.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it. At $58.61 the stock is +58% off its $37.08 low. On the company's own guided adjusted EPS the multiple is 10.2x, not the 9.3x the bull note used; on 2025 GAAP EPS of $4.56 (up 1%) it is 12.9x. Free-cash-flow yield is 7.65% on market cap, not the 8.1% you get by using enterprise value — and even that enterprise value is flattered by net cash that is partly encumbered by the India dividend-distribution-tax demand of roughly ₹2,800cr ($340–385M). Three flattering denominators were stacked at once in the original write-up. That was our error, not the market's.

The buyback is known. The index deletion is done. The share-gain story is visible in two printed quarters, so it is in the numbers everyone can see. And the AI-substitution fear is precisely the kind of fear that does not resolve on a single print — it re-prices the multiple slowly, which means "cheap" can stay cheap for years.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — AI substitution is real and this is a value trap (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).**
Revenue grows 4–5% while price-per-unit-of-work falls and headcount shrinks; margins hold for a year on mix, then break. We cannot build a tripwire for this because Cognizant does not disclose the numbers that would show it: no realized-rate-per-employee series, no discretionary-versus-managed-services split, no sequential bookings level. We would learn about it from the P&L two to six quarters after it started.
**Unknown-unknown class: disclosure-basis blindness** — the failure lives in a metric the issuer has never printed, so the absence of a bad signal carries no information. This is the same mechanism that made our original stop unusable (a stop the issuer cannot print is not a stop) and it is the single most important sentence in this deck.

**Failure narrative 2 — the bookings decline was not a comp artifact after all.**
Q3 (expected 28-Oct) prints bookings negative again on a clean, non-mega-deal comp, book-to-bill goes below 1.2x, and the constant-currency guide low end drops under 4%. Then the $0.5B TTM dip was the first data point of a trend and we mistook a trend for lumpiness. This one *is* monitorable — it is exactly the armed Q3 tripwire pack — but monitorable does not mean survivable: the stock would likely be back near $46 before we could act on the transcript.

**Failure narrative 3 — the peer channel keeps firing.**
Accenture Q4 FY26 (expected late September, date unverified) repeats the discretionary/geopolitics attribution, or another Indian-heritage peer cuts. CTSH has already gapped 5.6% on an ACN read-across once this year. We take a multi-point drawdown on somebody else's print, with no company-specific news to lean on.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
Our own size is irrelevant to the tape: even at a $1M book, 1.25% is roughly 210 shares against a mega-cap that trades millions of shares a day — call it four thousandths of one percent of average daily volume. There is no impact risk and no exit risk (the legacy lot is 90 shares, $5.3k, +41%). The structural risk runs the other way — flows are acting on *us*:
- **Predicted flow is supportive:** the $2B authorized buyback is a standing bid of roughly $8M/day, and the index-deletion selling from 22-Jun is largely behind us. That support can mask deterioration for two or three quarters, which is exactly how a value trap holds a price while the business erodes.
- **Doctrine interaction, and it is a bad one:** our "no adds before 2026-09-21" gate expires *directly into* Accenture's Q4 FY26 print. A rule written to make us patient instead schedules our buying immediately ahead of the single most reliable source of CTSH gap-downs this year. That is the gate inverting on itself. It is fixed below by moving the add gate behind the ACN print, not the calendar date.
- **Second doctrine conflict:** the armed price alert says HOLD at $37 and only ADD on three simultaneous conditions, while both benches set the re-entry gate at ≤$46. Those two instructions disagree about what to do between $37 and $46. Resolved below in favor of the $46 gate with the three conditions attached.
