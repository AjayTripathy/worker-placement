### Read this first — an owed diligence item is now closed, and the record was wrong
Our record carried the 2023 €1.1bn sustainability-linked facility as having a **5.0x net-leverage
covenant, tested semi-annually**, at *medium confidence, from press summaries rather than the
Universal Registration Document*. The red team used that number to dismiss the covenant worry.
Read against the URD itself (Note 17.6.1), **the covenant is 4.50x, not 5.0x — and it is a
springing covenant that activates only if S&P and Moody's both downgrade below BBB− / Baa3.
S&P already downgraded Verallia to BB+ on 13 March 2026.** One leg of a two-leg trigger is
already satisfied, and Moody's has Baa3 on negative outlook. Full treatment in section 2.

1 · The four-idea frame

What the market believes

Verallia is a European glass-container maker in a structurally shrinking end market, controlled 77%

by a Brazilian family holding company that has already tried to buy the rest and been refused. The

minority is along for the ride with no exit. Volumes are flat at best; still-wine consumption — a

core end use — declines a few percent a year and will keep declining. Margins peaked in the 2022–23

energy-and-pricing window at 24–25% and are never going back; the current ~21% is the new reality,

not a trough. Net income just fell 60%. Leverage sits at 2.6x with €1.78bn of net debt on a company

whose EBITDA is falling as fast as its debt. And the free float is 12% of a €2.3bn company, so

nobody who matters can own it. A holder of this view says: a cheap multiple on a melting ice cube is

not a bargain, it is arithmetic.

That is a real argument and the volume half of it is correct.

What we believe

The re-rating mechanism here is margin-independent, which is the whole point. We do not need

margins to recover to 24%; we need them to stop falling — and the H1 print says they have.

  • The deleveraging transfer is the engine. Net debt fell from €1,947.5m to €1,778.9m year over

year, roughly €169m, against 127.6m shares — about **€1.32 a share, or 7.2% of the €18.21 price,

transferred from lenders to equity holders in twelve months**, before any multiple change. Add a

€1.00 dividend (5.5% yield) and the cash return is real whether or not the market ever

re-rates the stock.

  • Margins have found a floor and it is higher than the bear's. H1 adjusted EBITDA margin was

20.7%, up 33 basis points, with Q2 at 21.4%. The hedge-roll headwind of −€17m was absorbed

by €26m of purchasing savings plus footprint actions (the Essen closure alone contributed +€8m in

Q2). The 19% "new normal" the bear needs is not evidenced.

  • Cash generation is running ahead of plan. H1 free cash flow €102.0m against €66.2m, +54%;

€121m excluding restructuring cash-outs. Full-year guidance was confirmed at ~€700m

adjusted EBITDA and ~€220m free cash flow ex-restructuring.

  • The minority is not trapped. The controller committed to no squeeze-out and to maintaining

the listing for three years (to ~mid-2028); the float refused €30 a share and the AMF's independent

expert backed them. The controller's own €30 mark is a soft anchor under a €18.21 stock.

The trade is carry plus deleveraging plus a possible re-rate, in that order of confidence.

Why we might have edge

Modest, and structural rather than informational. Three honest components:

1. A 12.26% float on a €2.3bn company mechanically excludes most institutional buyers, which

suppresses the multiple for reasons unrelated to the business. That is a real, persistent

discount we can accept payment for. It is also why we can never size this large.

2. The optics of the H1 print diverge from its substance in a checkable way. Net profit fell

60% to €26.7m — a headline that reads like deterioration — because of €43m of *disclosed,

non-recurring* restructuring. Ex-item earnings were €70m / €0.59 a share. The company disclosed

both plainly, so this is not a masking finding; it is a latency finding. The headline moves

the price on the day; the cash flow statement moves it over quarters.

3. There is a dated re-rate catalyst that most holders are not positioned for: the **Capital

Markets Day on 18 November 2026**, the first since the ownership change settled.

Countervailing honesty: this is a covered European mid-cap, not a neglected micro-cap. Our edge is

patience and a written kill list, not information.

Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

The strongest bear case, sharpened by this sweep's findings:

  • The deleveraging is not actually improving the leverage ratio. This is the finding that most

damages our own framing. Net debt fell 8.7% year over year — but **LTM adjusted EBITDA fell 9.1%,

from €762.0m to €693.0m. Leverage was 2.6x at H1 2026 and 2.6x at H1 2025 — flat**. The "down

from 2.7x" in our record is true only against December 2025 and March 2026. If EBITDA keeps

falling in step with debt, the deleveraging transfer to equity is an accounting illusion: enterprise

value falls with EBITDA and the equity holder captures nothing. **The thesis requires EBITDA to

stabilise, and that is a condition our record did not state.**

  • The H1 margin improvement is entirely a Q1 phenomenon that does not repeat. The **Q2 margin

fell year over year — 21.4% against 22.5%** — even as the H1 figure rose 33bp. The improvement

came from the 2022 energy-hedge roll-off landing in Q1. There is no second roll-off.

  • The full-year guide implies a nearly flat second half. LTM EBITDA of €693.0m at H1 implies H2

2025 was €341.4m; ~€700m full-year against H1's €351.6m implies H2 2026 ≈ €348.4m, only +2.0%.

Management is guiding to almost nothing. (Read the other way, with Essen savings and easing German

volumes already in hand, the guide looks beatable — but that is our read, not theirs.)

  • The covenant picture is worse than our record said. 4.50x rather than 5.0x, with the S&P leg

of the springing trigger already pulled. Headroom remains large — EBITDA would have to fall 43%

from €693m to €395m to breach — so this is a tail risk, not a live constraint. But we recorded it

as resolved on a number that was wrong.

  • The controller keeps creeping. BWSA moved from 77.17% to 77.22% of capital and 71.78% to

71.94% of votes over the half, via the scrip dividend. The red team correctly re-rated the creep as

slow (~+0.4pp a year, ~29 years to the 90% squeeze bar), but the direction is one-way and the

mechanism is one we can opt out of — which is why the ruled constraint is **take the cash dividend,

not the scrip**.

Mechanism falsifier

Dated and checkable: the Q3 2026 results, 27 October 2026 (press release after market close;

quiet period begins 6 October). The thesis is falsified if volumes turn negative year over year

or the adjusted EBITDA margin fails to hold above 19.9%.

The forward instrument is the Eurostat monthly production index for hollow glass (NACE C23.13),

footprint-weighted across France, Italy, Spain, Germany and the EU27, running on roughly a two-month

lag. First read: weighted +2.2%, but **France — the largest single weight — was negative three

months running (−0.6%, −9.0%, −6.1%)**. France is the named swing line. This is a market index, so it

carries basis risk against Verallia's own volumes: read it for direction, never for level.

Edge type

RISK_PREMIUM. Fairly-paid carry with a deleveraging kicker, a dated re-rate catalyst, and a

verified soft floor under the price. Not EDGE: the disclosure is clean and the market is not

misreading the data, only discounting it for float and end-market decline.
