Say the awkward part first. This is a shrinking company burning toward a heavy capex cycle, and its safety net is made of book value that erodes while you hold it — the tangible floor lost sixteen cents in the past year and will keep losing while losses run. We are not buying a compounder. We are buying an asset floor with sovereign shareholders standing under it, at a price a few percent below that floor, sized so that being wrong costs half a percent of the book.

Pane 1 · What we believe

A real floor, a real crossover, and a state that can't let it fail

Three legs. First, the floor is genuine. The bear case's headline kill — "tangible book is negative" — was an arithmetic error our court caught: it subtracted gross goodwill and intangibles (€3.24B) when the balance sheet carries them net of €2.15B in write-downs already taken. The honest bridge: €3.6B of equity less ~€1.05B net intangibles is ~€2.5B of tangible book, backed by €4.1B of satellites in orbit and €1.6B of cash — about €2.15 per share today.

Second, the business is crossing over. LEO (OneWeb) revenue grew +69.5% this year to €297M — a quarter of all revenue — and is guided +30% next year, now fully offsetting the GEO video decline: the company just guided its first growing year. Third, the shareholder register is the tail-protection: the French state (29.7%), UK government (10.9%) and allied sovereign holders around 45% — this is Europe's chosen space-connectivity vehicle, lead of the IRIS² constellation, with the selection outcome imminent. Wipeout scenarios require Paris and London to abandon their own infrastructure programme.

Pane 2 · What the market believes

A dilution machine feeding a cash furnace

The bear case is about flows, not assets: ~€4B of forward capex (OneWeb generation-2 plus IRIS²) against a business that lost €457M this year; a share count that went 475M → 1.18B in one recapitalisation at €1.35; a GEO video business in structural decline; and a fresh guide that says next year is merely slightly growing at a stable margin. On this view the tangible floor is irrelevant — you never get to liquidation; you get served successive dilutions instead, and the sovereign backstop protects the enterprise, not your share of it. The 2025 raise proved exactly that mechanism. This belief is coherent, recently validated, and is why the stock trades below tangible book.

Pane 3 · Why we might have edge

Almost nobody re-did the arithmetic — including the bear bench we hired

Our own red bench — instructed to kill this — got tangible book negative by double-counting write-downs already taken. If a dedicated adversarial process run on primary filings makes that error, the market's shorthand ("melting satellite operator, book value fiction") is likely resting on the same one. The floor bridge takes four numbers from one balance sheet. That, plus a register nobody prices (45% sovereign hands that cannot politically permit failure of their own constellation lead), is the mispricing case.

Two unpriced near-term items ride along: $504M of US C-band clearing incentive payments now expected (a third of the market cap in cash, rarely in models), and the IRIS² "First Rendez-Vous" outcome, which the company states will confirm its LEO-segment lead — binary-ish, imminent, and free at this price if the floor holds.

Pane 4 · Why it might be priced in

The floor moves — and fair is not cheap

Two honest concessions from our own court. The blue bench corrected the screen's "4× EV/EBITDA" to a real ~6.5× — fair for a declining-then-recovering satellite operator, not a bargain. And the floor is not a fixed line: tangible book was €2.31 when the court sat in July and is ~€2.15 now — the losses eat it at roughly €0.15–0.20 a year. Buying at €2.03 is buying a ~6% discount to a floor walking toward the price at that pace. The trade therefore has a clock: the crossover (LEO growth, C-band cash, IRIS² money) has to arrive before the floor does. That is why this is a 0.5–1% special situation with kill switches, not a conviction position.

"Tangible book" is one number hiding a stack. But zeroing all intangibles over-punishes: OneWeb’s ITU spectrum-priority rights — the reason sovereigns recapitalised at all — sit inside the excluded intangibles and are genuinely marketable. The €1.35 revealed-preference line is where those two errors demonstrably netted out for real buyers. This year’s €71.6M of losses on three named GEO satellites (AT1/AT2/AM6) supports mark-skepticism at the margin.

Read on the ladder: the €2.05 starter buys below the accounting floor; the €1.85 rung sits a third of the way down to the sovereign line; and a fill much below €1.35 would mean the state’s own price failed — which is a different thesis and the kill switches’ job.
