# PITCH — PONY — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no order, no starter: both benches land on reject-the-edge (not short), and the reject survives correcting both benches' own mistakes.

*Deck built 2026-08-21 from the completed adversarial court (red bench + blue bench) and the machine evidence pack. No new data was pulled while writing it; every number below is attributed to the bench or filing that produced it, including the ones our own benches got wrong.*

---

## What we believe

Pony.ai runs a robotaxi business that is working better than a chart down 69% from its high suggests, and is still not investable at $7.78.

The operating record, from the company's own Q2 release ([6-K EX-99.1, filed 2026-08-18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001969302/000110465926098113/tm2623382d1_ex99-1.htm)):

- Total revenue $36.2M, up 68.8% from a year ago. Robotaxi revenue $12.1M, up 691%.
- The fleet went from 1,159 cars at the end of 2025 to 1,975 at the end of June — a ~70% expansion in six months — and revenue *per car* did not fall while that happened. On a matched average-fleet basis it rose about 7% quarter over quarter (~$6,580 → ~$7,058 per car per quarter, i.e. roughly $28,000 per car per year). Holding per-unit revenue flat-to-up while adding hundreds of brand-new, low-utilization cars is a creditable result, not a red flag.
- Operating expenses grew 9.6% while revenue grew 68.8%. Cash used in operations fell 41% quarter over quarter, from $74.2M to $44.0M.
- $1.39B of cash and investments against $93.0M of total liabilities and no borrowings. Share count moved +0.002% in the quarter; financing activities in the first half were *negative* $376K. There is no dilution here and no financing cliff.

And yet we believe there is no trade, for one arithmetic reason and one disclosure reason.

**The arithmetic reason (this is the whole case).** At $7.78 the market cap is $3.37B. Subtract the $1.39B of cash and investments and the *operating business* is being valued at roughly $1.98B. Management's own target is more than 3,500 cars by the end of 2026. That is about **$567,000 of business value per car** — against roughly **$28,000 per car per year of gross revenue**. Put differently: hand the company a fully funded, fully deployed 3,500-car fleet tomorrow, free of charge, and at any revenue multiple we would actually underwrite (10–15× revenue per car) the business is worth *less than today's price*. Every 2026 milestone we could diligence is already inside the stock. What is being priced is the fleet of roughly 2029 and its unit economics — a number we cannot check.

**The disclosure reason.** The single line that would convert this from a story into arithmetic — **how many dollars of actual passenger fares the company collected** — has never been printed. Three quarters running (Q4'25, Q1'26, Q2'26) the company published a percentage ("+500%", "+456.5%", "+849.3%") and never a level, in any venue. Meanwhile revenue from partner-funded cars (OnTime Mobility, Verne, Uber — where a partner buys the vehicle) is booked *inside* the same "Robotaxi services revenue" line, with no split. We can therefore see that fare revenue is growing faster than the segment, but we cannot see how big it is. A name whose core revenue line is withheld cannot be sized at any size.

We are also not short. The balance sheet is strong, losses are narrowing both year over year and sequentially, the cost line is genuinely leveraged, and the stock is a momentum/retail name — all four are reasons a short is wrong here even if the valuation is.

## What the market believes

There are two markets in this stock and they disagree with each other.

**The sell side** — 18 analysts, consensus Strong Buy, average price target **$20.37** against a $7.78 tape — believes the terminal case: that a fleet several years out reaches tens of thousands of cars at mature per-car margins. That view is not about 2026 at all. It is a bet on the same ~2029 fleet our own math says is in the price; the analysts simply put a much higher value on it than we can defend.

**The tape** does not believe them. The stock is down 68.8% from its 52-week high of $24.92 and sits at $7.78, 22.2% above its 52-week low of $6.365. It has already bounced roughly a fifth off the low — this is not a fresh dislocation.

The most informative single data point about market belief: the 2,000-vehicle Uber Europe announcement landed **August 14**, and the Q2 earnings release landed **August 18**. The market had four full sessions to price the Europe deal, then took the beat down about 3%. Both the deal and the beat were absorbed as non-events.

The consensus revenue number for 2026 is $137.8M, but that figure is stale — it predates the Q2 print, and the first half alone was already $70.5M.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: less than the brief that opened this court claimed.

- **The candidate edge that survives.** Our per-car valuation arithmetic (the $567k-per-car figure) is a genuine disagreement with the $20.37 consensus target, and it points *down*. We can defend it from filed numbers; the analysts' target requires assumptions about 2029 economics that nobody has published. That is a real divergence — but it is a divergence we cannot express. We would have to be short to monetize it, and we won't be short a momentum name with a fortress balance sheet and improving operations.
- **The candidate edge that evaporated.** Our reconstruction of full-year 2026 revenue is ~$155–160M against consensus $137.8M. That looks like a 13–16% beat. It isn't edge: consensus is simply stale (pre-Q2), and our number is nothing more than management's own published guidance (robotaxi revenue at least 3.5× the 2025 figure of $16.6M). We would be "beating" an unrefreshed print by agreeing with the company. That is not a view, it is a data-refresh.
- **The methodological edge we did earn.** Both benches independently caught that the per-car revenue metric — the one number the entire bull brief rested on — was constructed from mismatched dates. That check is now a reusable detector (see *What we checked ourselves*, rows 1–2). It found an error in the brief *and* an error in our own red bench. That is worth more to the desk than this ticker is.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetrically, here is the case that our reject is the wrong call:

- **The stock is already down 69%.** A great deal of disappointment is in the price. Our entire reject rests on a valuation multiple applied to a business whose revenue is compounding at 69% year over year and whose robotaxi line is compounding at 691%. Applying a 10–15× revenue-per-car multiple to a company at this stage of an S-curve may simply be the wrong instrument.
- **Our per-car math undercounts the asset-light path.** In the joint deployment model the *partner* funds the vehicle. If that model scales, the capital intensity per car falls and the "value per car" framing understates the return on Pony's own capital. (Counterweight, and the reason we didn't move on it: Q2 capital spending of $32.2M against the quarter's net vehicle additions works out to roughly $60,900 per car — Pony is still buying most of them itself.)
- **The one fact that would flip us is unknown, not absent.** If Q2 fare revenue turns out to be, say, $7M+ of the $12.1M segment, then fare revenue per car is running around $4,100 per quarter, the ride business is genuinely real and self-standing, and the terminal case becomes gradeable. We are rejecting on the *absence* of that number, which is a decision about disclosure, not about the business.
- **Management's own margin disclosure cuts our way less than we assumed.** The Q2 release attributes the gross-margin improvement to "a higher contribution from Robotaxi services, including revenues generated under the joint deployment model, which generated relatively higher margins during the quarter." Blended margin rose 16.1% → 17.5% while robotaxi went from ~7% to ~33% of revenue. That combination is arithmetically impossible if robotaxi margin were near zero; it implies roughly 13–21%. It's management's own characterization and unaudited, so we discount it — but the claim that this business runs at no margin is not supported.
- **Crowding cuts both ways.** 18 analysts at Strong Buy means we are unlikely to be seeing something they aren't. It also means positioning is one-sided and a fare-dollar disclosure would be violently repriced.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these are load-bearing:

1. **Fare revenue in dollars — the central unknown.** Withheld for three straight quarters while percentages are published. Everything about the terminal case depends on it. It may never be disclosed.
2. **The split between passenger fares and partner-funded revenue** inside the robotaxi line, and customer concentration among the named partners. Not disclosed; the FY2025 20-F major-customer note has not been pulled.
3. **Robotaxi segment gross margin.** The 13–21% bound is inferred from management's own narrative about mix, not from a disclosed segment margin. Management-scoped statements are not audited and we discount them accordingly.
4. **The 1,159 year-end-2025 fleet count** — the number blue used to overturn red's "monetization is flat" finding. A second source agrees, but the SEC citation offered doesn't fit the date. **Re-pull before reusing the +7.3% figure.**
5. **Whether Pony's cash can fund European expansion.** No currency or jurisdiction split was ever obtained. Red asserted the cash is trapped onshore; that was never checked, and the Hong Kong listing suggests otherwise.
6. **Whether the revenue growth deceleration is seasonal.** Neither bench could run the seasonality check. The "the comps are collapsing" argument is currently unsupported by the desk's own tooling.
7. **No live market quote.** The broker connection was permission-denied for both benches. All prices in this deck are the evidence pack's $7.78 plus a $7.75 close on 8/20 — good enough for bands, not for execution.
8. **The Uber Europe economics.** "Contracted" is the company's word for 2,000 vehicles. Who funds the vehicles, in which cities, on what revenue split, and by when — none of it is public. The company's own language elsewhere concedes that "vehicle funding and ownership can sit with different partners depending on the market."
9. **The 2026-11-17 print date**, as noted — vendor-derived, not company-confirmed.
10. **No independent read on ride volume exists.** Our app-based detectors are non-diagnostic for a China-native, mini-program-based hailing business, and we have no substitute source.
