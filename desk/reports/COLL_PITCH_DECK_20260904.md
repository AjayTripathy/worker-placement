## What we believe

Collegium is a specialty pharma company that sells four pain and ADHD products. It throws off a lot of cash and carries a lot of debt. At $23.62 it is priced at roughly 3.9x this year's guided cash earnings — one of the cheapest multiples on the exchange — and it is sitting on a fresh 52-week low, down 53.5% from its high.

The screen that surfaced it was wrong about why. We believed "about 75% of revenue sits behind patents running to 2030–2037," and that a court trial on 12 April 2027 was a coin-flip that would decide whether the BELBUCA franchise (29% of sales) kept protection until 2032 or lost it in 2027. Both statements are false, and our own benches proved it from primary documents. The real numbers: 52% of revenue is protected past 2030, the JORNAY patent estate ends 23 March 2032 (not 2037), and BELBUCA's generic entry date was **fixed by private contract in 2018 at 23 January 2027** — five months from now, and completely independent of the April trial.

What we believe *after* that correction is narrower and less exciting: the contracted January date may be permissive rather than operative. Teva has a license to sell a generic BELBUCA on that date — but a license is not permission to manufacture. No generic buprenorphine buccal film appears anywhere on the FDA's first-generic approvals list, and the one other visible applicant (IntelGenx / Chemo Research) was rejected by the FDA on manufacturing quality grounds in April 2024. If Teva does not have a final approved application in hand, the date arrives and nothing happens. The market appears to be pricing the date as certain. That gap — certainty versus a contracted-but-unexercisable right — is the only live edge candidate left in this name.

Second, smaller belief: the reason the stock is at a low is *not* what our red bench said it was. Red argued the low is artificially propped up by the company's own $50M buyback, and told us to wait for a "clean" low after that program settles. One arithmetic step kills that: the whole program is about 1.95M shares spread over 55+ trading sessions against average daily volume of ~870k shares — 3 to 6% of daily volume. That is not a floor. Meanwhile short sellers added 1.44M shares over the same window. The low is real.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a melting ice cube with too much debt, and it is paid to believe it. **21.5% of the free float is sold short** (6.52M shares against a 30.35M float, 10.7 days to cover, up 28% month over month). That is not a fringe view; that is the consensus, held with money.

Specifically the market believes:
- BELBUCA goes generic on 23 January 2027 as contracted, and 29% of revenue starts eroding.
- NUCYNTA is already gone — Humanwell's generic tablet approved 27 January 2026, Hikma's authorized generic launched 11 March 2026. That is 17.6% of revenue, done, and it is why guidance was cut on 6 August from $865–895M to $825–855M and the stock fell 18.6% that session.
- 3.9x is not cheap for a company where the shareholders own only 41% of the enterprise and lenders own the rest.
- Management paid $706.3M for AZSTARYS in May 2026 — more than the *entire* company is worth today at $730M.

Sell-side coverage models Q3 revenue at about $205.07M against Q2's $199.9M actual, and — notably — is *more* optimistic on BELBUCA than our original thesis was, pointing out that Teva lacks tentative approval and gave up its first-filer exclusivity.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thinner than the screen suggested, and one of the three legs is our own homework rather than the market's.

1. **The operativeness question.** Everyone can read the 2018 settlement date. Fewer people have checked whether the licensee can actually ship. The FDA first-generic list shows no buprenorphine buccal film; the only other applicant was rejected in 2024. If the definitive check (Drugs@FDA application records) also shows no final approval, then a 29%-of-revenue cliff that the market treats as a scheduled event is instead a contingent one — and 21.5% of the float is short into it.

2. **The arithmetic nobody normalizes.** Two mirror-image stories are told about buybacks at 52-week lows — "the bid is holding it up" and "the float is shrinking into a squeeze." Both are unfalsifiable until you divide program shares by (average volume × sessions). We did: 3–6%. That single step dissolved our own red bench's one original objection. 3. **The leverage is symmetric and the market may be reading only one side.** Shareholders own 41% of the enterprise, so every 1% change in cash earnings is 2.4% of the equity. That cuts down hard — but it also means that if the January date passes without a launch, the same lever runs up. This is a weak leg; leverage is visible to everyone.

Against those: our original patent-map edge was **not** an edge, it was an error. We should say that plainly rather than quietly re-basing.

*Unknown-unknown class: privately-contracted and privately-served exclusivity terms that no public registry mirrors.* This is the exact class that already burned us once in this name — the BELBUCA January-2027 date was invisible in the Orange Book (which shows 2032) because it lived in a 2018 settlement press release. The Orange Book is a *ceiling* on exclusivity, never the operative date.

**Failure narrative 3 — the reflexive and structural leg: we lose to our own doctrine and to the crowd, not to the company.** Three interacting pieces:

- *Our size is not the problem.* A 0.4% starter is roughly 500–600 shares against 870k average daily volume — under 0.1% of a day's trading, far inside our 1%-of-volume publishing rule. We cannot move this tape and we will not have trouble exiting.
- *Our waiting rule is the problem.* Red's prescription was to wait for a "clean" low after the buyback settles. Blue showed the buyback cannot produce the flow reversal red is waiting for. If we condition entry on an event that has no mechanism, we will end up buying only after the shorts cover — which is precisely the worst fill. Waiting for a flow reversal is only sound when there is flow big enough to reverse.
- *The crowd is the real structural risk.* Our own flow rule says never bid into flow you yourself predict. Here the predicted flow is the *issuer's* — $100M of a $150M authorization still unspent, retiring 6.4% of a float that is 21.5% short. That is a squeeze setup, and buying a squeeze setup is not a value entry. If the January date turns out to be operative, the same crowding runs the other way and the exit is 10.7 days deep.
- *Doctrine collision:* our crowded-market rule says a crowded position is a sizing instrument, never an automatic rejection; our clean-court rule says going flat requires a named kill that survives audit. Zero kills survived as novel. Those two rules together mechanically produce a starter here — which means the position may exist because of how our rules compose, not because of what we found. That is a real failure mode and it is why the size is half of half.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that the 21.5% short base was simply right and earlier than us: BELBUCA's generic arrived on the contracted date, the authorized-generic defense we never saw a filing for did not materially exist, and 2.4x equity leverage on a shrinking earnings base turned an ordinary 20% earnings miss into a 45% loss.

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS

1. **The thesis mixes a six-month growth rate against a quarterly decline rate.** "Xtampza −14.5% / Jornay +38.9%" pairs XTAMPZA's **Q2** decline (−14.4%) with JORNAY's **six-month** growth (+38.9%). Matched: Q2 is −14.4% vs +41.2%; six-month is −4.5% vs +38.9%. Any bench arguing about deceleration must pick one basis. ([R9.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1267565/000162828026053851/R9.htm))
2. **"Nucynta is 25.1% of revenue" is not the Q2 mix.** On Q2 2026 filed revenue the franchise (IR + ER + both AGs) is 35,215/199,878 = **17.6%**; BELBUCA is **28.9%**, JORNAY 23.0%, XTAMPZA 22.5%, AZSTARYS 6.5% on a ~7-week stub. A trailing-twelve-month basis will give a different, higher Nucynta share. State the basis.
3. **AZSTARYS's \$12.9M is ~7 weeks (May 12 – June 30), not a quarter.** Annualising it is wrong in both directions; the company's own FY guide is \$65–75M.
4. **AG revenue is not comparable to branded revenue.** The \$5.1M of AG revenue is a blend of product-material sale plus an *estimated* profit share, on a product netting ~10–15% (IR) / ~20–25% (ER) of branded net price. Unit erosion and revenue erosion are therefore not the same number, and the "estimated" profit share is a management accrual subject to true-up.
5. **The XTAMPZA −14% contains a ~\$2.4M prior-year rebate-timing benefit** — roughly 4.6 points of the 14.4-point decline is comparison artifact, not current-period demand. Neither the filing nor the call decomposes the remainder into volume vs net price.
6. **Gross-to-net regimes differ by product and are not disclosed per product** (only JORNAY PM's "mid-60%" was given). Comparing net-revenue trends across BELBUCA, XTAMPZA and JORNAY is comparing three different GTN regimes, not three demand curves. The six-month GTN rollforward is further distorted by \$45.9M of rebate liability *assumed in the acquisition* and by prior-period adjustments of −\$3.7M (rebates) and −\$6.4M (returns). ([R9.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1267565/000162828026053851/R9.htm))
7. **Orange Book expiry ≠ loss of exclusivity.** BELBUCA's OB date is 2032-12-21; the operative date is the **2027-01-23** contractual Teva entry. XTAMPZA's OB date is 2036-09-02; the operative date is **2033-09-02**. Both operative dates are in settlement agreements, not the Orange Book. Any patent-cliff model built off Orange Book dates alone will be wrong on the two largest pain products.
8. **The '539 C_max limitation and the label PK are different estimands.** Claim 1 recites a **steady-state** C_max window of 0.156–0.364 ng/mL. The label reports **single-dose** C_max (0.17 ng/mL at 75 mcg, 0.47 at 300 mcg, 1.43 at 1200 mcg) and states only that steady state is reached before the 6th dose with dose-proportional C_max — it gives no per-strength steady-state C_max table. Whether the claimed window covers the whole marketed 75–900 mcg q12h ladder therefore **cannot be resolved from the label**, and a bench should not assert it either way. (Claim 9, the second independent claim, was not retrieved.)
9. **Drug-liking scores are not cross-comparable between products.** XTAMPZA's intranasal E_max of 61.81 was measured against **crushed IR oxycodone** (82.72) in a 39-subject study with that study's own subject pool and dose equivalence. It is not comparable to OxyContin's or RoxyBond's published liking scores from different studies with different comparators, doses and populations.
10. **JORNAY PM dose is not exposure.** At an identical 54 mg dose, C_max is 11.6 (children), 7.2 (adolescents), **6.0 ng/mL (adults)**. Any argument about adult uptake that reasons from mg-dose or from pediatric trial results without the ~2x exposure gap is mismatched.
11. **TRx growth ≠ revenue growth.** JORNAY PM TRx +13.1% against revenue +41.2% in the same quarter. The gap is price/mix/GTN/channel and possibly inventory, not demand — and the same wedge, inverted, may be operating on XTAMPZA.
12. **Simtriyo's Phase 3 endpoints (AISRS in adults, ADHD-RS-5 in children) are not the endpoints in JORNAY PM's trials (SKAMP analog-classroom, ADHD-RS-IV, PREMB-R AM).** Cross-trial effect-size comparison between these products is not supportable; JORNAY's differentiating endpoints are explicitly *early-morning* measures with no counterpart in the Simtriyo program.
