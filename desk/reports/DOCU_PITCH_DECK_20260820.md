# PITCH — DOCU — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — DECLINE / RP_UNCERTAIN: fairly priced at ~20x true owner earnings (probability-weighted fair value $60.09, +4.7% from the $57.39 court price), not impaired, but sitting on a long-term value the company itself just told us is less defensible than it claimed a year ago.**

---

## What we believe

Docusign is a healthy, cash-generative, debt-free business that is worth roughly what it costs. It is not a broken company and it is not a fraud. It is also not cheap enough to own.

Three things we believe, in plain terms:

1. **The growth is real but stuck.** Revenue has grown 7–9% a year for three straight years. The new product line ("IAM" — Intelligent Agreement Management, their AI-flavored contract platform) has gone from 2.3% of recurring revenue to 12.6% over roughly eight quarters, and total company growth has not budged. The other 87% of the business is flat. Even if IAM grows 50% next year and 40% the year after, total growth only gets to about 9.6% by 2030. The arithmetic caps the upside at roughly one extra percentage point over four years.

2. **The cash flow is real but much smaller than the headline.** Docusign advertises $1.12 billion of trailing free cash flow. Once you charge the real cost of paying employees in stock — both the $246.5M of cash the company spends withholding taxes on employee shares (which is recorded as a financing item and therefore never touches the free-cash-flow line) and the shares that actually get issued — the honest owner's earnings figure is about **$491 million**. Against an enterprise value of $9.93 billion that is **20.2x earnings, a 4.94% yield**. That is a fair price for an 8% grower. It is not a bargain.

3. **The buyback is genuine and the balance sheet is clean.** They bought back $318.4M in one quarter, share count is actually falling (202.5M → 190.9M over about fifteen months), and the board authorized another $2.0B in March 2026 leaving $2.4B available — about 22% of the market cap. No debt, no convertibles, no preferred. This is the strongest fact on the bull side and it is verified.

Net: **we do not buy it here.** Expected return is roughly 9–10% a year if nothing goes wrong — an equity-market return for a single-name risk, with a fat left tail we cannot size.

## What the market believes

At $57.39 the stock priced in **4.82% perpetual growth** in owner earnings (reverse-discounted at 10%). That is a market saying: *this is a durable cash machine that grows slightly faster than the economy forever, and neither the AI story nor the AI threat changes much.*

The market is not pricing a collapse — it would take a drop to about $49.95 to imply 4% perpetual growth and about $43.21 to imply 3%. It is also not pricing an AI winner — that would take 6% perpetual growth and a $73.53 price. The market has landed almost exactly on "fine, boring, fairly valued." **We agree with the market. That agreement is the reason we pass.**

One update since the court sat: the stock is now **$62.28** (up 8.5% from the $57.39 court price). It has traded *through* the probability-weighted fair value of $60.09. Whatever thin edge existed on 2026-08-07 has been consumed by the tape.

## Why we might have edge

We had one genuine edge and it turned out to point the wrong way, which is worth saying up front.

**The edge we built:** On 2026-03-17 Docusign announced in writing that it would stop reporting "billings" — the forward-looking bookings number it had published for eight years — and replace it with an annual "recurring revenue growth rate." It stopped reporting six weeks into what would turn out to be its weakest first quarter in three years. Retiring your leading indicator right before a weak print is exactly the pattern we screen for.

Our team rebuilt the billings figure from the company's own published formula and the raw balance-sheet lines, and validated it against the printed number for **eight consecutive quarters with zero error to the dollar**. Then we applied it to the quarter the company went dark on.

**What we found: no deterioration.** Reconstructed Q1 FY27 billings were $766.5M, +3.6% year over year — weak-looking, but Q1 is structurally the weak quarter every year (+5.2%, +4.2%, +3.6%). On a rolling twelve-month basis billings are **+9.35%**, flat across four quarters and *above* both revenue growth and recurring-revenue growth. And the one number the company cannot retire — deferred revenue on the GAAP balance sheet — grew from $1,447.2M to $1,594.7M, **+10.2%**, faster than revenue ([Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1261333/000126133326000074/docu-20260430.htm)).

So the edge is real but it is an **anti-finding**: the concealment we expected is absent, and the absence is the result. That is worth recording — it tells us the honesty screen works and it tells us not to short this name — but it does not create a position.

**The residual edge that does survive:** outside verification of Docusign's forward growth just went from four times a year on a fully reconstructible metric to once a year on a metric that reconciles to no financial statement line and is computed on an allocation method management controls. Our reconstruction closes that gap *today*. It **decays** — two of the inputs (refund liability, unbilled receivables) are now unobservable and will drift silently. Error is about ±1 percentage point now and widens every quarter. We are the only party in the market who can still audit the number, and that capability has a shelf life.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: because it is.

- **The valuation is checked twice by two independent methods that agree within 2%** (20.2x from the cash/share side, 19.8x from the expense side). There is no hidden discount.
- **The good news is already public.** The buyback, the record cash flow, the GAAP profit inflection (operating income +85%) were all in the June press release.
- **The bad news is public too** and the market has had five months with it: growth guided to +7.4% excluding currency, and non-GAAP gross margin *falling* (82.3% → 81.5%) at a company whose whole story is AI.
- **The one thing that might not be priced runs against us, not for us.** In the FY26 10-K Docusign **deleted** its prior claim that Intelligent Agreement Management is *"a new software category without incumbent competitors"* and **added** a new paragraph saying AI advances are making the core capabilities of its product *"cheaper and easier to replicate,"* such that it may increasingly compete with LLM providers, data platforms, and customers' own homegrown tools ([FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1261333/000126133326000021/docu-20260131.htm)). The issuer withdrew its own moat claim and added a substitution warning in the same document. If anything is mispriced here, it is that the terminal value deserves a *lower* multiple, not a higher one.

## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because several of these are load-bearing:

- **The print date.** 2026-09-03 comes from a market-data feed, not from Docusign. Until a company release names it, every dated sensor above carries date risk.
- **The size of the AI-substitution risk.** This is the whole ballgame for the terminal value and **nobody can size it** — not us, not the benches, and evidently not the company, which flagged the risk in words and quantified nothing. We are declining partly on an unquantifiable, and we should say so rather than dress it as analysis.
- **Whether the higher list prices for IAM are actually being collected.** List price is up 67–78%. Realized price shows up nowhere: gross margin is down, and the CFO twice declined to confirm any magnitude when asked directly. We have proof of a price *card*, not of price *received*.
- **Retention, quantitatively.** One qualitative sentence from the CFO — "better than the company average," on an admittedly small sample. Docusign is the only name in its eleven-company peer set that publishes no retention metric at all. That is not evidence of a problem; it is the absence of the evidence we would need to underwrite one.
- **Revenue per IAM customer.** It appears to have fallen roughly 25% in a quarter as the mix moved to smaller commercial customers, but that rests on comparing a ">25,000" figure to a "40,000" figure across different disclosure venues. Directionally consistent with management's own description of who is adopting; not precise.
- **Remaining performance obligation.** Reported rounded to the nearest $0.1B, which is ±4.3% on this base — a 9% grower and a flat business look identical. Directionally soft, unusable as a gate.
- **The durability of our own reconstruction.** It is exact today and decays. We do not know how fast. The first quarter where the reconstructed figure and the eventual annual recurring-revenue disclosure disagree materially will tell us — and that is a year away.
- **Our evidence pack lagged the filings by four quarters** on the quarterly income-statement tags. Both benches worked around it by going to primary sources. That worked here because two strong benches were watching; it would not have worked in a thinner review. Fixing the pack is a pipeline task, not a Docusign task.
