# PITCH — WING — PROPOSED VERDICT: REJECT — NO ENTRY, WATCH FILE WITH DEFINED REOPEN TRIGGERS (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

## What we believe

Wingstop is a franchise royalty business — nearly all of its ~2,700+ restaurants are owned by franchisees who pay it a percentage of sales — whose stock has fallen 68% from its high of $345.81 to $111.36, within 1% of its 52-week low. The original bull idea was that this selloff overshoots: that full-year guidance (same-store sales down 4% to 6%) was achievable without any recovery, and that the sales trend was already improving.

After the court, we believe the opposite on both counts:

1. **The sales trend is getting worse, not better.** Measured on a two-year basis (this year's decline stacked on last year's comparison, which removes easy-versus-hard base effects), domestic same-store sales went from −8.2 in Q1 to −9.4 in Q2 — a 1.2-point deterioration per quarter. The thesis had claimed a 1.2-point *improvement*; it had derived last year's quarterly numbers by splitting the half-year evenly, and the real split (Q1-25 +0.5%, Q2-25 −1.9%, per the company's own press releases) flips the sign.
2. **Guidance has no cushion.** If the trend merely stops deteriorating — freezes exactly where Q2 left it — the full year lands at about −5.9%, the absolute floor of the −4% to −6% guide. If the 1.2-point-per-quarter slide continues, the year lands near −6.8%: a miss, and a third guidance cut in roughly nine months (two cuts have already happened in six).
3. **The balance sheet makes this a levered bet.** Net debt is about $1.08 billion (long-term debt $1,210.6M less cash $127.5M per the Q2 press release), roughly 4.1x annualized adjusted EBITDA, financed through a securitization of essentially the whole business. At $111.36 the enterprise trades near 15.5x that EBITDA — a full multiple for a business whose core metric is shrinking.
4. **The franchisee pain is ahead, not behind.** Average unit volumes are down ~10% to $1.893M, but closures in Q2 were just 1 domestic and 3 international — franchisees have not capitulated yet. The store pipeline being opened today was signed in 2024–25 at better assumed economics; the supply response to today's volumes lags.

## What the market believes

The market is pricing a third guidance cut as the base case. The stock sits 0.8% off its 52-week low in a live downtrend, with roughly 60 trading days until the next earnings report (~November 3, unconfirmed) and no scheduled positive catalyst before it. About 30 sell-side analysts cover the name — this is not an overlooked or under-followed situation where we could plausibly know something the market hasn't processed. The market's read — the deterioration *is* the trend — is, on the evidence the court assembled, simply correct right now.

## Why we might have edge

Stated honestly, these survived the court only as watch items, not as reasons to buy today:

- **Company-owned stores tell a much better story.** The ~51 restaurants Wingstop runs itself faced *harder* year-ago comparisons (+1.4% and +3.6%) yet declined far less — their two-year stacks are −0.8 and +1.1, versus −8.2 and −9.4 for franchised stores. That 9–10 point gap is real, not a base-effect artifact (the blue bench checked). It suggests the brand itself may not be broken. Weight it lightly: 51 stores, geography undisclosed.
- **The headline volume decline overstates mature-store erosion.** With units growing 16–19% a year, a large share of the store base is young stores still ramping toward full volume, which mechanically drags the average down. Mature stores are eroding less than the −10.4% headline implies.
- **A third cut would hurt sentiment more than earnings.** Because revenue is a royalty on sales, guidance moving from −5% to −7% changes royalty revenue only ~1–2%. The November risk is to the multiple, not to earnings power — which bounds the fundamental (not the price) downside of a miss.

## Why it might be priced in

The stronger case is that there is no mispricing at all. The bull thesis's two load-bearing claims — improving trend, guidance with headroom — were both refuted on primary data. For guidance to hold even at its floor, the deterioration must stop cold *this quarter*, after two cuts in six months; hitting the midpoint requires ~1.8 points of improvement. The buyback flatters per-share figures (diluted shares down 7.5% year over year, debt-funded), so per-share growth overstates the business. New franchisees now underwrite openings at $1.893M volumes, which threatens conversion of the 2027 development pipeline — the unit-growth engine that carries the multiple. And with the mechanical mix effect noted above cutting both ways: the same young-store math that softens the mature-store read means reported volumes keep falling as long as growth continues. At 15.5x levered EV/EBITDA, 0.8% off the low, none of this is a bargain price for a stabilization that hasn't started.
