# PITCH — AVAP.L — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-06 — DECLINE (conviction 8/10). No position, no order, no starter. Both benches independently reached DECLINE; they disagreed violently about *why*, and the surviving reason is the boring one: the company earns about 6.7% on its aeroplanes and pays about 8.5% to borrow the money for the next one. Price at court 138.0p; tape today 141.325p (market value ≈ $113m).**

---

## What we believe

Avation is an aircraft lessor listed in London. It owns turboprops (ATRs) and single-aisle jets and rents them to airlines. It trades at roughly half its own stated book value, buys back a lot of stock, pays a small dividend, and is 21%-controlled by its chairman.

We believe three things, in order of confidence:

1. **The business does not out-earn its own cost of money.** The fleet nets about 6.7% after costs. Blended debt costs about 6.8%; the *next* dollar of debt costs 8.5% (the 2031 notes). So every incremental aeroplane the company buys with borrowed money transfers value from shareholders to bondholders. The chairman has publicly committed the company's cash to expansion — 13 firm ATRs, roughly US$265m of commitments, "expected to be fully funded from organic cash flows." That is the terminal path, and it is a value-destroying one at these spreads.

2. **The 50%-of-book discount is a choice of measurement, not a fact.** Stated book is $230.4m. Of that, $86.1m is a model-derived valuation of purchase rights on a 2011 ATR contract (Fitch removes it from equity entirely), and $57.6m is a revaluation surplus the company itself says would not exist if it carried planes at cost. Remove both and equity is $86.7m — about **108p a share on the current share count**. Against 141p today, that is 1.3× tangible cost-basis book, not 0.5× anything. The discount is real at one end of a three-point range and inverted at the other.

3. **The shareholder return is currently funded by shrinking the company, not by running it.** In the six months to 31-Dec-2025 statutory operating cash flow was **negative US$1.0m** (prior-year half: +US$54.6m). Buybacks, dividends, bond repurchases and capex — roughly US$45m of outflow — were funded by **US$85.8m of proceeds from selling the Boeing 777**. The half-year presentation headlines "Operating cashflow $39.9m," which is the pre-working-capital line, about 40× the audited figure on the same page of the same set of accounts.

What we do **not** believe: that this is a fraud, a liquidation, or an imminent credit event. Our own red bench argued that and lost most of it. Covenant compliance is stated in audited accounts. S&P upgraded to B (11-Nov-2025); Fitch affirmed B stable (08-May-2026). The feared October-2026 maturity is genuinely gone — the debt is US$300m of 8.5% notes due May-2031.

## What the market believes

The market is not making an error here so much as declining to pay for a promise. At 141p it is saying: *this is a small, controller-run, illiquid lessor that earns less than it borrows at; we will price it at a large discount to a reported book value we don't fully trust, and we will not pay up for the buyback because the buyback is being fed by asset sales.*

Evidence the market is pricing this coherently rather than ignoring it: the stock is −13% over 52 weeks, −18% since September 2025, and has sat in a 133–141p band for three months. The 13-July-2026 trading update — a positive one — traded 126,469 shares and closed at 138p, which is where it was before. Nothing about the situation is undiscovered. Roughly $55k of stock changes hands a day.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly stated, there are three places an edge could live, and none of them survived to a buy:

- **The purchase rights.** Avation holds rights to buy ATRs on 2011-base pricing into a genuinely supply-constrained market. Cirium values the 24 relevant aircraft at US$552m; the company carries the *rights* at $86.1m. If those rights are worth more than the model says, the tangible-book math above is too harsh. This is the one live asymmetry in the name. It is also, unavoidably, a non-cash Level-2 model number on a fifteen-year-old contract, which is exactly why Fitch strips it out.
- **airBaltic panic.** The second-largest lessee by book value carries a going-concern material-uncertainty paragraph. Most investors will stop reading there. Our blue bench went further and found that Latvia legislated Cape Town Convention Alternative A with a **60-day** repossession cap (Law "On Aviation" Art. 22¹, adopted 3-Dec-2020) and has applied deregistration authorisations since 1-Sep-2021. airBaltic's centre of main interests is Riga. So the lessor's downside there is roughly 60–90 days of lost rent, not a stranded aeroplane. That is a real, non-obvious, verified piece of work — it just points at "smaller loss," not "cheap stock."
- **The Braathens precedent.** When a lessee did fail (October 2025), Avation repossessed and re-leased the aircraft to Finnair on six-year terms in about nine months. The re-leasing machine works. The rates were never disclosed, which is the catch.

## Why it might be priced in

Because it is. The stock trades at a discount to reported book *because* the reported book contains two constructs that a rating agency and the company's own accounting policy note both discount. The buyback yield looks large *because* the denominator is a $113m market value being paid down with $85m of aeroplane sales. The dividend is 0.54%. Nothing here requires the market to be asleep.

Two specific things are priced that we would have expected to be mispriced, and weren't:

- The re-leasing capability is visible in the tape as *no reaction*: the Finnair placement and the July trading update both went out to a shrug.
- The bond, not the equity, is where the compensation is. The senior instrument pays 8.5% and sits ahead of a junior claim that yields about 3.7% all-in (roughly 3.2% buyback at the recent pace plus 0.54% dividend). If we want this credit, we should buy the credit.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The court's verdict is DECLINE, so there is no entry band.** The adjudication returned its reopen-gate list empty; what follows is reconstructed strictly from the benches' own arithmetic, and is labelled as such rather than passed off as verbatim court language.

**No purchase at any price until an evidence gate opens.** Price alone does not reopen this name, because the objection is structural: 6.7% earned against 8.5% marginal funding is not fixed by a lower share price on its own.

**Reopen band A — asset basis (price gate): 108p or below.**
That is tangible cost-basis equity per share ($86.7m ÷ 59,700,301 at GBPUSD 1.3457). At or below it you are paying nothing for the revaluation surplus and nothing for the purchase rights — the one genuine asymmetry becomes free. This is **−24% from today's 141.3p** and −12% below the 52-week low of 122.44p. It is a long way away, and that is the point.

**Reopen band B — evidence first, then price: 120–128p, and only if both of the following are true.**
1. FY26 audited results (expected early October) show **statutory operating cash flow ≥ US$60m** with buybacks funded from it rather than from aircraft sales — red's own stated bar; and
2. **AVAW expires unexercised on 31-Oct-2026**, or the company states that the 13.28% overhang is covered.
128p is roughly 0.62× the ex-revaluation book of ~206p. Even with both events clean, we are not a buyer above this, because blue's yield-gap objection survives both of them.

**Hard ceiling: no work above 155p.** At 155p the stock is ~0.75× ex-revaluation book with a negative 1.8-point spread between what the fleet earns and what the next aeroplane costs to fund. Above that level, retire the sensors.

**Sizing, if either band ever opens:** minimum starter only, and liquidity is the binding constraint, not conviction. At ~$55k a day of turnover, a 1%-of-volume ladder buys about $550 a day. Cap any position at **0.25% of the book** and at **five days' average volume (~$275k)**, whichever is smaller, accumulated on limit orders only. Note the company itself bought 100,000 shares on 03-Aug-2026 — 2.6× that entire day's 38,524-share tape. We would be competing with the issuer for the same thin float, which also means we should never bid into a day the company is likely buying.

**Trims/exits:** not applicable — no position exists and none is proposed.

**Catalyst dates that govern the above:** 17-Aug-2026 (airBaltic reconvened bondholder meeting — already passed, outcome unknown to us), 31-Aug-2026 (airBaltic's EUR 30m Latvian state loan repayable), ~02-Oct-2026 (Avation FY26 audited results, dated by proxy from FY25), 31-Oct-2026 (AVAW warrant expiry).

## What remains unverified

- **The 17-Aug-2026 airBaltic bondholder vote.** Held four days ago; result not in our evidence. Our blue bench's chairman quote put quorum probability at "very high," which is a forecast, not a result.
- **Whether NAV per share compounds at 1% or at 5%.** Red's half-year arithmetic (+1.09% in USD, with the buyback alone owing +2.64%) and blue's structural estimate (4–6%/yr) are flatly inconsistent and neither bench reconciled them. The adjudication declined without settling it. This is the largest open question in the file and the FY26 accounts settle it.
- **The 2031 indenture covenants.** Not obtained. We do not know what restricts payments, what minimum net worth applies, or how much of the asset base must stay unencumbered. We are reasoning about a levered company without its debt document.
- **Market value of 61% of the fleet.** The 14 narrowbody jets have no transaction evidence behind their carrying value. The chairman's "20% above book" is unaudited and untested.
- **Finnair lease rates** versus the Braathens rates they replaced — undisclosed, and no audited word until October.
- **The company's own concentration figure.** 41.9% (audited, FY25) and ~30% top-1 (CFO, at H1) have never been reconciled by the company.
- **Whether the >6-month receivables bucket (US$1,993k) has cleared,** and the identity of the lessees behind the deferred-payment accrued revenue — unnamed in the accounts.
- **Whether the four A220 leases have registered international interests and filed deregistration authorisations.** The Cape Town 60-day protection is the reason we downgraded the airBaltic risk from fatal to a sizing item; the paperwork behind it is not public.
