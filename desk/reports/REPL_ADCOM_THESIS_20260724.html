Replimune: a coin-flip the market has already priced as a coin-flip

July 24, 2026 · RP1 (vusolimogene oderparepvec) + nivolumab, advanced melanoma · FDA ODAC "late July" then PDUFA Aug 2 · this thesis is carried to the catalyst so the decision is pre-made · verdict: WATCH-ONLY, no pre-position

Replimune's melanoma drug faces an FDA advisory panel in the last days of July and a decision by August 2. It is the company's third attempt after two rejections, it seeks accelerated approval on a single-arm trial, and the FDA's objection both prior times was not a fixable paperwork gap — it was whether the data can be substantial evidence of effectiveness at all. The stock, at $10, sits between a ~$1.70 crash floor it visited three months ago on the last rejection and a ~$18–20 approval case; the options price a ~90% move and analyst targets run from $1 to $17. That is a binary the market has already marked as a binary. Our verdict is watch-only: no naked long into a defined-risk event where we hold no demonstrated edge over the priced coin-flip. This document exists so that when the panel votes, the reaction is execution — not research.

What is actually being decided (and when)

Live: $10.00 (bid/ask 9.99/10.01; prior close 10.46; −4.4% on the day; ~1.2M shares) · 52-week range $1.50–$12.50 · 82.7M shares · annual IV ~328%

Claim → finding. On June 26, 2026, Replimune disclosed (8-K, Exhibit 99.1) that the FDA accepted its BLA resubmission for RP1 + nivolumab in advanced melanoma as a "complete, class 1 response," with a goal date of August 2, 2026, and told the company to expect an advisory committee meeting in late July. The committee is the Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee (ODAC). The resubmission seeks accelerated approval on data from the single-arm IGNYTE trial (ORR ~33.6% by mRECIST; median duration of response ~24.8 months) in patients with confirmed progression on an anti–PD-1 regimen. Confirmed — primary 8-K

The date the prompt carried (~Jul-30) is the AdCom estimate, not a confirmed date. As of this writing the FDA has published "late July" but no exact ODAC date or the verbatim voting question. The PDUFA action date — August 2 — is firm. A same-sector sponsor (Capricor) has an AdCom on July 29, which is likely the origin of the "~Jul-29/30" figure. Exact AdCom date UNVERIFIABLE This is the single most important open tripwire: the FDA posts its own briefing documents ~2 business days before an AdCom, and that release — the agency's written read of the data — typically moves the stock before the vote.

The decisive finding: the objection is unchanged, and it is substantive

This is where the promoter framing ("productive scientific discussion," "urgency," "unmet need") diverges from the record. RP1's ORR is real and durable — that is not the argument. The FDA's two prior Complete Response Letters (CRL #1 July 22, 2025; CRL #2 on the April-10-2026 review) rested on a structural objection: the single-arm IGNYTE trial is not an adequate and well-controlled investigation providing substantial evidence of effectiveness, because of the single-arm design, the heterogeneity of the patient population, and the inability to isolate RP1's contribution of components in a combination with nivolumab. The confirmatory randomized trial, IGNYTE-3 (vs. physician's choice), has not read out.

The third resubmission adds no new randomized data. It bets on a changed FDA posture — a post-leadership-shakeup agency, melanoma-community pressure, and a public airing at ODAC — rather than on new evidence that answers the objection. That makes this a regulatory-judgment bet, not a data bet, and it is why an AdCom was convened at all: the FDA is putting a contested substantial-evidence question to a panel rather than deciding it internally.

But the CRL #2 disclosure cuts the other way, and it is the reason the stock is $10 and not $3. Replimune's April 10, 2026 CRL announcement (8-K, Exhibit 99.1) revealed a split inside the FDA: per the company, "the BLA clinical team thought the applicant had provided adequate evidence to support contribution of effect of RP1 plus nivolumab but leadership did not agree," and a new review team was appointed for the resubmission and did not meet with the company. So this is not a clean data-failure — the FDA's own clinical reviewers were persuaded on the exact point (contribution of components) that leadership then overruled. The third attempt is therefore a bet on whether an external panel of oncology experts (ODAC) will side with the clinical reviewers, and whether post-shakeup leadership will follow an ODAC endorsement. That is genuinely closer to a coin-flip than "twice-rejected, so doomed" — which is precisely how the market has it priced. What it does not do is erase the base objection: an AdCom is a public forum where a skeptical panel can still vote no on single-arm adequacy, and the confirmatory IGNYTE-3 is not yet in hand. The reviewer-split lowers the bear probability; it does not remove it.

Stakes, from the company's own mouth. The CEO stated on the second CRL that "without timely accelerated approval, the development of RP1 will not be viable," and the company then eliminated jobs and scaled back U.S. manufacturing. A third CRL is therefore likely terminal for the program as currently structured — which is exactly what pins the bear case to the ~$1.50 floor rather than a milder drawdown.

Cap structure & runway — verified before any valuation

Per doctrine, the balance sheet was pulled from the audited 10-K (FY2026, period ending 3/31/2026) before any view on the trade. Two findings matter.

The cap structure is clean. Confirmed — 10-K No convertible debt. Preferred stock is authorized (10M shares) but none is issued or outstanding — there is no preferred overhang and no in-the-money warrant dilution to surprise a "net cash / below cash" claim. The company's financing history is common stock plus pre-funded (penny) warrants — which are economically shares and are already in the count — and a Hercules term loan ($80M principal, interest-only through Sep-2027, balloon/maturity Oct-2027). The Hercules covenants are the one structural risk: a going-concern condition or a CRL could pressure the facility.

The runway is short and the auditor says so. Going concern — audited Cash + short-term investments were $268.9M at 3/31/2026 ($209.0M cash + $59.9M ST investments). FY2026 operating cash burn was $280.3M (~$70M/quarter, and accelerating with the commercial build-out). The 10-K states plainly that existing cash "will be sufficient to fund operating expenses...only into the first calendar quarter of 2027," and the auditor flags substantial doubt about the ability to continue as a going concern. Management's own fork is explicit: if the BLA is not approved, the company "will likely need to (i) execute a [restructuring]..." and/or raise capital on whatever terms are available. An ATM with Leerink is live.

Why this is CLEAN, not a catch. Every load-bearing risk here — the single-arm limitation, the two prior CRLs, the going concern, the need to dilute on a "no" — is disclosed in plain language in the company's own filings. The marketed takeaway does not diverge from the data; the risk and the data are both on the table. So this is not a honesty-alpha short. The catch is the asymmetry of the binary, not a lie — and that asymmetry is exactly why a naked pre-vote long must clear a high bar.

Timing × outcome, priced jointly

We do not multiply a separate timing probability by a separate outcome probability — the event and its magnitude are read together off one live surface. The tape gives a hard anchor most models can only guess at: on CRL #2 in April 2026, REPL crashed from ~$5.90 to ~$1.70 intraday and bottomed at $1.50. That is not a scenario — it happened three months ago, and it is the empirical "no" outcome. The recovery from $1.50 to $10 over the subsequent quarter embeds substantial approval hope. The options market corroborates the coin-flip: annual implied volatility ~328% implies a straddle move of roughly ±90% over the four weeks to the PDUFA, and sell-side targets span $1 (bear) to $17 (BMO Outperform), with Wedbush at $9 Neutral. The $1-to-$17 dispersion is the binary, priced explicitly.

Resolution pack — the branches, pre-committed

The point of carrying the thesis to the catalyst is that no fresh research happens inside the reaction window. Here is what we do on each branch.

Branch A — ODAC votes YES / FDA approves (by ~Aug 2)

The stock gaps toward $18–22. Action: do NOT chase the gap. The de-risking is real but the confirmatory IGNYTE-3 overhang and a near-term financing (approval unlocks a capital raise from strength) both cap the immediate upside. Re-diligence the commercial ramp — melanoma post-PD-1 TAM, launch readiness, the Hercules refinance — and set a lower re-entry on the inevitable post-approval consolidation, not a market order into the spike. This becomes a fundamental long candidate, graded fresh.

Branch B — ODAC votes NO / CRL #3

The stock breaks toward the ~$1.50–3 April floor; a dilutive raise or restructuring follows on management's own stated fork. Action: do nothing — do not catch the knife. The going concern becomes acute and the equity is hostage to financing terms. Only revisit if a specific path clears the overhang: a strategic partner/acquirer, a non-dilutive deal, or an FDA path to a defined new trial that the market can underwrite. Absent that, avoid.

Branch C — the briefing documents (the real leading tripwire, ~2 business days pre-AdCom)

The FDA posts its review documents before the panel meets. A hostile briefing book gaps the stock down ahead of the vote and effectively pre-announces the outcome; a constructive one re-rates it up. Action: this is the watch trigger. If a pre-position were ever contemplated (it is not, by default — see below), the briefing-doc release is the only information event that could justify a defined-risk, sub-0.5% expression — and only if it revealed a genuinely mispriced skew, which the current straddle does not.

Stage or watch? — WATCH-ONLY no pre-position

The house rule is that we do not stage naked pre-binary longs unless the edge is real and sized tiny. Here the edge is not established as positive: the expected value at defensible probabilities sits at roughly the live price, the options and the analyst dispersion both confirm the market has priced the coin-flip, and the downside is an observed ~85% drawdown to a going-concern raise. There is no honesty-alpha catch to exploit (everything is disclosed) and no obvious skew mispricing in the straddle. The default posture is therefore no pre-position — watch and react.

The only pre-position that could ever clear the bar would be a defined-risk, unlevered, sleeve-capped ≤0.5%-of-NLV long-premium or call-spread expression, and only if the independent court found a genuinely favorable, mispriced base rate versus the straddle — which is the specific thing the court above was tasked to test. Any envelope below is a candidate for the principal to stage, never staged by the analyst, and is contingent on that finding.

Honest limits

One confirmed fact cuts both ways: RP1 holds Breakthrough Therapy Designation. The FDA granted BTD (based on the anti–PD-1-failed IGNYTE cohort), and the original BLA went in November 21, 2024 under accelerated approval. The bull reads BTD as the FDA having seen real promise; the bear reads the sequence correctly — BTD did not prevent two CRLs. The substantial-evidence bar defeated the single-arm package twice despite the designation, which reinforces rather than softens the decisive finding: the objection is structural, and a marketing designation does not answer it. A countervailing bull factor is genuine: organized melanoma patient advocacy (e.g. AIM at Melanoma) is engaged, and advocacy pressure has swung close ODAC votes before.

Flagged UNVERIFIABLE / open: the exact ODAC date and the verbatim voting question (FDA has published only "late July"); the precise wording of the FDA briefing documents (not yet posted — the key leading tripwire); and the exact terms/covenant triggers of the Hercules facility under a going-concern condition (the 10-K discloses the facility and that covenants "could adversely affect operations," but not the specific default mechanics). None of these change the verdict — a fairly-priced, defined-risk binary is watch-only regardless — but all are on the record, and the briefing-doc release is the event that most changes the picture.
