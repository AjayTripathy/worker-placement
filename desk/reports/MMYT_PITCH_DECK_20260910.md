# PITCH — MMYT — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT — put is 2028 not 2030 (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

---

## What we believe

MakeMyTrip is India's largest online travel marketplace. Its *business* is fine and its *balance sheet* has a dated problem, and the stock at $48 (pack tape, −53.5% off the 52-week high of $103.24, +46.9% off the $32.67 low) is pricing something between the two.

Three things we think are true:

1. **The demand engine is still running.** Constant-currency gross bookings grew ~19.9% and constant-currency revenue ~16.1% in the most recent reported quarter. Nothing either bench produced contradicts those filed numbers. That leg survived the whole adversarial process untouched.
2. **The reported earnings collapse is largely a financing artifact — but only *largely*, and only *until July 2028*.** The company issued $1.4375B of 0.00% convertible notes labelled "due 2030." The accounting charge for those notes is non-cash today. It becomes cash later, and "later" is nearer than the label suggests.
3. **The gap between the stock and the street is real but unexplained.** Street price target is ~$75 against a $48 tape — the stock sits 36% below the consensus anchor while consensus revenue for FY27 is unchanged at $1.21B. Somebody is wrong; we cannot yet name the mechanism that closes it, and an unnamed mechanism is not a trade.

What we do **not** believe any more: the original brief's claim that the converts are "a 2030 cash claim." That was refuted by the issuer's own closing press release. Holders can require the company to buy the notes back **for cash at 100% of par on July 3, 2028** ([BusinessWire, 2025-06-23](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250623022449/en/MakeMyTrip-Announces-Closing-of-Offering-of-US$1.25-billion-0.00-Convertible-Senior-Notes-Due-2030-and-Full-Exercise-of-Option-to-Purchase-Additional-Notes)). At $48 against a $121.50 conversion price, that put gets exercised with near-certainty. The brief was wrong by two years, and we say so plainly.

---

## What the market believes

The market believes two things at once, and they are not obviously compatible:

- **Sell-side:** revenue is fine, dollar earnings are impaired. FY27 revenue held at $1.21B while FY27 EPS was cut from $0.547 to $0.487. Target ~$75. That is precisely our own view of the units — so on the *facts* we agree with the street.
- **The tape:** −53.5% from the high, and — importantly — the market has already *seen* the good print. On the release the stock closed $61.78, +8.5%; BofA raised its target to $71 and Goldman reiterated Buy the next day. Then the tape round-tripped ~20% lower over the following weeks.

That last sequence is the most uncomfortable fact in this file. The bull evidence was published, was rewarded, and was then re-sold. "The market hasn't seen the print" is not available to us as an argument.

At $48 on ~94.06M shares the equity is ~$4.52B; adding $643M of net debt gives ~$5.16B enterprise value, or **~98.6x** consensus FY27 IFRS EPS of $0.487. That multiple only makes sense if you believe the IFRS earnings number is fake. After the 2028 put, it isn't fake — it converts into real cash interest at refinancing.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: thinner than the original brief claimed.

- **The refi gap is arithmetically small, and most people are not doing the arithmetic.** The scary version is "$1.4375B due vs $794.3M of liquidity, coverage 0.55x." The correct version adds seven quarters of cash generation before the put: ~$182M/yr of operating cash flow ⇒ ~$319M, plus ~$45M/yr of deposit income ⇒ roughly **$1.19B against $1.4375B, a ~$246M gap ≈ 17% of principal**. That is a refinancing to arrange, not a solvency wall. If the market is discounting the scary version, that is a gap we can trade — *once we can see the bond*.
- **The bond price is the single cheapest decisive datapoint and nobody has pulled it.** A 2030 note quoted near par says the market expects the cash put to be paid without drama. A note quoted in the 70s–80s says the opposite. Neither bench fetched it. That is a genuine, fixable information asymmetry — and it is cheap to fix, which is exactly why we should not pretend it is edge until we've fixed it.
- **One bench found a real capital-destruction fact the other missed:** 18.4M primary shares were sold at **$90** in June 2025, alongside the converts, to fund the buy-in of Trip.com's Class B stake (45.3% → ~17%). Stock retired near $90 now trades at $48 — roughly 45% impairment on ~$3.1B of deployed capital, with a demonstrated ~17% seller still resident. That is a governance/capital-allocation read most screens will not surface.

## Why it might be priced in

- **We agree with the street on the mechanism.** Volumes fine, dollar EPS impaired. When your differentiated insight is the consensus view, the burden shifts entirely onto "the tape is wrong," which is the weakest form of a thesis.
- **The refuting evidence already traded.** +8.5% on the print, then −20%. Whatever the market learned, it learned it after being told the good news.
- **The put date is not secret.** Convertible desks model to the earliest holder put by construction. Assuming a $1.4B refinancing wall is undiscovered by the people who own the paper is not a defensible assumption.
- **98.6x on IFRS EPS is a screen output**, not an insight. Every value screen in the world already sees it.

We flag, symmetrically, that our own doctrine forbids rejecting a thesis solely because it is "priced in" or because we agree with consensus — and one bench leaned on both. Those rejections were correctly overturned. But overturning them does not manufacture edge; it just means the case has to be won on the funding evidence, which we do not have.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The refinancing repricing (monitorable).** The company refinances the 2028 put into straight or higher-coupon paper. Post-refi, roughly **$90–100M/yr of what is today a non-cash accretion charge becomes actual cash interest** on a business generating ~$182M of operating cash flow. That is over half of operating cash flow consumed by debt service, permanently. The stock doesn't crash on the announcement; it just never re-rates, and the "IFRS EPS is optics" argument dies quietly. *Tripwires: 2030 bond quote, any 6-K announcing a facility or new issue, cash interest paid in the 20-F.*

**Failure narrative 2 — Currency eats the story (monitorable).** USDINR at ~94.5–95.4 versus ~87–88 a year ago is roughly an 8% year-over-year drag that *recurs* in the September quarter. Worse, the structure is amplifying, not offsetting: a **USD-denominated** cash put serviced by **depreciating INR** earnings. Constant-currency bookings can grow 19.9% forever and the reported dollar line still shrinks. Whether the notes are FX-hedged is unknown to both benches. *Tripwires: USDINR spot, the 20-F debt-note hedge disclosure.*

**Failure narrative 3 — The private capital action (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).** Trip.com still owns ~17%, has already demonstrated it will sell, and its intent is negotiated in private. So is any board decision to pre-fund the 2028 put with a discounted equity or equity-linked raise. Both would be decided in rooms we cannot see, and both are disclosed *after* the price is set — the 13D/A or the pricing PR arrives with the damage already done. There is no pre-signal, no alt-data proxy, no filing cadence to watch. **Unknown-unknown class: private counterparty intent / negotiated capital actions disclosed only post-execution.** A related sub-class sits alongside it: **Indian regulatory and channel structure** (GST treatment of OTAs, ONDC-style disintermediation, airline direct-booking pushes) — policy that arrives fully formed with no US-filing footprint we monitor. We have no sensor for either. We are naming this rather than inventing a tripwire we don't have.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** Estimated 20-day ADV is roughly **1.6–2.0M shares (~$80–95M/day)** — an estimate, not a measured figure, because we did not pull the volume series. At the proposed starter size (⅓ unit, ~0.5% of book) our clip is on the order of **5–8% of one day's volume**: exitable in a day on a normal tape, but the exit we care about is the bad one, where the reason we are selling is the same reason everyone else is. Two structural amplifiers stack against us on that day: a **~17% strategic holder with proven willingness to sell**, and **~5.93M shares (+6.3% dilution)** that mechanically arrive from the legacy $230M 0.00% notes due Feb 2028, which are **in the money** at a $38.80 conversion price against a $48 tape. Supply is not scarce here.

The doctrine interaction is the subtler risk. Our rules forbid "priced-in" and "we agree with consensus" as rejections — correctly, because those are lazy kills. But applied mechanically they push us to keep a dead thesis alive on procedure, and our gate list makes that worse: every gate (bond ≥92, INR <96, take-rate flat) resolves *upward*, so we buy only after the news is good and the price is higher. We would be building a ratchet that systematically pays for confirmation. If the bond prints 94 and the stock is $58 when we get there, the gap to the $75 anchor is 29%, not 56%, and the position is a different, worse trade than the one written here.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ that we treated a solvable ~$246M refinancing gap as the whole question, and the refinancing got solved — on terms that permanently convert non-cash accretion into ~$90–100M/yr of real cash interest against ~$182M of operating cash flow, so the balance sheet stops being scary and the equity still doesn't re-rate, while INR depreciation quietly erases the constant-currency growth we were paid to believe in.

**Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated. It splits on a single binary (bond ≥92 / facility disclosed, or not), producing mass at ~$75 and at ~$32 with a thin middle. Structurally, fork-dated bimodality is the setup where **long calls beat stock if IV is cheap versus σ_table**, because the stock pays you the mean while the option pays you the fork. If the chain comes back with 9-month IV materially **below 46%**, the honest recommendation flips from "none" to a small call position at $55–60. If IV comes back **above ~55%**, the fork is already paid for and the route is premium-selling gates (CSP at $40, where our table says the premium is worth ~$3.14) rather than long optionality.

Every candidate expiry above (~9 months, i.e. June 2027) puts the loss branch in **tax year 2027** — the loss is deferred out of the harvest year. Our rule excludes deferred-loss expiries during a harvest year **unless the edge numerically dominates**. Here the edge is **+6.9% EV on unverified inputs**. It does not dominate. A December-2026 expiry would land the loss correctly but is too short to span the gate resolution the whole thesis depends on.
