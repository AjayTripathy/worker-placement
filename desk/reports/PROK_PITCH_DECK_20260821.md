# PITCH — PROK — VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position, no order, no entry today; both benches land on "don't buy," and the honest reason is that the stock is roughly fairly priced for a coin flip we cannot measure.

---

## What we believe

ProKidney is a single-product company. The product is rilparencel — a cell therapy made from a patient's own kidney tissue, re-injected to slow chronic kidney disease in diabetic patients. Everything about the company rides on one late-stage trial (PROACT 1) reading out around mid-2027.

Our view, after both benches argued it:

1. **The headline efficacy number does not mean what the bulls say it means.** The Phase 2 trial (REGEN-007) reported that treated patients' kidney function declined 4.6 units/year more slowly after injection than before injection — advertised as "about 3× the FDA's bar." But that comparison is each patient against their *own past*, not against a control group. Patients entering a trial also get their standard medications tightened up at the same time, and patients selected *because* they were declining fast tend to decline more slowly afterward regardless of treatment. The Phase 2 design cannot separate those effects from the drug. So this is not evidence of a large effect; it is **an absence of measurement**. ([full Phase 2 results](https://investors.prokidney.com/news-releases/news-release-details/prokidney-presents-full-results-phase-2-regen-007-trial))
2. **The FDA's own wording pre-loads exactly that problem.** The agreed accelerated-approval bar is "at least 1.5 mL/min/1.73m²/year improvement" — *"versus sham controls"* and *"in the setting of patients receiving appropriate standard of care therapies."* The Phase 3 has a sham arm that gets the same medication tightening. So the Phase 3 is measuring a quantity the Phase 2 never estimated.
3. **Therefore our probability of success is just the base rate** for a kidney Phase 3 — call it ~40%, with honest error bars. It is an assumption, not a measurement.
4. **The company must raise money before the answer arrives.** Cash was $181.6M on 6/30; the quarter burned $43.3M and research spend is growing 39% year over year. The company's own 10-Q filed 8/10/2026 says there is "substantial doubt" about its ability to continue as a going concern. Funding through the readout plus a year needs roughly $180M, which at a normal financing discount means **35–60% more shares**.
5. **Put those together and the stock is priced about right.** At ~$1.51 and ~440M shares after the raise, using the sell-side's *already-cut* peak sales estimate of $900M, today's price pays for roughly a 42% chance of success. That is essentially our base rate. There is no gap to trade.
6. **The downside is not bounded.** One asset, going concern, one binary date. A miss is a stock around $0.20–0.40, not $1.07. A fairly priced bet with an uncapped downside is a pass, not a hold-your-nose buy.

**Net: FLAT. No starter position. No automatic re-entry just because the price falls.**

## What the market believes

- **The bulls** capitalize the 4.6 number as though it were a controlled treatment effect, and mark the company against a multi-hundred-million to billion-dollar peak-sales outcome.
- **The marginal seller has already moved.** BofA cut its peak sales estimate from $1.8B to $900M. Standing price targets of $9 (Citi), $8 (UBS) and $3 (Morgan Stanley, Hold) are July-2025 artifacts sitting against a $1.51 tape — nobody is trading off them.
- **The tape reads distress, not disbelief in the science:** −57% from the 52-week high, ~16% off the low of $1.30, with a $200M at-the-market share-sale program authorized — roughly 44% of the company's market value in potential new supply hanging over it.
- **On 8/21 the stock moved +10.2% intraday with no filing behind it.** We could not explain that move. It is unexplained, not dismissed.

So the market is not asleep. It is pricing a real drug with a real regulatory agreement, discounted hard for money it does not yet have.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly stated, and each one is thin:

- **We read the trial design, not the press release.** The distinction between a within-patient before/after comparison and a sham-controlled comparison is the whole thesis, and retail-facing coverage repeats "3× the FDA bar" without it. If the market is systematically over-reading that number, we are correctly on the other side.
- **We did the two halves of the math together.** One of our own benches built a per-share value table on today's share count while separately forecasting 46–50% more shares — and concluded "financing is not the binding constraint." Multiply the two and financing *is* the binding constraint. That mistake is easy to make and probably common in published notes on this name.
- **We know how the money will actually be raised.** A $200M need against a ~$455M company cannot be dribbled into the market quietly; it almost certainly requires an announced deal. That tells us where to watch and roughly when.
- **We can see the insider record.** A sitting director sold his entire common holding on 8/18–8/20, eight days after the going-concern disclosure.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetrically, here is why we probably have no edge at all:

- **The going-concern language is on the public record**, in the company's own filing, since 8/10. Nothing about the funding problem is private information.
- **The dilution math is arithmetic anyone can do.** Cash, burn rate, and the size of the share-sale program are all disclosed.
- **The sell side already cut.** We are agreeing with the analyst who moved, not disagreeing with the consensus. Agreeing with the marginal mark is not edge.
- **The insider sale is timing-explainable.** 8/18 was the first trading window after the quarterly filing — the normal time for insiders to transact. His shares were bought for cash in January 2022, before the company went public via merger, almost certainly around $10. Selling at $1.37 is realizing an ~86% loss. That is a person cutting a bad trade, which is only weakly informative about what he thinks the 2027 readout will show.
- **Our probability of success is a guess.** If the true peak sales are nearer the old $1.8B than the cut $900M, the dilution-adjusted value at a 40% success rate is $2.86 — the stock nearly doubles and we were wrong to pass.
- **The price already sits at the base-rate value.** "Fairly priced" is the *definition* of no edge.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry. There is no buy band today, at any price, on price alone.** What follows are reopen conditions, stated with the benches' own arithmetic.

**Reference math (blue bench, at ~$1.51 and ~440M shares after a raise, using BofA's cut $900M peak):**

| Chance of success | Value per share | vs. $1.51 |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | $1.07 | −29% |
| **40% (our base rate)** | **$1.43** | **−5%** |
| 50% | $1.79 | +19% |

At the old $1.8B peak, the 40% line is $2.86. At a bear $400M peak, the 40% line is $0.64. A failed readout is worth roughly $0.20–0.40.

**Reopen requires BOTH of the following. Neither alone is sufficient.**

- **Trigger A — the money is raised, at a known price.** An announced, sized offering (a 424B5 prospectus supplement, registered direct, or PIPE) that establishes a clearing price. Watch in parallel for the quieter channel: the Class A share count on the cover of each successive 10-Q/10-K. If that count climbs materially with no announced deal, the at-the-market program is doing the work and there will be no announcement to wait for.
- **Trigger B — a real comparison group exists.** Any estimate of rilparencel's effect *against a separate control* — the Phase 2 protocol and statistical plan showing the baseline period was prospectively defined under already-optimized medication with balanced arms, a matched external-control analysis, or the count of Group 2 patients who crossed over to a second injection. **Absent Trigger B, a cheap post-raise price is still buying an unmeasured coin flip.** Do not skip this one.

**If and only if both fire, the indicative bands would be:**

- **Starter, minimum size, 0.25% of the book:** at or below **$0.90** — the price that pays for a 25% chance of success against the cut peak, i.e. a genuine discount to base rate with the financing resolved.
- **Add:** at or below **$0.64** — the price that pays for a 40% chance even under the bear $400M peak scenario. This is the "wrong about the drug's commercial size and still fine" level.
- **No add above $1.07** under any circumstance, since that is the 30%-chance line and offers no margin.
- **Hard stop on the thesis:** a failed or ambiguous Phase 3 readout. There is no partial credit here and no trimming plan that survives it — the tail is $0.20–0.40 and we should size as if that is the outcome.

**Sizing if reopened:** maximum 0.5% of the book, all-in, across both tranches. One asset, one date, going concern, unbounded downside. This does not qualify as a risk we can be fairly paid to own, because "fairly paid" requires the loss to be bounded and here it is not.

## What remains unverified

Stated as gaps, not as reassurance:

1. **The true chance of success.** Our 40% is a category base rate, not a measurement of this drug. Every valuation number in this deck is downstream of it.
2. **Whether the director actually holds nothing.** The Form 4's derivative section was empty, and that section reports transactions rather than holdings. He may still hold exchangeable units or options. The proxy ownership table settles it; we have not read it.
3. **The crossover count in Phase 2** — how many low-arm patients received a second injection. This number decides whether the trial can say anything about dose at all. Not disclosed in any source we read.
4. **Whether the baseline decline period was prospectively defined**, and whether the two arms were balanced on starting kidney function and medications. Requires the protocol and statistical plan.
5. **Daily trading volume and short interest.** Broker connection denied to both benches. We do not know how contested an exit would be, or whether the at-the-market program could in fact absorb $200M quietly — which is the load-bearing assumption in blue's argument that an announced deal is coming.
6. **Manufacturing cost and capacity.** Never examined. It sets the gross margin in every sales scenario above.
7. **The competitive field.** No sweep of other kidney-function-slope programs against the company's own competition disclosure was run.
8. **The 2026-11-09 earnings date.** Vendor-derived. No company release confirms it.
9. **The 8/21 price move.** Unexplained.
10. **The peak sales figure itself.** We used $900M because that is where the analyst who most recently moved put it. We have not built our own commercial model, and the difference between $900M and $1.8B is the difference between "pass" and "double."
