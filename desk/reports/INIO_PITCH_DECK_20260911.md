## What we believe

INNIO N.V. (ticker INIO, CIK 2109150) makes big gas engines and generator sets — the kind that sit behind data centers, factories and grids as on-site or backup power. It IPO'd in June 2026 and the stock has since fallen 53% from its high ($42.95) to $20.30, sitting 6.6% above its 52-week low of $19.04.

What we believe, stated plainly:

1. **The order book is real and it is getting better, not worse.** Backlog went from $4.9B to $6.6B in a single quarter; quarterly intake was ~$2.3B; unsatisfied performance obligations are $8.8B with about 79% expected inside 24 months (10-Q, 2026-07-28: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002109150/000119312526319072/d118344d10q.htm; 8-K Ex-99.1: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002109150/000119312526318820/d174544dex991.htm). Neither bench was able to refute the demand leg.
2. **The margin scare is a mix ceiling, not a decay.** Equipment segment margin went 13.4% (Q1'25) → 7.1% (Q1'26) → 13.8% (Q2'26). The trough is behind, the level is capped by Equipment being ~65% of revenue (Equipment ~11.4% H1 margin vs Services ~30.8%).
3. **The guide is demanding but not absurd.** H1 revenue was $1,606.5M against an FY guide of $3.8–3.9B, so H2 must be ~$2,244M — up ~39.7% half-on-half. FY25 was already +33% half-on-half, so this is a ~7-point step-up on the company's own precedent, not a new cliff. On EBITDA, H2 needs ~$443M on ~$2,244M = **19.8% implied margin, versus ~20.6% actually delivered in H2'25.** The guide asks for last year's second half, not a miracle.
4. **The edge, if there is one, is a multiple argument.** At $20.30 the equity is $15.2B, net debt ~$1.58B (debt $2,618.7M less cash $1,039.9M), so EV ≈ $16.8B = **23.0x the midpoint of guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA ($720–740M)**. The street's average target of $44.6 (10 analysts) implies ~36x next year's EBITDA. The disagreement is entirely about how much credit the 10 GW capacity plan deserves.

And the concession up front: **our own bear bench's fair-value band ($17.50–$21.40, 15–18x FY27 EBITDA of ~$0.98B) contains today's price.** On our own arithmetic this is a fairly-priced stock with an optionality tail, not a bargain. That is why this is a starter and not a position.

## What the market believes

The market is not disputing the order book. It is discounting three other things:

- **A guider with no record.** The 2026 guide was *introduced* at the first post-IPO print — there is no beat/miss history to lean on. Price is haircutting the guider's credibility while conceding the demand data.
- **A supply problem.** Only 90M of 750M shares (12%) trade. Advent still owns ~88%, roughly $13.5B at today's price, and it took ~$2.43B out in a **100% secondary** IPO where INNIO received **zero proceeds** (https://www.innio.com/en/news-media/press-releases/innio-announces-pricing-of-upsized-initial-public-offering/). A ~180-day lockup off the 2026-06-04 trade date lands around 2026-12-01. Everyone can see this; it is the whole post-IPO press narrative.
- **A multiple that got ahead of itself.** 23.0x EV/EBITDA is rich for a reciprocating-engine OEM unless the data-center power cycle is durable. The derating is a re-rating of the multiple, not a rejection of the backlog.

The market is *not* pricing the bull case: the street's published consensus is Buy with a target more than double the tape. So the marginal seller is not the analyst community — it is flow.

## Why we might have edge

- **Naming the disagreement precisely.** Both sides agree on FY26 ($720–740M). The gap is 23x (us/tape) vs ~36x (street) on FY27, and it resolves on one observable: whether Equipment margin lifts while capacity triples. That is a gradeable question with a dated test (the Q3 Equipment segment margin), not a vibe.
- **A mechanism the sell-side write-ups don't price.** A company with a $15B market value but a 12% float cannot attract the float-adjusted passive bid (MSCI's standard-index free-float screen sits at 15%). In the normal post-IPO derating, index buying catches the fall. Here it can't — until the sponsor sells *more*. That inverts the usual reading: **the overhang isn't only a risk, it's a prerequisite for the re-rating.** Supply delivered at a known price is our entry signal, not our stop.
- **Two mis-framings we can trade around.** (a) The bear "base effect" attack on intake conflates the $1.7B prior *backlog* with the ~$0.55B prior *intake* — the load-bearing number is the sequential $4.9B → $6.6B, which no base effect touches. (b) "79% of RPO inside 24 months" was framed as bad; a 21% long tail is normal where service contracts run a decade.
- **Timing asymmetry.** Nobody is forced to own this before ~Dec 1. We can be paid to wait in size only after the one variable we cannot currently date is dated.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: mostly, it is.

- **Everything in our bull case is published.** Record intake, $6.6B backlog, one-off IPO charges, the maiden guide — all in the company's own PR and in ten analysts' notes. One bench called this a fatal blow to the *edge claim* (not the company) and it lands. We are not holding private information; we are holding a different multiple opinion.
- **Our own fair value brackets the tape.** 15–18x FY27 → $17.50–$21.40. We are buying at $20.30 — the upper half. There is no margin of safety at this price, only a call option on capacity-plan credit.
- **The lockup is common knowledge.** So is the float. So is the 88% holder. Anything this visible is at least partly in the price; the open question is only the *date* and the *clearing price*.
- **The bear number both benches nearly missed is real:** H1'26 EBITDA margin ~17.7% vs ~21.0% in H1'25 — a 330bp year-on-year compression across a full half. That is cleaner and more damaging than any half-on-half arithmetic, and it is not obviously "transitory."
- **Expected value is thin.** Our own scenario table (below) gives $22.25 against a $20.30 tape: **+9.6%**. That is not a fat pitch. It is a starter.

## What could go wrong

**Failure #1 — The supply air pocket (has a tripwire, but the tripwire is late).**
Advent registers a secondary into a 12% float. Because the name is not yet in the float-adjusted passive indices, there is no mechanical buyer to absorb it. A beta-3.61 stock that already manufactured both a $42.95 and a $19.04 print on thin float can gap 20–30% on an announced deal before any fundamental news. Tripwire: an S-1 / S-1MEF / 424B filing or a 13D/G amendment — but those print *after* pricing is decided, so the tripwire tells us the trade, not the escape.

**Failure #2 — The maiden guide misses, and the miss is a credibility event, not an arithmetic one.**
H2 has to carry ~61% of the year with a factory mid-ramp. If Q4 slips even a few weeks, a first-ever public guide gets cut, and a company with no track record gets no benefit of the doubt. Multiple and estimate compress together — that's the $11.50 branch in our table. Tripwire: Q3 print (date contested), Equipment segment margin, any 8-K guide revision.

**Failure #3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE.**
The undisclosed terms of the concentrated order book. One ~1.1 GW order plus hyperscaler follow-ons and a Rehlko framework agreement dominate the backlog delta. The **dollar value, the cancellation clauses, the deferral rights and the buyer's identity are all undisclosed** — the non-disclosure is itself the finding. If those contracts are slot reservations with cheap walk-away terms, "backlog" is a softer number than it reads, and *we will learn this only when a customer walks*. There is no filing, sensor, alt-data pull or satellite image that front-runs it: framework agreements do not file 8-Ks when they quietly shrink, and the counterparties are unnamed, so we cannot even screen them.
**Unknown-unknown class: counterparty-quality opacity in a single-customer-concentrated capital-equipment book** — a demand-durability unknown that is structurally unobservable to a public-market outsider until realization. Related named-but-unpriced siblings: a possible pre-IPO dividend recap (3.6x YE25 leverage on an Advent carve-out is recap-consistent; no distribution evidenced, S-1 related-party note unread) and the share of the $205M free cash flow that is actually customer advances (contract-liability balance unread).

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
- *Our own size is not the problem.* A starter of 0.5% of book is immaterial against an estimated $40–80M/day of dollar volume (ADV was **not** in the pack — that is a gap, estimated below). We are a price-taker here; we cannot move this tape and we should not pretend our exit is the risk.
- *Somebody else's size is the whole problem.* The predicted flow is one holder with ~$13.5B against a ~$1.8B float. The ratio is roughly 7.5:1. Every sizing decision we make is a bet on the manner of that delivery, not on engines.
- *Doctrine interactions.* (i) Float-starved index ineligibility means no passive catch on the way down and a potential index-add bid *only after* dilution of the sponsor stake — the two forces are sequenced, not simultaneous. (ii) Maiden-guide credibility discount means fundamentals cannot rescue the flow break until the second or third print builds a record. (iii) The lockup (~Dec 1) and the contested Q3 print date (Oct 27 vs Dec 2) may **collide**, stacking an earnings gap on a supply gap — though note that claim stacks two unverified dates and we flag it as such rather than trading it.
- *Crowding note:* consensus being bullish while price is −53% means our long is not contrarian versus research; it is contrarian versus flow. Those are different bets with different half-lives.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid a fair-value price for what is actually a supply problem — Advent delivered its 88% stake into a 12% float before the capacity-plan credit ever arrived, and the multiple, not the order book, did the damage.

**EV = $22.25 vs a $20.30 tape = +9.6%.** Thin, and it is the honest number. A 9.6% expected return with a 15% chance of −43% is a starter-sized idea, which is exactly what both benches concluded by other routes.

**Dispersion.** σ_table = **$8.18 = 40.3% of spot** over the ~12-month resolution horizon.

**Bimodality.** The table is fork-dated on the lockup (~Dec 1) and the contested print. The distribution is genuinely two-humped — orderly supply vs air pocket — not lognormal. Any option pricing that assumes a smooth σ will misprice the wings, and premium-selling strategies are more dangerous than a single σ implies.

**Expression math (from the table itself).**
- C_ours(K=22.5) = Σ pᵢ·max(Sᵢ−K,0) = **$2.90**
- C_ours(K=25) = **$2.19**
- C_ours(K=30) = **$1.19**
- P_ours(K=17.5) = **$0.90**

Against those, a one-year at-the-money call on a $20.30 stock costs roughly 0.4 × S × IV: **$2.92 at 36% IV, $3.65 at 45%, $4.87 at 60%, $6.09 at 75%.** So the break-even implied vol for buying the $22.5 strike is **≈ 35.7%.** In a name with beta 3.61 and a 52-week range of $19.04–$42.95 — realized vol plausibly 70%+ — a chain-level IV under 36% is close to unthinkable. **Conclusion from the arithmetic: our own table does not support buying calls.** Cheap-IV-routes-to-calls does not apply; our σ_table (40%) is *below* plausible IV×√T (60–85%), which routes structurally toward premium *selling* — the $17.50 put we value at $0.90 would likely quote several times that.

**But the recommendation is not "sell puts," for three stated reasons.** (a) **No chain is in the pack** (open item #7). We will not substitute an underlying aggregate IV feed for chain-level midpoint IV and real strike quotes — that is precisely the TSSI error where the aggregate feed read 71 against a chain at 101. Without real quotes, VRP (IV − RV) and the IV percentile are **NOT COMPUTED** and any premium-selling conclusion is unearned. (b) Our table is probably *under*-dispersed: it prices fundamentals but not the flow tail that failure narrative #1 names, and selling wings on an under-dispersed table is how you get run over by an 88% holder. (c) Tax.

**TAX OVERLAY.** The loss branch must land in the intended tax year. The fork (lockup ~Dec 1, possible Dec 2 print) sits days from the **2026-12-31** boundary. Any expiry in January 2027 or later defers the loss branch into 2027 — excluded during a harvest year unless the edge numerically dominates, and a +9.6% EV does not dominate anything. A December-2026 expiry would keep the loss in-year but places all the gamma directly on the two contested dates. Both routes fail on their own arithmetic.

**INSTRUMENT — OUTPUT OF THE ABOVE: COMMON STOCK, starter size, no options leg.** Long calls fail the break-even-IV test (need <36%, expect 60–85%). Premium selling is blocked on missing chain data, an under-dispersed table and bimodality. Covered calls are unavailable at starter size. Cash-secured puts are re-examinable **only** after the chain is pulled with real midpoints and after the lockup date is read — and then only with a pre-2026-12-31 expiry.
