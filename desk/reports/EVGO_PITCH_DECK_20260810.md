## What we believe

EVgo's demand is real and growing: energy delivered rose 13% year over year, and charging revenue rose 19%. The business problem is not customers — it is money. Growth is entirely bought with capital spending (throughput per stall actually fell about 2% year over year), and the funding picture just got worse: the Department of Energy loan advances were cut from $1.05 billion to $625 million, the stock trades at $1.59 (down 69% from its high, only 13% above its 52-week low of $1.41), and management still plans to roughly 2.5–3x the build in 2027. Total available liquidity is about $684 million ($121.8M unrestricted cash + $409M undrawn DOE + $153.4M Voyager facility) against first-half cash use of roughly $106 million — adequate for now, not obviously adequate for the 2027 plan. The new Tesla partnership (EVgo owns hundreds of V4 Superchargers; Tesla operates and maintains them; deployments start fall 2026) is strategically real but adds owned capital spending with no disclosed economics. Until the funding question resolves, there is nothing here that pays a shareholder to wait.

## What the market believes

The market answered the key question for us on August 5, 2026: EVgo announced the marquee Tesla Supercharger deal and the reduced funding guidance the same morning, and the stock fell 12.7% net. A partnership headline that would normally move a $1.59 stock sharply higher was fully swamped by the funding cut. The market is pricing this as a financing story, not a demand story — it expects the 2027 build to require dilutive equity issuance at depressed prices, and it is not paying anything for the Tesla optionality until economics are disclosed. At 13% above the 52-week low with no confirmed catalyst until the next earnings report (estimated November 9, 2026 — not company-confirmed), the market's stance is "show me the money."

## Why we might have edge

- **The dilution fear is overstated in the popular telling.** The scary "share count doubled in three years" reading is an accounting artifact of EVgo's Up-C structure: 23 million Class B units held by LS Power were redeemed into Class A shares, which inflates the Class A count without creating new economic ownership. Measured correctly on total A+B units, dilution was about +19% over the period — real, but a fraction of the headline. Our own red bench initially made this exact error (see the checks table).
- **Liquidity is better than the bear math.** Counting only unrestricted cash against burn ignores $562 million of undrawn, build-specific facilities. The near-term runway question is not acute.
- **The Tesla deal has a hidden cost benefit**: Tesla operates and maintains the chargers, offloading ongoing operating cost even as EVgo carries the capital cost.
- **We have defined, checkable tripwires** (hiring taper, G&A, Tesla economics, financing form) that would tell us the funding fork resolved favorably before the market fully reprices it.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands we say it: much of the bear case is consensus. The market already knows the DOE cut, already shrugged off the Tesla deal, and already prices a raise. Meanwhile, several of our residual bull hopes are unproven: the claimed +5% realized price per kilowatt-hour may be partly the mix effect of a dying OEM-credit revenue stream, not durable pricing power (no retail-only price series is disclosed). Cost discipline is not corroborated — job postings surged (6 in May → 10 in June → 42 in July), a hiring wave, not a taper, even if the visible postings skew toward build-side roles rather than overhead. And the controlling shareholder, LS Power (55% economic control via Class B), monetized 23 million units in a December 2024 secondary — the insider with the best information has been reducing. If the funding fork resolves badly, an equity raise at these prices would be genuinely punishing, and LS Power's structural position could let it prime any rescue at the operating-company level ahead of public holders.
