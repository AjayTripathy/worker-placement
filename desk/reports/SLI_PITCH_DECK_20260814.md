# PITCH — SLI — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: NO ENTRY at $2.37 — stay out; the stock is still absorbing dilution damage while its financing is unresolved. Re-open only on the specific filing triggers below, via a fresh court, not an automatic buy.**

## What we believe

Standard Lithium (NYSE American: SLI, ~$2.37, roughly 246.4 million shares outstanding as of August 7, 2026) is a real, advanced lithium project — not a fraud and not a story stock. Its South West Arkansas project, a 55/45 joint venture with Equinor, has a completed definitive feasibility study: US$1.45 billion of all-in construction cost (that figure already includes a 12.3% contingency cushion), 22,500 tonnes per year of lithium carbonate, and a 20.2% unlevered pre-tax internal rate of return ([feasibility study press release, Oct 14, 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/10/14/3166207/0/en/Smackover-Lithium-Files-Definitive-Feasibility-Study-for-Its-South-West-Arkansas-Project-North-America-s-Highest-Grade-Reported-Lithium-Brine-Reserve.html)). The pilot plant's output was just used successfully to make actual battery cells (press release filed with the SEC on August 13, 2026). The lithium price environment supports the project rather than threatening it.

But we believe the stock is currently un-ownable, for one reason: the company slipped its final investment decision, and it is paying for the delay by continuously selling new shares into a depressed market. The day after the second-quarter report that disclosed the slip, the company filed a new US$50 million at-the-market stock-sale program — while about US$14 million of the previous program was still unused. It sold roughly $13.9 million of stock (about 6 million shares) in the five and a half weeks from July 1 to August 7, into a $2.20–2.50 tape. Last year the same program raised money at an average of $4.01 per share; today each dollar costs the company nearly twice the shares. Until the project's debt and offtake are actually signed, every week of delay is funded by dilution at bad prices. The damage is not "in the past" — it is arriving continuously.

## What the market believes

The market has cut the stock 63% from its 52-week high of $6.40 and it now sits about 24% above its 52-week low of $1.91 — so this is not a fresh panic low; some bounce has already happened. The market's implicit view is roughly ours: good project, unfinanced, serial share-seller. The prospectus supplement itself says the project remains "subject to… finalizing additional key contracts and securing suitable project financing" — the market is taking the company at its word that nothing is signed. Management's more optimistic framing (a target of roughly 80% of production under offtake contracts, and lender "indications of interest") exists only in the earnings call transcript; none of it appears in the vetted SEC documents, and no new offtake agreement has been filed since March 9, 2026. The market is pricing the vetted documents, not the call — and the courts concluded the market is right to.

## Why we might have edge

- **We did the balance-sheet arithmetic the headlines don't do.** The bull framing — "$137 million of cash plus $64 million of stock-sale capacity comfortably covers the equity check" — double-counts. The $137.3 million of cash at June 30 is the same cash management has budgeted to burn through June 30, 2027 (about $36 million per half-year, mostly joint-venture contributions). At a year-end investment decision, free cash is closer to $100 million. The "comfort" only exists if you pre-spend ~11% dilution through the stock-sale program.
- **We also did the arithmetic in the other direction, which cuts in the company's favor.** Using the feasibility study's true $1.45 billion all-in cost, an assumed $1.1 billion of project debt, and $225 million of committed Equinor-side contributions, the residual equity check is about $125 million total, of which SLI's 55% share is roughly **$69 million** — not the ~$96 million our own red bench first calculated. Against ~$100 million of free cash, the base case actually covers, thinly (~$31 million of margin), *without* the stock-sale program. The real risk is fragility: every $100 million the debt package comes in light adds $55 million to SLI's check.
- **We measured the dilution in real time.** By comparing remaining stock-sale capacity across two filings five weeks apart, we established the ~$13.9 million July–early-August sales pace as fact, not inference. That is a measurable tripwire (see reopen conditions), not a vibe.
- **We know exactly what would change the picture**: a signed debt commitment or a second offtake agreement, each of which must arrive as an SEC filing. Those are watchable, binary, and dated.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands we say this plainly: at −63% from the high, the market has already punished the stock for most of what we found. Our central conclusion (good project, dangerous financing gap, continuous seller) is broadly the consensus one. Several things could make staying out wrong:

- The Equinor partnership terms are public and favorable ([Equinor press release, May 8, 2024](https://www.equinor.com/news/20240508-partnership-standard-lithium)): $30 million already paid, a $60 million funded work program (including a $33 million carry of SLI's share), and **up to $70 million more paid to SLI if positive investment decisions are taken**. Our original analysis wrongly called these terms undisclosed. That $70 million, if it attaches to this project's decision, roughly doubles SLI's cushion and could make the equity check a non-event.
- The lithium price is a tailwind, not a headwind. Our red bench claimed a "six-month slide"; that was wrong. Chinese benchmark lithium carbonate is around ¥148,000/tonne, having *risen* from January to a near-three-year high in May (¥180,000–200,500) before pulling back ~25% over about three months. A firm price makes both the debt package and offtake economics easier to close.
- The battery-cell validation (August 13 filing) and the feasibility study's built-in contingency mean technical risk is largely retired. If financing closes on schedule, today's price likely never returns.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry at any price today**, because the blocking finding is a financing binary, not a valuation. No price makes the stock ownable while the debt package is unsigned and the share count is rising weekly — a cheaper entry mostly means the dilution got worse. Accordingly, these are **reopen bands**, not buy orders. Position today: **0% of book, no resting orders.**

Reopen triggers (any one reopens the case for a fresh court; none is an automatic buy):

1. **Signed project-debt commitment of at least US$1.0 billion, in an SEC filing** (a 6-K — a press release filed through the SEC — not a call transcript). This is the single fact that flips the classification: with debt signed, the ~$69M equity check is covered by cash on hand with margin, before any Equinor decision payment.
2. **A second offtake agreement filed with the SEC before the end of fiscal Q3 (~September 30, 2026)** — the company's own guided window. An on-time delivery here would be the first vetted-document evidence supporting management's more optimistic call commentary.
3. **Confirmation in a vetted document that the $70M Equinor decision payment attaches to this project's investment decision.** This alone would double the cushion and could justify reopening even without the debt filing.

- **Price below $1.91** (a new 52-week low) while the stock-sale program is active: the company would be issuing at ever-worse prices and the equity-check dilution math worsens nonlinearly. If we're reopened later, a break of $1.91 first would argue for a smaller starter, not a cheaper bargain.
