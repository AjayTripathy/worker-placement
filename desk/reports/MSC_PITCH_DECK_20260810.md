## What we believe

Studio City (MSC, the Macau casino resort, $1.81, down 73% from its 52-week high) is **not** the mispriced, fundamentally-undamaged stock our original brief claimed. The court broke the thesis on primary data:

- The headline Q1 "beat" (+14.5% year-over-year cash earnings) was **table luck, not business strength**. The casino kept 36.9% of what mass-market gamblers put on the table this Q1 versus 32.8% a year ago — both far from the ~34% long-run norm, in opposite directions. Adjusting both quarters to normal luck, Q1 earnings were **down roughly 13%**, not up 14.5%.
- Worse, the volume itself is shrinking: mass-market table drop was **$901.3M vs $923.9M a year ago (−2.4%)** while the overall Macau market grew ~7% ([Q1 2026 earnings release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/30/3284837/36898/en/Studio-City-International-Holdings-Limited-Announces-Unaudited-First-Quarter-2026-Earnings.html)). The company is losing share on volume; luck masked it.
- The equity is a thin sliver on a big debt stack: ~$349M market cap against ~$1.94B net debt (~6.4x annual cash earnings). At that leverage, about 6.4 years of current earnings just covers the debt — and the casino license expires **2032-12-31**. Much of the equity's value is an option on license renewal, on terms the Macau government sets. The 2022 retender kept all six incumbents, so the option is worth something — but it is not decidable this week.

What we'd still own: a levered recovery instrument **if** the business proves it can grow volume, not luck. That is a post-print question.

## What the market believes

The market is treating MSC as a levered stub of a decelerating Macau: Morgan Stanley [cut its 2026 Macau revenue and industry earnings forecasts](https://www.ggrasia.com/ms-cuts-2026-macau-ggr-forecast-and-industry-ebitda-estimate-on-weak-growth-up-to-4q), Jefferies cut 2026 as well, and July market revenue fell 8.4% year-over-year. At 6.4x leverage, a ~10% cut to earnings estimates at an unchanged multiple mechanically takes out on the order of 60% of the equity — which is roughly the excess drawdown we saw. Our brief called the sell-off "self-sustaining flow, not analysis." The evidence says the opposite: the market is doing the analysis, and the arithmetic largely checks out.

## Why we might have edge

- **The decomposition itself.** Restating results in volume-vs-luck terms (drop and hold, not headline revenue) is the correct lens and most headlines don't use it. It flipped our own thesis; it will also tell us faster than consensus whether Q2 is a real inflection.
- **The leverage cuts both ways.** The same arithmetic that produced −73% produces violent upside on a genuine volume recovery. A confirmed volume-led beat re-rates the stub hard.
- **The renewal option is not zero.** The bear's "terminal clock" is real, but the 2022 retender retained every incumbent on painful-but-survivable terms. The refinanced notes (mature 2031, before license expiry) also remove any near-term bankruptcy cliff.
- **We have a precise, pre-committed re-entry gate** derived from the company's own numbers, so we can act on the print faster than a market re-reading headline revenue.

## Why it might be priced in

- Everything decisive here is public: the drop decline is printed in the company's own Q1 release, one fetch away — our red bench missed it and the blue bench found it, which means sell-side desks can find it too.
- The sell-side is already cutting estimates; the de-rate reflects analysis, not abandonment. There is no orphaned-flow discount to harvest.
- The stock is +15% off its 52-week low. Buying here is buying a bounce, not a dislocation.
- The 2032 license fork is well known and structurally unpriceable by us — no reason to believe we handicap the government's terms better than anyone else.
