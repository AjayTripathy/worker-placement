# PITCH — PATH — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — NO ENTRY. Fairly priced, no special edge, and no fill at $14.06 (1.3% under the six-month high, +37% in ten sessions, into an unconfirmed print); two alert gates armed, both reopen conditions dated.**

---

## What we believe

UiPath is a decent business that is not growing, priced roughly for what it is.

Three things are true at once, and the argument only works if you hold all three:

1. **The advertised turn is mostly bought, not earned.** The number that got the stock re-rated on 2026-05-29 was "net new annual recurring revenue up 81% year over year, $27M → $49M." A $190M acquisition (WorkFusion, closed 2026-02-05) landed five days into that quarter and its revenue is folded into the same total, with no organic/inorganic split disclosed anywhere. Our two benches converged independently on WorkFusion contributing **$16–26M of that, most likely $19–22M**. Strip it out and organic net new was roughly **$28M against $26.6M** — up about 5%, not 81%.

2. **But the company is not breaking either.** The strongest version of the bear case — "no new customers for three years, the business is dead" — did not survive checking. Customers paying over $1M/year grew **18%** and accelerated; customers over $100k grew 11%; the company keeps **97 cents of every dollar** of last year's revenue before any upsell. What's leaving is the small tail, and it's worth about 1.7% of revenue. The balance sheet is $1,415M of cash and securities against **zero debt**, share count is **down 4.5%** because the buyback exceeds stock compensation, and free cash flow was $372M last year.

3. **We have no informational advantage at this price.** The market already paid for the inflection story — +11.8% on the print day, on 58.7M shares. Then it round-tripped the whole thing to $9.93 by late June, broke 16% on two enormous-volume days in late July, and melted +38% in nine sessions back to $14.09 with **no company disclosure explaining any of it**. As of 2026-08-21 it is $15.92 — another 13% higher, 4.6% under the six-month high, and 73% above the May low.

So: a fairly-priced, fairly-run, non-growing software company, in the middle of a flow-driven move we cannot explain, thirteen days ahead of a print whose date we haven't confirmed. That is a name to watch, not a name to own.

## What the market believes

The market's working story, as best we can read it off the tape and the guide:

- **Bull version:** retention bottomed at 107 and turned up to 109, net new revenue nearly doubled, the founder-CEO's cost turnaround produced the first profitable year, and the company is being repriced from "melting RPA vendor" to "profitable orchestration layer for other people's AI agents." At $15.92 that's roughly **3.6× enterprise value to recurring revenue** and a ~5.4% free-cash-flow yield — cheap for software if growth is about to resume.
- **Bear version, also in the price:** the stock is still 20% below its 52-week high, and the multiple sits at a fraction of profitable-growth software because the market does not believe the growth line. Management guides the next quarter to **+9.8% revenue against +17.3% just delivered**, with operating profit down 18% sequentially.

The market is not confused about the facts. It is undecided about whether the June-quarter step was real. That is exactly the question the 2026-09-03 print answers — which is why buying ahead of it is paying for an answer we're about to get for free.

## Why we might have edge

Four places where we know something the tape plausibly doesn't:

1. **The acquisition arithmetic.** UiPath's own definition of recurring revenue contains no carve-out for acquired contracts, and the word "organic" appears **zero times** in the Q1 press release. We bounded WorkFusion four independent ways and got the same answer each time. The break-even for the 81% to be organic is paying **more than 9× recurring revenue** for a distressed 2011-vintage vendor — while UiPath's own stock trades near 3×. Sell-side has not published this split because the company hasn't given them one.

2. **The distress evidence on the acquired asset**, from records nobody screens: WorkFusion's UK accounts show its share fair value falling $0.91 → $0.58 through 2024, a going-concern discussion citing a cost-reduction exercise and reliance on debt "to be raised in the near future," and a funding pattern of $60.0M (2021) → **$8.8M from a single investor** (2022) → $15.4M (2025).

3. **The purchase-price allocation reads like a technology-and-team purchase, not a revenue book.** Developed technology ($44.5M) exceeds customer relationships ($39.8M) — a book bought for its recurring revenue allocates the other way — and 17% of the price ($32.3M) is a deferred tax asset, i.e. accumulated losses.

4. **Nobody has ever asked.** Across nine earnings calls, no analyst has pressed management on the flat customer count, and management has never once used the words "invoicing," "billing terms," "upfront," "prepaid," or "unbilled" despite a five-quarter shift in exactly that mix. That's an unexamined flank — but note it cuts both ways, and it is a disclosure-quality observation, not a thesis.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: most of it is, and the honest reading is that our edge is analytical, not actionable.

- **The step-up was pre-announced.** The Q1 revenue guide was issued **2026-03-11, five weeks after WorkFusion closed** — with the acquisition already inside the guided number. The actual beat that guide by only ~$4.5M. There was never an acquisition-driven surprise for the market to mis-pay for.
- **The acquisition spend was on the face of the press release**, day one: "Payments related to business acquisitions, net of cash acquired **(149,403)**" versus (24,821) a year earlier. Anyone reading the release saw $149.4M of M&A before the detailed filing landed a week later.
- **The tape already paid, then unpaid, then repaid.** The +11.8% re-rate on 2026-05-29 was the market pricing this exact inflection. Whatever it was worth, it was transacted.
- **The valuation is not distressed.** ~3.6× recurring revenue and ~16× guided operating profit on a company guiding ~10-11% growth is not obviously wrong in either direction. This is the definition of "priced."
- **We are late to the move.** +38% in nine sessions before the court, another 13% since, into a print. Every unit of our analysis says "fairly valued"; the tape says "someone with more conviction than us is already buying."
