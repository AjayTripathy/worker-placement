# PITCH — TDUP — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION): DO NOT BUY — stay flat at $3.21; the "78× overreaction" story dissolved under audit; revisit only on the Q3 print (rev ≥ $89M with gross margin ≥ 79.5%) or a fall to ~$2.00–2.15

## What we believe

ThredUp's crash — from $6.28 to $3.21 on the August 5 second-quarter report, on top of an earlier slide from a $12.28 high — looks at first like a massive overreaction: roughly $400M+ of market value erased over a guidance cut of only $5.3M in expected operating profit (the benches called this "~78× the fundamental revision"). **Our own adversarial review killed that story.** The profit being cut was never real economic profit. The company's full-year "adjusted EBITDA" guide of about $16.3M (4.7% of ~$346M revenue) is *smaller than its own stock-compensation expense*, which is running at roughly $23M a year ($11.4M in the first half alone). Strip that adjustment away and the business loses money even in its best quarter on record (a $5.9M GAAP net loss in Q2, −6.5% of revenue), while the share count grows ~7% a year to pay employees. The market didn't misprice a $5M line item; it correctly repriced a business whose profitability was an accounting definition, the moment its growth halved and management admitted the demand was bought with discounts. We recommend no position.

## What the market believes

The market now prices TDUP at about $3.21 — 5.6% above its 52-week low, down 74% from its high — which works out to roughly $382M of enterprise value (net of $57.4M cash and $17.7M term debt, excluding store/warehouse leases), or about 1.1× this year's guided revenue and ~24× guided adjusted EBITDA. The consensus story behind the crash: revenue beat in Q2 (+17%, orders +22%), but management cut second-half profit guidance by ~25%, blaming a $7M promotion headwind to clear aged inventory, and revenue-per-order fell 4.2% — meaning the growth was purchased with discounts. Sell-side price targets of $6.00–6.50 still exist, so the market is not treating this as a zero; it is treating it as a decelerating, promotionally-dependent marketplace that no longer deserves a growth multiple.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: the court concluded we mostly don't, and the deck should say so. The candidate edges, and what survived:

- **"Fresh dislocation" edge — did not survive.** The one-day halving looks like panic, but both benches confirmed the cut and the beat share a single cause (promotional dependence, in management's own words on the call), so the beat cannot be used as evidence that demand is intact.
- **Balance-sheet resilience — survived, but it only bounds the downside.** The company holds ~$35M more cash than debt (excluding leases) and generated +$2.4M of free cash flow in the first half. There is no financing cliff and no forced-seller dynamic. This makes TDUP a candidate to *watch*, not to own.
- **Clean insiders — survived.** What looked like a pre-crash insider selling cluster was fully resolved by reading the actual filings: mandated sell-to-cover tax sales and director stock grants taken *in lieu of cash pay*, zero discretionary sales. Mildly reassuring.
- **The real potential edge is future, not current:** if fourth-quarter gross margin recovers after the inventory clearance, the "one-off promo" story beats the "structural discounting" story, and the stock is cheap. That is a re-court trigger, not a reason to buy today.

## Why it might be priced in

- The pre-crash price of $6.28 was already down 49% from the 52-week high — the growth story had been exiting for a year (though our red bench overstated this; see the table).
- Sell-side coverage exists with $6.00–6.50 targets, so this is not an orphaned, unexamined name where mispricing hides.
- At ~1.1× revenue, the stock is *not obviously below* where it traded in its 2022–23 no-growth period — the blue bench found from public record that it likely traded *below* 1× revenue back then, meaning today's multiple may still be above the historical trough. (Flagged UNVERIFIED — see below.)
- Most fundamentally: with adjusted EBITDA below stock-comp expense and a persistent GAAP loss, there is no positive earnings power for the market to have mispriced. "Cheap versus a definitional profit" is not cheap.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The court's verdict is no-entry.** These are the reopen bands, from the benches' own math:

- **No buy at $3.21.** No averaging-in, no "small starter." The kill is arithmetic (adjusted EBITDA < stock comp), not sentiment, and it does not improve with a lower price alone.
- **Reopen band 1 — fundamentals (the main one):** the Q3 print, estimated 2026-11-02 (unconfirmed). Reopen the case only if revenue lands **≥ $89M** *and* gross margin lands **≥ 79.5%** (i.e., above the 78.0–79.0% guide, showing the promo hit was contained). A beat achieved with further margin erosion does NOT reopen — that confirms the bear. The truly discriminating margin test is Q4 and Q1-2027 gross-margin *recovery* after the clearance window closes.
- **Reopen band 2 — price:** below **~$2.15**, the stock reaches ~0.7× guided revenue (~$242M enterprise value on $346M revenue, using 131.3M shares and ~$40M net cash) — entering the territory where the 2022–23 no-growth regime plausibly traded. A break of the 52-week low at **$3.04** is a mechanical look-again trigger, not a buy. Before acting on either, someone must actually pull the 2022–23 price bars and verify the trough multiple — neither bench did.
- **Reopen band 3 — data:** independent evidence that discounting is *declining* while orders hold (site promotion-intensity tracking, app-ranking/traffic panels). These monitors do not exist yet — see below.
- **Sizing if reopened:** any future position starts at scout size (≤0.5% of book) and only after a fresh court, because the dilution (~7%/yr share growth) and the stock-comp inversion must have visibly narrowed, not just the price.
