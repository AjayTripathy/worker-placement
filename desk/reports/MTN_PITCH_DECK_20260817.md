# PITCH — MTN — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-18 — KILL** — Reject at $146.60; no position, no order, size zero: the reason is capital allocation, not the bad winter.

## What we believe

Vail Resorts owns a genuinely scarce collection of ski mountains and runs a subscription pass that gets a large share of the season's revenue in the bank before the first snowflake falls. That is a good business. Our problem is not the business — it is what management is doing with the cash and the fact that we cannot produce a single number about next year that differs from what everyone else already assumes.

Three things drive the reject:

1. **The company is paying out far more than it earns in cash, and borrowing the difference.** Over the last twelve months Vail generated about $175M of free cash flow and handed shareholders about $569M — $318M of dividends plus $251M of buybacks. That is 324% of free cash flow. In the same twelve months, debt net of cash rose from $2,224M to $2,635M — up $411M, or 18.5% — helped by an $850M note issue against $447M repaid. A 6% dividend yield that is funded by borrowing is not income; it is the balance sheet being converted into a payout, on an asset whose earnings depend on the weather.

2. **We have no differentiated view of next year.** Analysts have already cut this fiscal year's earnings by roughly 42% to $4.33 a share, which sits inside the company's own guided range of $3.59–$4.55. The average price target is $147.23 against a $146.60 price — four tenths of one percent. We looked hard for a number we could disagree with and did not find one. Being right alongside everybody else is not an edge; it is a fair price.

3. **The price is not cheap enough to make up for the first two.** On our corrected math the enterprise value is about $8.25B (35.8M diluted shares at $146.60 = $5.25B, plus $2.635B net debt, plus the $364.4M Whistler Blackcomb minority stake). Against the low end of guided resort profit of $735M that is 11.2x — on what everyone agrees is a trough year. On a normal-snow $850M it is 9.7x. Neither is a distressed price for an operator this leveraged.

What we do **not** believe: that the bad winter is the story. Our own red bench argued that hardest and our blue bench took it apart. The customer money already committed for next season is essentially flat (deferred revenue $562.1M at 4/30/26 vs $569.8M a year earlier, −1.4%), and next year's consensus earnings are already *below* two years ago rather than assuming a heroic bounce. The snow matters at the margin, one cycle later. It is not the reason to pass.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a fairly valued, high-yield, weather-hit franchise on its way back to normal. Specifically:

- Earnings this year collapse to roughly the company's guidance and then partially recover. Next year's third-quarter estimate is $10.10 a share, against $8.81 actually reported this year — but against $10.46 two years ago and $9.54 three years ago. So the market is pricing a partial return toward the old level, not a boom.
- Revenue this year around $2.84B, down 4.3%.
- The dividend holds. The 6.06% yield ($8.88/yr against $146.60) is being treated as real.
- The stock has already been marked down: it is 11.4% below its 52-week high but 23.7% above its 52-week low, and roughly flat over a year. Nobody is panicking and nobody is excited.

There is also a large short position — 16.38% of shares outstanding, 5.84M shares, about 7.9 days of average volume to cover — which says a meaningful minority believes the payout is unfundable.

## Why we might have edge

Three places a real edge could exist. We could not close any of them this round, which is itself the answer.

- **The payout arithmetic is not what the yield screen shows.** Anyone ranking stocks by dividend yield sees 6.06% and stops. The full picture — 324% of free cash flow returned, net debt up 18.5%, buybacks funded by a bond issue during the worst season in memory — takes reading the cash flow statement and the balance sheet a year apart. Yield-oriented buyers are the marginal holder here and they are, in our view, mispricing the durability of the payment.
- **The pass sells the *next* year during the *current* one.** Season passes for 2026/27 were sold through the spring of 2026 — while customers were experiencing a record-poor winter. Units sold fell 10%, days 8%, dollars 5%. Because dollars fell less than units, realised price per pass rose about 5.6%: the company raised price and lost the low-frequency end of the customer base. So the pass does not insulate against weather; it delays the effect by a year, into next season's on-mountain spending. That mechanism is not, as far as we can tell, in anyone's published model.
- **Nobody has put a price on the mountains.** Vail's Colorado and Utah core operates on U.S. Forest Service special-use permits (8,376 acres at Keystone, 3,801 at Beaver Creek, 8,900 leased at Park City) — an irreplaceable footprint that has never been marked against a private transaction at this $8.25B enterprise value. If a real comparable sale existed and it implied a floor above the current price, that would be a number the market does not have.

The honest weight: the first two argue *against* owning it, and the third is unbuilt. An edge that only tells you to stay away, plus an unfinished asset valuation, is not a position.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The earnings cut is fully in the numbers.** Consensus is inside the company's own guidance. There is no unrecognised collapse to trade.
- **The recovery is modest, not aggressive.** Our red bench called next year's estimate a "+14.6% snapback" and treated that as an unrealistic assumption embedded in the price. Blue showed that was an artifact of measuring from the bottom: $10.10 next year is *below* the $10.46 printed two years ago. The market is not assuming much.
- **The customer commitment did not actually shrink.** Money already collected for next season is down only 1.4% year over year. The scary −10% unit number is a partial read (the selling season runs into early December) and is far larger than the balance sheet effect.
- **The dividend risk is visible to everyone.** A 16% short interest is the market telling you the payout question is already asked. And a cut, if it comes, could be *good* for the stock — it would free roughly $155M a year for debt paydown.
- **The valuation is not an outlier.** 11.2x trough profit / 9.7x normal profit is an ordinary multiple for an ordinary situation.
- **The price action offers nothing.** Down 11% from the high, up 24% from the low, flat on the year. There is no dislocation to lean on.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**There is no entry. Size zero. No order is staged and none should be.**

The courts set reopen conditions instead, and these are used verbatim:

- **NONE. Re-court needs ALL THREE:**
  1. the 9/28 print with pass **UNITS** flat-to-up through late September;
  2. a dividend reset to **≤$4.50** (frees ~$155M/yr to deleveraging — a **POSITIVE** catalyst);
  3. a marked private-market comp for the resort portfolio.
- **Entry zone if reached: EV/EBITDA <8x on NORMAL snow ⇒ $105–115.**

Reading those in plain terms:

- **$105–115 is the price band, not the trigger.** It comes from putting the enterprise value at under 8x a normal-snow profit of about $850M — roughly $6.8B of enterprise value, which after subtracting $2.635B of net debt and the $364.4M minority leaves about $105–115 per share. That is 22–28% below today. A drop into that band **on its own does not buy anything** — the three conditions above are also required. Price alone getting there most likely means the payout broke, which is the scenario the band is meant to survive, not chase.
- **Units, not dollars, on the pass number.** Dollars can be manufactured with price increases — they already were, at +5.6% per pass. The decisive number on 9/28 is unit count.
- **A dividend cut is a buy signal here, not a sell signal.** Cutting the annual payment from $8.88 to $4.50 or less redirects roughly $155M a year to paying down debt and removes the thing that is actually killing the case.
- **The asset mark is the missing homework.** Until someone prices those Forest Service permit franchises against a real transaction, there is no floor under this equity that we can defend.

**Catalyst dates:**

- **2026-09-28 — fourth-quarter and full-year results.** Unconfirmed by a company press release; corroborated two ways (a data vendor's calendar, and last year's analogue where the equivalent 8-K was filed 2025-09-29). This is roughly 28 trading sessions out — not near-term. Pre-print position: flat. It carries pass sales through late September, which is the single decisive number on the only leg where a disagreement could exist.
- **Early December 2026 (approx. 2026-12-08, unconfirmed) — final pass-selling-season results and first-quarter figures.** The spring −10% unit read was a partial season; this is the complete one.
- **Dividend declaration**, historically alongside the results release — the condition-two trigger.

**Trims/exits:** not applicable. We own nothing. If the price falls into $105–115 without the other two conditions, that is not an entry; that is confirmation of the thesis we are declining to fight.

## What remains unverified

- **The snow reversion rate — the single biggest hole.** How often does a season at 55% of median snow revert to normal the following year, and by how much? The connector that would answer it returned nothing five times because it only recognised the ticker while the benches were sending resort names. It has been fixed and verified since, but **the court decided without it.** Read the verdict knowing that.
- **What the resorts are worth to a private buyer.** Forty-two resorts, thousands of acres of permit rights, never marked against a transaction. Both benches flagged this and neither closed it. It is the one place a non-consensus number could come from.
- **Our own forecast for next year.** We do not have one. We abstained rather than manufacture a number, which is honest but means the reject is "no edge," not "we know something."
- **Whether the pass number gets worse or better.** The −10% unit figure runs only to 26 May 2026 and the selling season continues into early December. Our red bench treated a partial read as final; the full number is still ahead.
- **Live broker pricing.** The IBKR snapshot was permission-denied for both benches. Price came from the evidence pack plus one other venue; they match, but the broker leg is unconfirmed.
- **The next earnings date.** 2026-09-28 is derived from a data vendor and corroborated by last year's pattern; no company announcement names it.
- **Cash on hand.** The two benches quoted $371.4M and $387.3M. Immaterial to the verdict, unreconciled.
- **The short position's size and composition.** 16.38% of shares outstanding was taken from a screen and not independently verified.
- **Related-party transactions.** The current proxy's related-party note was never pulled.
- **The board appointment's meaning.** Adding MGM's CEO may signal a loyalty or hospitality monetisation push, or may be an ordinary governance addition. Speculation either way.
- **Whether the thermal readings mean anything.** The shrinking heat anomaly on the mountains mildly supports "unlucky year" over "permanent warming," but cleared trails versus surrounding forest is a known distortion. Treat as suggestive only.
- **How quickly the lost passholders show up in on-mountain spending.** The customers who left were low-frequency (days per pass actually rose 2.2%), so the profit effect should track the −5% dollars rather than the −10% units — but that is an inference, not a measurement.
