# PITCH — 058850.KS — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED  — OWNABLE** (conviction 8.0/10; the adjudication outranks both benches, and where it contradicts them, it wins)

KTcs Corp (KOSPI 058850) — Korean call-center and directory-services operator, majority-dependent on KT Corp. Spot ₩2,190 (21 Aug 2026). Market cap ₩83.1B (roughly US$58–60M — we never pulled a live won/dollar cross, so treat the dollar figure as approximate).

**Provenance warning that applies to every number below:** the machine-built evidence pack came back empty on entity, filings, and audited XBRL — correctly, because this is a Korean issuer with no US SEC presence. That means essentially every financial figure in this deck comes from an aggregator's rendering of the filings (stockanalysis.com), cross-checked against Korean press, **not from the original filings themselves.** Neither bench opened the actual half-year report. That is the single largest weakness of this deck and we say it up front rather than in a footnote.

---

## What we believe

We believe you can buy this company for less than the cash sitting in it, and that the people running it have started returning capital in a way that is unlikely to stop.

Concretely: at ₩2,190 a share you are paying less than the ₩2,586 per share of cash and short-term investments on the balance sheet, and about one-third of the ₩5,985 per share of stated book value. The company owes no interest-bearing money to anyone. Its net working capital — current assets minus current liabilities, so cash after every near-term bill is paid — is ₩121.6B against a ₩83.1B market value. It pays a ₩100 dividend, a 4.6% yield, out of only 14.5% of its earnings, so the dividend is not stretched and there is a great deal of room to raise it.

In March 2026 the company cancelled 1,280,550 of its own shares (about 3.1% of the outstanding count), and it still holds another 3,465,721 in treasury. A Korean law change that took effect on 6 March 2026 requires legacy treasury stock to be either cancelled or covered by a shareholder-approved retention plan by roughly 6 September 2027. So there is a dated, legally-driven decision point on 9.1% of the share count.

What we do **not** believe is that this is a growth story or a turnaround. Revenue has fallen in three consecutive reporting periods. We believe this is a cheap, cash-heavy, shrinking business where the downside is unusually well-covered by assets and the upside comes from capital returns rather than growth.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a captive supplier being slowly automated out of existence by its own customer.

That is a coherent view and it is largely correct on the facts. KTcs exists to answer phones for KT — directory service, KT contact centers, KT product retail. KT is simultaneously rolling out AI contact-center technology that does exactly what KTcs's ten thousand or so human agents do. When that technology works, KT keeps the savings and KTcs loses the volume. The market has priced this: the stock is down about 21% over a year, sits 31.5% below its 52-week high, and made a new low into the single most popular theme in Korean equities (the government's "value-up" corporate-governance push). Investors have had every chance to bid this name on cheapness and governance reform. They have sold it instead.

The sibling company KTis (058860) trades at a comparable discount and is down 12.4% over the same year. So the market is not singling out KTcs — it is discounting the whole structure of "listed captive of KT."

## Why we might have edge

Three reasons, in descending order of confidence.

**First, the balance sheet is being misread as levered when it is not.** The company's ₩53.2B of "total debt" on aggregator screens is entirely lease obligations — capitalized rent on the leased call-center floor space, created by an accounting rule (IFRS 16) rather than by borrowing. There is no short-term debt line at all. A screen that treats that as borrowing produces a positive enterprise value of ₩38.2B and the stock looks ordinary. Strip the leases and enterprise value is **negative ₩15B** — the market is valuing the operating business at less than nothing. Our own red bench made exactly this mistake, which is decent evidence that a machine screen makes it too.

**Second, the legal clock on the treasury stock is dated and public and almost nobody is watching a $58M Korean small cap.** The company already cited the amended Commercial Act §343(1) in its own March cancellation disclosure. There is a real forcing event with a real deadline.

**Third, the shrinkage may be better than it looks.** Revenue fell ₩78.6B from FY24 to FY25, but operating income rose from ₩7.0B to ₩29.8B. Even after adjusting FY24 upward for a one-time ₩20.2B receivable write-off (the TMON/WeMakePrice insolvency), operating profit was flat to slightly up on 7% less revenue. That pattern is what it looks like when a company sheds near-zero-margin work — in this case KT product distribution — rather than losing profitable business. We flag this as *plausible*, not confirmed: we could not read the segment note that would prove it.

## Why it might be priced in

We think a fair-minded reader should weight these heavily.

The automation threat is real, dated, and accelerating, and we have no instrument on it. The second half of 2025 ran an operating margin of about 1.85% — the business is barely profitable outside its seasonally stronger first half. The most recent quarter's operating profit fell 24.7% year-over-year. Revenue has declined three periods running.

The "AI transition is working" argument that the original deck rested on does not survive. First-half 2026 margin was 4.25%; first-half 2025 was 4.03%. Roughly 4% is simply what the first half of the year looks like at this company. There is no transition evidenced in that number — and, to be symmetric, none refuted either.

The dividend has been frozen at ₩100 for three years and was *cut* from ₩120 in FY2022. A 14.5% payout ratio can be read as dry powder or as an owner with no intention of paying more; we cannot distinguish those from outside.

And the size is a genuine constraint, not a footnote. At ₩83B of market value this desk can never own enough of it to matter. That caps the position, and it should cap the amount of further work we do on it.

1. **Pull the resting ₩2,080 bid.** Both benches independently reached this and we adopt it. ## What remains unverified

Stated plainly, because the list is long and some of it is load-bearing.

**Nobody read the filings.** Not the half-year report, not the annual report, not the related-party note. Every financial figure in this deck — revenue, operating income, cash, leases, book value, share count as of each period — is an aggregator's rendering, cross-checked against Korean press. That is adequate for a first pass and inadequate for a real position. The exchange's disclosure viewer is one click away and neither bench opened it.

**KT's share of revenue is unknown.** This is the number that decides whether the automation thesis is a five-year problem or a two-year problem, and we do not have it.

**Whether the FY25 revenue decline was margin-accretive mix or the start of a melt is unproven.** The operating-income series is consistent with the benign reading. It is also consistent with cost-cutting into a decline. The segment note settles it; we do not have the segment note.

**We have no idea what anyone else expects.** No analyst count, no estimates, no price targets. The coverage tool does not reach this market.

**Average daily volume is unmeasured**, so the position cap is a guess informed by market capitalization rather than by tradeable liquidity.

**No live currency rate was pulled** by either bench at any point. All dollar figures in this deck are approximate.

**The merger question is genuinely open, not settled.** Blue showed that a share-for-share exchange between two similarly-discounted listed companies preserves the discount rather than realizing it, and that red's fatal tag rested on a single 2022 press speculation. That is a good rebuttal. It is not proof that no cash transaction will ever be proposed. We have set a sensor and a sell rule rather than pretending to know.

**The discovery/crowding read is contaminated.** The tool was run with KT Corporation passed as the parent, and it returned an institutional score of 0.85 alongside zero attention and unknown coverage — that combination is the parent's profile bleeding onto the subsidiary. KT is a crowded stock. KTcs is a US$58M orphan. We are treating the crowding signal as unusable rather than as evidence either way.

**Finally, on the deck we inherited.** Four of its numbers were wrong and all four erred in the bullish direction: the dividend yield, the cash balance, KT's ownership stake, and the inference drawn from the first-half margin. The original document is void and none of its reasoning is carried forward here. What survives is what the two benches rebuilt from filed series — which happens to be a better case than the deck made, but it is a different case, reached a different way. The pattern of errors in the source deck is a reason to hold the position small independent of anything about the company.
