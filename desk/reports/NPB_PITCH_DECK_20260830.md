# PITCH — NPB — VERDICT

ADJUDICATED  — STARTER-small, no raise: hold the existing 290 shares of Northpointe Bancshares at ~$17.11 as a fairly-priced compounder (0.98x book / 1.02x tangible book against a 14.7% return on tangible equity, near-zero charge-offs, and 7.7% uninsured deposits); the slot raise is rejected by both benches and the court closed at 6/10 conviction, which leaves the name RED PENDING until the Q3 print.

---

## What we believe

Northpointe is a Michigan bank that mostly does one thing: it buys residential mortgages from other originators, warehouses them, and sells most of them on. It came public in February 2025 and the stock has gone essentially nowhere since, while earnings per share grew about 21% in the first half of 2026. That combination — flat price, rising earnings — means the market is paying a *lower* multiple for each dollar of profit than it did a year ago.

The business is genuinely good on the measures that usually matter for a bank: it earns roughly 14.7% on tangible equity, its net charge-offs (loans actually written off) ran 0.03% annualized, and it funds its own growth out of retained profits rather than by issuing stock. Tangible book value per share is compounding at roughly 14% a year. At about 1.0x tangible book, that compounding *is* the return — we do not need the market to re-rate the stock to make money, we need the bank to keep earning.

We also believe the low multiple is *partly deserved*, and we say so plainly. The bank has almost no free deposits (5.3% of deposits pay no interest), so it buys its funding at close to full market rates. A bank that rents its funding earns a thin spread — net interest margin is 2.33% — and thin-spread banks are correctly worth less than franchise banks. So the honest case is not "this is cheap and will re-rate." It is: *this is fairly priced, it compounds at ~14%, and we get paid the compounding at a multiple that probably stays where it is.*

One thing the court corrected in both benches: the common assumption that Fed rate cuts help this bank is backwards in the near term. Because the mortgage assets it holds float, the margin gets squeezed *first* when short rates fall — margin already fell 9 basis points as SOFR came down. The rate-cut benefit, when it comes, arrives through *gain on sale* (more mortgage volume, better sale spreads), which is a later and less certain lever. Assuming rates stay flat, the run-rate is roughly $2.40 of earnings per share.

## What the market believes

The market believes this is a wholesale mortgage lender with a bank charter, and prices it accordingly at about one times book. The specific worries embedded in that price:

- **Thin, bought funding.** Only 5.3% of deposits are free; interest expense eats 60% of interest income. This is well understood — it's in the published "margin strain" coverage.
- **Cyclical earnings.** 59% of pre-tax profit came from gain on selling mortgages, the most cycle-sensitive line in banking. Second-quarter results missed the (thin) consensus on both earnings ($0.60 vs $0.68) and revenue ($64.3M vs $66.5M).
- **Competitive pricing.** The company itself said mortgage-purchase spreads tightened; it is buying volume by paying up.

The market does *not* appear to have an active opinion on the name at all. Analyst coverage is one or two estimates; institutional ownership is around 15%; our own attention measure reads 0.32 out of 1. This is an under-followed stock, not a crowded one. Nobody is fighting over it.

## Why we might have edge

Three candidates, honestly ranked:

1. **Weakest but real: the multiple-compression observation.** Earnings up ~21% in the first half, stock flat since the IPO. If the bank simply keeps earning $2.30–$2.40 and keeps growing tangible book at low-double-digits, the compounding accrues to us at an unchanged multiple. That is not an insight; it's patience, and patience is only edge if the earnings hold.
2. **The funding tail is smaller than it looks.** The blue bench found — correctly, and neither the pack nor the red bench had it — that 52% of deposits are brokered certificates of deposit, and called that "hot money" that could run. The court checked the next layer and found the run cannot happen the way 2023 happened: uninsured deposits are 7.7% of the total, and term CDs contractually cannot be withdrawn on a panic. The 2023 bank-run vector is *absent* here. That is a real, checkable, non-obvious correction — and it is the single most load-bearing thing this court produced.
3. **The insider is buying with size.** The CEO bought 78,312 shares in the open market on August 25–26 at about $16.95 — roughly $1.33 million of his own money, four days before our tape print and 1% below it — taking his stake to 9.3%. ([Form 4, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336706/000205756726000006/wk-form4_1787780658.xml)) That is not proof of anything, but it is the most probative single fact in the file and both benches agreed on it.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is. At $17.11 the stock trades at 0.98x book and 1.02x tangible book, about 7.4x trailing earnings, sitting 12.9% below its 52-week high and 11.3% above its low — the middle of its own range. There is no dislocation to harvest.

More specifically:

- **The thin margin and the cyclical gain-on-sale are consensus.** The company discloses both; the trade press writes about both. We are not first to either.
- **The 1.0x multiple may be exactly right.** A bank funded at close to full market rates should not trade like a franchise bank. Our own case concedes this — we are betting on earnings compounding at a *held* multiple, not on a re-rating.
- **We have no fair value materially above the price.** The blue bench's computed range was $13.60–$21.90 with a mid of about $18. Against a $17.11 tape, that is a ~5% gap — inside the noise of the assumptions that produced it.
- **The under-followed status cuts both ways.** Nobody is looking, which is why the compounding could be free — but it is also why the multiple may never move, and a multiple that never moves means our entire return depends on execution we cannot influence.

Both benches, working independently and disagreeing about nearly everything else, reached the same disposition: no raise. That convergence is itself evidence there is no priced-out gap here.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The compounder simply stops compounding (most likely).** Mortgage-purchase spreads keep tightening, gain on sale keeps shrinking (it fell 11.9% year-over-year already), and tangible book growth decays from 14% toward 8%. The multiple stays at 1.0x, correctly, and we own a fairly-priced bank that no longer earns its way. We lose nothing dramatic; we lose four years of opportunity cost. *Tripwire: monitorable.* Gain-on-sale dollars, mortgage-purchase-program yield, and tangible book per share, every quarter.

**Failure narrative 2 — The funding cost ratchets up under us.** Net interest margin is 2.33% with essentially no cushion. Roughly $2.74 billion of brokered CDs reprice on a schedule. A 20 basis point widening in what the bank must pay costs about $10.5 million a year pre-tax — roughly 9% of annualized pre-tax profit; 50 basis points costs about 22%. Levered 12x, that is the difference between a 14.7% return on tangible equity and a 10% one, which would re-rate the stock *down* to 0.8x book. *Tripwire: monitorable.* Interest expense over average deposits each quarter; the brokered share on the company's own earnings slides.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: something breaks inside the counterparty book.** Northpointe does not originate most of these mortgages; it buys them from correspondent originators. The loss vector is therefore not "housing goes down" — it's one correspondent committing fraud, or an agency issuing a wave of repurchase demands, or a concentration in a handful of sellers we cannot see. Between quarterly filings there is *no public series* that discloses which correspondents, at what concentration, with what repurchase-reserve trend. We checked for related-party transactions (whether any counterparty or deposit source is affiliated with the CEO) and the check came back **unavailable** — we could not read the proxy's related-party table this session. We also could not read the FDIC call reports that would corroborate the loan and non-accrual figures at the bank level, and the short-interest connector errored, so we cannot see whether anyone else is positioned against this.

> **Unknown-unknown class: off-disclosure counterparty concentration.** Risk that is real, dated, and structurally invisible between prints — it does not degrade gradually into a metric we watch; it arrives already complete, as an 8-K. Every monitoring system we have is built to catch trends. This class has no trend.

**The reflexive and structural leg — what *we* do to ourselves.**

Our position is 290 shares, about $4,960. Average daily volume is unmeasured this session (the broker connector was permission-denied), but on a roughly $600M-market-cap NYSE listing, a $5,000 order is a rounding error. **Market impact is not our risk. Our process is.**

- **This position exists by default, not by decision.** Both benches explicitly rejected the raise; the name is on the book because "hold a minimum starter unless there's a named kill" is the default. That default is defensible once. Repeated across 30 names it builds a portfolio nobody argued for.
- **The resting bid was accidental short volatility.** The order that filled this — BUY 290 @ $17.00 — was a resting bid placed under the bands-rest-by-default rule and dated retroactively to 2026-08-05. A passive bid on a fairly-valued name with no catalyst only fills when something moves against us. That is exactly the pattern our own doctrine warns about, and it is how we got here.
- **Our reopen band is where our own predicted bad news lands.** The proposed add level, $15.00, sits essentially on the 52-week low of $15.378. If the stock trades there it will be *because of* something — most plausibly the Q3 print. Bidding into flow we ourselves predict is backwards. The $15.00 band below is therefore conditioned on *why*, not just on price.
- **Concentrated doctrine risk.** The court granted this name the "fairly-paid risk" label specifically by overturning the blue bench's funding-tail objection, using the 7.7% uninsured figure. If that single read is wrong — if a meaningful share of those brokered CDs is uninsured or callable — then the label is wrong, and the label is what permits sizing up later. One number is carrying the classification.
- **Tax clock.** The lot was acquired 2026-08-05; it is short-term until August 2027. At a $0.105 per share gain the tax consequence is immaterial today, but any trim before August 2027 is taxed at ordinary rates.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that mortgage-purchase spreads keep compressing and funding costs keep ratcheting until tangible book stops compounding at 14% — and we discover that the 1.0x multiple was never cheap, just accurate.
