## What we believe

Bilibili is a real, cash-generating business trading at a price that only makes sense if you assume its cash is worthless.

The arithmetic, using the benches' own numbers and audited company figures:

- Share count after the 2026-09-04 deal: 422.5m − 20.39m repurchased = **402.1m**. At the pack tape of **$15.23**, market cap = **$6.12bn**.
- Cash after the deal: $3.58bn on hand + $0.70bn raised − ~$0.30bn spent on buybacks = **$3.98bn**. Debt: **$1.39bn** (old 2030 notes + new 2031 notes).
- **Enterprise value = $3.53bn.**
- Audited FY2025 cash from operations was **RMB7.15bn (US$1,022m)**, up 19% from RMB6.01bn. Take out ~RMB1.17bn of stock comp and ~RMB1.5bn of capex and you get roughly **RMB4.5bn ≈ US$640m** of owner cash.

That is **5.5x**. Even if you write down a quarter of the cash for the cost of getting it out of China, it's **7.1x**. Our house threshold for advancing a China-ADR operator is under 8x. It clears — and it only fails if you give the cash **zero** credit, which prices it at 11.7x.

So the belief is narrow and honest: *we are not buying a growth story, we are buying a cash-conversion story at a low multiple, and the single thing that decides whether we are right is how much of that cash actually sits outside mainland China.* We do not know that number yet.

## What the market believes

The market believes the cash doesn't count and the growth is over.

- The stock is at **$15.23 — half a percent off its 52-week low of $15.16**, down **58%** from the 52-week high of $36.40 and down **43%** from the 26-week high of $26.76.
- Total revenue grew **8%** last quarter (RMB7.94bn) while advertising grew **28%** — which means everything that isn't advertising is flat or shrinking. The market is extrapolating the 8%, not the 28%.
- The most informed holder just sold. Tencent placed ~26.37m Class Z shares at HK$115.38 (~US$400m, proceeds to Tencent, not the company) and moved US$200m of that into the company's *debt* instead. Tencent swapped an equity claim for a senior claim.
- The Street's forward revenue number (~+10.6%) sits above the printed run-rate. We agree with the Street on advertising and disagree only on the cash discount. **That is not an information edge; it is a taste difference.** Our red bench said so directly and we are not going to dress it up.

## Why we might have edge

Three specific things, all mechanical rather than visionary:

1. **The EV was mis-built by a professional, in this very court.** Our own red bench put post-deal EV at $4.5–4.8bn by forgetting to add the $700m raised and forgetting to retire the 20.39m shares bought back. That is a ~28% overstatement, and it alone flipped the multiple from 5.5x to failing. If our own adversarial bench made that error under instruction, sell-side screens running the same convert-plus-buyback structure will make it too. The mispricing has a named mechanism: **convert-plus-buyback deals break naive EV screens.**
2. **The "trapped cash" story is being told without checking the put calendar.** The obvious read — cash-rich company borrows offshore, therefore the cash can't get out — ignores that holders of the existing 2030 Notes (RMB4,939.4m, ~US$690m) can force a cash repurchase at par on **June 1, 2028**. Pre-funding a known put 21 months out is an ordinary treasury act, not a confession. The company's own 20-F shows the VIEs repaid RMB0.7bn / RMB1.7bn / RMB1.8bn (US$263.9m) upstream to offshore subsidiaries and WFOEs in 2023/24/25 — money does move, via intercompany loans, not only dividends ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1723690/000119312526157842/d91984d20f.htm)).
3. **The cash-flow number is audited and public, and one bench declared it uncomputable.** The XBRL feed in our evidence pack came back blank for revenue, stock comp, share count and operating cash flow. Red concluded the test couldn't be run. The number was sitting in the company's own FY2025 results release. Anyone whose pipeline depends on the structured feed gets the same false blank.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetrically, here is the case that we have no edge at all:

- **The tape has already voted with the deal in hand.** The converts, the AI-capex use of proceeds and the Tencent exit are all public, and the stock is at its 52-week low. That is a market that looked at the same facts and marked them down.
- **The cheap multiple is manufactured by the cash we can't verify.** Every dollar of the $3.53bn EV depends on netting $3.98bn of cash that is, by disclosure, sitting inside PRC/VIE entities. Nobody in either court has seen an offshore/onshore split. If the honest haircut is 100%, the stock is at 11.7x and there is no trade.
- **The block price is not a valuation mark and we will not pretend it is.** The HK$115.38 print was the "Reference Price" of a concurrent delta placement — the mechanism that sources the convert buyers' short leg. The buyers are short by construction; the company itself was allocated 6,795,540 of those shares. Citing that price as market validation is circular. Both benches agreed. That support leg is dead.
- **We agree with consensus on the operating business.** Our disagreement is entirely a balance-sheet-discount disagreement, and there is no scheduled event that forces the market to resolve it in our favor.
- **Roughly $700m of convertible paper is now hedged against this stock.** Convert arbitrage desks buy dips and sell rallies. That mechanically damps exactly the re-rate we'd be paid for.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The cash was never ours to net (tripwire exists).**
The 20-F or the closed-deal 6-K discloses offshore, non-PRC cash well below US$1.5bn — say $600–900m — and the market correctly prices the rest at a heavy discount. The multiple we are underwriting quietly moves from 5.5x toward 11.7x and the stock has no support until the zero-cash-credit fair value of about **$9.28**. *Monitorable:* any 6-K/20-F line splitting offshore vs. onshore cash; any new offshore raise before June 2028 (a second raise says the first one wasn't enough).

**Failure narrative 2 — The owner cash is smaller than the cash-flow statement suggests (tripwire exists).**
Bilibili's capital spending is dominated by content and licensing, not buildings: amortization of intangibles was RMB1,772.4m in 2024 against only RMB554.3m of PP&E depreciation. If licensed-content purchases belong in the capex line, the RMB4.5bn of owner cash compresses toward RMB3.4bn, and the 8x fair value drops from $16.70 to about **$13.52** — below today's price. Neither bench raised this until the blue bench's own new finding; it is the best live kill route against our own thesis. *Monitorable:* the FY2026 investing-activities split.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE. Jurisdictional regime change.**
A change in how Beijing or Washington treats this structure — VIE enforceability, minor-protection or gaming rules, capital-account tightening at SAFE, an audit-inspection re-escalation, an index or sanctions action — arrives without a filing precursor, because filings are the *output* of policy, not a leading indicator of it. There is no dataset we can arm a sensor against; by the time it is in an 8-K equivalent, the price has already moved.
**Unknown-unknown class: extra-jurisdictional policy shock — unmonitorable-by-filing.** Two sibling classes also sit here with no sensor: (a) **informed-holder asymmetry** — Tencent has board-adjacent information we do not, and its decision to move from equity to credit is the only trace we get; (b) **audit/related-party integrity risk** in a VIE chain, which by definition surfaces as a surprise or not at all. The only defense against this class is position size, not detection.

**The reflexive / structural leg.**
Our own footprint is irrelevant: a $5–10k starter against an estimated $180–300m of daily dollar volume is roughly 0.005% of a day — we cannot move this and we cannot get trapped in it. *We do not have ADV in the evidence pack; that range is an estimate and is flagged below.* The flow that matters is not ours:

- The $700m convert implies roughly $350–420m of hedge short — and the ~26.37m-share placement at ~US$14.79 covers essentially all of it. The supply is **pre-placed**, which is good (no borrow squeeze, no drip) and bad (the natural sponsor of a rally has already been paid).
- Those hedged books are long gamma. They **sell into strength and buy weakness**. Our upside path is structurally damped and our downside path is structurally cushioned — which is another way of saying this name may simply sit near $15 for two quarters.
- Doctrine interactions: the no-entry-near-print rule does not bind (the next print is ~45 trading days out and is *unconfirmed*). The lockup calendar sensor **fired partially** — the Tencent secondary plus the company repurchase is an insider-overhang event with no lockup gate, and the residual Tencent stake and any new placement lockup are unknown, so there is a dated overhang we cannot date. The FATAL-tag discipline saved us from treating a dual-class share-count artifact as a dilution event. The base-effect precedent (attribution of a pre-event decline to the event) was invoked against our red bench and sustained.
- Honest reflexive tail: if the offshore-cash disclosure is bad, everyone holding this for the same net-cash reason exits through the same door on the same day, and the long-gamma cushion inverts once dealers' strikes are far away.

*If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be* **that the cash we netted against the market cap was never ours to net — either because moving it offshore costs far more than a friction parameter, or because it was already spoken for by the June 2028 par put — so the 5.5x we underwrote was really 11.7x, and no filing told us in time.**

**The output of that arithmetic:**
- **Do not buy calls.** At any plausible chain IV for this name, upside options cost 4–9x what our own scenario table says they are worth. The gated adds cannot be expressed in calls.
- **The $14 put is the only instrument where the chain plausibly pays us more than our table.** Break-even is **37.5% IV** — above that, writing it is positive expectancy on our own numbers; below it, it isn't.
- **σ_table (31% annualized) vs. IV × √T:** our table is materially tighter than this stock's likely implied vol. Two honest readings: either implied vol is rich (route to the premium-selling gate) or our tails are too thin (scenario 5 at 10% may be underweighted for a name that has already fallen 58%). We flag both rather than picking the convenient one.
- **VRP (IV − RV): NOT COMPUTED — the pack contains no realized-vol series and no chain.** Estimated RV in the **50–65%** range from the 52-week range and the post-deal move; IV percentile unknown, and post-event IV typically deflates once a deal prices. **This must be pulled before any option is written.**
- **Use chain-level midpoint IV for the actual strike, never the underlying's aggregate IV feed.** They diverge badly — the case where an aggregate feed printed 101 against a true chain midpoint of 71 is exactly how a "rich IV" conclusion becomes a losing short. Everything above is a *rule*, not a filled ticket, until real quotes exist.
- **Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated on a single binary (the offshore cash disclosure), and outcomes 1 and 5 sit $9 apart. If that fork resolves before December expiry, a single-strike short put is a bad shape; if it resolves in April 2027 — the likelier case — the December window is the quiet part and premium selling fits.

**Tax overlay.** December 18, 2026 is deliberate: it is the last expiry whose outcome lands in tax year 2026. Any January or March 2027 expiry pushes the loss branch into next year and is excluded during a harvest year unless the edge is numerically overwhelming — and here it is not, since EV ≈ spot. One caveat stated plainly: **a cash-secured put does not create a 2026 loss when it goes wrong** — assignment converts it into stock at a $12.93 basis, and no loss is realized until that stock is sold. If this is a harvest year, the put route defers the very loss it looks like it books. And if we hold the starter and sell it at a loss inside 30 days of writing the put, the wash-sale rule disallows it.

**Instrument recommendation (the output, not a preference): STOCK, starter size only. No options today.** The call math rules calls out. The put math is attractive only above 37.5% chain IV, which we cannot confirm from the pack, and its loss branch doesn't land in the intended tax year anyway. **Re-run this section the moment a real chain is pulled** — if the December $14 put shows a midpoint above **$0.60** with chain IV above 45%, the arithmetic flips to writing it against the Add-1 level, and that is how the first add gets funded.
