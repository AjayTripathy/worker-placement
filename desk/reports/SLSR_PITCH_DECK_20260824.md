# PITCH — SLSR — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position today; a pre-registered, event-gated ladder that only buys after the financing condition is confirmed received.

---

## What we believe

Solaris Resources owns one very large, undeveloped copper project (Warintza) in Morona-Santiago, Ecuador. The company's own pre-feasibility study puts the after-tax value of the built mine at about **US$4.6 billion** at a $4.50/lb copper assumption, against **$3.7 billion** of upfront construction cost. Copper today is about **$6.59/lb** — roughly 46% above the study's assumption.

The whole company is worth about **$1.44 billion** on the market. So the market is paying roughly 31 cents for every dollar of study-price value, and about 15 cents for every dollar of spot-price value.

We believe three things:

1. **The prize is real and large.** Even if you assume copper falls to $3.00/lb — a depression price — a *fully financed* Warintza is worth roughly $8.02/share, essentially today's price of $8.64. At $3.50/lb it's $14.66. At today's copper it's $55.72. The gap between price and value is not an accounting artifact; it is a probability. (Numbers are our blue bench's corrected table, using Ecuador's marginal government take of about 39.5% — a royalty, a labour profit share, and corporate tax.)

2. **The discount exists for a defensible reason, and that reason is dated.** The company is not financed. It carries a going-concern warning and says in its own filings that it needs the third $50 million payment from Royal Gold to fund the next twelve months. Royal Gold says it expects to pay in **Q3 or Q4 2026**. That is a specific, near-term, observable event. When it lands, the going-concern language should come off the next set of accounts, and the single loudest bear talking point disappears.

3. **The right way to own this is to wait for that event and then pay up, not to buy the uncertainty cheap.** The stock is thin (about $1.16M/day on the US line), but not so thin that we can't build a starter position in the days *after* good news. We do not need to pre-position. Buying now means paying for a foreign government registry filing we cannot see, cannot influence, and cannot track.

**Our proposed position today is therefore zero, with a written trigger list.**

## What the market believes

The market is not confused about this name; it is divided about one number — the probability the mine gets built.

- Seven to eleven sell-side analysts cover it. Their price targets are **$14.58, $16.00, and $21.10** depending on which aggregator you use — a spread of roughly ±19% around the midpoint. That is coverage, not consensus.
- Every published target sits *below* the value of a financed mine at the study's own copper price ($27.96/share). So the street is applying a haircut of 25–48% for financing, permitting, and time.
- The market also already knows about the going-concern warning. The same paragraph appears in this quarter's accounts, last quarter's accounts, and last year's annual statements. The stock traded at **$11.48** with that language on file. It is a disclosed, chronic condition, not new information.
- The two structural worries — the $3.7 billion construction bill with no announced funding plan, and the unstarted Indigenous consultation process — are the published bear case, discussed openly in research.

So: the market believes this is a real orebody with a genuine chance of being built, discounted heavily and appropriately for the fact that nobody has yet solved the money or the consent.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly, the edge here is narrow and we should say so.

**The one real candidate: the price deck is stale.** Street targets of $14.58–$21.10 sit at 0.52–0.75× the value of a financed mine *at the study's $4.50/lb copper*. But copper is $6.59. Either the analysts are marking a ~60% chance of being built at a stale, low copper price, or they are marking spot copper with a ~30% chance. Those are very different worlds and they are not being reconciled in public. If copper stays anywhere near current levels through a financing event, the copper-price update and the probability update arrive at the same time, and the same targets have to move twice.

**The second candidate: event timing.** We have read both the company's filing and the counterparty's filing. Royal Gold — the party who has to pay — has told its own shareholders in a US quarterly filing that it anticipates paying in Q3 or Q4 2026. That is a more specific commitment than anything Solaris has published. Most retail and much of the generalist market will not have cross-read the payer's filing. That does not give us a price edge today, but it does give us a *calendar* edge: we know roughly when to be watching, and what document to watch.

**What is emphatically not edge:** we do not have a differentiated view on copper, on Ecuadorian politics, on the orebody, or on construction cost. We agree with the street's direction and are more conservative than the street's level.

## Why it might be priced in

Most of it is.

- **The discount is normal.** Unpermitted, unfunded, single-asset copper developers routinely trade at 20–40% of net asset value. Solaris is at 31% — squarely mid-band. There is no anomaly to close unless something de-risks.
- **The going-concern warning is old news.** It has been on file across at least three reporting periods and it was in the newswire headline of the very filing at issue. It cannot explain the 24.7% decline from the 52-week high, because the same words were on file at the high.
- **The bear case is public.** Both structural risks — capex and consultation — are in published research.
- **We have no number the street doesn't have.** Our value estimates are built from the company's own published study. We are not modelling anything proprietary.

The strongest version of "it's priced in" is this: the entire debate reduces to P(built), and P(built) is genuinely unknowable to us right now. A discount that reflects an unknowable is a fair price, not a mispricing.

## What could go wrong

*(This is the pre-mortem. Three failure narratives, then the reflexivity leg, then the required closing sentence.)*

**Failure 1 — The tranche arrives, and it turns out that was the good news.**
Royal Gold pays the $50 million. The going-concern paragraph comes off. We buy our starter on the confirmation, at a higher price. And then nothing else happens for eighteen months, because the actual binding problem was never the $50 million — it was the **$3.7 billion**. Fifty million dollars funds a feasibility study and a permitting effort; it does not build a mine. We will have bought the removal of a small obstacle and mistaken it for the removal of a large one, and we will hold a thin, illiquid, pre-revenue developer through a long empty stretch while copper decides our P&L for us.
*Tripwire: yes, and it's cheap.* Watch for a term sheet, a strategic partner, or a joint-venture announcement putting an actual number on Solaris's share of construction cost. If twelve months pass after the tranche with no such announcement, the thesis has not advanced and we should exit regardless of price.

**Failure 2 — Dilution eats the prize.**
There is no scenario where Solaris funds $3.7 billion without issuing a great deal of something. Every share count in our value table (167 million shares) is the *current* count. If the company ends up funding a 30% equity share of construction at, say, a $2 billion market cap, that is roughly a doubling of shares outstanding, and every per-share number in this deck halves. Our "prize table" is a table of *enterprise* value that we have naively divided by today's share count. That is a known, structural overstatement, and we are stating it plainly rather than burying it.
*Tripwire: yes.* Any prospectus supplement, base shelf renewal, or bought-deal announcement. We should re-run the entire valuation on the announced terms before adding a single share, and we should treat a follow-on as the base case, not the tail.

**Failure 3 — Consent never comes, and we never see it not coming. (No monitorable tripwire.)**
Warintza sits on territory where free, prior and informed consultation with Shuar communities is legally required. The company signed cooperation agreements with PSHA and FICSH in September 2025 and said the consultation process "was expected to commence in the coming months." **Eleven months later there is no published, dated commencement order from the Ministry of Energy and Mines.** Separately, the Royal Gold payment is conditioned on perfecting security over Ecuadorian mining concessions — an act performed by an Ecuadorian government registry, not by Royal Gold and not by Solaris. Royal Gold has described this as "underway" for over a year.

This is the failure we cannot instrument. Ecuadorian consultation and registry processes **do not produce dated public artifacts as they progress.** There is no docket to poll, no 8-K equivalent, no scheduled hearing. The process is silent right up until it produces a result — and the result can be a court injunction, a referendum, a community blockade, or a ministerial decision that arrives as a finished fact. Satellite imagery can show us clearing at the site, but pixels cannot tell us whether consent was obtained; that is precisely the question, and imagery is structurally incapable of answering it.

**Unknown-unknown class, named: *silent sovereign process* — a decisive dependency that runs inside a foreign institution with no public disclosure surface, where the absence of news is genuinely uninformative and the first observable is the outcome itself.** This is the same class as an unpublished regulatory review, a sealed foreign court proceeding, or an undisclosed sanctions determination. Our entire monitoring apparatus is built on filings and prints; against this class it is blind. We should assume we will learn about a bad outcome from the tape, not from a document.

The honest consequence: **any position in this name carries a fraction that is uninsurable and unhedgeable by our normal methods, and it must be sized as if it can go to zero without warning.** Note also that the equity is structurally last in line: the Royal Gold stream is secured by all-asset general security agreements plus pledges over the shares of the operating subsidiaries. If the project is not built, the secured party can take the asset base and common equity recovers nothing. The downside here is not "a bad quarter." It is zero.

**The reflexive and structural leg — our own footprint, predicted flow, and how our own rules interact.**

- **Our size is not the problem; the crowd's is.** The US line trades about 134,000 shares a day, roughly $1.16 million. A 0.25% starter is small enough against that to be a non-event for us. (Caveat we should state: this is the *US* line only — Solaris is dual-listed and the Toronto line is the primary market, so true liquidity is better than $1.16M/day, but we have not measured it. We are sizing against the worse number.)
- **We are, by design, buying into a crowd.** Our entire ladder is triggered by a positive press release. That means we plan to buy at the exact moment when every other holder of this name is also trying to buy, in a thin, retail-heavy stock where a de-risking headline could reprice it 20–40% in a session. This directly violates the spirit of our own rule about not bidding into predicted flow. We are accepting that violation knowingly, because the alternative — pre-positioning ahead of a sovereign registry act with no observable — is worse. But we must price it: **assume our fill on the trigger is materially above the last pre-news print, and budget for that in the entry cap.** If we cannot get filled inside the cap, we do not chase. Missing the trade is an acceptable outcome; paying an unbounded price is not.
- **The price gates in this deck must be alerts, not resting orders.** A resting good-till-cancelled buy under a stock with a live binary event is an accidental bet that nothing happens — we would get filled precisely and only in the scenario where the news is bad. Every level below is a *notification to think*, never a working order.
- **Falling-knife adverse selection.** Our "cheap enough to buy without a catalyst" level ($5.60) is very likely to be reached *because* the tranche failed or consultation went wrong. A price gate here is not a value trigger; it is an invitation to buy into information we don't have yet. It is written below with an explicit disqualifier for exactly this reason.
- **Doctrine interaction, stated plainly:** our house rule says a clean court with no named kill defaults to a minimum starter rather than flat. This court is *not* clean — there is a named, primary-sourced, dated kill (unfinanced, going-concern, secured against the whole asset base). So flat is legitimate here. But the counter-rule also applies: three of the five kills are things everybody already knows, and consensus kills bound *size*, not *upside*. A future adjudicator could reasonably convert this to a small starter today rather than zero. We are recommending zero, and we are flagging that the disagreement is real and reasonable.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought the small financing milestone as though it were the large one — paying up on a $50 million tranche receipt in a thin, crowded print — and then held a still-unfunded, still-unconsented single-asset developer while either a silent Ecuadorian consultation or permitting outcome, or the dilution required to raise $3.7 billion, took the per-share prize away from us.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**The courts say no entry today. Below are the reopen conditions.**

Why no entry: the binding uncertainty is a near-term, dated binary (the tranche) whose resolution we cannot observe in advance, in a name where the re-rate is capturable *after* the announcement because we are small relative to daily volume. We are not paid to guess a foreign registry filing.

**Reopen band 1 — the financing trigger (primary).**
- **Condition:** a company press release or 6-K confirming **RECEIPT** of the third $50 million Royal Gold tranche. The word must be *received*, *funded*, or *drawn* — **"underway," "anticipated," or "expected" does not trigger.**
- **Action:** buy **0.25% of book**.
- **Price cap: pay up to $13.00.** That is roughly 0.46× the financed value at the study's own copper price ($27.96), still inside the normal 0.2–0.4× developer band on a *de-risked* footing, and it explicitly budgets for a gap-up of up to ~50% from today's $8.64. If the print takes it above $13.00, **we do not chase** — the trade re-enters only on a pullback below the cap or on trigger 2.
- **Execution:** work the order over up to three sessions, no more than 10% of that day's volume, limit only. No market orders in a $1.16M/day line.

**Reopen band 2 — the consent trigger.**
- **Condition:** a **dated** commencement order for the free, prior and informed consultation process from Ecuador's Ministry of Energy and Mines, or an equivalent official act with a publication date. The September-2025 community cooperation agreements are a precondition, not the process, and do not count.
- **Action:** add a second **0.25% of book**, taking us to 0.5% total.
- **Price cap: pay up to $18.00** (about 0.64× study-price financed value).
- This trigger is independent of trigger 1 in principle, but if it fires *first* we take only 0.25% and still require the financing trigger for the second tranche.

**Reopen band 3 — the price-only trigger (deliberately hard to fire).**
- **Level: $5.60 or below.** That is 0.20× the study-price financed value — the bottom of the normal developer band — and roughly 30% below the depression-copper financed value of $8.02/share.
- **Action:** 0.25% starter.
- **Hard disqualifier:** this trigger is **void** if the decline is accompanied by, or follows within 20 sessions of, (a) any disclosure that the tranche will not be paid or the security cannot be perfected, (b) any adverse consultation, licensing, or judicial development in Ecuador, or (c) an announced equity financing. In those cases the price is the news, and buying it is buying the wrong side of information we don't have.
- **This is an alert, not a resting order.** Nothing rests in this name.

**Cancel / do-not-add conditions.**
- **2026-12-31:** if the third tranche has not been *received* by year-end, Royal Gold's own stated window ("third or fourth quarter of 2026") has lapsed. Cancel the entire ladder and re-open the file from scratch.
- Any prospectus supplement, base shelf renewal, or bought-deal announcement: **freeze all triggers** and re-run the per-share math on the announced terms before adding anything. Every per-share figure in this deck assumes 167.1 million shares.
- Twelve months after the tranche is received with **no** announced partner, joint venture, or term sheet putting a number on the $3.7 billion: exit any position regardless of price. The thesis will not have advanced.

**Trims and exits (only relevant if we get filled).**
- **First trim at $16.80** (~0.60× study-price financed value) — sell one-third. This is where street targets cluster and where an unfinanced developer stops being cheap on any conventional frame.
- **Second trim at $27.96** (1.0× study-price financed value) — sell half of the remainder. At full study-price value with construction not yet funded, the market is paying for outcomes we have no basis to underwrite.
- **Stop:** none on price. This is a binary-outcome position sized to survive going to zero. A price stop in a name whose failure mode is a gap does not protect us; correct sizing does.

**Sizing discipline.** Maximum 0.5% of book, in two 0.25% steps, both event-gated. **Size to total loss, never to the value table.** The value table describes what we win; the security package described in Note 8 describes what we lose, and what we lose is everything.

1. **Whether the study's $4.6 billion value is calculated before or after the Royal Gold stream.** If it is *before*, every row of our value table is overstated. Our own work suggests the stream costs only ~3% of value, so the practical impact is small — but the question itself is genuinely unresolved and we are asserting the answer doesn't matter rather than knowing it.
2. **Ecuador's marginal tax rate of 39.5%.** We built this from the royalty, labour profit share, and corporate tax rates. The study's own sensitivity table was not published in the press release, so we could not check our slope against the company's. This is the single input driving our entire copper-sensitivity table.
3. **The 2026-11-05 reporting date.** Derived from a data vendor. No company release names it. Treat as approximate.
4. **Every price in this deck.** IBKR quote unobtainable this session (permission denied). $8.64 is a vendor tape from 2026-08-24. Our two benches even disagreed slightly on copper spot ($6.58 vs $6.59) — immaterial, but symptomatic.
5. **Whether the security perfection in Ecuador is close to done.** "Underway" for over a year. We have no way to check a foreign registry's queue position. This is the crux of the near-term binary and we are blind to it.
6. **Consultation status.** No dated commencement order in eleven months. We do not know whether this is normal bureaucratic latency or a stalled process, and we have no source that would distinguish them.
7. **How the $3.7 billion gets funded.** No term sheet, no partner, no announced carried interest, no indication of how much of it Solaris would bear. Every per-share number here assumes today's 167.1 million shares survive contact with that financing. **They will not.**
8. **True liquidity.** We measured ~134,000 shares/day on the US line only. Toronto is the primary market and we did not measure it. Our liquidity assumptions are conservative but imprecise.
9. **Street target reconciliation.** Three sources give three analyst counts and three averages. We could not determine which reflects current coverage, so we treated the range as the estimate.
