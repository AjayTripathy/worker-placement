# PITCH — FIGR — PROPOSED VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Buy a half-size starter position in Figure Technology Solutions (FIGR) near $28.60, sized to survive a bad August 13 earnings report; no additions before that report; mandatory re-review of the whole case immediately after it.**

## What we believe
Figure runs a marketplace for home-equity loans, and its business is accelerating faster than Wall Street's numbers assume. First-quarter revenue of $167 million was up 92% year over year; July marketplace volume hit a record $1.70 billion, roughly double a year ago. Yet analyst forecasts of $718–730 million for full-year 2026 imply only about 40% growth over last year's $515 million — which mathematically requires revenue to *decline* in the second half while volume is doubling. The company has issued no full-year guidance, so those forecasts aren't anchored by management; we think they're simply stale. Critically, both figures are on the same accounting basis: the company's reported (GAAP) revenue and its "adjusted net revenue" differed by only 0.1% in Q1, so the growth-versus-forecast gap is real, not an accounting artifact. The July record is also organic — the Kiavi acquisition had not closed as of August 8, so it cannot be inflating the numbers ([company metrics dashboard](https://figure.com/investors/metrics)).

## What the market believes
The stock has fallen 63% from its 52-week high of $78 to $28.60, though it has already bounced 18.6% off its August 4 low of $24.11. The seven covering analysts are actually bullish — average price target $51.14, a Buy consensus — but their revenue forecasts embed the second-half slowdown described above. Short interest is modest at about 7.4% of float. The credit market is more skeptical: in July the company paid 8.5% on $600 million of five-year unsecured notes ([pricing announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/09/3325253/0/en/figure-technology-solutions-announces-pricing-of-private-offering-of-600-million-of-senior-notes.html)), a junk-grade borrowing cost — though the second court established these notes fund the Kiavi acquisition, not day-to-day operations.

## Why we might have edge
- The core mispricing claim survived a deliberate attempt to kill it, and came out stronger. The prosecution argued the revenue comparison mixed two accounting bases; primary-source checking showed the two bases are identical to within 0.1%, and the prosecution's own alternative model (revenue of ~$160M in Q2) would have required revenue to fall while volume rose 47% — it contradicted already-reported results.
- Forecasts implying a second-half revenue decline sit against confirmed +92% Q1 revenue growth and +100% July volume growth, with no company guidance to justify them.
- The July volume record is confirmed organic — the acquisition that could have padded it hadn't closed.
- The resolving event is dated and near: Q2 results on August 13, before the market opens (confirmed by company press release of July 15).

## Why it might be priced in
- Part of the payoff already happened: the stock rose 18.6% off its low in the three sessions before this review. We are not buying at the dislocation; we are buying after a partial recovery.
- The sell side is already bullish with a $51 average target, so "the market is too pessimistic" is a muddled story — the pessimism lives in the revenue line, not the ratings.
- The genuinely open question — whether Figure's ~5.8% revenue-per-dollar-of-volume holds as its loan mix shifts — is exactly what the August 13 report will resolve, and a weak number has real downside.
- Insiders filed nine Form 4s and one Form 144 (a notice of proposed sale) during June–July while the bullish dashboard data was public. We could not read the filing bodies (SEC site blocked both benches), so we don't know if these are routine automatic sales.
- After Kiavi closes (expected in August, $717 million cash), the public dashboard loses its clean organic readability — the window where this thesis is cheaply checkable is closing.
