# PITCH — TEAM — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — PRICE-GATED, NO POSITION: the business passed our examination and the honesty case cleared, but we refused to buy a stock that had just gapped +30% overnight; the first third only unlocks at $126 or below, and as of 2026-08-18 the stock is $158.34, so we own nothing and that is the correct outcome.**

---

## What we believe

Atlassian is a good business that just finished a good year, and we came within a hair of buying it for the wrong reason.

The specific thing we believe, stated plainly:

1. **The reported cash-flow decline is mostly a timing artifact, not decay.** Free cash flow fell 6.8% in fiscal 2026 ($1,319.1M vs $1,415.5M) while revenue grew 26%. That looks alarming. It is explained by two things the company disclosed: a very large unpaid-customer-invoice balance at the June year-end, and roughly $170–199M of cash severance from a restructuring that did not exist the prior year. Add those back and cash flow grew somewhere between 17% and 29%.
2. **The invoice build is seasonal, and we can prove it.** Atlassian bills enterprise renewals into its December and June quarters and collects the cash in September and March. Reconstructed quarter by quarter from three separate filings, fiscal 2026 went +$241.4M (cash in), −$367.4M, +$4.3M, −$362.7M — summing exactly to the −$484.5M full-year figure. Three-quarters of the whole year's build landed in the final quarter alone. That is an invoicing spike on a demonstrated collection cycle.
3. **The company is behaving honestly.** It told investors, in its own words, that ending the Data Center product line pulled future purchases into fiscal 2026, sized the benefit at roughly four points of operating margin, guided the payback (Data Center revenue −17% in fiscal 2027), and instructed investors to use recurring-revenue instead of reported revenue. A company that names its own distortion, measures it, and hands you a better yardstick is the profile we are built to *clear*, not catch.
4. **The valuation is fair, not cheap — and fair is not a reason to buy today at this price.** At the court price of $143 the stock was ~27x reported cash flow and ~20x normalized cash flow. At today's $158.34 it is roughly **30x reported and ~22x normalized**, about **20x** fiscal-2026 adjusted operating income. Nothing about that is a bargain.
5. **So the trade is a price trade, not a thesis trade.** We are not waiting for evidence. We have the evidence. We are waiting for a price.

## What the market believes

The market believes the fiscal-2026 fourth-quarter print was a genuine inflection and paid up +29.4% in one session, from $110.17 to ~$143, and has since carried it to $158.34 (it touched $165.98 along the way). The bull case being paid for: cloud revenue re-accelerated to 31% growth, recurring revenue guided +18%, record large-deal activity ($3M+ customers +50% year over year, $5M+ customers +70%), share count shrinking, and an AI product (Rovo) with real engagement. Implied volatility sat at 92% — the 94th percentile of the last 13 weeks — which is the options market saying it does not know what happens next either.

What the market is *not* pricing, or is pricing charitably: that fiscal 2027's reported numbers will look ugly on purpose (Data Center down 17%, four margin points of pull-forward rolling off, three more points given up shifting employee pay from stock to cash), and that nobody outside the company can size the AI revenue at all.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: our edge here is **not** finding a mispricing. It is **not making a mistake that a careful reader could easily have made.**

Our first analyst built a rejection around what looked like a concealment: the release stated free cash flow with no prior-year comparison, while every other metric carried one. That is exactly the pattern we hunt. Our second analyst pulled the *prior year's* release — a year when cash flow grew and the comparison would have flattered them — and found word-for-word the same construction. The template never carried a prior-year clause. The "concealment" was a formatting convention.

That is the transferable skill: **always pull the control document from a year when the company had no motive to hide.** A formatting difference is only evidence if the formatting actually differed.

The second piece of edge is arithmetic nobody else appears to have done: decomposing the receivable into quarters across three filings until it tied exactly to the annual figure. A structural cash-drain and a seasonal one look identical in an annual number and completely different in a quarterly sequence.

Where that leaves us: we can now buy this business at a price with a verified thesis and a measured falsification test, instead of guessing. That is worth something. It is worth less than a discount.

## Why it might be priced in

Fully stated, because these are good objections:

- **Three of four things we learned are things the company itself disclosed.** The pull-forward, the four-point margin benefit, the −17% Data Center guide, the pay-mix shift — all in the shareholder letter. Anyone who read it knows what we know.
- **The gap never filled.** Our entry level sits 20.4% below today's price and the stock has gone *up* since the court, not down. A gate the market keeps walking away from is not a plan; it is a preference.
- **Our own conclusion is "fairly valued."** Fairly valued is, by construction, priced in. We are not claiming a discount. We are claiming quality at a fair price with a good falsification test, which is a much weaker claim.
- **We can't verify the AI story.** Neither can the market — but the market is paying for it and we would be too.
- **The bear case that survived is a price case, and price cases decay.** If the business keeps compounding, $126 becomes permanently unreachable and our discipline converts into a permanent miss. We flagged our first analyst for exactly this pathology (he set a $105 gate and then wrote that the tape was "unlikely to revisit" it — a permanent no dressed as a condition). We are now one step away from the same error at $126, and we have armed a sensor against ourselves accordingly.
