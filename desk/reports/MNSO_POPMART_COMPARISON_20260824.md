# MNSO vs POP MART — the licensed-retail / owned-IP comparison
**2026-08-24 · purpose-built for the MNSO 8/28 print decision (pack gates: GM ≥43.3% AND SG&A flat → starter ≤0.5%, field-upgraded to full 0.5% if both pass) · all figures from primary filings cited inline · gates NOT changed by this memo**

**The one-line verdict up front:** these are different businesses wearing the same "China IP retail"
costume — Pop Mart manufactures IP and earns 70/40 margins on it; Miniso rents IP and earns 43/14
margins retailing it. The comparison's real value for Thursday is a control experiment: Pop Mart's
fresh 1H26 print (out 8/20) shows a company holding operating leverage through a deflating hype
cycle — which sharpens, not softens, the question Miniso must answer about its own cost line.

---

## 1 · The structural difference (the spine)

**Pop Mart owns its IP.** Labubu/The Monsters, Molly, Skullpanda — created in-house or exclusively
licensed from artists. No licensor royalty stack, pricing power on hits, and IP appreciation accrues
to the P&L: FY2025 gross margin **72.1%** (RMB 26,765M gross profit on 37,120M revenue; FY25 annual
results, HKEX 3/25/26); 1H26 gross margin **69.7%** (11,966M on 17,173M; interim announcement,
HKEX 8/20/26). It is an IP-creation company with retail attached.

**Miniso rents its IP.** Disney, Sanrio, Toca Boca, Star Wars — royalty-bearing licenses layered on
value-retail merchandise. March-quarter 2026 gross margin **43.3%** (RMB 2,464M on 5,688M; 6-K
EX-99.1, 5/27/26) — and that 43.3% IS the desk's pack gate, i.e., the gate tests whether Miniso can
hold the *ceiling* of licensed-retail economics. The ~26-point gross-margin gap to Pop Mart is
structural, not executional: a royalty stack plus value-price-point retail cannot converge on
owned-IP economics. Miniso is a value retailer with a licensed overlay, plus TOP TOY — its Pop
Mart-adjacent format.

**Three facts jump out of the table:**
1. **Growth has converged.** Pop Mart +23.8% vs Miniso +22–23% for the identical six months. The
   Labubu supercycle (FY25: +184.7%) is lapping; the two companies now grow at the same rate with a
   26-point margin gap between them.
2. **Pop Mart's overseas leg is *contracting*** — −11.1% total, overseas *online* down ~44% YoY.
   The global collectible hype wave is deflating in the channel where hype lives (online drops),
   while PRC re-accelerated +47%. The market has repriced this violently: the stock's 52-week LOW
   printed within the last 13 weeks, ~55% below the high, *through* a +23.8% revenue half.
3. **The cost-line control experiment (the decisive one for Thursday):** Pop Mart's distribution
   costs grew +23.1% on +23.8% revenue — operating leverage held even in a deflating cycle (its OPM
   compression is the other-losses line, largely non-operating). Miniso's S&D grew **+44% on +28.5%
   revenue** in the March quarter. One of these companies has a category problem; the other has a
   Miniso problem.

## 3 · TOP TOY — the clone, honestly measured
Q1 2026: revenue **RMB 514.5M, +51.4% YoY** (~9% of group revenue); **355 stores** (+75 YoY),
majority China Retail-Partner model, 39 overseas (6-K 5/27/26). It is working *as a growth line* —
but it gets **Miniso economics, not Pop Mart economics**: predominantly curated third-party pop toys
at retail margins, carried inside a group at 43% GM, and it took strategic financing in 2025 whose
preferred-share redemption liabilities now run fair-value losses through the P&L (RMB 21.4M in Q1,
excluded from non-IFRS). A Pop Mart multiple applied to TOP TOY's revenue is a category error the
memo explicitly declines: no owned-IP engine, no 70% GM, no hit franchise disclosed.

## 4 · Read-through for Thursday
**The tailwind/drain question is UNVERIFIABLE as cross-elasticity** — no disclosure ties collector
wallet share between owned-IP blind boxes and licensed value retail, and we won't hand-wave one.
What IS verifiable:
- The *category* is not Miniso's problem this half: Miniso's China business accelerated five
  straight quarters while Pop Mart's overseas contracted — their demand engines are different.
- The *shape* of Pop Mart's derate is the cautionary read-through: −45–55% off the high through a
  +24% half, because the market stopped paying hype multiples the moment the E-side (overseas,
  online) cracked under an intact headline. That is precisely the desk's MNSO kill shape (E falling
  while R compounds) — Pop Mart shows what the market does to it, at any margin level.
- Pop Mart's held operating leverage makes Thursday's SG&A gate MORE diagnostic: if Miniso's S&A
  ratio deteriorates again, it cannot be blamed on the industry.

## 5 · Valuation gap and what it implies
The market pays ~11–15× (post-derate) for owned-IP economics with hype-cycle risk, and ~9–10× for
licensed value retail with a bloating cost line. **Miniso's discount to Pop Mart is structural and
mostly correct** — a 26-point GM gap and a royalty ceiling deserve a different multiple; "MNSO is
cheap vs Pop Mart" is not an argument this desk will use. The cyclical question inside the
structural one is the only live opportunity: Miniso at ~9× adjusted earnings is cheap **iff** the
SG&A surge is front-loaded store-opening investment that normalizes (the hiring data says openings
are accelerating — the spend is real either way); it is fairly priced or worse if the +44% S&D
growth is the new run-rate. That is, verbatim, what the pack's second gate tests Thursday. The Pop
Mart comparison adds one asymmetry: the deflating collectible cycle mildly *devalues* any "TOP TOY
optionality" leg of a bull case — do not pay for it.

## 6 · Decision relevance (gates unchanged)
1. **The gates stand exactly as armed** — GM ≥43.3% AND SG&A flat → 0.5% (field-upgraded); this memo
   changes no levels.
2. **The SG&A gate gains diagnostic weight:** the closest peer just demonstrated cost lines can
   track revenue through a downcycle — an SG&A miss Thursday is idiosyncratic, and the CLOSE-FILE
   branch should be executed without relitigation.
3. **If both gates pass**, enter knowing what you own: licensed-retail economics at ~9× with
   execution risk — not a Pop Mart discount, and no collectible-cycle kicker credited.
