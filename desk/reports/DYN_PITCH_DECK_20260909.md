# PITCH — DYN — VERDICT

## What we believe

Dyne Therapeutics is a pre-revenue drug developer with two shots on goal and enough cash to take both of them.

1. **The Duchenne (DMD) leg is close and probably real.** A biologics application is filed for the exon-51 skipping drug (the court record calls it z-rostudirsen), with an FDA decision date of **2027-01-21**. A competitor's cousin drug just got priority review in Duchenne on a biomarker alone, which tells us the regulator's door for this class is open, not closing.
2. **The myotonic dystrophy (DM1) leg got genuinely damaged on 2026-09-08**, when Novartis/Avidity's del-desiran missed its main functional test in a placebo-controlled Phase 3. That damage is real and it lands on Dyne, because Dyne's own accelerated-approval path in DM1 rests on **the same functional test** — video hand opening time, i.e. how fast a patient can open a stiff hand.
3. **But the tape is not obviously wrong.** At the pack price of **$19.87** the company is worth about **$3.71B** market value, roughly **$2.58B** after netting the cash. That sits *above* what the Duchenne exon-51 drug alone is worth if fully funded (~$16.76/sh) and *below* what a multi-exon franchise is worth (~$27.48/sh). That is the definition of fairly priced with a coin-flip attached.
4. **Therefore: the original long thesis is dead as written, and we do not replace it with a short.** A filed application, a January decision date, and Dyne's own placebo-controlled hand-opening signal make the upside tail too fat to be short.

Position: **FLAT. No position, no orders.**

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: **thin, and mostly on the bear side.** What survived the courts:

- **Prize-table discipline.** Most buyers are anchored to a $39 target. Our own arithmetic says $39 needs roughly **$1.5B of peak sales at a 4x multiple** — which requires the Duchenne franchise *plus* DM1 approval, i.e. the exact leg the Novartis failure impugns. We can name what the price already assumes; the average target holder cannot.
- **One fact both bears and the tape underweighted:** Dyne's ACHIEVE study already showed a **placebo-controlled** improvement in hand-opening time at 12 months, and its DM1 drug is a different chemistry (an antisense oligo) from Novartis's (an siRNA knockdown). They share a delivery vehicle, not a mechanism. That distinguishes Dyne's evidence from the open-label, natural-history-matched comparisons that broke for Avidity.
- **Cash-structure clarity.** We know the widely quoted "$898.5M cash" figure is stale (it is the pre-offering 6/30 number), and that the correct pro-forma net cash is about **$1.13B** ([Q2 8-K exhibit 99.1, primary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001818794/000119312526323940/dyn-ex99_1.htm)). Anyone pairing pre-offering cash with post-offering share count is off by ~$240M of enterprise value.
- **A pre-committed re-entry ladder** below the funded Duchenne-only floor. Edge in a binary is usually price discipline, not insight.

## What could go wrong (PRE-MORTEM, v1.7)

**Failure narrative 1 — We buy the $14 re-open gate and it was a real repricing, not an overshoot.**
If the DM1 readout misses in Q1 2027 *and* the market simultaneously decides the multi-exon Duchenne pipeline (exon 53/45/44) deserves near-zero credit, the funded-value floor is not $14 — it drops toward the $12.48 line ($400M peak at 3x), and a dilutive raise pushes it lower still. **Tripwire exists:** an 8-K/press release on the readout plus any new stock-sale filing. Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — Manufacturing/quality, not science, kills the January approval.**
Dyne has **never shipped a drug** — zero approvals, zero recalls, zero commercial history in the FDA record — and relies on contract manufacturers we have not inspected. A refusal letter driven by facility or chemistry issues would remove the near-term leg entirely and reprice the name below the Duchenne-only floor. **This one has NO monitorable tripwire.** Inspection findings at a third-party plant are not disclosed to us before the decision, and there is no public sensor we can arm that reads them. **Unknown-unknown class: third-party-manufacturing / CMC-quality opacity at a pre-commercial issuer** — a class where the first observable event *is* the loss.

**Failure narrative 3 — We are right about the science and wrong about the flow.**
Sponsor distribution is running: a Form 4 on 2026-08-14 shows **2,160,516 shares sold at $25.00** by an indirect fund holder, with Rule 144 notices on 8/13–8/14, 9/4 and two more on 9/8. Separately, a company that raised $431M at $20.50 with ~$900M already in the bank has shown it will raise again — most likely right after a January approval, which is exactly when we would want to be adding. Good news gets sold into supply. **Tripwire exists:** Form 144/Form 4 cadence and any new shelf takedown.

**Reflexive / structural leg.**
Our own size is not the reflexive risk. At a proposed gated starter of 1.0–2.0% of book, we estimate we would be **1–3% of one day's volume** (estimated average daily volume 2.5–5M shares, ~$50–100M notional, elevated post-gap; **we have no live volume rail — this is an estimate, not a measurement**). We cannot move this tape. Two other flows can: (a) continuing 144/Form 4 sponsor distribution, and (b) a post-approval equity raise, which is the *predicted* flow and points the wrong way for an add-on-good-news plan. **Doctrine interaction to be honest about:** our two knowledge-graph candidates point in opposite directions on the same facts — one says a peer's biomarker-moved-but-function-failed result makes the *surrogate itself* suspect and therefore damages us more; the other says a focal name with its own randomized data on that endpoint is over-punished by class read-through. Both fired here. We are not entitled to pick the comfortable one, and the FLAT verdict is partly an admission that the two cancel.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid a fair price for a coin flip we told ourselves was cheap — buying a $14–16 "floor" that was really a repricing, into sponsor supply and a post-approval equity raise, on a hand-opening endpoint that a bigger, better-funded competitor had already failed in placebo-controlled conditions.

The EV sign flips on parameter choices we cannot defend to two decimals, and the choice of a 3x versus 4x sales multiple moves fair value by more than the entire claimed edge. **A +15% EV that vanishes under a 10-point probability tweak is not an edge; it is noise with a spreadsheet.** This is the arithmetic behind FLAT.

**Volatility read — cannot be completed, and that decides the instrument.** There is **no options chain and no price history in this pack**, so we have: no chain-level midpoint volatility, no real quotes for the strikes above, no realized volatility, no volatility percentile, and therefore **no volatility risk premium (implied minus realized) number**. Our estimate for realized volatility post-gap is 65–85% annualized — an estimate, not a measurement. **The rule stands: chain-level midpoint volatility and real strike quotes only, never an aggregate underlying volatility feed — the lesson where an aggregate feed printed 71 while the chain printed 101 is exactly how a "cheap volatility" call becomes a rich-volatility purchase.** We will not repeat it from an even weaker position, which is no data at all.

**Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated — two separate forks (2027-01-21, then Q1 2027) — so the outcome distribution is genuinely two-humped and a single lognormal volatility number will misprice it in both directions. Long options straddling both forks are the structurally right shape *if and only if* chain volatility prints meaningfully below the ~70% the table implies.

If we transact in 2026 at all, it should be stock, not options.
