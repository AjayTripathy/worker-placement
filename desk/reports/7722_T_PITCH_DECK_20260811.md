Subject: 7722.T — Kokusai Co., Ltd. (EDINET code E02335), a small family-controlled Japanese maker of industrial testing and balancing equipment for auto and battery manufacturers. TSE Standard market, roughly $70M market value, zero analyst coverage. Last close ¥758 (August 8; Tokyo was closed August 11 for Mountain Day, so this is current, not stale). Down 34% from the February high of ¥1,147; the 52-week low is ¥711 (May 20). We hold no position and have no orders in.

## What we believe

This is a cheap, unleveraged, dividend-paying cyclical that just reported a bad quarter — and the honest answer to "is the bad quarter timing or demand?" is *we don't know yet, and neither does anyone quoting the backlog.*

The bull spine, as it survived our internal review:

- The company paid a ¥35 dividend last fiscal year — a 4.6% yield at today's price — and has raised the payout three years running (¥20 → ¥30 → ¥35 paid, with ¥40 forecast for this year). We underwrite the ¥35 actually paid, not the ¥40 forecast.
- The balance sheet is confirmed strong: ¥8,537M of cash and deposits against ¥20,801M of total assets (we verified this on the balance sheet itself after our own red team mistakenly flagged it — see the table below). No meaningful leverage.
- On last year's operating profit of ¥2,086M, the whole enterprise trades around 2x operating profit. But last year was close to a 19-year high; on a mid-cycle profit of roughly ¥1.3bn, the multiple is closer to 3.3x — still cheap, not absurdly cheap.
- The August 7 first-quarter report was ugly (revenue −25.5%, operating profit −60.5%, only 15.8% of the full-year plan done versus ~31% at this point last year), and the stock gapped down 7% on the next session. Management blames parts shortages stretching out manufacturing and acceptance timelines — a timing story, not a demand story. If that's true, profits are deferred, not destroyed.

Our proposed classification: this is *fairly paid risk pending verification* — a carry-and-cyclicality holding, not an edge trade — and the fairness itself is not yet verified because one balance-sheet line (customer advances) is still unread.

## What the market believes

Almost nobody is watching. Zero covering analysts, ~6% foreign ownership, a controlling family, thin volume (230,100 shares traded on the gap-down day). What the tape does say:

- Sellers were derating this stock for six months *before* the bad quarter: ¥1,147 in February down to ¥711 in May, a 38% slide with no headline. The market — such as it is — was already selling the cycle turn.
- The −7% gap after the August 7 report landed on that already-sold base. The bad news is at least partly in the price.
- Implicitly, the market is pricing this as a cyclical entering a downturn at a controlled company whose family has tolerated a price below book value indefinitely — meaning cheapness alone has never forced a re-rating here, and may never.

## Why we might have edge

1. **Structural neglect.** A $70M uncovered, family-held name cannot be "efficiently priced" in any strong sense. Our blue team's point stands: the six-month derate proves sellers acted, not that the price is right. Our own history with orphan names is that our fair-value estimates run conservative.
2. **The Feb–May derate already happened.** We would be buying after a 38% slide plus a post-earnings gap, not ahead of one. That is timing support, not timing risk.
3. **A verifiable, dated resolution point.** The half-year report (~November 6) will show whether first-half operating profit recovers toward the full-year plan (our threshold: cumulative operating profit ≥ ¥750M). The timing-vs-demand question resolves on a calendar date, on the company's own numbers.
4. **The downside structure is honest.** No debt, ~40% of assets in cash, a paid 4.6% yield while we wait, and a founder-family register with no lockup or offering overhang (listed since 2001; no follow-on in the three-year disclosure history).

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands this section be as strong as the last one, and it nearly is:

1. **The "demand intact" evidence is circular.** The bull case leans on backlog up 10.3%. But backlog = orders minus completed acceptances; when acceptances stall (which is exactly what happened — revenue −25.5%), backlog rises *mechanically* even if new order intake fell. The one number that would settle it — new order intake (受注高) — is not quoted anywhere we could reach. Until we read it, "demand intact" is unproven.
2. **Last year was the cycle peak.** Operating profit of ¥2,086M sits near a 19-year high; the two years before that were *losses* (−¥39M and −¥613M). "2x operating profit" is a peak-cycle statistic. Mid-cycle, this is a ~3.3x business, and in the worst case for the unread advances line, 5–6x — cheap, but ordinary-cheap for an uncovered Japanese cyclical.
3. **The dividend spine is partly a forecast.** The advertised 5.3% yield uses the ¥40 *forecast* — made by a company that just printed profit down 60% against plan. The paid, trailing yield is 4.6%, and forecasts get cut.
4. **A soft demand tell we initially under-weighted:** the company's own explanation mentions "longer payment terms with clients." Customers stretching payment terms is demand-adjacent weakness, not just a parts problem.
5. **The controlling family has tolerated sub-book pricing for years.** There is no activist, no coverage, and no forced catalyst for a re-rating. The re-rating case is patience plus dividends, nothing more.
