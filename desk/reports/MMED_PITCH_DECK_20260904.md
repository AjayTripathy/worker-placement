## What we believe

MiniMed Group is the diabetes-device business Medtronic carved out and floated on 2026-03-09. Medtronic sold 28.0 million shares at $20.00 and kept 252,813,348 — **90.03% of the company**. The public owns about 10%, worth roughly $661 million, inside a $6.6 billion company.

We believe three things:

1. **The business is fine and is not the question.** First quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended 2026-07-31) did $843 million of sales, up 16.6%, with consumables up 13.8% to $261 million ([8-K exhibit, 2026-09-01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2062583/000162828026059699/exhibit991-fy27q1earningsr.htm)). Management raised full-year organic growth from ~10% to ~10.5% and held the profit-margin guide at ~16%.
2. **The thing everyone is waiting for is a supply event, not a demand event.** Medtronic still has to get rid of 90% of this company. Its preferred method — stated in its own 2026-09-01 release — is a **split-off**, where Medtronic offers its MiniMed shares to its own shareholders in exchange for Medtronic stock. To get people to swap, the offer is priced at a discount (Johnson & Johnson used 7% for Kenvue in 2023). Arbitrageurs take the discount and sell the shares they receive. **The awaited catalyst pushes this stock down, not up, while the offer is open.**
3. **The stock is not cheap enough to absorb that.** At $23.61 the company is worth about $6.33 billion after cash, or **1.92x expected fiscal-2027 revenue and about 12x expected adjusted profit**. That is above Tandem (1.8x) — a peer with real problems — for a business that earned **$5 million of accounting operating profit on $843 million of sales** in a quarter that had an extra week in it.

Net: a good business, at a full price, with a large, dated-in-principle but undated-in-fact wave of supply hanging over it. **We do not own it and we should not buy it today.**

## What the market believes

The market believes the re-rating is finished and correct. The stock went from $10.65 in April to $24.43 last week — up 122% — on two pieces of genuinely good news:

- Abbott's dual glucose-and-ketone sensor got FDA clearance on 2026-08-25 ([FDA announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-authorizes-first-wearable-device-continuously-monitors-both-ketone-levels-and-blood-sugar)), and MiniMed's contract with Abbott was amended on 2026-06-01 to include it.
- The 2026-09-01 results beat and the guide went up.

Underneath that, the market believes the split-off is a *good* thing that removes an overhang, and prices it as a vague positive with no date. It is also, quietly, believing that the "~16% adjusted profit margin" is the real earning power of the business, because **no balance sheet and no cash-flow statement have been filed yet** — the first 10-Q does not exist.

## Why we might have edge

Four places where we are not with the crowd:

1. **We have the sign of the catalyst right.** The desk's own prior note called the split-off "the cleanest dated mechanical structure on the board" and meant it as a reason to be long. That is backwards. The exchange offer is a discounted sale that pays arbitrageurs to be short this stock through the offer window. It is a reason to have a **calendar**, not a position.
2. **We know Medtronic's optimal launch price is MiniMed's high.** Medtronic is buying back its own stock using MiniMed shares as the currency. The higher MiniMed trades, the fewer shares Medtronic has to give up. Buying at $23.61 — 3% off the all-time high — is loading the counterparty's gun.
3. **We have read the supplier file and the market has read it as a Convatec story.** Every MiniMed-branded infusion set is legally manufactured by Unomedical (Convatec) in Reynosa, Mexico, and that plant is under an **open FDA warning letter dated 2026-01-08** citing >5,000 leak complaints, an unvalidated leak test, and a complaint documenting a death ([FDA warning letter CMS #719428](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/unomedical-device-sa-de-cv-719428-01082026)). The same supplier already constrained Tandem's supply from late 2025 through 2026. We found no analyst or press note connecting that letter to *MiniMed's* sole-source exposure. A company about to run the most legally intensive transaction a public company can run just lost its lawyer. The offer is probably further away than people assume.

## SCIENCE BRIEF DIGEST

*(Added because the science brief exists and a deep-tech deck without a digest ships scienceless. Not a substitute for any required section.)*

**The one question the technology poses:** how much of MiniMed's clinical performance is under its own engineering control? Answer: less than the marketing implies. Both physically critical consumables are made by other companies — the insulin infusion set by Convatec/Unomedical, the glucose sensor by Abbott.

**Why an infusion-set leak is the dangerous failure, not sensor error.** A pump user has no reservoir of long-acting insulin in the body. If insulin leaks out at the tubing connector, the fluid path is still open, so the pump's pressure alarm never fires — delivery silently stops. From there: no insulin → fat breakdown → ketones → diabetic ketoacidosis, in roughly **4–6 hours**. The FDA letter documents exactly this mode: >5,000 leak complaints between January 2023 and June 2025, 210 tubing-detachment complaints producing >80 hyperglycemia reports, and a leak test that was never validated. This is why continuous ketone sensing is the mechanically correct safety net — and why being last in the queue for it matters.

**MiniMed's own differentiated product is the one with the finding against it.** The Extended Wear Infusion Set claims 7-day wear. The pivotal trial says **83.8% of sets survive to day 6 and 77.8% to day 7** ([PMID 35263188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35263188/)) — about one in five does not reach its label. The FDA letter states that Unomedical's own risk assessment rated potential harm from this product "life threatening."

**Sensor accuracy, independently measured, is worse than the brochure.** Head-to-head against reference methods, Medtronic's Simplera measured 11.6% error against one reference but **13.9% and 16.6%** against two others, versus 9.5–10.1% for Abbott Libre 3 and Dexcom G7 ([PMID 39902649](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39902649/)). Vendor claims of ~8.5–9% are **vendor data with no peer-reviewed or FDA source we could find.**

**Two forward products cannot be date-anchored.** The Vivera fully-closed-loop pivotal is described by the company as "enrollment complete" but **is not findable on ClinicalTrials.gov** under any MiniMed or Medtronic Diabetes sponsor search — while Insulet's equivalent trials are registered (NCT07521475, NCT07593625). The MiniMed Fit patch pump "submission" cannot be verified until it clears. Treat both 2027 launch dates as company assertions.

**The bull item the science supports:** consumables grew 13.8% and Convatec named MiniMed as a growth driver. Whatever is wrong at Reynosa, it is not currently limiting MiniMed's shipments.

**The structural leg — our own size, the predicted flow, and our own rules.**
Our size is not the problem: a 0.5% starter is tens of thousands of dollars against a float that turns over tens of millions a day, so we will never move this tape. The problem is **direction and doctrine.** We have a rule that says never bid into flow we ourselves predicted; we are predicting a discounted sale of 252.8 million shares. We have a rule that says a clean court with no named kill defaults to a starter position; that rule would put us long here unless we name the kills, so we name them explicitly: **(a) the awaited catalyst's flow runs against a long, (b) the price is above our own probability-weighted value, (c) a $661 million float facing a tenfold expansion caps any position at "starter" and makes the exit path the binding constraint.** And we have a tax rule: 2026 is a harvest year, so any expression whose loss lands after December 31 defers that loss out of the year we need it.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought the split-off as a catalyst when it is a supply event — paying up at the high, into the discounted exchange offer that Medtronic is incentivized to launch at exactly that price.**
