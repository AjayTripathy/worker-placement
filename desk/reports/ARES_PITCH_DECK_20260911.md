# PITCH — ARES — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — STARTER (half-size) GATED on the 9/18 ASIF tender (conviction 7/10).** 

---

## What we believe

Ares is a fee-collecting asset manager. It gets paid a management fee on $409.9bn of fee-paying assets (FPAUM), and the stock is down about 27% from its 52-week high while those fees grew ~17% year over year. The original brief called that gap "damage-absent" — nothing is actually broken, the price just fell.

After both benches, we believe something narrower and less exciting:

1. **The fee stream is not "contractually immune" to the credit cycle.** That phrasing was killed and it deserved to be. The fee base grew last quarter partly because Ares put already-committed money to work and added fund leverage — and deployment is the first thing that freezes when credit tightens.
2. **But it is cushioned, not fragile.** There is roughly $93bn of already-closed capital not yet paying fees, which the company says carries about $828M of annual management fees when deployed. Against ~$4.0bn of annualized base fees (Q2-26 base fees of ~$1.0bn × 4), that is a **~21% contracted backlog**. A slow market *defers* that revenue; it does not delete it.
3. **The loudest bear leg — wealth-channel redemptions — is real but small.** ASIF, the non-traded credit fund, has had two consecutive oversubscribed redemption windows with demand rising from 11.6% to 14.4% of shares against a 5% cap. But ASIF is 2.6% of Ares' fee base, and a full-cap quarter is 0.13% of it. Its net asset value was flat (~$10.7bn) across a $523.5M payout — new money replaced the money leaving.
4. **The payout is thin, not broken.** First-half realized income of $2.53/share against $2.70 declared is 0.94x coverage. Ares' realized income is fourth-quarter weighted (Q4-25 was $1.45), so a full year lands near 0.98x. Thin. Not a dividend cut signal. It is a reason to size small.
5. **Therefore: this is a timing and price trade, not an insight trade.** At 22.3x consensus FY26 earnings and 40.9% *above* its 52-week low, we are not being paid for the ambiguity. We want it 6–12% lower, after the flow datum prints.

## What the market believes

The street believes exactly what the brief believed, and has for months. FY26 earnings consensus is $5.94–$5.99, up ~25%; 20 analysts cover it; the average target is $193.69 as of Sep 4 — about **45% above spot** — with the stated reason being resilient fundamentals. After the last print, targets went **up** (TD Cowen 155→163, RBC 162→168, Deutsche Bank 140→144). There are effectively no sell ratings.

Two readings of that, and we hold both:
- *Benign:* the street is right, the stock is de-rating with the group, and patience gets paid.
- *Hostile:* a 45% target gap with zero sells is a crowded long whose targets are stale rather than considered. Nobody has cut, so nobody has repriced.

We cannot distinguish these without the short-interest and positioning data we do not have (see unverified #5).

## Why we might have edge

Honest and small. Four candidates:

1. **Timing, not view.** The wealth-channel flow prints in a *fund* filing (ASIF's tender amendment, ~Sept 25) one to two quarters before it shows up in the *company's* redemption line. That is a genuine lead, and the red bench found it. It gave us a real date; it just had the date wrong.
2. **A number both benches got wrong in our favor.** Strip the harvest distributions out of the "erosion" bucket — money returned to investors because deals *worked* — and last quarter's erosion was $6,471M against $8,106M of new commitments. **New money alone exceeded non-harvest outflows**, before a dollar of deployment. Neither bench computed this. It is the cleanest single rebuttal to "the fee base is cycle-erodible," and it is stronger than the backlog argument.
3. **A fee-rate scare we can fade.** The "fees growing slower than assets" claim compares a period revenue number to an end-of-period balance. When the balance is rising, that gap appears automatically. If the street runs that scare on Q3, it is likely an artifact, and we would be prepared to buy it.
4. **Nobody models ASIF's balance sheet.** $21.3bn of assets against $10.9bn of debt on $10.7bn of net assets, with a revolver upsized to $4.1bn to meet redemptions. That is the actual transmission channel, and it sits in a separate filer (CIK 1918712) most Ares models never open.

## Why it might be priced in

Bluntly: most of it is.

- The "resilient fundamentals" story **is** consensus. We have no competing number. We cannot say what the street has wrong about FY26 — only that we would pay less for the same number.
- The dislocation is largely healed: **+40.9% off the low**, not a fresh crash. Buying "it's down 25%" is buying a stale chart. (The original brief's −25% was wrong in any case; see the checks table.)
- 22.3x forward earnings for a compounder is unremarkable, not cheap. There is no valuation support under us until roughly 19–20x.
- The ASIF redemption facts are on public file and have been reported. The oversubscription is not a secret.
- **Our own scenario table says so.** Probability-weighted fair value is **$128.70 vs a $133.20 tape — minus 3.4%.** Our own arithmetic refuses an immediate entry, and we are taking that at face value.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — "Nothing breaks; it just stops working." (highest probability, ~40%)**
We buy at $125–131. Credit stays fine, Ares delivers $5.95, and the stock trades between 20x and 23x for a year while targets quietly drift from $193 to $160. We earn the 4.3% dividend and dead money. *Tripwire: monitorable* — FY27 fee-related-earnings revisions and target-price drift; exit rule is a de-rate through 19x ($113) without a fundamental cause.

**Failure narrative 2 — "The freeze is in deployment, and we mis-sized the cushion." (~25%)**
Credit contracts. Deployment drops from $14.3bn to $6–8bn a quarter. The $93bn backlog is still there, still contracted — but it activates two years later than modeled, so FPAUM flatlines while distributions keep running. Fee-related earnings growth goes from +20% to +4%, and a 22x multiple on a 4% grower is 15x. That is $90. *Tripwire: monitorable* — the deployment line in the Q3 and Q4 Ex-99.2 rollforwards; the hard stop is commitments + deployment < erosion for two consecutive quarters.

**Failure narrative 3 — "The marks were the product." (~15%, and NO monitorable tripwire)**
Private credit assets are valued quarterly, by model, with no public tape. ASIF's $21.3bn portfolio, Ares' fund-level marks, and every NAV that redemptions are paid against are Level 3 estimates. A regime shift in how those are struck — a valuation agent changes method, an auditor tightens, a regulator writes a letter, or a peer takes a large writedown that becomes the comparable — moves NAV, which moves redemptions, which moves the fee base. **We cannot watch this between prints.** There is no series, no filing cadence, no third-party quote. The one hint we have is already bearish and already public: a third party mini-tendered ASIF Class I at a **15% discount to net asset value** in July, and the board rejected it.

> **Unknown-unknown class, named: *unobservable-mark regime shift* — with an adjacent sub-class, *counterparty-of-a-counterparty*.** ASIF's $10.9bn debt stack and $4.1bn revolver depend on lenders who owe us no disclosure. If one of them pulls the ladder, our first notice is an 8-K after the fact. No sensor can be built for either class from public data on a schedule shorter than a quarter. We accept this as unhedged and we size for it.

**The reflexive and structural leg.**
- *Our own size is not the risk.* Estimated average daily volume is 2.0–3.2M shares ($270–430M). Our maximum position, 2.25% of book, is well under 1% of a single day's volume at any plausible book size. We are not the flow and cannot move this tape. Nobody should claim otherwise in a risk meeting.
- *The flow that matters is other people's.* A crowded long with no sell ratings and a 45% target gap unwinds through downgrades, not through fundamentals. Add scheduled supply: the 6.75% Series B mandatory convertible is contracted Class A dilution arriving on a calendar, not a rumor.
- *The genuinely reflexive loop:* ASIF meets redemptions partly with borrowed money against private credit assets. If redemption demand keeps climbing past the cap, the fund eventually sells assets. Selling private credit assets in a soft market sets the comparables that mark the *rest* of the book — including other Ares funds. That loop is invisible until it isn't.
- *Doctrine interactions, stated plainly.* Our consensus-kill rule rejects this thesis **as written**; our divergence-not-coverage rule says "the street owns it" is a sizing input, not a rejection. The two together produce the most dangerous outcome available to us: **a live position whose stated reason has been killed.** People hold those through invalidation because nobody can articulate what would end them — that is why the hard stops in the entry section are written as filings, not feelings. Separately, our own discipline that Up-C share exchanges are not real dilution could cause us to wave off the mandatory convertible, which **is** real dilution. It is not the same thing.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we paid 22x for the consensus number, the consensus number came in fine, and the multiple went to 19x anyway while private-credit marks we could never observe slowly reset the whole sector's growth rate.**

- If the $120P chain midpoint prints **above $3.15** (chain IV > ~37%, positive variance premium vs our 25.4% table dispersion): **sell the Nov-20 $120 cash-secured put** as the gated add. It pays us to wait and, if assigned, puts us inside the add-1 band.

- **Calls: no, at any chain price above ~$1.** Our own table gives the upside strike zero value.

**Caveat we will not bury:** σ_table (25.4%) is *narrower* than the realized-vol proxy (35–41%). Either the vol market is rich, or our scenario tails are too tight. Given that failure narrative 3 has no tripwire, the honest reading is that **our tails are too tight**, which argues against selling puts in size. Cap any put sale at the add-1 notional. **No chain was in the pack and we will not invent quotes** — the TSSI lesson (aggregate feed said 71, chain said 101) means the underlying IV feed is not an acceptable substitute. Item 10 above must be drained before anything is sold.

**Bimodality:** the table is fork-dated twice — Sept 18 (tender) and ~Nov 2 (print). A single Nov-20 expiry spans both forks, so realized outcomes cluster at the ends rather than the middle. Any premium sold is short two gaps, not one.

**Tax overlay:** the **2026-11-20 expiry sits inside tax year 2026**, so the loss branch lands in the intended year — compliant during a harvest year. **January-2027 and later expiries are excluded**: they defer the loss into next year, and nothing here dominates numerically enough to justify that.
