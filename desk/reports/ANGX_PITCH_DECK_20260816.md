# PITCH — ANGX — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: WATCH, no entry today — the bear case on demand is refuted, but we will not buy an unauditable margin plus a dated ~7% dilution event; re-open on the two conditions below.** (Red bench: kill conviction 4/10. Blue bench: WATCH conviction 7/10. No position, no orders.)

## What we believe

Angel Studios' subscription business is real and the "collapsing demand" story is wrong. We verified this in the primary Q2 filing itself ([10-Q, filed 2026-08-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1865200/000186520026000070/angx-20260630x10q.htm)), not from press coverage:

- Angel Guild (the membership product) did **$174.1M in first-half revenue, up 113.5%** year over year, and **$90.7M in Q2 alone (+94% YoY, +8.9% vs. Q1)**.
- **Deferred revenue — cash customers have already paid for future service — grew $16.0M in six months to $82.5M.** People do not prepay a service they are abandoning.
- **Operating cash flow was +$18.8M** in the first half, versus −$20.4M a year ago, and the improvement is operational, not financing dressed up (financing netted to −$1.1M). Honest caveat: roughly $16M of that cash flow *is* the deferred-revenue build, so the steady-state business is approximately cash-breakeven, not a $19M generator.
- Q2 total revenue **grew 27.5% even though the theatrical film line was nearly empty** ($2.8M vs. $32.6M a year ago). The subscription business absorbed the entire hole.
- A third leg neither side had focused on: **content licensing grew 176%** to $25.3M in the half.

What we do *not* believe is that this is a clean balance sheet or an auditable business. It carries $78.0M of debt at 10–15% interest, stockholders' equity is negative ($−29.3M), and the company's own twelve-month liquidity statement lists, among its resources, "the ability to sell its digital assets if necessary" — a 303-bitcoin treasury that lost a third of its value ($26.5M → $17.7M) in the first half, with those losses excluded from the profitability metric management markets.

## What the market believes

At $4.37 (August 14 close), the market treats ANGX as a broken SPAC-merger stock: down 79% from its 52-week high, but **already doubled off the $2.045 low**. The last real market-clearing price for a large slug of stock was the **April 2026 offering at $2.10**, done roughly a month after insider lockups expired — that offering *was* the exit door for early holders. The market sees expensive hard-money debt, negative equity, a bitcoin-flavored liquidity cushion, a film business that just printed a near-zero quarter, and a pending merger (S-4) that will add roughly **10.15 million shares (~7% dilution) by its October 31 deadline**. Five to twelve analysts cover it — this is not an undiscovered name, and the Q2 beat has been public since August 4.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The headline bear fact is a comparison artifact, and we caught it.** The −48.5% theatrical decline that looks like demand damage is a base effect: the year-ago period contained *The King of Kings*, Angel's second-highest-grossing film ever ($19.4M opening weekend — about 93% of this entire half's theatrical line), while this Q2 had no wide release. A decline against a record slate is not a demand collapse.
2. **Prepaid cash is hard evidence.** The $82.5M deferred-revenue balance (+107% YoY) is contractual money in the bank corroborating subscriber growth — harder to fake or spin than a press-release member count.
3. **A widely-feared accounting question turned out to be moot.** The concern that a 10-year content-amortization policy was hiding losses dissolves on one primary read: total capitalized content is only **$5.4M** against $226.8M of half-year revenue. Angel mostly doesn't capitalize its slate at all; creator costs flow through revenue-share.
4. The licensing line (+176%) has been litigated by no one, including us.

## Why it might be priced in

1. The stock has **already doubled off the low**; the easiest part of the re-rate may be done, and the Q2 beat is twelve days old and covered by the Street.
2. The single most important number — **the margin Angel keeps on Guild revenue after paying filmmakers — is not disclosed anywhere in the filings**, and churn isn't either. A market discount for unauditable unit economics is rational pricing, not mispricing.
3. **Outside demand signals are not confirming.** Our organic-attention tracker reads *decaying* (39.1) while the company spends $117.7M a half on sales and marketing, and our app-store review tracker could not even resolve the company's app listing. Blue bench plausibly argues the attention decay just reflects a no-release quarter — but "plausibly explained away" is not "refuted."
4. The ~7% dilution event is on the public calendar (October 31). Everyone can see it; anyone wanting in can wait behind it, which is exactly what we propose to do.
