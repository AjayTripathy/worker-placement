# PITCH — GOTU — PROPOSED VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: REJECT — no position into the unconfirmed late-August earnings report. Both the prosecution and the defense-side reviewer reached the same answer: the "stock is backed by cash" story is an accounting illusion, and what's left is a deteriorating business under absolute founder control.**

## What we believe

Gaotu Techedu (NYSE: GOTU, $1.68, roughly $407M market value) is a Chinese online tutoring company that looks, at first glance, like a stock trading below its pile of cash. We believe that pile is mostly not the shareholders' money, and that the business behind it is getting worse at the margin:

- **The cash "floor" doesn't survive the balance sheet.** The company holds about RMB 3.26 billion of cash and short-term investments — but it also owes RMB 4.28 billion in total liabilities. Even the most generous honest netting (subtracting only the borrowings and related-party debts, and treating customer prepayments as an obligation the company services at cost rather than refunds at face value) leaves net liquid backing of roughly 30–72% of the market cap. Under stricter netting it's far less. Under no defensible math is it *more than* the market cap, which is what the original bull case claimed.
- **A large share of the "cash" is customer float.** Prepaid, undelivered tuition (deferred revenue of ~RMB 1.79 billion) equals about 60% of the cash and short-term investments. That money belongs to future course delivery, not to us.
- **The profit engine is decelerating while the marketing bill grows.** In Q1 2026, net income fell 72% year over year, operating income fell 80%, selling expenses reached 50 cents of every revenue dollar, and operating cash flow was an *outflow* of RMB 828 million — 74% worse than a year earlier. New cash collections (gross billings, +12.1%) are now growing *slower* than recognized revenue (+13.2%), meaning the backlog that fuels the reported "beats" has started draining rather than building.
- **The control structure caps our upside.** Founder Larry Chen holds roughly 90.9% of the voting power (not independently re-verified, but consistent with filings), the company settled a securities-fraud matter in its GSX era, and the debt sits inside the onshore contractual entities (the "VIE" structure) that US holders own only by contract. If the stock ever does trade far below true net cash, the most likely resolution is Chen taking it private at his price, not ours.

## What the market believes

At $1.68 — down 59% from the 52-week high and 20% above the 52-week low — the market is pricing GOTU as a melting-ice-cube Chinese education stock: some credit for the revenue growth and the buyback (~RMB 704 million repurchased through June 1, 2026, per the company), heavily discounted for the collapsing profitability, the cash burn, the China/VIE legal wrapper, and the founder's total control. The tape has been drifting *toward* the low, not bouncing off it. Whatever "cheap on cash" story exists is one many screens surface — the market has seen it and declined it.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: less than the original pitch claimed. What survives as genuinely non-consensus knowledge from this review:

1. **We did the netting the screens don't.** We now know, line by line from the company's own Q1 balance sheet, exactly how much of the liquid stack is spoken for — and we know the corrected range (30–72% of market cap) rather than either the bull's fantasy (96%+) or our own prosecutor's initial overshoot (7%). That gives us a *real* re-entry price, below which the math genuinely does back the stock.
2. **We caught the billings crossover early.** Gross billings growth slipping below revenue growth is a one-quarter, 1.1-point signal — small, but it's the leading indicator, and most coverage watches the lagging one.
3. **We know what would prove the cash is reachable** (the annual report's consolidating schedule showing money at the offshore parent) and we know it hasn't been shown yet.

## Why it might be priced in

- The stock has *already* fallen 59% from its high; the market has done a lot of the discounting we're describing.
- The bear case's freshest facts are all in the company's own June 2 press release — public for two months.
- The strongest remaining bull point is real: deferred revenue grew 24.1% and directly feeds the upcoming quarter's reported results, so a headline "beat" at the late-August print is the *modal* outcome. Our billings-crossover concern threatens the second half of the year, not the next headline. Shorting or avoiding into a likely beat is only right because the balance sheet gives no floor if we're wrong — it is not because we expect a bad headline.
- The buyback is real money (~$98M validated offshore by the repurchases themselves) and supports the price.
