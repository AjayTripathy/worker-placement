# PITCH — LXEO — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no market order; a laddered starter of up to 0.5% of book via resting limits at $4.25 and $3.75, with zero sensors currently armed and the entry levels sitting inside the issuer's own live selling window.

---

## What we believe

Lexeo Therapeutics is a clinical-stage gene therapy company with two cardiac programs and no revenue. The stock is at **$5.06** (the two benches worked off $5.13 intraday; the evidence pack's later tape print is $5.06 — the difference does not change any conclusion, but every number below is shown at both where it matters). That is 54% below its 52-week high of $10.99 and 27% above its 52-week low of $3.99. So this is a name that fell hard and has partially bounced — not a fresh break.

What we actually believe, stated plainly:

1. **The company is worth roughly $220–226 million net of cash.** Fully diluted for the 7.35 million pre-funded warrants the cover page of the 10-Q leaves out, the share base is about 89.4 million, not the 82.0 million on the cover and not the 43.6 million our pack's stale XBRL field reported. At $5.13 that is a $459M market value less roughly $233M of net cash, so about **$226M enterprise value**; at $5.06 it is about **$219M**. The original brief that started this court said $180M. That was wrong by roughly 25%, and it was wrong in the direction that made the idea look cheap.

2. **At that price the market is paying about a 15–21% chance that the lead program works.** If you assume the Friedreich's ataxia cardiomyopathy program (LX2006) were approved and fully funded — 3,000 addressable patients in the US and EU, 50% penetration over ten years, $2.5M one-time price, 70% operating margin, 12% discount rate — the present value is about $19.25 per share. Today's enterprise value divided by that prize implies roughly a 15% probability of success. Adjust for the fact that approval realistically arrives in 2029, not tomorrow, and the implied probability rises to about 21%. Adjust in the other direction for the fact that the second program (LX2020, for PKP2 arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy) addresses a much larger patient pool and is currently being valued at zero in that math, and the implied probability falls again. **The honest answer is a band of roughly 10–25%.** The industry base rate for a Phase 2 asset reaching approval is about 15%. The band contains the base rate. We cannot tell from price alone whether this is cheap or fair.

3. **The one thing that is not ambiguous is supply.** The company sold stock in April at $5.65 and again in July at $4.70 — a lower price, five weeks before this court convened. It has used $39.6M of a $75.0M at-the-market program, leaving about **$35.4M of live selling capacity**, which at $5.13 is another 6.9 million shares, or about 7.7% more stock. On top of that sit 7.35 million pre-funded warrants that are economically free-trading shares which never appear on the share-count line most people read.

4. **Therefore: no market buy.** The defensible position is a small, patient bid below the level management has demonstrated it will sell at, sized so that being wrong costs us little, held as an option on a specific data readout rather than as a valuation trade.

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we believe, which is the core problem with this idea.

- Four specialist healthcare funds filed ownership disclosures between August 7 and August 14: Balyasny at 9.99%, Affinity at 8.4%, Paradigm at 5.3%, OrbiMed at 5.0%. These are exactly the funds that read gene therapy 10-Qs. Balyasny took pre-funded warrants in the October 2025 offering at $7.9999 and is roughly 36% underwater.
- The July at-the-market sale is disclosed in a 10-Q filed August 12 that all of those holders have read. Our red bench initially tagged it a *novel* finding; our blue bench correctly re-tagged it **consensus** — it was novel to *us*, not to the tape.
- The only external price anchor either bench could produce is a J.P. Morgan target cut from $12 to $9. That is roughly 75% above the current price. So the sell side is more optimistic than we are, not less.
- The stock's 27% bounce off the low tells us someone is already buying this drawdown.

In short: the market is applying a normal failure probability to an early-stage asset, in a name that specialists already own, and the published research is more bullish than our own math. There is no obvious group of forced or uninformed sellers here.

## Why we might have edge

Four candidate sources, ranked by how much we actually trust them:

1. **Cap-structure arithmetic that most screens get wrong (moderate confidence).** The 10-Q cover page says 82,046,255 shares. The company's own Q2 weighted-average share count — both basic and diluted — is 85,626,751, which is *higher* than the cover count. That is only arithmetically possible if the penny warrants are inside the earnings-per-share denominator but outside the cover count. Any screen that computes market value from the cover page understates this company by about 9%. That is a repeatable error we can detect systematically, and it is the basis of a proposed new detector (see "What remains unverified").

2. **The timing of the issuance pattern (moderate confidence).** Selling at $5.65 and then at $4.70 into a drawdown, while a press release the same day markets a "runway into 2028," tells you something about how management views its own cost of capital. That is not a secret, but it is the kind of thing that shows up in a subsequent-events footnote rather than a headline.

3. **A free external read-through on the second program (moderate confidence, and genuinely useful).** Rocket Pharmaceuticals' RP-A601 and Tenaya's TN-401 are competing PKP2 gene therapies. Rocket has reported raised PKP2 protein on cardiac biopsy in all three Phase 1 patients — an independent replication of the biological mechanism LX2020 depends on. That means competitor data releases are a tradeable information channel for LXEO that does not require LXEO to say anything. It cuts both ways: it validates the mechanism and it contests the market.

4. **Patience as the edge (low confidence, but honest).** We have no position, no deadline, and no client pressure. If the correct answer is "wait for a specific piece of data at a specific price," we can actually do that. Most holders of this stock cannot.

**What is *not* an edge:** we do not have a differentiated read on whether the drug works. Neither bench claimed one.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated at full strength, because we think this is the stronger half of the argument:

- **The probability math lands on the base rate.** A $219–226M enterprise value against a roughly $1.5B fully-funded prize implies about a 15% chance of success. The published base rate for Phase 2 → approval in this category is about 15%. When your "mispricing" reproduces the textbook prior to the decimal, the most likely explanation is that the market did the same arithmetic.
- **The specialists are already here.** Four disclosed positions in one week from funds whose entire job is this sector. There is no informational vacuum for us to fill.
- **The sell side is above us.** We are not fading an optimistic consensus; we are *below* the only published target we could find. Our disagreement with the street is about level, not direction — and our own math says the street is on the optimistic side. Being more bearish than J.P. Morgan on a $9 target while the stock trades at $5.06 is not an edge; it is agreement with the tape.
- **The cheapness claim was an adjective, not a number.** The brief that opened this court said "$180 million for two RMAT-designated programs" and never once stated a probability of success. Once you correct the enterprise value and state the probability, the cheapness disappears into a band.
- **The financing overhang is a known known.** Everyone holding this stock has read the same 10-Q we did.

Our red bench called this a valuation kill at full force. Our blue bench overturned that on doctrine — valuation gates the *price* you pay, it does not kill an idea. We think blue is procedurally right and red is substantively closer to the truth. That tension is why the verdict is a low limit rather than either a flat refusal or a starter at market.

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — The grind (most likely; monitorable)

We buy at $4.25. The at-the-market program keeps selling into every 5% rally, because that is exactly what it did at $5.65 and then at $4.70. Cash burn runs about $23M a quarter, so roughly ten quarters of runway — but that runway does *not* include the confirmatory trial the FDA has not yet agreed to, and does not include any commercial build. Management raises again anyway, because management has told us by its actions that it will. The stock spends 2027 between $3.50 and $5.50 while the share count climbs 8–15%. We are never *wrong* enough to stop out and never *right* enough to size up. Cost: a 0.5% position dead money for eighteen months, plus the attention.

**Tripwire:** the Q3 10-Q cover count in November, and any Leerink prospectus supplement. If the cover shows more than ~82.5M shares, the program is still running.

### Failure narrative 2 — The 6-month miss (second most likely; partially monitorable)

The pivotal SUNRISE-FA 2 trial is randomized, open-label, 13 patients versus 13 untreated controls, with left-ventricular mass index on cardiac MRI at six months as the primary endpoint, powered to detect a 15% effect. The FDA *removed* the cardiac frataxin protein co-primary at the company's regulatory meeting — meaning the mechanistic readout that would have told us "the drug is doing the biological thing" is gone, leaving one imaging measurement in 26 people. In an open-label trial with an untreated control arm, a 15% imaging effect can be produced by enrollment imbalance, by MRI reader drift, or by the natural variability of a small cohort. If the readout is ambiguous, the FDA can decline to accept the surrogate, and the accelerated-approval path resets by two-plus years. The company has already told us in writing that it is "in ongoing discussions with the FDA regarding the confirmatory evidence strategy … to support full approval." That plan is undated, unagreed, and not in the budget behind the "runway into 2028" claim.

**Tripwire:** an 8-K or press release stating FDA *agreement* on the confirmatory-evidence strategy and on LVMI as reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit. Its absence past mid-2027 is itself the signal.

### Failure narrative 3 — Class contagion (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE)

A serious adverse event occurs in **someone else's** cardiac AAV gene therapy trial — Rocket's, Tenaya's, or a sponsor we are not tracking. High-dose systemic AAV in cardiac indications has a real complement-activation and hepatotoxicity risk profile, and this field has seen trial deaths before. A clinical hold or a DSMB action at another company can reprice every cardiac AAV name 20–40% in a day, and can cause the FDA to raise the safety bar for the entire class, including for LX2006's accelerated-approval path. LXEO would not file anything, because nothing happened at LXEO.

**Why there is no tripwire:** the triggering event occurs at a different legal entity, on a disclosure plane we do not watch, at an unscheduled time. There is no LXEO filing, no LXEO press release, no LXEO calendar date. Our entire sensing apparatus for this name is pointed at LXEO's own filings and LXEO's own price, and this event produces neither until after the fact.

**Unknown-unknown class named: off-entity class-safety contagion — adverse events at third-party sponsors sharing a delivery modality, with no issuer-side disclosure venue and no scheduled date.** This is a general gap in how we watch clinical-stage names, not an LXEO-specific one. Related, and in the same class: pre-funded warrant holders can sell without generating any filing until an annual 13G amendment, so a 7.35M-share supply event can occur with no contemporaneous public record at all.

### The reflexive and structural leg

This is the part that makes us least comfortable, and it deserves to be said directly.

- **Our bid sits inside the seller's window.** We propose resting limits at $4.25 and $3.75. The company sold stock at $4.70 five weeks ago and has $35.4M of capacity left. The most likely way our $4.25 limit *fills* is that the at-the-market program is selling into it. We would be, mechanically, the counterparty to the issuer's own supply — which is close to the definition of adverse selection. The mitigating fact: the remaining capacity is capped at about $35.4M, and management has so far sold at progressively lower prices rather than at any price, which suggests some price sensitivity. It is a mitigation, not a defense.
- **A resting GTC limit into an undated catalyst is an accidental short volatility position.** Both benches said "GTC, not into the print." But the live catalyst — 12-month durability data from HEROIC-PKP2 — is expected in Q4 2026 with **no announced date**. We therefore cannot reliably stand the order down before it. If that data is bad and the stock gaps from $5.00 to $3.00, our $4.25 limit fills on the way down, in full, at a price that no longer reflects anything we believe. **This is a direct collision between our resting-order doctrine and this specific name's catalyst structure, and we do not have a clean answer.** The only partial answers are: (a) cancel the ladder on any LX2020-related press release or any unexplained volume spike, which requires a sensor we have not armed; or (b) accept the gap risk explicitly as the cost of the option, which at 0.25% per rung is survivable.
- **Size versus liquidity is unverified.** We do not have a verified average daily volume for LXEO. At a 0.25% rung on a $459M-cap biotech, we are very likely well inside any reasonable participation limit — but "very likely" is not "checked," and our own publishing standard requires ladders at or under 1% of ADV. **This must be measured before any order is placed.**
- **Doctrine interactions checked:** no integrity flag was established for this issuer, so the tainted-risk discount does not apply. No meme-momentum characteristics. No existing position, so no tax-lot or wash-sale interaction. The name is not in any factor sleeve.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be that we bought a fairly-priced base-rate lottery ticket from a seller standing next to us in the same window — the company issued stock into our bid, the six-month imaging endpoint came back ambiguous rather than clean, and the stock ground lower on dilution while we waited for a data point that never arrived on a date we could plan around.**

5. **LXEO's own competition disclosure versus the trial registry.** Blue partially closed this by identifying Rocket and Tenaya from outside sources, but nobody diffed the company's own stated competitive set against what is actually registered. That diff is where omissions live.
6. **Average daily volume and realistic fill capacity** at $4.25 and $3.75.
7. **Whether the confirmatory trial is funded.** The company markets a "runway into 2028." The confirmatory trial required for full approval is undated, unagreed with the FDA, and — as far as we can determine — not costed inside that runway figure. We have not sized it.
