## What we believe

1. **The franchise was already shrinking before the counterparty event.** Book value per share went $18.60 (6/30/25) → $16.29 (12/31/25) → $17.19 (6/30/26) while EPS "grew" 14.2% — because the share count fell from 47.2M to 41.043M, with 870,129 shares bought in Q2'26 at an average $57.46 versus $17.19 of book (3.3x). That last mark is ~14% underwater today. Net income was up about 1.3–1.5% y/y. That is EPS manufactured by the divisor, not by the business. Primary: 2Q26 8-K ex-99.1, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001295401/000162828026051050/tbbk-20260730xexx991.htm
2. **Net interest margin fell 4.44% → 3.85% (−59bp)**, which management attributes to shifting into fintech loans "for which we primarily earn fee income." So the fee stream now sitting at counterparty risk is the same stream that *replaced* spread income. The two exposures compound; they do not offset. This is the single strongest fundamental leg in the whole file and our red bench missed it.
3. **The exposure is two-legged, not one-legged.** If Chime moves its banking to its own charter, TBBK loses program fees *and* a large block of cheap deposits (average interest-bearing liabilities $8.94B at 1.86% per the 10-Q). A fees-only model understates the hit by roughly 2x. Chime has publicly guided to ">$100M net synergies, driven by sponsor bank fee savings" — the synergy only exists if the fees stop (https://www.chime.com/newsroom/chime-announces-agreement-to-acquire-stride-bank/).
4. **Capital is thinning into the exact event that would demand capital.** Average equity fell $845M → $701M (−17%) against $9.70B average assets (10-Q, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001295401/000162828026054157/tbbk-20260630.htm). The buyback that flatters EPS and the balance sheet that would have to absorb a deposit-replacement event are drawing on the same pot.
5. **But the headline bear kill is wrong.** The "revenue −9.8%" line is an accounting artifact: credit-enhancement income fell $43.2M → $25.8M and the 10-Q states the paired provision fell $17.4M with "no related net impact." Excluding that pass-through, non-interest income *rose 16.7%* to $47.3M. Anyone shorting this on "revenue contraction" is shorting a gross-up.
6. **Therefore: not a healthy compounder that overshot, and not a fraud.** It is a shrinking, buyback-flattered, ~91%-rented deposit book at ~2.88x book ($49.48/$17.19), whose largest tenant has publicly monetized the option to leave. That is a name to own at a price, and the price is lower than here.

## What could go wrong

**FAILURE #1 — "The artifact doctrine ate the trade" (omission failure; monitorable).**
We proved the revenue kill was a gross-up. We then let that finding do too much work and stayed flat while the actual outcome was benign: Chime turns out to be ~10% of fees, the Cash App program ramps, TBBK reaffirms $8.10–8.30, and the stock is $70 by mid-2027. The specific mechanism of failure is our gate design: we require an *explicit* post-9/8 reaffirm, and companies almost never issue explicit standalone reaffirms — they simply repeat the guide in a deck. Under our own "unchanged ≠ confirmed" boilerplate rule, that repetition doesn't trip the gate, so the gate is close to unsatisfiable and we are structurally short the recovery.
*Tripwires (armed as gates below):* 8-K Item 8.01 quantifying Chime; conference/PR guide language; price gate at $55; CHYM 10-Q account split.

**FAILURE #2 — Non-public repricing (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).**
Unknown-unknown class: **SUPERVISORY / CONTRACT OPACITY — non-public regulator correspondence and NDA-gated counterparty economics.** Two versions, same class. (a) An informal agreement, MRA, or consent order is under negotiation with the OCC over BaaS oversight; it is invisible until it lands as a single 8-K, and sector precedent (multiple BaaS sponsor banks over the last three years) is that these arrive with no public precursor, then cap growth, force capital build, and *stop the buyback* — which is the load-bearing input for the EPS guide. (b) TBBK and Chime privately renegotiate program economics — fee reset, deposit-sweep unwind, or an exclusivity/wind-down schedule — under an NDA, and we learn the terms from a 10-Q footnote after the price has moved.
There is no filing, no tape, no connector, and no alt-data series that reads either one in advance. Nothing in our sensor list touches it. We are stating this plainly rather than inventing a proxy: **this risk is unhedged and unmonitorable, and it is the reason position size is capped at 3% of book even at our best price.**

**FAILURE #3 — Reflexive/structural: we are a passenger to a bid that can disappear.**
- *Our own size vs ADV:* estimated ADV is 0.7–1.1M shares (~$35–55M/day; **not in the pack — see unverified #5**). A ⅓ starter at 1.0% of book is on the order of a few tens of thousands of shares — well under half a day's volume. **We are not the reflexivity here.** Our entry and exit are not price-moving in normal tape.
- *The predicted flow that is:* TBBK retired 870,129 shares in one quarter — about 2.1% of shares outstanding, ~13.8k shares/day, roughly 1.5% of estimated ADV. Small daily, enormous cumulatively: ~8%/year of float. That price-insensitive buyer is what has been absorbing the de-rate. Average equity is down 17%. If capital ratios or a supervisory action bind, the buyback stops — and it stops at the *same moment* the deposit-replacement need appears. Then we lose the marginal bid, the EPS bridge, and the capital cushion in one print. Float has also shrunk ~22% in a year, which makes any index/passive rebalance or 13G-holder exit disproportionately violent in both directions.
- *The real liquidity risk is correlated exit, not our size:* every holder's trigger is the same document (an 8-K quantifying Chime). On that day ADV is meaningless — the whole book tries to leave through one door.
- *Doctrine interactions:* (i) blue's new `passthrough_gross_up_revenue_artifact` detector is calibrated on exactly one observation and, if left as-is, will suppress a *genuine* revenue kill next quarter; (ii) red's `buyback_flattered_eps_at_premium_to_book` candidate fires on "revenue decline," which means it would have **false-fired here** — it must be amended to exclude pass-through lines before we let it gate anything; (iii) our prize table is anchor-and-adjust off two anchors — the pre-event $64.41 and the 1.5x-book Stride comp — and blue has already ruled the second one a category error, so our EV of $43.75 inherits anchoring bias in both directions and should not be treated as precision.
