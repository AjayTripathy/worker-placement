## What we believe

AIRO Group Holdings ($8.44, down 67% from its $25.90 high but already up 48% off its $5.71 low) is a small drone/aerospace roll-up reporting second-quarter results before the market opens on Wednesday, August 13 — two days from now. Neither side of the trade is takeable before that report.

The long is blocked because the company's full-year guidance carries real cut risk. Management reiterated 15–25% revenue growth but introduced a new "negative mid-to-high-teens" adjusted-EBITDA guide at Q1 — after already knowing Q1 came in with a gross margin of only 26.6%, less than half its 2025 norm of 58–61%. Hitting the year requires both record second-half revenue and a full margin recovery at the same time, and nobody — including us — has established whether Q1's margin collapse was one-time product mix or a structural problem. On top of that, management has a demonstrated habit of selling stock into strength: it priced a follow-on offering roughly 90 days after its June 2025 IPO — inside the 180-day lockup, meaning the banks waived it — and used about $19.6M of company cash to buy 1,116,312 shares back from insiders at $18.50, more than double today's price ([closing press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250915246275/en/AIRO-Announces-Closing-of-Upsized-$89.4-Million-Public-Offering-and-Full-Exercise-of-Underwriters-Option-to-Purchase-Additional-Shares)). Share count grew 67% in a year. Any post-earnings rally is likely to be met with new supply.

The short is blocked because it is crowded: 20.3% of the small 17.3M-share float is already sold short, with 7.4 days to cover, and the company publicly announced a major RQ-35 drone delivery on July 16. A good print could squeeze violently. The bear case is not a secret.

So the belief is narrow and honest: this is a genuinely two-sided coin flip two days before a decisive data point, and the right position for a coin flip is zero. The decisive line on Wednesday is **gross margin** — it tells us whether Q1 was an accident or the new reality.

## What the market believes

The stock is priced for distress-with-doubt: down two-thirds from the high, but bid up 48% from the low. Only three analysts cover it (average target $16.50, roughly double the price), so "the market" here is thin. The short side — over a fifth of the float — believes the guidance is broken and dilution is coming. The residual longs believe the Q4 2025 quarter ($48.3M of revenue at a 61.4% gross margin, with positive operating income) proves the model works and Q1 was lumpy delivery timing, a view the July delivery announcement supports. The market has, in effect, already had our argument with itself.

## Why we might have edge

- **The governance finding is real and mostly unremarked.** The inside-the-lockup follow-on with a company-cash insider buyback at $18.50 is a management-facilitated insider exit at 2.2× today's price. Both benches confirmed it from primary press releases (our red bench actually undercounted it — the true figure is 1,116,312 shares, not 978k). This caps how much we should ever pay for a recovery: strength gets sold.
- **We did the seasonality work the bears skipped.** The popular bear line — "the guide is arithmetically dead" — fails when you lay out 2025's actual quarters ($11.8M, $24.6M, $6.3M, $48.3M). The model is violently back-loaded and has already printed the kind of quarter the guide needs. Knowing the guide is *at risk* but not *dead* is a better-calibrated view than either crowd holds.
- **A satellite check nobody else ran.** Our building-footprint analysis of the sole US RQ-35 production site shows no physical expansion since 2021, which cuts against the company's "production scale" press-release language — and, symmetrically, against any easy assumption that capacity for a 3.6× second-half ramp exists.
- **We know what we're waiting for.** One number (gross margin) plus one filing check (is a shelf registration live?) converts Wednesday's noise into a decision.

## Why it might be priced in

Symmetry demands we say it: with 20% of the float short, the bear arithmetic on the guide is the *consensus* speculative position, not an insight. The three-analyst coverage means the $16.50 target proves thinness, not mispricing — our own blue bench warned against reading "crowded" as "correctly priced," but it equally blocks us from claiming the downside is ours alone. The dilution risk is visible to anyone who reads the September press release. And the stock's 48% bounce off the low suggests some recovery hope is already paid for. Whatever edge exists here is in *discipline and timing*, not in a fact the market lacks.
