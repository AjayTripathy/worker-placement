# PITCH — CLSK — PROPOSED VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: No entry at $11.52. Move to event-gated WATCH — the buy trigger is a definitive Texas lease (any tranche of the 885MW exclusivity) or the tenant being named with a credit rating, NOT the $1.9B debt close; the ~$8.50–9.00 price band is proposed but flagged, because a bare price trigger on a bitcoin-levered stock fires exactly when the thesis has gotten worse.**

## What we believe

CleanSpark is a bitcoin miner in the middle of converting itself into a data-center landlord. The conversion is real: it has signed a 20-year lease on a 175MW data center in Sandersville, Georgia with an unnamed tenant the company describes as a "high investment grade global technology company" ([lease press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cleanspark-secures-twenty-year-lease-with-high-investment-grade-global-technology-company-for-data-center-in-sandersville-georgia-302824483.html)), and the same press release discloses that this tenant has also taken a letter of intent plus exclusivity over CleanSpark's entire Texas portfolio — 718 acres, up to 885MW, five times the signed project.

But the mining business that funds the interim is impaired at today's bitcoin price: revenue down 30.5% year over year, adjusted EBITDA of −$113M, a nine-month net loss of $996.9M, and in July the company sold 98.8% of the coins it produced (579 of 586) — it is living hand to mouth. Our central belief, which both of our internal review benches converged on: **even if the $1.9B of project debt closes tomorrow, the fully-funded Sandersville project plus the company's actual debt load does not justify upside from $11.52.** At reasonable real-estate valuations, the funded 175MW project is worth roughly $4.50–$12.40 per share before subtracting a net-debt drag of about $3.50 per share. The market price already assumes roughly $1.4B of additional value for the mining/power platform. So a debt-closing headline is not a buying signal. The thing that would actually change the math is the Texas expansion converting from an option into a signed lease — that multiplies the project pipeline rather than merely funding the one already priced in.

## What the market believes

The stock trades at $11.52 (live broker print; the data pack's $12.09 Yahoo quote was superseded), down 49% from its 52-week high of $23.61 but up 51% from its $8.00 low — the low predates the Georgia lease announcement, so this is a name that has already re-rated once on the conversion story. At this price the market is: (1) giving the company credit for the funded Georgia project, (2) imputing about $1.4B (~$0.78M per contracted megawatt across 1.8GW under contract) to the power platform — at or slightly below what comparable miner-to-datacenter conversions have fetched, and (3) treating the $1.8B of convertible notes as straight debt, correctly, since the conversion prices ($24.05 on the 2030 notes) are far above the stock. The market is not paying much, if anything, for the Texas option — but it is also not pricing the mining business's cash bleed as an existential problem.

## Why we might have edge

- **The prize-table arithmetic.** Most coverage frames the question as "does the $1.9B financing close?" Our benches showed that question doesn't gate the upside: fully funded, the Georgia project plus net debt lands at or below today's price at any defensible capitalization rate. Anyone buying the debt-close headline is buying an event that resolves nothing. We won't.
- **The treasury is smaller and more tied-up than the marketing suggests.** The monthly update advertises 13,931 BTC / $814.9M; the audited balance sheet shows 12,205 BTC / $714.3M on the bitcoin line, and the update's own footnote discloses that 4,070 coins — **29% of the marketed treasury** — are posted as collateral or receivables against derivative trades. The truly-current liquid cushion is $794.7M, not the ~$1.02B a casual read gives ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/827876/000119312526338382/clsk-20260630.htm)).
- **A 2028 debt wall the "due 2030/2032" label hides.** Holders of the $650M 2030 convertible notes have a one-time right to force repurchase at par in June 2028 — the same year rent from Georgia is just starting (the facility reaches service in late 2027). The debt collides with the ramp, not after it.
- **Capital allocation cuts against the story.** The company spent $463.2M buying back 30.4M shares at an average of ~$15.24 — now 24% underwater — during quarters of negative EBITDA, while facing a $1.75–2.1B construction bill with no project debt closed and no at-the-market equity program in place.
- **The upside everyone leans on is partially sold away.** 350 of July's 579 coins sold went out via exercised call options — the treasury is systematically short calls, capping the bitcoin-recovery scenario that bulls (and our own benches) treat as the main upside engine.

## Why it might be priced in

Honest symmetry: the lease re-rate already happened ($8 → $11.52), and the mining impairment is fully telegraphed in monthly press releases, so neither is new information. The ~$0.78M-per-contracted-megawatt the market is imputing to the platform is at or below the going rate for these conversions — the platform may be fairly priced, not overpriced, which weakens the short-leaning half of our math (and we concede no rigorous comparable-transaction pull was run; that number is plausible, not verified). The 885MW Texas exclusivity is disclosed in the same public press release we cite — the market can read it too, and if the tenant is named (behavior like taking exclusivity over an entire 718-acre portfolio looks like hyperscaler land-banking, not a weak credit), the stock may gap through our entry bands before we act.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry at $11.52** (red: reject, 8/10 conviction; blue: watch with gates, 7/10). Per the benches' own math, these are the **reopen bands**:

- **Reopen A — event (primary):** A definitive Texas lease on **any tranche of the 885MW exclusivity**, disclosed by 8-K or press release. This is the tradeable event — it multiplies the project pipeline rather than funding the one already priced. Do not buy the headline blind: re-run the valuation table with the new megawatts and re-adjudicate inside the filing window.
- **Reopen B — event:** The Sandersville **tenant named with a public credit rating**. An A-rated hyperscaler at 17% yield-on-cost re-rates the entire table.
- **Reopen C — price, FLAGGED:** ≤ **$8.50–9.00**, where the funded-175MW valuation plus the encumbered-treasury floor roughly equals the price and the Texas option comes free. **Caution for the adjudicator:** this stock is a 1.0-beta proxy for bitcoin, so a bare price trigger fires precisely when bitcoin has fallen and the mining leg is worse — the gate self-triggers on a deteriorated thesis. Proposal: condition it (e.g., price ≤ $9.00 **with** bitcoin ≥ ~$60k / mining margin intact at trigger) or disarm it in favor of the event gates.
- **Explicit non-trigger:** the $1.9B project-debt close, alone, is **not** an entry signal at any price near current levels.
- **Sizing if any gate opens:** the benches gave no number; given the unhedged bitcoin exposure and the short-call overhang on the treasury, we propose a capped starter of no more than ~0.5–0.75% of book at re-adjudication, not before.
