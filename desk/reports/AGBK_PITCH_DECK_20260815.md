# PITCH — AGBK — PROPOSED VERDICT

Company: AGI Inc ("Agibank"), a Brazilian consumer lender listed in the US (SEC CIK 2081206). Price $6.14, essentially at its 52-week low of $6.07 and down ~50% from its high of $12.21 (Yahoo close, 2026-08-14). We hold no position and have no orders.

## What we believe

Agibank's second-quarter results (reported 2026-08-05) show a business spending heavily to grow while its core profitability erodes:

- **Pre-tax profit collapsed ~69% year over year** — R$115.0M vs ~R$368M for the quarter. Our line-by-line bridge of the financial statements attributes essentially all of the decline to two items: loan-loss provisions up R$210.7M and selling/admin costs up R$139.2M. Part of that is a mechanical artifact of fast growth — accounting rules force lenders to book expected losses on new loans up front, before those loans have earned much — and management says as much in the release.
- **But the growth-accounting excuse doesn't cover everything.** Net interest income (what the bank earns on lending, after funding costs) was essentially **flat (+1.0% year over year) while the loan book grew 21.2%**. New loans earn interest from day one, so flat income on a much bigger book means the earnings per loan fell roughly 17%. That is genuine, present-tense margin compression — driven by the shift toward safer, lower-yielding secured lending (88% of the book and rising) — and it was the single most important finding of this review. Neither the original thesis nor the prosecution had it; it emerged from reading the statements directly.
- **Reported net income is propped up by an unusual tax benefit.** The effective tax rate was *negative* 74% this quarter — the company earned a tax credit — which it attributes to parking IPO proceeds in tax-favored instruments. The tax line swung by roughly R$180M between the first and second quarters. Quarter-to-quarter net income is therefore not a meaningful measure of this business right now; pre-tax profit is.
- **The headline "21.6% return on equity, ~1× book" cheapness case is an illusion.** Equity grew 51% in six months because the February IPO added R$1.24B of fresh capital. Trailing returns were earned on the old, smaller equity base. On the full current equity of R$4.8B and a normalized tax rate, forward return on equity works out to roughly 7–10%. For a Brazilian bank, ~1.09× book against 7–10% returns is fair at best.

One important concession: the original thesis's flagship "structural" claim — that a new 1.99%/month government rate ceiling caps exactly the product growing 48% per quarter — **was wrong and is struck**. Primary sources show the cap applies only to loans where a worker voluntarily pledges their FGTS savings as collateral; that FGTS-collateralized product *shrank* 6.7% in the quarter and is ~3% of the book. The fast grower is uncapped private payroll lending, itself only 3.8% of the book. The margin problem is real, but it comes from business mix, not regulation.

## What the market believes

The stock has been cut in half since the February IPO and sits within 1% of its all-time low, so the market has already priced in substantial disappointment — plausibly the pre-tax profit collapse itself, which was disclosed on August 5. The 180-day IPO lockup expired on 2026-08-10; because no resale registration has been filed, pre-IPO holders can only sell gradually under Rule 144 volume limits, and the tape appears to be absorbing that steady trickle of supply. We could not read short interest or positioning data this session (our brokerage data connection was denied), so we genuinely do not know how crowded either side of this trade is.

## Why we might have edge

- Every load-bearing number above comes from the company's own SEC-filed interim statements and press release ([financial statements exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2081206/000129281426004073/ex99-1.htm); [press release exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2081206/000129281426004070/ex99-1.htm)), reviewed by EY, not from broker summaries.
- The flat-interest-income-on-a-growing-book finding required combining two footnotes and does not appear in the company's own framing, which emphasizes client growth and "upfront expected-loss provisions."
- We caught a disclosure inconsistency: on the earnings call, management suggested the tax rate "normalizes to positive" later this year; the written release instead calls the tax benefit "a feature of our capital management." When the spoken and written stories differ, we treat that as information.
- We know exactly what to grade next quarter on: management pre-committed to "sequentially higher income." We will grade that promise on pre-tax profit and on interest income versus loan growth — the two lines that can't be flattered by the tax credit.

## Why it might be priced in

- A 50% drawdown to the IPO low is a lot of bad news absorbed already. The pre-tax collapse is public; anyone reading the release saw it.
- If the provision build really is mostly growth accounting, pre-tax profit mechanically recovers as loan vintages mature — the bull case requires no heroics, just patience, and the market may re-rate quickly on the first clean quarter.
- The tax shield, whatever its accounting aesthetics, is real cash benefit and may persist, supporting reported earnings.
- We have no positioning data (see gap above), so we cannot rule out that the bear case is the crowded side.
