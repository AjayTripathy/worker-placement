# PITCH — TPG — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no entry on this record: the long case survives the attack on the merits but has no stated fair value and no price gate, so we hold flat at $49.87 and re-court on the September TCAP filing and a management-fee-multiple comparison.

---

## What we believe

TPG Inc. (the listed alternative asset manager, ~$327B under management) is being lumped into a fear that structurally does not reach it.

The fear across the private-credit complex in 2026 is a run on *redeemable* money: the semi-liquid, retail-distributed credit funds that promise quarterly liquidity are hitting their repurchase caps. Ares' $22.6B non-traded fund (ASIF) limited withdrawals again — 14.4% of shares requested in Q2, against a ~5%/quarter cap. That is a real, visible, repeated event, and it has re-priced the whole sponsor group.

TPG's exposure to that channel is one vehicle: TCAP, with $2.69B of net assets. That is 0.8% of TPG's assets under management. Almost all of the rest of TPG's credit business (Angelo Gordon / Twin Brook direct lending) is drawdown fund capital — money that is legally locked and carries **no redemption right at all**. Investors cannot ask for it back, so the run mechanism has nothing to act on.

So: the transmittable base of the fear is roughly TCAP alone, and TCAP is (a) tiny, (b) still net-raising — net assets went $2,384.9M → $2,687.5M in six months *after* paying out repurchases, and shares outstanding rose 106.6M → 109.7M in six weeks.

Meanwhile the underlying business printed a strong quarter. Q2 2026 GAAP revenue was **$1,841.4M vs $920.5M** a year earlier, and accrued-but-unrealized carried interest ("capital allocation-based income") went **$351.5M → $1,136.5M**. Diluted EPS $0.39.
Primary source: [TPG 10-Q, Q2 2026 income statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1880661/000188066126000049/R4.htm)

The stock is $49.87, 27% below its 52-week high of $68.56, and only +1.8% above where it closed on the day it reported.

## What the market believes

Two things, and they are not the same thing.

1. **Sell-side:** 16 covering analysts, all Buy, average price target $60.50, ~16x forward earnings. The street is not bearish. It broadly agrees TPG is not damaged.
2. **The tape:** short interest is ~12.3M shares, roughly 7.7% of the 160.1M Class A shares (~8.5% of tradable float) — high for a name every analyst likes. And the stock will not hold a rally: it spiked +8.5% on 8/13 (to $55.21) on no company filing other than a passive ownership amendment, then gave the entire move back — at $49.87 it now trades *below* the pre-spike close of $50.88.

So the market's revealed belief is: the earnings are fine, but somebody keeps selling. The most likely explanation is mechanical supply — TPG's partners convert private units into listed Class A shares on a recurring schedule (6.0M on 5/29/26, 7.0M on 8/14/26; ~13.0M so far in 2026 after ~35.9M in 2025). That is ~17% annual growth in the listed share count, not from issuing new stock but from existing owners moving into the tradable class.

## Why we might have edge

- **The sign of the TCAP fact.** Both of our benches agreed TCAP is 0.8% of assets. Our attacking bench read that as "too small to prove anything." That is backwards: because locked drawdown capital cannot be redeemed, 0.8% is not a *sample* of the exposed money — it is approximately *all* of it. Small exposure is the thesis, not a weak version of it. This is a reasoning inversion that a screen sorting sponsors by "private credit exposure" would make the same way.
- **Fund-structure literacy.** Comparing TCAP's ~2% repurchase rate to ASIF's 14.4% without adjusting for fund age and size is what the sell-side note does. We know that comparison is contaminated (below) — but we also know *why*, which lets us read the September filing correctly instead of reacting to a headline number.
- **A visible, dated, near-term resolution.** TCAP publishes its repurchase results on a roughly quarterly cadence (5 Mar 2026, 2 Jun 2026 → next expected early-to-mid September). Most of the group's fear has no scheduled resolution; this one does.
- **The rally is unpaid.** As of $49.87 we would be buying at essentially the post-earnings price, not chasing.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated plainly, because this is the stronger half of the argument today:

- **We are not variant with the street.** 16 Buys, $60.50 target. Our "damage-absent" conclusion is consensus. Being right alongside everyone is not edge.
- **We have no fair value.** Neither bench produced one. Without a number, "cheap" is an assertion, and a price gate cannot be set honestly. This alone is disqualifying under our own rules.
- **The TCAP series is genuinely deteriorating.** The actual repurchase schedule reads 0.40% → 1.70% → 1.30% → **2.10%** (June 2, 2026). The bull brief's "≤2% since inception, 100% honored" was a cumulative statistic that hid a rising latest print. TCAP may simply be earlier in the same cycle as ASIF, not immune to it.
- **The September catalyst is asymmetric against us.** A benign print (~2%, fully honored) is already what everyone assumes; only a bad print carries new information. We would be buying ahead of an event whose good branch pays nothing.
- **The balance sheet moved the wrong way and nobody explained it.** Debt obligations went $1,722.5M → $2,343.7M in the first half (+$621M) against $944.7M of cash, while total equity fell $394M. How much of that sits at consolidated fund vehicles versus the parent is unresolved.
- **Persistent share supply.** ~17%/yr growth in listed shares from partner conversions is a real headwind that recurs on a schedule.
- **Crowded, not undiscovered.** Sixteen analysts raised targets after the print. The refutation we would be paid for has already been published.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say do not initiate on this record. So this section states reopen conditions, not an order.**

The reason is not the price — it is that no one produced a fair value. Under our own rule, a thesis with no stated fair value gets no price gate. Everything below is conditional and takes its numbers from the benches' own arithmetic.

**Current position: none. Open orders: none. Pre-catalyst stance: flat.**

**Reopen Band A — starter, requires an unrun test to pass first**
- Trigger: price **≤ $50.00** *and* a fee-earnings comparison shows TPG at **≥25% discount** to Ares / Blackstone / Apollo on management-fee profits alone (i.e., the discount sits in the recurring fee stream, not in uncertain carry).
- Size: **0.5% of book**, one tranche.
- Note honestly: the price half is already satisfied at $49.87. The valuation half has never been run. **We are not entitled to the entry until it is.**

**Reopen Band B — add, requires the September event to clear**
- Trigger: TCAP's next repurchase disclosure prints **≤2.0% of shares, fully honored, with the repurchase payable flat or lower than $52.6M**, and price ≤ $52.00.
- Size: add **0.5%**, taking the position to **1.0% of book maximum**.
- The 1.0% ceiling holds until a fair value exists. No fair value, no larger size.

**Disqualifiers — no entry, close the file for one quarter**
- TCAP's September disclosure prints **>2.5%**, or introduces any pro-ration or gating language.
- Price closes **below $43.66** (the 7/31 base from which the entire post-earnings move began). That would mean the re-rating fully unwound and something we do not understand is driving it. Re-court from scratch; do not average down mechanically.

**Paid-away condition**
- Price **above $52.02** (the 8/18 close) before the fee-earnings test is run: the entry was in the dispersion, and the dispersion is gone. Stand down rather than chase.

**Exits / trims:** none defined, because we hold nothing and have no fair value. The street's $60.50 target is *not* our fair value and must not be used as an exit. If we enter under Band A, an exit level is owed within that same session.

**Catalyst dates**
- **~early-to-mid September 2026** — TCAP quarterly repurchase disclosure. **Date inferred** from the March 5 / June 2 cadence; **not confirmed** by any company announcement. This is the decisive near-term event.
- **2026-11-04** — TPG Q3 earnings. **Derived from a data vendor, not confirmed** by a TPG press release. ~53 trading days out; not near enough to change today's stance.
