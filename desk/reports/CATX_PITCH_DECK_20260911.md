## What we believe

Perspective Therapeutics is a pre-revenue radiopharmaceutical company with three clinical programs built on the same alpha-emitting isotope (lead-212). At $2.815 the whole company is worth about $321M on the 114.15M share cover count, or about $340M if you count the 6.598M pre-funded warrants from the February raise as real shares (they are — they cost almost nothing to exercise). Against $237M of cash at 6/30/26, that means the market is paying roughly **$84M–$103M** for three clinical programs plus whatever isotope-manufacturing capacity actually exists.

Put in plain terms: using the red bench's own valuation settings (3x peak sales, 6 years of discounting at 12%, 30% chance of success, 120.75M share-equivalents), today's price implies the market is paying for about **$226M of risked peak sales across all three programs combined**. That is a low bar for a platform whose lead indication (SSTR2 neuroendocrine tumors) already contains multi-hundred-million-dollar approved drugs.

We also believe three things that cut against us, and we believe them just as firmly:
1. The burn is ~$34–38M per quarter, not the $26.5M the original brief used. Cash runs out in early 2028 and the company will almost certainly raise in **mid-2027 — before any Phase 3 result**.
2. The only efficacy number anyone quotes (43% response rate, 10 of 23) is the **longest-follow-up half of a 46-patient cohort**. The other 23 patients' efficacy has never been reported. That is the single most load-bearing unknown in the file.
3. A direct competitor — AlphaMedix, same isotope, same target, same indication — already **hit all Phase 2 primary endpoints, holds Breakthrough Therapy designation, and is funded by Sanofi/Orano Med** ([Sanofi release, 2025-10-08](https://www.sanofi.com/en/media-room/press-releases/2025/2025-10-08-05-00-00-3163053)). CATX has not activated a Phase 3 site.

So the belief is narrow: **the price is cheap against the asset, but the cheapness is not yet buyable, because the one number that would justify it is embargoed until October 19.**

## What the market believes

The market believes two contradictory things at once, and that contradiction is the only real divergence in this file.

- **Sell-side:** 15 analysts, "Strong Buy," median price target ~$12.93–13.00, range $6–18. Forward consensus EPS −$1.09. Hedge funds hold ~54.7% of the register.
- **The tape:** down 54% from the 52-week high of $6.16, 44% above the low of $1.955, and 48% of all share-equivalents sit underwater from a **$3.79** February book ([Feb-2026 pricing 8-K EX-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/728387/000119312526035508/catx-ex99_1.htm)).

The tape is pricing ~$226M of risked peak sales. The analysts are pricing a partnered, licensed NET franchise. Both cannot be right. The market's operative belief — the one that sets the price — is that this is a **second mover behind a funded competitor, with a dilutive raise landing before the data that would justify the valuation.** We think that belief is roughly correct today and possibly wrong after 10/19.

## Why we might have edge

1. **The divergence is real and has a named mechanism.** The street prices a partnered franchise; the price prices a second mover at 30% probability of success. A 3–4x gap between published targets and a conservative funded fair value is not "crowding" — it is dispersion with a mechanism. Our blue bench overturned the red bench's "we agree with the street" kill on exactly this ground: analyst count is a sizing input, never a kill.
2. **The structure is unusually asymmetric in *cash* terms.** Net cash is ~70% of the price. We are not paying much for the science. That is a real (if decaying) cushion — one that most microcap biotech setups do not offer.
3. **The competitor cuts both ways and the market has only priced one side.** Sanofi/Orano Med funding a 212Pb SSTR2 asset validates the mechanism and creates an M&A comparable. Reported long-term renal insufficiency and achalasia in the AlphaMedix data ([Targeted Oncology/NANETS coverage](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/dr-strosberg-highlights-positive-212pb-dotamtate-data-in-gep-nets)) is precisely the axis CATX's own vetted deck markets (stable creatinine, n=68). A tolerability differentiator inside a validated mechanism is an underpriced path.
4. **Denominators are not like-for-like.** AlphaMedix's 60% figure is PRRT-naïve patients; CATX's cohort is PRRT-mixed. Anyone reading "60% beats 43%" is comparing two different populations.
5. **We know exactly what would resolve this, and it has a date.** That is rarer than it sounds.

## Why it might be priced in

1. **The competitor is specialist-known.** Our own red bench tagged this kill CONSENSUS. Every radiopharma specialist knows AlphaMedix has BTD and met its endpoints. We are not early to that.
2. **The burn is public.** The company itself guides cash "into late 2027," which implies ~$37M/quarter. The market can do that division. A mid-2027 raise is not a secret.
3. **The cheapness *is* the published consensus view.** "Cash plus a free pipeline" is what the Strong Buy ratings say, and that view has been wrong from $6.16 to $2.815. Being right for the same reason as a crowd that is already losing is not edge; at best it is timing.
4. **The selection problem in the 43% is visible to anyone who reads the footnote.** The 95% confidence interval on 10/23 runs roughly 23%–66%. A third venue already cites 39%. The market may simply be discounting a number it doesn't trust.
5. **The one thing not priced in is the one thing we can't see:** the second-half Cohort-2 data. That is why this is a gate, not a trade.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: isotope feedstock and regulatory-silent risk.**
The entire platform depends on a lead-212 supply chain (Th-228 → Ra-224 → Pb-212 generators). Who supplies the parent isotope, on what contract term, and at what allocation is **not in any filing we can pull**. Orano Med — the competitor's own sponsor-side partner — is simultaneously one of the world's few Pb-212 producers. A feedstock allocation change, a generator qualification failure, or a CMC deficiency would reach us only as an after-the-fact trial-pause press release. The same applies to an FDA clinical hold, an IND deficiency letter, or a private partner walk-away: none of these are filed before they are announced.
*Unknown-unknown class, named:* **PRIVATE-COUNTERPARTY / REGULATORY-SILENT** — specifically *single-point upstream input concentration controlled by a competitor-affiliated supplier*. There is no sensor, no satellite, no XBRL tag, and no base rate we trust for this. We cannot arm against it; we can only size for it. This is the reason full size is never appropriate here regardless of how good the data is.
