# PITCH — ARX — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION): Buy a half-size starting position (~0.5% of book) near $12.30–12.50 before the August 13 report, sized down for insider selling and an unresolved regulatory rumor, with adds and exits keyed to the third-party premium number in the print — red bench dissents and would wait until after the report.

## What we believe

Accelerant (ARX) runs a marketplace that connects specialty insurance underwriters with capital. The bear story that has cut the stock 59% from its high is that the marketplace is partly an illusion: a large share of the premium flowing across it comes from Hadron, an insurance carrier affiliated with Accelerant — the company buying its own volume. We believe that story is going stale. Premium from carriers *other than* Hadron has grown from $61M to $275M over five quarters (verified letter-perfect against page 10 of the company's own [first-quarter presentation](https://s205.q4cdn.com/859787934/files/doc_presentations/2026/May/13/1Q-2026-Accelerant-Presentation-vF.pdf)), Hadron's share of the mix has fallen from 67% to 41%, and management guides it to 35–40% for the full year. Meanwhile the terrifying headline loss (−$6.49 per share over the trailing year) is almost entirely a $1.38B one-time, non-cash accounting expense triggered by settling insider profit interests at the July 2025 IPO — it was offset by an equal capital contribution and says nothing about the ongoing business. Strip it and the company trades at roughly 9x its adjusted earnings while the genuinely third-party side of the business scales.

One honest amendment forced by our own court: the original thesis also claimed Hadron's *dollar* volume had turned down. That claim is struck — the company's own full-year guidance implies Hadron premium of $805–920M in 2026 versus a $188M first quarter, i.e. management plans for Hadron dollars to keep growing. The clean claim is that the non-Hadron side is scaling, only that.

## What the market believes

The market is pricing a circular-volume story with a regulatory tail. The stock sits at $12.30–12.46 against a 52-week range of $9.18–$30.48. Contributing beliefs, each with some basis: insiders have been steady sellers since the lockup opened (six weekly Rule 144 notices from mid-July through August 3, plus an 80,000-share sale by a CEO-linked entity at $12.06 under a pre-set plan); a blog-level report claims a state insurance regulator is examining the company (uncorroborated anywhere else, and no company disclosure exists); underwriting profitability faded last quarter (underwriting EBITDA fell from $11M to $7M); and the second-quarter earnings guide of $60–66M is flat-to-down against the $66M just printed — so the market sees no near-term earnings acceleration to force a re-rating.

## Why we might have edge

The core dislocation claim survived a hostile prosecution. The red bench attacked the data's provenance and lost — the mix-shift series is real, read directly from the primary source. Its most dangerous charge, that Accelerant-sponsored Flywheel vehicles sit *inside* the "non-Hadron" bar and fake the diversification, was refuted on the company's own taxonomy: Flywheel I/II are collateralized *reinsurers* funded by outside investors, and the disclosed series measures carriers' *direct* written premium, which a reinsurance vehicle cannot inflate. And the red bench's biggest data escalation — treating the −$6.49 GAAP loss as a $1.3B chasm the bulls were hiding — was overturned as an accounting artifact of the IPO. If the market is anchored on that same headline loss and the same stale mix narrative, the gap between the $275M-and-growing third-party number and a price 59% off the high is our edge. The August 13 report is a near-term chance for the market to see the updated number.

## Why it might be priced in

Three honest concessions. First, the people with the best view of the mix line are selling at exactly this price — the insider distribution is confirmed and ongoing, and it directly contradicts "the market just hasn't noticed." Second, our original tripwire was soft: management's own guidance nearly guarantees the Hadron *percentage* falls into the 35–40% band, so a headline "mix improved" print proves little by itself — the market may already treat the percentage as choreographed. Third, the earnings guide is flat-to-down for the quarter, the underwriting margin faded, net revenue retention slipped to 116%, and a regulatory rumor — however thin — is unresolvable before the print. The red bench weighed these and recommended owning nothing until after the report; that dissent is part of the record.
