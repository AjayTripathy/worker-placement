# PITCH — FISV — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION): Do not buy now. Log as WATCH. Reopen at the Q3 report (2026-10-28, date unconfirmed) or the moment any SEC filing puts a dollar figure on the 2025 one-time revenue base — both benches rejected entry, red at 7/10 conviction, blue sustaining the rejection.

## What we believe

Fiserv is a payments and bank-technology company whose stock has fallen 61% from its 52-week high of $140.42 to $54.39, and now trades at roughly 7.5x the midpoint of management's own 2026 adjusted-EPS guidance of $7.20–$7.40. The original pitch believed three things: (1) the collapse in the Financial Solutions segment (down 8% in Q2) is mostly an optical artifact — last year's base contained one-time revenue, and an Argentina currency drag that is now rolling off; (2) the underlying volume business is healthy (Clover point-of-sale revenue $570M, up 13% excluding Argentina and one-timers; payment volume $75B); and (3) the stock is cheap both absolutely and against peer Global Payments.

After the adversarial review, what we *actually* believe is narrower: the absolute cheapness is real, the Argentina drag is genuinely unwinding (visible on the balance sheet, not just in management's telling), but the central claim — that the "one-time revenue" explanation can be verified from filings before the market sees Q3 — is false. The explanation exists only in management's spoken remarks. We cannot check it, so we cannot own it yet.

## What the market believes

The market believes management has lost credibility. Guidance has been cut three times; Q2 adjusted EPS fell 26% year-over-year to $1.84; organic revenue growth guidance is now roughly flat to down 1% (all confirmed in the company's own earnings release: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/798354/000079835426000028/fisvq226earningsrelease.htm). At 7.5x guided earnings, the price says the market treats the guidance itself as suspect and the Financial Solutions decline as possibly structural — bank clients leaving, not accounting noise. The market is also unwilling to pay for a bridge ("here's what growth looks like excluding Argentina and one-timers") that the company will only present in slides and on calls, never in an SEC filing.

## Why we might have edge

- **The Argentina drag is verifiably ending, from primary documents.** The blue bench found this in the 10-Q, and it is the strongest surviving pro-thesis fact: the anticipation-funding balance tied to Argentina shrank from $1,223M to $1,007M, and Argentina currency-remeasurement losses collapsed from $64M to $9M in the first half. That is a headwind mechanically disappearing, not a management story.
- **The relative-valuation panic may be overdone at the level of the whole company.** The volume metrics (Clover, payment volume) are growing, the company retired $1.41B of debt for $1.23B (a $154M gain), and it bought back $300M of stock in the first half. None of that is what a business in structural collapse does.
- **A cheap, falsifiable test is coming.** Management claims Financial Solutions organic growth exits Q4 at a positive rate. Q3 either shows the sequential path toward that or it doesn't. If it does, the stock is a single-digit multiple on stabilizing earnings.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The comp-based "cheapness" claim was wrong on our side.** The pitch said Fiserv trades at "half Global Payments' multiple." The blue bench verified Global Payments' own guidance ($13.60–$13.80 adjusted EPS, per its press release: https://investors.globalpayments.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/514/global-payments-reports-second-quarter-2026-results): GPN trades near 6.4–6.7x versus Fiserv's 7.45x. Fiserv is at *parity or a premium* to its closest peer, not a discount. The relative-value leg of the thesis is dead.
- **The core bullish explanation is unverifiable by design.** The red bench searched SEC full-text: the phrase "nonrecurring revenue" has appeared in **zero** Fiserv filings, ever (https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22nonrecurring%20revenue%22&ciks=0000798354), and "periodic revenue" last appeared in 2018. The Q2 release quantifies nothing; the 10-Q footnotes don't isolate it; management declined to give the dollar amount on the call. Two company venues chose not to decompose it. If the explanation were as clean as claimed, publishing it would be easy. The choice not to is itself information.
- **Cash conversion was deteriorating before the guidance cuts.** First-half operating cash flow fell 10% ($2,313M → $2,082M), and the Q1 operating-cash-flow series has fallen three straight years (1,130 → 831 → 648, in $M) while adjusted EPS rose. The "adjusted" earnings were losing cash backing before the story broke.
- **Leverage narrows the gap further.** Total debt is $27.9B against roughly $30B of equity. On an enterprise-value basis, Fiserv is even less obviously cheap than the P/E suggests.
