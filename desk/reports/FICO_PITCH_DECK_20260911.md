# PITCH — FICO — VERDICT
**ADJUDICATED 2026-09-16 — FLAT into the pricing card (conviction 7/10).** The court found nothing to own at this price; the reopen gates below are the only path back.

## What we believe

FICO sells the credit score that the U.S. mortgage machine has been required to use. In 2026 it roughly doubled the per-score price on that card, and mortgage-origination revenue grew ~97% year over year on barely-growing loan volume. Price, not volume, is the engine.

On 2026-09-04 the housing regulator (FHFA) told Fannie and Freddie to accept VantageScore 4.0 from every lender, and Equifax immediately put VantageScore 4.0 on the shelf at $4.50 a score through end-2027. So the question is no longer "will FICO lose units" — it's "what can FICO charge next year with a $4.50 substitute legally usable everywhere."

Three beliefs, stated at the confidence we actually hold them:

1. **The damage is deferred, not absent.** Nothing has been disclosed since the order because nothing has had a chance to be disclosed. The guide that the market is multiplying ($42.43 non-GAAP FY26) was raised twice against the *old* regime and expires with the 2026 card.
2. **The valuation leg is genuinely intact.** At the pack tape of **$996.385** the stock is **23.5x** guided FY26 EPS, a **−50.1%** drawdown, **+14.4%** off the 52-week low, ~**$22.6B** cap on 22.70M implied diluted shares. A +50% move needs **$1,494.58 = 35.2x** — still *below* the ~47x the tape paid at the high. The prize doesn't require a new multiple regime, only a partial unwind.
3. **Even the bull case has a growth problem.** If the card holds at $10, FY27 mortgage revenue laps a doubled card and decelerates to unit growth (low single digit). 23.5x survives that; a growth story does not. That is the finding neither bench made first and it cuts against our own bull trigger.

## What the market believes

The tape has taken out half the equity value over roughly a year, and the 9/4 order moved it another 15–18% in a day. That is not one panic gap; it is repeated, informed repricing — while management raised guidance twice. The most defensible read of consensus: *the mortgage price card is a one-time, regulator-revocable windfall, so capitalize it at a low multiple and assume the 2027 card comes down.*

Two things the market is **not** obviously doing: (a) it has not made a new low on the 9/4 order — the pack's 52-week low of **871.13** sits *below* the ~$927 intraday of 9/4 (date unverified, see open item 4); and (b) it is not treating this as an orphan — FICO is a well-covered large cap, so neither "nobody's looked" nor "everyone's crowded" is available to us as an edge claim.

## Why we might have edge

- **We priced the right variable.** The thesis under review bounded the damage by assuming a 20% unit loss at an unchanged $10 card. Price *is* the variable under attack. Repricing the same pulls to $4.50 is a −$625–627M revenue event ≈ **$21.5 pre-tax per share**, ≈ **$16.8 after tax (~40% of the guide)** before a single unit is lost. Knowing the damage runs on the price axis, not the volume axis, is the whole trade.
- **We think both benches' entry trigger is wrong, in both directions.** FICO's own 2026 card already offers **$4.95/score + $33 per funded loan**. Break-even against the $10 legacy card is **33 ÷ 5.05 = 6.53 score pulls per funded loan** — inside the plausible tri-merge × re-pull × pull-through range. So a 2027 headline of "$4.95" may be *revenue-neutral by design*, not a cut. The testable quantity is **realized revenue per funded loan**, and almost nobody is framing it that way.
- **Channel retaliation is an under-modelled mechanism.** FICO's direct-license program cut out the three bureaus that distribute its score; one of them promptly guaranteed the substitute's price for two years. FICO now depends for distribution on counterparties it disintermediated and who are paid to push the alternative. The counter-edge (which cuts our way): the score is a small slice of an ~$80–100 all-in tri-merge report, so lender sensitivity to a $5 score delta is likely far lower than full-substitution math implies.
- **Timing edge, not analytic edge.** The binary publishes on a *known kind of date* (~Oct 1 analog) and it is a press release, not a slow fundamental. We can be flat through it at zero carry cost and still buy the outcome we want.

## Why it might be priced in

- 23.5x for a business facing a regulator-blessed $4.50 substitute is not obviously cheap: if the street already models a $4.95 card, we are paying ~39x the damaged number. **We do not have FY27 consensus** (open item 3), so the claim "the market has discounted 3x the damage" is currently ungradeable — and by our own grading rule a gap cannot carry a verdict either way.
- Half the de-rate happened *before* 9/4. Serial informed selling against rising guidance is the market saying "the card is temporary" for months. That is exactly our thesis, already in the price.
- The marginal bid may have been the company. FICO repurchased **$1.96B in Q3 FY26 at an average of $1,120** (1.75M shares ≈ 27.8k shares/day) — i.e. management's own mark is **12.4% above today's tape**, currently about a **$216M** mark-to-market loss. Buying next to a price-insensitive buyer is comfortable; discovering that buyer was the whole float-clearing bid is not. Whether remaining authorization is cash- or debt-funded is **unverified** (open item 5).
- Negative book equity is treasury-stock arithmetic in a capital-light licensor, not a solvency tell — we are not counting that as a bear point, and we flag that our red bench leaned on it.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — the revenue-neutral trap in reverse (has a tripwire).** The 2027 card prints at $4.95 + a back-end fee, we read "back-end = neutral," buy the starter, and the realized revenue per funded loan turns out to be well below the $10-card equivalent because the per-funded-loan fee is discounted, capped, or applies to a narrower base. Tripwire: Q1 FY27 mortgage-origination revenue per funded loan vs MBA origination counts; card text itself (fee base, caps, effective date).

**Failure narrative 2 — comp exhaustion, the bull case losing money (has a tripwire).** Card holds at $10, we buy it as "all clear," and FY27 mortgage growth collapses to unit growth. The multiple doesn't crack, but the growth narrative does and the stock goes nowhere while carrying regulatory tail risk. Tripwire: FY27 guide at the Q4 print — anything under ~$50 non-GAAP EPS tells us management is not underwriting the card into a contract year.

**Failure narrative 3 — off-card bilateral repricing (NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE).** The published card holds at $10, we buy it, and FICO quietly repricess its largest lenders and bureau partners under confidential enterprise agreements — volume commitments, rebates, direct-license bundles — so realized price falls while the headline card never moves. There is no artifact: no 8-K, no card revision, no segment line that isolates it, and the first public evidence is a revenue miss we would learn about simultaneously with everyone else. **Unknown-unknown class: private contract renegotiation invisible to disclosure** (the published-price-as-fiction class). We cannot arm a sensor for this; the only mitigation is size, which is why the starter is capped at one third of a sleeve.

**Reflexive / structural leg.** Our own size is not the reflexive risk: a starter of **≤0.5% of book** is immaterial against FICO's daily turnover (ADV unverified, open item 7; working estimate 400–700k shares ≈ $400–700M notional, so our clip is well under 1% of a day). The reflexive risk belongs to the issuer: the buyback ran at ~4–7% of estimated ADV in Q3 and is the one price-insensitive bid in the name; if authorization is debt-funded and leverage tightens, the bid that has been absorbing momentum selling goes away at exactly the moment the card disappoints. Predicted flow around the catalysts is one-sided — quant momentum and index-relative sellers have been the marginal supply all year, and a card cut adds forced growth-mandate selling on top. Doctrine interactions: our **print-proximity** doctrine does *not* trigger (print ~36 trading days out) but the **binding catalyst** is ~13 trading days out, so the "no position inside a decisive print" rule has to be applied to the *card*, not the earnings date; the **orphan/priced-in** doctrine is inapplicable (well-covered large cap), which removes our usual excuse for tolerating an unverified consensus number; and the **damage-classification** doctrine reclassifies this DAMAGE-DEFERRED, which forbids a full sleeve pre-catalyst even though the valuation screen would allow it.
