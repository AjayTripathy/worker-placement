# PITCH — SBS — VERDICT

ADJUDICATED 2026-08-15 — NO POSITION, WATCH: we do not buy Sabesp today, but the reason we first gave for not buying it turned out to be wrong, so the case is being re-argued on a corrected record.

---

## What we believe

Sabesp is the water and sewer utility for the state of São Paulo, privatized in July 2024. The ADR (SBS) trades at $4.55, about 36% below its 52-week high and 9% off the 52-week low; the local shares (SBSP3) are at R$23.79.

Our original idea was simple: the stock has fallen a long way, so the expensive-ness that kept us out before must be gone. That idea is dead. The share price fell 31% over the year, but the company borrowed heavily at the same time — net debt went from about R$23bn to R$34.2bn, a 48% increase, with R$6.4bn of that added in the first six months of 2026 alone. When you buy a stock you are effectively buying the equity *and* assuming the debt. Add the two together and the whole business is valued at roughly R$115bn, or about 8.25 times a ~R$14bn annual cash-earnings run rate. The last time we looked at this name we said 7.4x was the level at which it stopped being expensive. So the enterprise is not cheaper than it was. On the adjusted earnings series it is more expensive. The share price fell; the price of the *business* did not.

What we now believe, after a third review that overturned part of our own case: this is not a broken company, and it is not a permanent value trap. It is a utility building an enormous asset base — the regulated asset base it earns a return on grew from R$11.0bn to R$19.0bn in six months — funded with debt, and paid for that build with a delay. The argument is about how long that delay is and whether 8.25x is a fair price for it. On our corrected reading the delay is roughly one year, not five. That makes the name materially more interesting than our first verdict implied, and it is why the case has been sent back rather than closed.

We also believe the profit picture got worse in a way we initially mis-explained. Adjusted operating cash earnings fell 3.2% on 6.7% higher adjusted revenue, so margin dropped from 64.2% to 58.3% — nearly six percentage points — because operating costs excluding depreciation rose 24.5%. We first told ourselves this was purely a financing-line problem. It was not; roughly half of it is operating.

## What the market believes

The market is bullish and well-informed. This is not a neglected stock. Analysts cover it heavily, the consensus target is about R$34 and XP's is R$38.30, against a R$23.79 share price — the street thinks the stock is roughly 40% too low. So there is no "nobody is looking" story here.

The market's reasoning, as best we can reconstruct it: the debt-funded build is real and will eventually be remunerated, so today's leverage is an investment, not a wound. Meanwhile the price has fallen because near-term cash earnings are going backwards — the financing line swung from −R$118m to −R$1,016m as debt and rates bit — and because there are open questions about how the regulator treats the company at the next big tariff reset.

What the market does *not* appear to believe is that the October governor's election matters to this stock. We assumed it did. We were wrong, and this is the single most important correction in the file.

## Why we might have edge

Three places where our work found something the tape does not obviously reflect:

**1. We read the actual signed contract, and the market's shorthand (and ours) was reading the wrong document.** The widely circulated version of the concession contract is the February 2024 *consultation draft*, which describes the asset base being revalued only at multi-year revision points. The signed August 2024 contract, Annex V clause 3.7, says the equilibrium tariff is updated **annually** during the first two cycles. Sabesp's own 20-F says the same in plain English: "In the first two cycles, the RAB will be updated annually." And the arithmetic corroborates it — the January 2026 tariff increase was 6.11% against trailing inflation of 4.26–4.68%, i.e. about 1.5 percentage points *above* inflation, which cannot happen under a pure inflation-only formula. The lag between spending capital and being paid for it is about a year, not "nothing until November 2029."

**2. We can price the election properly and most commentary cannot.** Prediction markets have Tarcísio at 93.5% and five polling houses cluster on a 15–19 point lead, roughly 3.5 standard deviations. That is a real signal. It is also uncollectable: at 93.5% you risk 14.4 units to win 1. And separately, the stock never priced the risk in the first place — see the next section.

**3. We have the leverage arithmetic done from the primary filing rather than from a screen.** We pulled the 2Q26 6-K itself ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1170858/000129281426004252/sbsitr2q26_6k.htm)) and completed the balance sheet past where our own evidence extract was truncated: borrowings R$5,289,831k current plus R$46,367,971k non-current, less R$4,243,477k cash and R$13,197,409k financial investments, gives R$34.2bn net debt — which ties exactly to management's own commentary of "R$34 billion in 2Q26 versus R$23 billion in 2Q25." Screens that quote a headline P/E on this name are describing a different security than the one you would actually own.

## Why it might be priced in

**The election gate — which was our headline reason to wait — collects nothing.** Four independent tests found no election sensitivity in the stock at all. A regression on election odds produces a maximum t-statistic of 1.09 (i.e. noise). The decisive test is the level test: roughly 35 points of Haddad-victory risk were retired over the period, and across that same window SBSP3 fell 30% and underperformed its peers Sanepar and Copasa by 19.4 points. A stock that was carrying an election discount goes *up* as that risk drains away; this one went down and lagged. Ten dated campaign events were checked and none moved the stock more than two standard deviations — including the day Haddad said he would "review the clauses" of the concession, on which Sabesp's own move was **+0.32%**. Total election content in the R$10.22 gap between consensus and spot: about **R$0.12**. Waiting for October buys us nothing, because there is no discount there to collect.

**The bull case is the consensus case.** Street targets 40% above spot mean the "paid-later asset base compounds into a re-rating" story is the *majority* view, not our discovery. If we are right about the annual updates, we are agreeing with people who already own it.

**And the genuine bear case is narrower and more specific than we first argued — but it is real, and it is not obviously in the price.** The allowed return is frozen at 11.91% real pre-tax until January 2030 and is not re-openable even under an extraordinary revision, so if capital costs rise the company eats it. There is an efficiency factor ("Factor U") that can cut up to 10% a year off the allowance. There are hard May and August asset-valuation cutoffs — capital spent on the wrong side of a date waits a full extra cycle. And the emergency revision valve only opens if solvency is actually compromised, which is not a valve you want to be relying on. That, not "no remuneration until 2029," is the honest short case.

## What remains unverified

- **The annual asset-base update rests on one arithmetic confirmation.** The contract clause and 20-F language are read and quoted; the *economic* effect is evidenced by a single tariff decision (Jan-2026, 6.11% versus 4.26–4.68% inflation). One observation is not a pattern.
- **The emergency revision clause.** The first team explicitly listed "any in-cycle extraordinary revision mechanism I missed" as an open item. We know it exists and is conditioned on solvency being compromised; we have not read it end to end.
- **Whether our old 7.4x benchmark was struck on reported or adjusted earnings.** On the reported series today's comparable is ~7.5x — essentially flat, not worse. The "no re-rating happened" conclusion holds either way; "the enterprise got more expensive" holds only on the adjusted series.
- **The ~R$14bn earnings run rate** is our annualisation, not a company figure. The valuation is proportionally sensitive to it.
- **Consensus R$34 / XP R$38.30** — dates and composition not verified; some may predate the 2Q26 report.
- **Polling** was read at the aggregate level (five houses, +15–19). We did not open the individual polls.
- **The construction-accounting revenue split** is inferred from the gap between reported and adjusted revenue rather than read off a disclosed reconciliation.
- **The concession contract PDF we first worked from** ([semil.sp.gov.br](https://semil.sp.gov.br/desestatizacaosabesp/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2024/02/Contrato-de-Concessao-1-1.pdf)) is the February 2024 consultation draft. It is cited here so the error is traceable, not as authority. The signed August 2024 text governs.
