# PITCH — FLUT — VERDICT

**ADJUDICATED 2026-08-07 — DECLINE. No position at any size until (a) one federal appeals court rules on the merits of whether federal law overrides state gambling law, AND (b) the September–November state regulator files read clean for the states whose tax rates did not change; re-open the case below ~$75 while that legal question is still open.**

Both benches independently reached REJECT at 8/10 conviction. This is not a close call that got rounded down — it is a decline on a legal coin-flip stacked on a levered balance sheet, and the fact that we *like* the business analysis is exactly why the file needed to be written down.

---

## What we believe

Flutter owns FanDuel, the largest sports betting business in the United States, plus a large international betting business (Paddy Power, Sky Bet, Sisal, PokerStars and others). The stock is $99.71 as of the evidence pack (2026-08-21, our broker feed), against a 52-week high of $309.32 — down 68%, and only 11% above its 52-week low of $89.76. At adjudication two weeks ago it was ~$93.65.

A collapse that size normally means one of two things: the market has panicked about a story that isn't true, or the business genuinely broke. We went in expecting the first and found the second.

**1. The scary story is real but small.** The story is that prediction exchanges — Kalshi, Polymarket, and now similar products from the betting companies themselves — let people bet on sports without a state gambling licence, without state gambling taxes, and at roughly a tenth of the price. That is a genuine competitive mechanism, not a fantasy: in June 2026 roughly 82–85% of Kalshi's activity was sports, and only about half a percent was politics. Polymarket's sports share went from 3% in 2024 to 58% in 2026. This is a sportsbook competitor wearing a financial-exchange licence.

But the size is single-digit. Three independent methods agree. The company says the effect is small and mostly *additional* betting rather than *stolen* betting. Eight states' monthly public betting reports imply the drag on the licensed market is about 1–1.5 percentage points of year-over-year growth. And reconciling exchange volume against licensed volume implies gross activity equal to 5–8% of the licensed market, of which roughly 70–80% is new demand rather than diverted demand. Different methods, same conclusion. **On this specific claim, the company is telling the truth.**

**2. And that is precisely why this is not a bargain.** If the scary story is small, then a 68% decline is not the market over-reacting to a story we can arbitrage. It is the market correctly pricing what is in the filings:

- FY25 profit guidance of $3.295bn at the peak; delivered ~$2.855bn. FY26 guided down twice more, to $2.655bn — the most recent cut, on 2026-08-05, was $210m and was entirely the US business, which was cut 21.6% in a single quarter.
- The international business — 83% of group profit — is running down 22% because the UK's tax on remote gaming went from 21% to 40% by statute on 1 April 2026. That's roughly a $320m hit this year before mitigation, about a quarter of which management expects to offset.
- Debt has gone from 3.7x to 4.3x profit. The share buyback has effectively stopped.
- To hit even the reduced full-year guidance, the US business has to earn ~$522m in Q4 alone — 69% of the entire year's US target, up 66% year over year — in a quarter where the NFL season starts a week late (about $50m of profit) and $270m of extra marketing spend lands.
- The CEO leaves 2026-09-30. The FanDuel CEO already left in May.

**3. There is a live legal binary on top of all of it.** Whether federal commodity law overrides state gambling law for these exchanges is genuinely unresolved. One appeals court gave Kalshi a narrow 2-1 win at a preliminary stage in April. The only *final* ruling on the merits went the other way — a Utah federal judge granted the state summary judgment on 2026-08-04. Three more appeals courts have heard argument and not ruled. A Supreme Court deadline in the New Jersey case falls 2026-09-03. A tribal-sovereignty claim from the Ho-Chunk Nation survived dismissal in May and reaches the same question by a different door.

**At 10x enterprise value to guided profit and a 2.8% owner-earnings yield, we are not being paid enough to underwrite that coin-flip at 4.3x leverage.** That is our whole thesis.

## What the market believes

Roughly what we believe, which is the problem. The market has already moved past the exchange-competition story: on days when the federal regulator does something that legitimises prediction markets, *both* Flutter and DraftKings rally double digits (DraftKings +11.3% on one such day). If the market were trading Flutter on fear of Kalshi, that would be impossible. It is trading Flutter on the earnings collapse above.

Where the market sits on the legal question, it sits in the middle — which is defensible, because the middle is where the evidence is. This name is widely covered, heavily owned, and thoroughly discussed. We should assume the consensus view is informed.

## Why we might have edge

Three things, and we should be honest that only the third is really an edge:

1. **We did the reconciliation nobody publishes.** The gap between "5–8% of the market is on exchanges" and "the licensed market only lost 1–1.5 points of growth" is not a contradiction — it is the finding. It means most exchange betting is *new*. Most commentary quotes one number or the other and reaches the wrong conclusion.
2. **We cohorted the state data by tax change.** Splitting states whose tax rates changed from states whose rates didn't is what separates "technology is eating this business" from "legislatures are taxing this business." The answer is mostly the second. Anyone reading national totals cannot see that.
3. **We have a free, decisive, dated test that runs before the next earnings report.** September–November state betting reports, on a clean NFL comparison, for the states where tax rates did *not* change (Michigan, Tennessee, Indiana, New York, Ohio). If those come in flat or better, the competitive drag is bounded and the case reopens on price alone. If they come in negative, the story is worse than the company says. Either way it costs nothing and it publishes before the November print.

The transferable lesson for our knowledge graph: *a company's own volume number is evidence about the company, never about its market.* FanDuel reported handle up 2.2% while the market underneath it was down 3.9% — share gain masquerading as market health. DraftKings has now stopped disclosing betting volume separately at all, blending it with prediction volume into one "$13.1B, +15%" line. Issuer volume metrics are degrading exactly where they are most load-bearing.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost certainly it is. Every element of our bear case comes from public filings, public statutes, public court dockets, and free state regulator reports. Our information arrived *after* the price moved, not before — at every single step. The risk first appeared in both companies' SEC filings in the week of 2025-08-07, which was the exact week the stock peaked, and the stock has tracked it down for a year since.

Against ourselves, the strongest bull argument is valuation arithmetic: if you value the international business at 9x profit and subtract $10.48bn of net debt, the remaining FanDuel stub is around 13x its reduced earnings — not obviously expensive for the largest player in a structurally growing market. At more conservative international multiples, FanDuel is around 16x, which is. That range is the real disagreement, and it is not resolvable without knowing whether the earnings guide is now a floor.

Two more genuine points for the bull side that we concede outright:

- **Exchanges can't kick out winners.** Kalshi can cap a user's overall position but can't refuse a specific bet from a sharp customer; a sportsbook can and does ban them. Over time that pulls the smartest customers *off* the books and leaves the books the profitable recreational pool. FanDuel's structural margin rising 40bps is consistent with exactly this.
- **The parlay profit pool is not being destroyed.** FanDuel's margin is going *up* on same-game-parlay adoption. The "exchanges destroy the high-margin product" version of the bear case is refuted.
