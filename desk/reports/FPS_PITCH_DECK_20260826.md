# PITCH — FPS — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — no position today; both benches land on FLAT at $32, and the disagreement is about *which* reason, not whether.

---

## What we believe

Forgent Power Solutions (NYSE: FPS, CIK 2080126) makes engineered-to-order electrical gear — switchgear, transformers — for data-center and utility customers out of Dayton, Minnesota. It is not a story stock. The business is real and the growth is real: quarterly revenue of $378.7M against $186.2M a year earlier, nine-month revenue of $958.4M against $515.6M, up 86%. We checked whether that growth was manufactured by acquisitions and it was not — all four deals (MGM, PwrQ, States, VanTran) closed before the fiscal year that forms the comparison base, so the comparison is like-for-like. Stock-based compensation is $5.5M over nine months, which is unusually small for a recent IPO and means the adjusted-earnings number is not inflated by paper pay. No customer is above 10% of nine-month revenue. The debt was just repriced *down* (SOFR+300 to SOFR+225 on a $600M term loan), which is the credit market voting yes.

So the honest belief is: **this is a good, undamaged, organically growing manufacturer that has fallen 50% from its high — and it is still too expensive.** Two things convince us of the second half:

1. **The multiple.** On a corrected fully diluted count of 304.7M units, the enterprise value is roughly $10.26B, or $10.46B once you include the $207.3M owed under the Tax Receivable Agreement. That is **32–34x this fiscal year's adjusted EBITDA and about 7x revenue**.
2. **The cash.** Nine-month operating cash flow was **$35.2M against $210.2M of adjusted EBITDA — 17% conversion, and *down* from $52.2M on half the revenue.** Receivables rose 72% and inventory 53%. This is what a 2.3x book-to-bill looks like in build-to-order equipment: the backlog is being funded out of the balance sheet. Enterprise value to annualized operating cash flow is roughly 219x.

The second point is the one nobody is talking about — not our benches' original brief, not the sell side, not any short report we found. The entire public conversation is bookings and backlog.

## What the market believes

The market believes the same fundamental story we do and has priced it accordingly. Ten analysts cover it, the consensus rating is Strong Buy, and the consensus target is **$59.30 against a $32 tape** — an 84% gap. The published explanation for the gap is exactly the one our own originating brief offered: the private-equity sponsor keeps selling, so the stock is depressed by supply rather than by anything wrong with the business.

The market is also, we think, quietly assuming the backlog converts to cash on a normal schedule. Nothing in the price suggests anyone has discounted 17% cash conversion.

## Why we might have edge

Three places, ranked by how much we trust them:

1. **Cash conversion (strongest).** This is a public number in a public 10-Q that appears to be uncited anywhere. If it is structural rather than a one-time IPO-year working-capital build, the company grows into a funding gap and the "cheap versus 2027 earnings" case gets a dilution problem attached to it. That is a real analytical edge, not a faster-reader edge.

2. **Reading the ownership filings correctly.** Two widely repeated facts about this stock are wrong. Coatue's 7.5% stake, filed August 14, is a snapshot of a **June 30** position — the passive-filer deadline is 45 days after quarter end, so the filing date carries no August information at all. And the sponsor is not at 51.5%; after the July secondary it is at roughly **37.2% (35.0% if the greenshoe was exercised)**. The supply overhang is meaningfully smaller than the bull case's own framing, and the "a big fund just bought it here" datapoint does not exist.

3. **Understanding the corporate structure.** The July offering *looks* like a $695M primary raise on the cover. It was not. The company used every dollar to buy units back from the sponsor's partnerships — the share count did not move and the company kept no cash. Anyone modeling that as new capital or as dilution is wrong in both directions.

Note honestly: edges 2 and 3 correct the *narrative*, and both corrections cut **against** the trade we were considering. They make us more accurate, not more bullish.

## Why it might be priced in

- **The valuation math is public.** Anyone with the 10-Q and a calculator gets to 32–34x. The required fourth-quarter margin step (about 25.4% versus 22.4% in Q3) is equally derivable. We should assume the buy side has it.
- **Our view is the street's view.** We think the business is undamaged and worth more than 32x is comfortable with; the street thinks it is worth $59. We are not disagreeing with consensus — we are agreeing with it and declining to pay. That is a legitimate reason to stay flat, but it is not an edge.
- **The Tax Receivable Agreement is a captioned line on the balance sheet.** It is not hidden. It is simply omitted from casual EV math.
- **The cash-conversion finding is the one thing that may genuinely not be priced** — but "we found it in a public filing" is a fragile claim to make about a NYSE-listed $10B company with ten covering analysts. It is possible the street has it and treats it as normal for the industry. We have not verified industry-normal conversion rates for engineered-to-order power equipment. That is a hole.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — We stay out and it works (the omission loss).** The September print lands with fourth-quarter bookings above $500M, backlog above $2.3B, and fiscal-2027 revenue guidance at $2.2B or better. At that guide, today's 32x becomes roughly 18x forward and the stock goes to the street's number. We are flat, we watch an 80% move, and our "discipline" cost us the single best industrial in the AI-buildout supply chain. *Monitorable:* yes — the 10-K and the guidance are dated events. This is the most likely way this decision is wrong.

**Failure narrative 2 — We buy at the $24 gate and the gate was the beginning, not the bottom.** Data-center order digestion arrives; a hyperscaler pushes deliveries a quarter; the 2.3x book-to-bill collapses toward 1.0x and the working-capital build reverses into a revenue air pocket. The 17% cash conversion becomes the reason the company has to raise equity at $18 rather than a curiosity. *Monitorable:* partly — bookings and backlog are reported quarterly, so we get one look per quarter, which is slow for a fast-moving cycle.

**Failure narrative 3 — The backlog is not what it says it is, and there is NO tripwire.** "Backlog" here is a company-defined, unaudited number. We do not know the cancellation terms, the pricing escalators, the change-order exposure, or whether a customer has quietly qualified a second source. There is no filing that discloses this, no counterparty who confirms it, no permit, no shipping record, and no sensor we could build that would see it. **Unknown-unknown class: private commercial-contract state — the terms and durability of the revenue backlog exist only inside bilateral agreements that are never disclosed to us at any frequency.** We would find out the same day everyone else does, from a guide-down. This class is what actually kills industrial roll-forward theses, and we are structurally blind to it.

**The reflexive and structural leg.** Our own size is not the problem — the problem is what we would be buying into and what our own rules do to us:

- **We would be bidding into flow we ourselves predict.** The bull case is "the sponsor's selling is what makes it cheap." The sponsor still holds 35–37%. A lockup released on August 4, and the July underwriting agreement resets a customary 90-day lock to roughly **September 29** — landing on top of the fiscal-year print. Buying a starter before that is the exact behavior our own rule against bidding into predicted supply forbids, and the originating brief contradicted itself on precisely this point.
- **Doctrine interaction.** Our default is that a clean court with no named kill produces at least a small starter. That default only applies at or below fair value. Here the named kill *is* valuation, so the response is a price gate and nothing else — no starter, no "small position to stay engaged." Two of our rules point opposite ways here and the price gate wins; if we later decide it shouldn't, that is a doctrine change, not a trade.
- **Liquidity versus our size: UNVERIFIED.** We never pulled average daily volume. For a $10B NYSE name a 0.5–1.5% starter is almost certainly trivial relative to daily turnover, but we did not check, and we should not write "our size is immaterial" without the number.
- **Coatue as a structural factor is unresolved.** They may be underwater from a pre-July entry — our red bench asserted "35% underwater," and that is inference, not data; their cost basis is unknown and no sale has been filed. If they are underwater and impatient, they are 20.5M shares of potential supply. If they are not, they are nothing. We do not know.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ — filled in: **that we paid a full multiple for a backlog that converts into revenue but not into cash, and the equity raise that eventually funds the working-capital gap prices below our entry.**

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry today at $32.** The courts say no, so what follows are reopen conditions.

**Gate A — price only, no new information required.**
- **Buy nothing above $24.00.** The arithmetic: 20–25x this year's adjusted EBITDA on the corrected 304.7M unit count clears the equity at **$19–$25**. $24 is the top of that band and also sits **below the $25.99 52-week low**, so this gate cannot open without a new low. That is deliberate. At $24 the enterprise trades near 24x, which is the first level where we are paid for 17% cash conversion.
- If it opens: **starter 0.5% of the book**, one tranche, and a second 0.5% only at **$21.00 or below**. Maximum intended position on Gate A: **1.0% of the book.**
- **Do not place either as a resting good-till-cancelled order through the print window.** A resting bid into an unscheduled earnings date is an accidental bet on a quiet event. Use price alerts and stage manually.

**Gate B — information first, price second (the more likely path).**
Triggered only by the fiscal-2026 10-K (due ~9/28) or the accompanying release showing **all three**:
1. Full-year operating cash flow conversion **above 50%** of adjusted EBITDA, with IPO one-time costs isolated;
2. Fiscal-2027 revenue guidance **at or above $2.2B**;
3. Q4 bookings **above $500M** and backlog **above $2.3B**.

If all three clear, fair value moves to 18–20x forward EBITDA (~$550M on a $2.2B guide at current margins), which clears the equity at roughly **$30–$34** after debt, cash and the TRA.
- Then: **starter 0.5% of the book at $31.00 or better**, and a second **0.5% at $27.00 or better**. Maximum intended: **1.0%**.
- **Timing constraint:** do not buy the first tranche until the **~September 29 lockup reset has passed**, or until a filing shows the sponsor below 25% or under a signed long-dated standstill. Buying before that is bidding into supply we ourselves forecast.

**Additional independent reopen conditions (either one lifts the multiple objection without a price move):**
- The 10-K quantifies the TRA obligation at a present value **below ~$150M**.
- A dated 8-K, 13D/A or 13G/A shows the sponsor **below 25%**.

**Trims / exits:** none defined — there is no position. If Gate B fills, the first review is the following quarter's cash-flow statement; conversion falling back below 30% is a full exit, not a trim.
