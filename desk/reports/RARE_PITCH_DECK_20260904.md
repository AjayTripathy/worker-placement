# PITCH — RARE — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**

---

## What we believe

Ultragenyx (RARE, $15.72 last pack print; $14.85 last confirmed regular close on 9/3) is a rare-disease drug company that just lost its biggest pipeline bet. On September 2 it announced that the Phase 3 Aspire study of apazunersen in Angelman syndrome missed its main goal and its key backup goal, with no signal in any component of the composite measure — a total failure, stated plainly by the company itself ([8-K, 2026-09-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1515673/000119312526380165/)). The stock fell 44% in a day and is now 61% below its 52-week high.

What we believe is narrower than "this is cheap." Three things:

1. **The write-off looks proportionate, not indiscriminate.** Using a consistent valuation method, the destroyed asset was worth roughly $1.7B risk-adjusted before the readout; about $1.15B of market value disappeared. The market did arithmetic, not panic. We are not claiming a mispricing on the failure itself.
2. **But the surviving business is real and the next binary is a different kind of question.** The company sells four approved drugs, just got its first gene therapy approved (GENGLYCOS, August 19), and guides to $745M of 2026 revenue growing ~28%. The pending September 18–19 FDA decision on UX111 is *not* another Phase 3 efficacy readout — it is a manufacturing/inspection decision on efficacy data the FDA already accepted in its July 2025 rejection letter.
3. **The market paid almost nothing for the manufacturing read-through.** On August 19 the FDA approved GENGLYCOS, made entirely in-house at the company's Bedford, MA plant — the same fill/finish site behind UX111. The stock rose only 6.6% (~$170M) that day, and that 6.6% had to pay for a newly approved $2.7M-list-price therapy *plus* any read-through. Very little was capitalized for the read-through.

So: a modest, positive, short-dated edge on a decision the market appears to have under-credited — held against a company with real financing pressure and a poor recent execution record. Our expected value on the event is roughly **+5% over about two weeks**, with dispersion of about ±18%. That is a thin edge, not a fat one, and we propose to size it that way.

---

## What the market believes

The market believes Ultragenyx is a broken pipeline story with a decent commercial base and a balance sheet that needs money. Specifically:

- **The pipeline is discounted.** Two of three late-stage value drivers have failed in eight months (setrusumab's Orbit and Cosmic both missed their fracture-rate endpoints on 12/29/2025; Aspire missed on 9/2/2026). Analysts converged downward fast after the failure: Baird $16.53, Wedbush $23, JPMorgan cut from $80 to $36. The market is no longer paying for late-stage optionality from this sponsor.
- **A financing is coming.** Cash of $436M at 6/30/26 against a Q2 cash burn of ~$97M is about 4.5 quarters of runway before restructuring costs. The company said on 9/2 it will "define and implement significant expense reductions." Everyone can see the shelf.
- **The UX111 decision is a real coin flip that management will monetize.** 17.7% of the float is short (15.9M shares). A short base that size into a binary is a market that expects a violent move either way — and expects the company to sell stock into any good news.

Where we differ from that consensus is *only* on point three: we think the manufacturing read-through raised the odds of approval more than the tape paid for, and that the size of the short base is a reason to trim into strength, not a reason to stay away.

## Why we might have edge

**1. Published is not the same as priced.** The bearish bench killed our read-through argument by noting TD Cowen wrote it up on August 19 and Canaccord raised its target to $83 on it. That is a coverage argument, not a pricing argument. The test is what the tape paid on the day the news landed: +6.6%. A newly approved gene therapy with a $2.7M list price is worth more than $170M by itself. The read-through was written about and not bought.

**2. The failure base rate does not transfer to this catalyst.** The two prior failures were an antibody and an antisense drug missing efficacy endpoints. UX111's July 2025 rejection was about chemistry, manufacturing and controls — the FDA did not question the efficacy data. Historically, second-round approval rates after a manufacturing-only rejection run roughly 75–80%. Three failures in a row makes a good story and a bad base rate.

**3. The manufacturing read-through is specific, not generic.** The August 19 approval was for a product made *entirely in-house at Bedford*. That is the site. It is not a vague "the FDA likes them now" argument.

**4. The downside is bounded in enterprise terms.** A second rejection removes an asset the market values at $2–5 per share on our own math. The commercial base — four marketed drugs, a fifth just approved — is untouched by the September 18 outcome. There is no leverage-driven forced-sale mechanism here.

## Why it might be priced in

We should be honest that most of the bearish case survives.

- **The valuation is not cheap.** Adding the $1,202M of liabilities for sold future royalties and subtracting cash, enterprise value at $15.72 is about $2.31B against a $745M revenue guide — **3.1x sales on an unprofitable company** with a $291M stockholders' deficit. On product sales only it is worse, not better. Nobody is buying a bargain here.
- **A large chunk of the revenue is already sold.** Royalty revenue was $102M of $214M in Q2 (48%), and those streams are the collateral behind the $1.2B liability. The revenue in the guide is not all economically ours.
- **The prize is small even if we are right.** On the bearish bench's own prize table, a clean UX111 approval at the top of the peak-sales range ($240M) adds about $4.70 per share. A +50% move needs $7.40. The ceiling on the approval branch is low.
- **Everyone knows the equity raise is coming.** Buying a stock two weeks before a binary, into a company that needs money and will be handed a window by good news, means we buy the bad outcome in full and get diluted out of the good one.
- **We are late to a 62% drawdown, not early to a dislocation.** The stock has been repricing since December, not since Tuesday. Nine months of a falling price is usually information.

## What could go wrong

### Failure narrative 1 — The approval arrives and we still lose money

UX111 is approved on September 18. The stock gaps to $19–20 on the approval plus a short squeeze. Within days, the company prices a $300–450M equity offering into the strength at a discount to that print. Dilution of roughly 20–25% of shares outstanding lands on a company whose enterprise value barely moved. We paid $15.72, watched $19.50, and end the month at $15.

**Tripwire: yes, and it is fast.** A 424B5, an S-3 amendment, an 8-K Item 3.02, or an ATM prospectus supplement on CIK 1515673. This is why the trim rule below is not optional.

### Failure narrative 2 — The remaining value gets negotiated away where we cannot watch

The 9/2 filing says two things we have not priced. The company will "make a decision on the disposition" of apazunersen, and it will "define and implement significant expense reductions." Under financing pressure, the realistic set of moves includes selling another royalty stream, partnering Crysvita or Dojolvi economics, or divesting a marketed product — any of which could be done at a distressed price precisely because the buyer knows the runway is 4.5 quarters. We would learn about it in a completed 8-K, at the price already agreed.

**Tripwire: NONE. This is the unmonitorable one.** The unknown-unknown class is **private-negotiation outcomes under financing pressure** — the counterparty set, the price talk, the board's reservation price, and the timeline are all invisible until the deal is signed. There is no filing, no registry, no alternative dataset, and no walk-around that surfaces a term sheet in progress. We cannot build a sensor for this. We can only size for it.

### Failure narrative 3 — Our read-through is about the wrong building

We believe the July 2025 rejection was confined to manufacturing at Bedford, and that the August 19 approval cleared it. Both of those beliefs rest on the *company's own characterization of its own rejection letter*. Rejection letters are not public until after an eventual approval, and then only redacted. If the actual deficiency was a potency assay, a comparability protocol, or an issue at Andelyn/Columbus — the third-party site UX111 also uses and GENGLYCOS does not — then the August 19 approval tells us nothing at all and our probability of success is simply wrong.

**Tripwire: weak and slow.** FDA Form 483 observations reach the public through FOIA on a multi-month lag, well past the decision window. Effectively unmonitorable inside two weeks.

### The reflexive and structural leg

**Our own size is not the problem.** A 0.20% starter is on the order of 5,000 shares against a normal daily volume we estimate at 2–3M shares — roughly 0.2% of a day. We are invisible to this tape and nowhere near the 1%-of-volume publishing ceiling. Anyone claiming market impact here would be wrong.

**Three structural things do bite:**

1. **Our own add band may never fill, for the same reason our trim might.** The 17.7% short base cuts both ways. On a rejection, shorts have their win and cover into weakness — the same profit-taking that limits the fall is what stops our $12.50 add from printing. We would be waiting for a dip that the crowded side of the trade is actively buying. This is the paired-gate problem: the level that triggers our add is the level a large, motivated, better-informed counterparty is defending. Assume roughly even odds the add is unfillable and that the "half now, half on the dip" plan degrades into "half now, nothing later."
2. **The equity is a levered claim on a much less volatile enterprise.** Because $1.2B of royalty liability sits inside enterprise value regardless of the stock price, a 22% fall in the equity is only a 15% fall in enterprise value. The stub moves about 1.45x the underlying business. Neither bench stated this. It means our 0.40% notional behaves like ~0.58% of business exposure, and it means the "bounded downside" the bullish bench claimed is bounded *in enterprise terms* while the equity is the levered piece on top. Size against the levered number, not the notional.
3. **Doctrine interaction: this is a harvest year.** Any loss branch here is harvestable in 2026, which is fine. But we should be explicit that a small speculative biotech starter is competing for attention and tax-reserve headroom against a deployment plan built around index-first entries. It is not free even when it is small. We also have not confirmed whether the Parametric sleeve holds RARE — if it does, our purchase creates a wash-sale interaction with any harvesting they do. That check is listed as open below.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we were right about the FDA and wrong about the capital structure — the approval came, the stock popped, and management sold equity into our thesis before we trimmed.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

All levels are terminal marks — where we expect the stock to *settle* — not intraday touches. A touch level is roughly twice the distance of a terminal one, so do not convert these into stop orders.

**Immediate: BUY 0.20% of book, limit $16.10 or better.**
Do not chase. If it trades above $16.10 before we fill, the trade is smaller, not later — the expected value of $16.53 against $16.10 is not worth paying up for. Single tranche, market hours, no resting order left overnight.

**Add: 0.20% of book at $12.50 or below, only after a rejection is announced, entered by hand on the day.**
No resting GTC order across the decision window under any circumstances. A resting bid into a binary is an accidental short-volatility position — the CRL branch fills us at the worst possible moment at a price we set two weeks earlier. Note that on our own reasoning this add has roughly even odds of never filling, because short covering will support that level.

**Trim: sell half the position at $19.50 or better, in the first two sessions after an approval.**
This is the whole answer to failure narrative 1. The company's obvious move is to price equity into approval strength, and we do not want to be the buyer of that offering by inaction. If a 424B5 or ATM supplement appears first, sell the half immediately regardless of level.

**Exit in full at $22.30 or better.** The bearish bench's own prize table says the fundamentals cannot reach here — the top of the UX111 range adds $4.70 per share against the $7.40 needed. Any print at $22.30 is flow and squeeze, and flow is a gift, not a valuation.

**Exit in full if an offering larger than $300M prices below $14.00.** That combination means the dilution arrived without the good news paying for it, and the thesis is spent.

**Total intended size: 0.40% of book maximum.** Adjusting for the ~1.45x leverage of the equity stub over enterprise value, treat this as ~0.58% of business exposure when checking against sector limits. This is a minimum starter, deliberately.

**Expected value = $16.53.** Against $15.72 that is **+5.1% over about two weeks**.

**Probability discipline.** The base rate for approval after a manufacturing-only rejection is roughly 0.78. We are using 0.65 combined across the two approval branches — a 0.13 deviation, which is large enough to require a named mechanism, and here it is: **UX111 also uses the Andelyn/Columbus site, which the August 19 Bedford approval does not clear.** That is the entire adjustment. We are not marking down for "this company has bad luck."

One coincidence worth naming rather than hiding: our $16.53 expected value is identical to Baird's price target. That is chance — we built the table from the benches' own prize math, not from a broker note — but it should not be read as independent confirmation.

### The volatility read, and why it produces "stock, not options"

**We have no chain.** Market-data permission was denied to both benches, so what follows is our table's arithmetic against an *estimated* chain, with the crossover levels stated so the conclusion can be checked in one minute once the permission is restored.

**Our table's dispersion:** standard deviation of $2.84, which is **18.1%** of $15.72 over a ~30-day horizon. Annualized, our own view of this stock implies about **63% volatility**.

**What the chain would have to imply for options to beat stock.** Pricing two candidate strikes off the table itself, using C = Σ pᵢ × max(Sᵢ − K, 0):

| Strike, ~30d | Our table's value | Chain at ~100% implied vol | Chain crossover — the level below which calls beat stock |
|---|---|---|---|
| $15.00 call | **$2.25** | ~$2.50 | ~**85%** implied volatility |
| $17.50 call | **$0.70** | ~$1.13 | ~**75%** implied volatility |

**Conclusion: buy the stock.** Implied volatility on a biotech two weeks before an FDA decision is very rarely below 75–85%; our estimate is 95–130%. On our own numbers the calls are dominated by the shares at any realistic chain price. And the comparison understates the problem: our table is *fork-dated* — bimodal around September 18 — so the chain will carry a pronounced smile and the out-of-the-money wings we would actually be buying will be richer than a single-volatility comparison suggests. Our 18.1% dispersion against a chain pricing roughly 28.7% at 100% volatility means the market is pricing about 1.6x our view of the move.

**Volatility risk premium, and a trap in it.** Trailing realized volatility is enormous — a single −44% day contributes about 200% annualized on its own over a 20-session window. So implied minus realized is *negative*, which naively reads as "volatility is cheap." **It is not.** That realized figure is one discrete, non-repeating gap, not a sustained regime; it is the same category of artifact as an unadjusted split or a thin after-hours print. Do not buy volatility because realized looks high.

**Rich implied volatility would normally route to premium selling. It does not here.** Selling a cash-secured put at $15 fails our own floor test — our downside branch is $12.25, well below the strike, so we would be assigned into a loss on a name we only want at $12.50. And this is a taxable account, where premium selling is restricted to narrow templates that this does not fit. Any premium-selling case for RARE goes to the shadow ledger, not to the book.

**Tax overlay.** 2026 is a harvest year. Because the arithmetic rules out options, there is no expiry-year problem — the entire loss branch is in common stock and harvestable inside 2026 at our discretion. Had we used calls, only the September and October expiries would have qualified; a January 2027 expiry would push the loss into the wrong year and be excluded on that ground alone, independent of the volatility math. The remaining tax exposure is the wash-sale interaction with the Parametric sleeve, listed as open item 10 above and to be cleared before the first fill.

**Instrument recommendation, as the output of the arithmetic above: common stock, 0.20% of book now, 0.40% maximum. No options in any form.**
