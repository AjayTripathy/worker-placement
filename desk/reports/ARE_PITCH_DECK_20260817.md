## What we believe

Alexandria Real Estate is the largest landlord to biotech and pharma laboratory tenants. The stock has fallen about 45% from its 52-week high, which is what put it on our screen. We think that fall is mostly deserved, and that at $46 the price already reflects a reasonable-but-not-generous view of the business.

Three things drive that:

1. **Earnings are going to fall in the second half of this year, and management has already told everyone so.** Alexandria earned $3.46 per share of adjusted funds from operations in the first half of 2026 and reaffirmed a full-year range of $6.35–$6.45 on August 3. Subtract: the second half has to come in at $2.89–$2.99, a 14–16% step down from the first half. So the "cheap at 7.2x earnings" argument is wrong on its own terms — measured against the rate the company will be exiting the year at ($5.78–$5.98 annualized), the stock trades at **7.7–8.0x**, not 7.2x.

2. **Rents on renewal are going down, not up.** First-half 2026 releasing spreads were −7.4% on a straight-line basis and −9.6% on cash. Occupancy is 86.9%. One recently re-leased building (3000 Minuteman) went to a new tenant explicitly "at lower rental rates than initial underwriting." The buildings still under construction — roughly $1.2B left to spend, 71% pre-leased — will deliver into that same market.

3. **The balance sheet is doing the work the income statement isn't.** The company is levered roughly 63% debt in the capital stack (about $13.8B of debt and preferred against $8.0B of equity value). On August 12 it raised $1.0B of junior subordinated notes at a 7.250% coupon that resets but never goes below 7.250%, out to 2057 ([underwriting-agreement 8-K, filed 8/14/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1035443/000103544326000067/)). That's expensive money, and it is money that does not reduce leverage.

Where that lands on value: at a 7.8% capitalization rate — which is what today's price implies — this is neither cheap nor absurd. It is priced roughly where a stressed-but-normal lab building trades. Our margin of safety only appears at an 8.5–9.0% cap rate, which is **$32–$36 per share.**

## What the market believes

The market believes roughly what we do, which is the problem. Alexandria is in the S&P 500, covered by every major sell-side desk, and the guidance is public and reaffirmed. The stock has already rallied 17% off its $39.42 low — **after** the second-quarter print and **through** the $1B junior note deal. Nobody is asleep here.

The bull case a buyer at $46 is expressing: life-science demand troughs in 2026–2027, occupancy stabilizes in the high 80s, the company sells $2.9B of assets and other capital sources as promised, leverage lands inside 5.6x–6.2x, and the dividend holds. On that path the stock is worth $51–$61 (7.0–7.5% cap). To get to $69 — a 50% gain — you need a 6.6% cap rate, which requires a genuine recovery in lab real estate, not merely competent execution.

## Why we might have edge

Honestly: **we don't have much, and we should say so plainly.**

Two things we found that are not obviously in the consensus write-up:

- **The "$2.9B disposition plan" is not $2.9B of dispositions.** The company's own words are "$2.9B of dispositions, sales of partial interests **and other capital sources**." Sell-side summaries, our own screen, and our own first-pass brief all restated it as if it were asset sales. If the $985M of net proceeds from the August junior notes counts as an "other capital source," then roughly a third of the plan was just filled with paper that does not deleverage. A second tell: the stated *uses* ($1.75B to construction + $1.68B to debt reduction = $3.43B) exceed the $2.9B of *sources*.
- **The second-half arithmetic.** The 14–16% step-down is two numbers from the same press release, differenced. Neither of our benches did it on the first pass; we don't believe it's a secret from the market, but it does reprice the "cheap multiple" claim decisively.

Neither is a durable information advantage. This is diligence work — it tells us what to pay, not that we know something others don't.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost certainly it is. Every leg of our case is company-guided or company-disclosed: the FFO range, the releasing spreads, the occupancy, the capital plan, the coupon on the new notes. A widely covered index constituent with reaffirmed guidance does not hide 26% of its earnings from the street.

That cuts against one of our own arguments. Our red bench's headline kill — that $295M/year of capitalized construction interest is a hidden subsidy to reported earnings — cannot simultaneously be true and consistent with the same bench's finding that the stock is fully covered and fully modeled. Our blue bench caught this: both cannot be fatal.

There is also a real argument on the other side that we initially read backwards. Choosing 7.25% debt over issuing stock at $46 looks like distress. At roughly 0.65x the value the assets would fetch at a 7.0% cap, **refusing to sell stock is the right call for existing shareholders.** That is good capital allocation, not a warning sign.
