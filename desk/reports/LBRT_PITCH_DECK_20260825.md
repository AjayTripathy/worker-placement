# PITCH — LBRT — VERDICT

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — No position, no orders. Both benches land on the same answer for three different reasons: stay flat, and reopen only on a signed power-offtake contract (or on price reaching the levels below).

---

## What we believe

Liberty Energy is two businesses wearing one ticker, and only one of them is currently earning anything.

The first is hydraulic fracturing — pumping sand and water into shale wells for oil and gas producers. It is a real, cash-generating business in a soft part of its cycle. Revenue in the June 2026 quarter was $1,188.6M, essentially flat against the same quarter two years earlier ($1,159.9M). But the profit on that revenue has fallen hard: adjusted EBITDA margin went from 17.4% a year ago to 12.7% now, and adjusted earnings per share fell from $0.12 to $0.09 even as revenue rose. Pricing and cost, not volume, is where this cycle is expressing itself.

The second is a power-generation venture — building gas-fired generating capacity, largely aimed at data-center demand. Management said on the July 2026 earnings call that it has **secured roughly 3 gigawatts of capacity through 2029**, at **$5–6 billion of total capital**, targeting **17–18% unlevered returns** and a **5–6 year cash payback**. First power is guided for late 2027 to the first half of 2028.

Our belief, stated plainly:

1. **The upside is genuinely large and is not in the price.** If that 3 GW program were fully funded and contracted tomorrow, on management's own numbers it produces roughly $1.0B of annual EBITDA. At 8× that is about $8.0B of enterprise value against $5.5B of capital spent — about **$2.5B of value created, roughly $15 per share**. At 10× it is roughly $28 per share. The existing frac business, valued at 5× its trailing EBITDA of $530.8M and net of $772M of net debt, is worth about **$11.55 per share** on its own. The stock is $18.71. So the fully-funded version of this company is worth materially more than today's price.

2. **It is not ownable yet, because the one fact that converts capex into cash flow does not exist.** Turbine slots are secured. Deposits are on the balance sheet and rising ($116.0M at year-end 2025 → $248.0M at June 30, 2026, plus $219.2M of construction in progress). **There is no executed power purchase agreement disclosed in any filing.** No megawatts under contract, no term, no price per kilowatt-month. That is sunk capital against optional revenue — the least attractive configuration a buildout can be in.

3. **The company cannot self-fund the program it has committed to.** Cash is $555.4M and the undrawn revolver is $448.3M — call it $1.0B of liquidity against a $5–6B program, with a second-half 2026 outflow that plausibly runs near $900M. First-half operating cash flow was only $141.4M against $355M of capex (a working-capital drain converted about half of EBITDA into cash), so first-half free cash flow was roughly **negative $214M** — while the company continues to pay a $0.09 quarterly dividend, about $59M a year, reaffirmed on July 14, 2026.

So: high roof, short ladder. That is a "wait for the contract" position, not a "reject" and not a "buy."

## What the market believes

The market holds two contradictory views at once, and the gap between them is itself informative.

**Sell-side analysts** are uniformly positive: 11–13 analysts, all rated Buy, price targets $23–$36 with an average of $29.19. Their 2027 estimates are $4.98B of revenue (+8.7%) and $0.32 of earnings per share. Notably, that growth is essentially **frac-only** — first power arrives too late to contribute meaningfully to 2027 — which means the sell-side is underwriting a completions-market recovery on top of a power option they have not yet put numbers to.

**The tape disagrees.** The stock is $18.71 against a 52-week high of $34.48 — down about 46%. It is up 88% from the 52-week low of $9.90, so this is a de-rating from a high, not a distressed name. On July 22, 2026 the company reported and raised its capital spending plans sharply; the stock fell the following session. (How far is disputed — see "What we checked ourselves.") Importantly, **roughly 30–37% of the total drawdown happened *before* that disclosure**, which means the broader frac de-rating is doing real work here, not just this one self-inflicted event.

Reported short interest is 8.93% of float, but we do not read that as bearish sentiment: a large portion is likely mechanical hedging against the company's convertible bonds (see below), so the usual "is this crowded?" read is corrupted here and we are declining to use it.

## Why we might have edge

**1. The prize is only disclosed in one venue, and it is the venue most people don't read.** The 3 GW / $5–6B / 17–18% return figures came from the earnings *call*. They are not in the filed press release or the 8-K. Neither are the FY26 capital spending numbers that everyone is now modeling. An investor working only from filings cannot see the upside case; an investor working only from headlines cannot see that the number has no filing behind it. We have read both and can hold both facts.

**2. We separated "secured" from "contracted."** Both of our own benches initially blurred this, and most coverage still does. "3 GW secured through 2029" describes *equipment procurement* — turbine slots — not signed customers. The distinction is the entire investment case. Deposits are irreversible; revenue is not yet committed.

**3. We correctly identified the financing sequence as a governance tell.** In February and March 2026 the company raised **$1,295M of zero-coupon convertible notes** ($770M due 2031 at a $34.50 conversion price; $525M due 2032 at $37.44), and spent **$186.5M of cash on capped call hedges** with caps at $65.10 and $72.00. Four to five months later it disclosed the capital program those proceeds fund. The balance sheet shows the program pre-dated the raise — $116.0M of capital deposits were already sitting there at December 31, 2025. Raise first at $26–29, confess the spend after, and the $186.5M spent on hedges is now permanently worthless at $18.71. That sequencing is a real signal about how this management team communicates.

**4. We know how the first good headline will trade, and it is not the obvious way.** There are 36.3 million shares' worth of convertible notes struck 85–100% above today's price. As the stock fell, the funds that own those bonds bought back their short hedges. When a power contract is announced and the stock jumps, those same funds must mechanically re-short into the rally. That is predictable supply arriving exactly when the good news does. The naive trade — buy the announcement — is buying into a wave of selling we can see coming.

**5. Two of our diligence tools are live but unfired, and one could settle the central question.** Customs bill-of-lading data on turbine and generator shipments consigned to Liberty or its power subsidiary would independently corroborate (or refute) both the $248M of deposits and the "3 GW secured" claim. We do not currently have that access keyed to the right consignee names. Similarly, municipal permit records would go live the moment the company discloses a site or county for the power campus. Neither is a hypothetical — they are specific, buildable checks on the load-bearing claim.

## Why it might be priced in

Honest counterweights, several of which come from our own analysis:

- **The stock is already down 46%.** Nobody is treating this as a clean growth story. The de-rating has happened.
- **On an apples-to-apples basis the base business is not cheap.** Enterprise value is about $3.83B against trailing EBITDA of $530.8M — **7.2×**. It only looks like 6.3× if you first strip out the $467M of deposits and construction-in-progress that generate no EBITDA yet, which is defensible but is a choice.
- **The frac cycle may already be discounted.** If you assume mid-cycle margins of 20% on ~$4.6B of revenue (~$920M of EBITDA) and apply just 4×, the frac business alone is worth roughly $17.80 per share — nearly today's entire price. Under that reading, the market is *already* paying for a normalized frac recovery and assigning the power venture roughly nothing, which would make it a much better entry than we're crediting. We flag this as the strongest bull argument against our own patience.
- **The upside math is fragile in an important way.** Our "$15–28 per share of value created" assumes the full $5.5B gets spent and every megawatt gets contracted at management's stated return. Neither is committed. Value that requires $5.5B of financing the company does not have is not the same kind of value as cash flow.
- **The capital program is already financed at the corporate level for now.** The converts carry a 0% coupon, no maintenance covenants, and cause no dilution below $34.50. So the "they'll have to do a dilutive equity raise at these prices" fear — which was our original thesis — is wrong for the near term. They pre-funded.
- **Everything hinges on a management claim.** The 17–18% unlevered return has no signed contract behind it. It is an assertion on a conference call.

## What could go wrong

*(Pre-mortem. Written as if the position already lost money.)*

### Failure narrative 1 — The frac cycle keeps compressing and there is no floor at 20% margins
We treated "mid-cycle" as 20% EBITDA margins because that's roughly where 2024 sat. But 2024 may have *been* the peak, not the middle. Margins go 17.4% → 12.7% → single digits as pressure-pumping capacity stays oversupplied and pricing keeps eroding. The $11.55/share frac floor and the $17.80/share mid-cycle case both evaporate, because both are multiples applied to an EBITDA number that keeps shrinking. Meanwhile the power buildout consumes cash the frac business is no longer producing, and the dividend gets cut — which in this shareholder base triggers its own selling.
**Tripwire: monitorable.** Quarterly adjusted EBITDA margin. Bear line: below 10%. Bull line: back above 17%.

### Failure narrative 2 — The offtake signs, but at a rate that doesn't clear 17–18%
The contract arrives. The stock pops. We ladder in on the pullback, as planned. Then it emerges over the following year that the actual price per kilowatt-month is well below what was needed to justify $1.83M per megawatt of capital — because by 2028 the behind-the-meter gas market is crowded, and Liberty is a late entrant negotiating against sophisticated hyperscaler procurement teams. The 17–18% becomes 9–10%, the value created collapses from $2.5B toward zero, and we own a leveraged utility developer instead of an asymmetric option.
**Tripwire: partial only.** We can see *whether* a contract signs. We very often cannot see *at what rate* — companies routinely announce megawatts and terms while withholding pricing, and there is no filing obligation to disclose it. So this failure can run for four to six quarters before the numbers reveal it.

### Failure narrative 3 — The demand regime shifts, and nothing Liberty files tells us
**This is the one with no monitorable tripwire.** The entire prize rests on data-center power demand persisting through 2029 at prices that clear a 17–18% unlevered return. That demand does not live inside Liberty Energy. It lives inside a handful of hyperscaler capital budgets, inside grid interconnection queue policy, inside ERCOT and PJM market rules, and inside the competitive supply of nuclear, solar-plus-storage, and utility-scale gas. Any of those can break the thesis, and **none of them generate an 8-K, a press release, or a price move in LBRT that we could distinguish from ordinary sector beta.**

**Unknown-unknown class named: off-issuer counterparty-demand regime break.** The entity whose behavior determines our outcome is not the entity we are monitoring. Our surveillance is pointed at Liberty's disclosure plane; the driver sits on someone else's plane, and we cannot even name the counterparty because no contract has been announced. This is the same structural blindness that catches us whenever value depends on an unnamed customer's budget.

A related, smaller unknown-unknown: **single-venue truth.** Our entire prize table — 3 GW, $5–6B, 17–18%, and the FY26 capital guide — exists only in an earnings call transcript. If any of it is later walked back, softened, or was simply wrong when said, no filing amends it and no gate fires. We would find out at the next call, if we're lucky.

### The reflexive and structural leg

- **Our own size against average daily volume: not a factor.** A 0.25% starter position in a name with a $3.05B market cap is a rounding error against daily turnover, and we would be laddering it across multiple sessions anyway. *(Average daily volume is not in the evidence pack and we have not verified it — stated as a judgment, not a measured fact.)* The flow risk here is not ours.
- **The flow risk that is real is the one we predicted.** 36.3 million shares of convertible hedge will be re-established into any good-news rally. Our own rule — never place a bid into flow you yourself forecast — means we must *not* buy the announcement. It also means our planned entry (laddering into the pullback 3–10 sessions later) only fills if the arbitrage selling actually materializes at the size we expect. **If we are wrong about the hedge ratio, the pullback never comes and we simply miss the whole move.** Our two benches disagreed about that hedge ratio by a factor of three (see the table), so this is a live risk, not a theoretical one.
- **The short-interest read is unusable.** Because 8.93% of float short is largely mechanical hedging rather than opinion, we cannot tell whether this idea is crowded or ignored. One of our standard conditioning checks is simply offline for this name.
- **Doctrine interactions.** (a) Our "a flat verdict requires a specific named reason" rule is satisfied — the reason is *equipment secured, offtake absent*, not vagueness. (b) Our "clean court gets a minimum starter" default is being **overridden**, which we should say out loud: this is not a clean court, it is a court where our own opening classification was refuted. (c) No resting orders will be placed ahead of the October print — a good-till-cancelled bid into an earnings event is an accidental short-volatility position. (d) The company is not flagged for integrity problems, so normal pricing applies; but the raise-then-confess sequencing and the continuous insider selling (four Form 4s and five Form 144s since June 1, 2026, straight through the drawdown) are a governance discount we are applying to the *sizing*, not to the fair value.

**If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be** that we correctly identified a large, real option, correctly refused to chase the headline that revealed it — and then either never got the pullback we were waiting for, or got filled into a contract whose actual price per kilowatt-month was never disclosed and turned out not to clear the return management advertised, while the fracturing business that was supposed to fund the wait kept compressing underneath us.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**Both benches say no entry today. These are reopen conditions, not orders.** Nothing is to be placed on the book. No good-till-cancelled bids ahead of the October print.

### Primary reopen — event, not price
**Trigger:** an executed power purchase agreement disclosed with **megawatts, contract term, and price per kilowatt-month**. Megawatts alone, or a "letter of intent," does not count — the rate is the entire prize table.

**How we would act on it — deliberately not on the announcement:**
- **Do not bid the pop.** We forecast mechanical selling from convertible bond hedging into any rally; buying the headline means buying our own predicted supply.
- **Wait 3 to 10 sessions.** Ladder into the arbitrage-driven pullback.
- Tranche 1: 0.10% of book, at or below the announcement-day close.
- Tranche 2: 0.075% of book, 5% below tranche 1.
- Tranche 3: 0.075% of book, 10% below tranche 1.
- **Total starter: 0.25% of book.** Hard cap **0.75% of book** until contracted offtake covers at least half of committed capital spending. The governance sequencing (raise at $26–29, disclose the program after; $186.5M of hedges bought at unreachable strikes; ongoing insider selling) is a sizing discount, not a valuation discount.
- If the pullback never comes, **we miss it.** That is an accepted, explicit cost of the flow rule.

### Secondary reopen — price alone, no contract required
These are levels where you stop paying for the power venture and start being handed it.

- **$14.40 or below — reopen the court.** Frac business at 5× trailing EBITDA ($11.55/share) plus the power buildout carried at exactly the cash already spent on it ($467M of deposits and construction in progress ≈ $2.86/share). At this price you pay nothing for the venture above its sunk cost. *This reopens a court; it is not an automatic buy.*
- **$11.60 or below — deep-value reopen.** Frac stub alone at 5×, power valued at zero. Requires re-testing whether a dilutive equity raise has become likely at that price, which would change the math against us.

### Bull confirmation (any one raises size or accelerates the ladder)
- Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin back **above 17%** — reopens the "the damage is cyclical, not structural" reading on a cash basis.
- FY26 capital spending guidance **cut below $1.1B**, with long-lead deposits pushed into 2027.
- A closed **non-recourse financing at the power subsidiary**, serviced from that entity's own contracted revenue. We would not credit parent-company borrowing capacity for this.

### Bear confirmation (any one closes the file, not just the entry)
- Quarter-end liquidity **below $700M** with capital spending tracking to the guide.
- Any **S-3 shelf registration or at-the-market equity program**.
- Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin **below 10%**.
- The capital guide reaffirmed at $1.5B or higher **with still no signed offtake**.

### Catalyst dates
- **~2026-10-07 (estimated, unconfirmed)** — earnings timing press release. The company's pattern (timing PR 2025-10-08 → print 2025-10-16; timing PR 2026-07-08 → print 2026-07-22) implies one lands about a week before the print. **This is our first real confirmation of the print date.**
- **2026-10-14 (UNCONFIRMED)** — Q3 2026 results. This date is derived from a data vendor, not from any company announcement. No timing press release exists in EDGAR through August 13, 2026. Roughly 35 trading days out.
- **Q4 2027 – H1 2028** — management's guided date for first power. Not near-term, but it is the date the whole valuation discounts back from.
- An offtake announcement is **undated and could land any day**, including before the October print. This is why the reopen is event-triggered rather than calendar-triggered.

### Sizing summary
Current: **0.00% of book.** Maximum on the primary reopen: **0.25% starter, 0.75% hard cap.** No position established before a signed offtake or a price in the reopen bands.

## What remains unverified

Stated without hedging, because several of these sit underneath conclusions we drew anyway:

1. **The FY26 capital spending guide (~$1.5B) appears in no filed document.** It is transcript-only. Every liquidity projection in this deck — the ~$900M second-half burn, the ~$100M year-end liquidity estimate, the covenant headroom math — inherits that uncertainty. **Plausible, not confirmed.**
2. **The entire power prize table is a management claim on a conference call.** 3 GW, $5–6B, 17–18% unlevered return, 5–6 year payback. No signed contract stands behind any of it. Our upside case is built on an assertion.
3. **The July 22 price reaction is unverified from actual price bars.** One source says −22%, another says −13.7%. IBKR price history was permission-denied to both benches. We reconciled the discrepancy arithmetically, not with data.
4. **The convertible hedge ratio.** Our two benches assumed deltas of 0.15 and 0.35–0.50 respectively. The difference determines whether the post-announcement pullback we intend to buy is 5% deep or 15% deep — or barely exists. **We are staking our entry mechanics on a number we have not calculated properly.**
5. **Whether a residual tax receivable obligation survives** the collapse of the up-C structure (zero Class B shares outstanding). Neither bench pulled the note. If material, it is an enterprise-value line item missing from both valuations.
6. **The exact definition of "fixed charges" in the credit agreement** — specifically whether growth capital spending counts. If it is excluded, the covenant concern is materially smaller than we've modeled.
7. **The split of the 12.7% consolidated margin** between fracturing and power startup costs. The company reports a single segment. It is possible the frac business is meaningfully healthier than the headline suggests and the power venture's early operating losses are doing more of the damage than we credit — which would be bullish and we cannot currently test it.
8. **Average daily trading volume** is not in the evidence pack. Our judgment that our own size is immaterial to the tape is unverified.
9. **The October 14 print date** is vendor-derived. No company announcement confirms it.
