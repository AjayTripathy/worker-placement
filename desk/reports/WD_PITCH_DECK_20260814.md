# PITCH — WD — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Buy a quarter-size starter (~0.5% of book) at ~$42.71 now; any addition is blocked until two specific verifications land (GSE-reform economics read + the November print confirming the fraud charges are finished and the dividend held).**

## What we believe

Walker & Dunlop is one of the largest lenders and loan servicers for U.S. apartment buildings, working mainly through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac programs. The stock has been cut roughly in half over the past year (from a high of $87.76 to $42.71, about 3% above its 52-week low) after the company discovered roughly $134M of fraud in loans it had originated, fired the responsible banking team, and began repurchasing or indemnifying the affected loans.

We believe the damage from the fraud itself is bounded and mostly finished:

- The company's own second-quarter filings put the gross exposure at roughly **$250M** (repurchased and indemnified loans of $193.3M with $54.3M of markdowns already taken, plus a $21M quarterly credit provision) — painful, but a fraction of the ~$1.5B of market value that has been erased.
- The vetted 8-K earnings release (not just a call transcript) states the **Freddie Mac investigation is concluded with no further repurchases expected**, and the Fannie Mae reviews are "nearing the conclusion." (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1497770/000110465926091461/wd-20260806xex99d1.htm)
- The scary-looking Q4 2025 earnings collapse was cost composition, not a broken business: that quarter's release shows **revenue flat at $340M and loan volume up 36%**, with the earnings miss explained almost entirely by ~$42M pre-tax of fraud-loan expenses and impairments (about $0.94 per share after tax — which bridges the reported $0.28 back to roughly the $1.22 the market expected).
- The franchise grew *through* the scandal: market share up about 3.5 percentage points, servicing portfolio still growing.

If the charges truly terminate this year, we own a market-leading, growing franchise at roughly half its pre-crisis price.

## What the market believes

The market is not pricing a $250M hole — it is pricing three bigger fears:

1. **A regime change at Fannie and Freddie.** The administration is pushing to privatize the two agencies (a planned ~$30B IPO; new 2026 purchase caps of $88B per agency). Walker & Dunlop's core business depends on a program where it shares the first losses on loans it sells to Fannie Mae in exchange for attractive servicing fees. Privatized, profit-driven agencies could reprice those terms against the company. This risk is live, unquantified by anyone — including us.
2. **Earnings and dividend stress.** Reported (GAAP) earnings were $0.09 per share last quarter against a $0.68 dividend. Corporate cash fell from $299M to $161M in six months, against $844M of corporate debt. The "core earnings" figure the company points to ($1.19, up 3%) is defined to *exclude* credit provisions, write-offs, and loan repurchase losses — exactly the damage in dispute — so the market reasonably discounts it.
3. **A legal tail.** At least four law firms have announced shareholder investigations into the February disclosure. No class action has been verified as filed, but the tail exists.

The stock's failure to recover on the "investigation concluded" news suggests the market has moved on from the fraud to these larger questions.

## Why we might have edge

- **The bear case's headline number is an artifact.** The scariest figure in the filings — a $14.4B "maximum quantifiable contingent liability" on the $69.5B of loans where the company shares losses — is decade-old boilerplate. It stood at $14.1B in the prior period and existed essentially unchanged when the stock traded at $90. A number that didn't move cannot be what repriced the stock. The same filing note shows a historical loss rate of 0.3 basis points and a $49M loss allowance. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1497770/000110465926091536/wd-20260630x10q.htm)
- **The "earnings power collapsed" story is refuted by the company's own audited-adjacent release.** Flat revenue, +36% volume, and a charge-driven bridge for the miss (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1497770/000110465926019956/wd-20260226xex99d1.htm).
- **The termination claim is now in a vetted document**, not just a call transcript — the Freddie no-further-repurchases language is in the filed 8-K exhibit.
- **The market may be conflating** a one-time, bounded fraud cleanup with permanent impairment, at a price 3% above the 52-week low.

## Why it might be priced in

Stated symmetrically — these are the reasons the court refused a full position:

- **The GSE privatization risk is real, live, and we have no model of it.** If privatized agencies cut the loss-sharing economics, the earnings power we're buying genuinely shrinks. The market's refusal to rebound may be correct, not irrational. This is the single contested leg neither bench resolved.
- **The company's "core earnings" defense is circular.** A metric defined to exclude the disputed damage cannot prove the damage is contained. Until GAAP earnings and the dividend reconcile, dividend-cut risk is unpriced in our case.
- **Part of our "bounded and terminating" story is still transcript-only.** The Q3 charge guide of $12–16M and the claimed Freddie forbearance running to late 2027 appear in *no* vetted filing. The vetted 10-Q declining to bound the remaining liability is itself information.
- **The exposure grew, not shrank, on inspection**: our own thesis said ~$220M; the actual Q2 filings support ~$250M.
- The litigation tail and the possibility that the November print brings new charges are both live.
