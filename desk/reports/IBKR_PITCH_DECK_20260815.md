# PITCH — IBKR — PROPOSED VERDICT (PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION)

**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION: Do not buy at today's price (~$91.42). Keep it as a price-gated watch: the case reopens below ~$73.40 or if the price ever becomes verifiably fair — it does not auto-buy.** (Both benches converged here at conviction 8/10.)

## What we believe

Interactive Brokers is an excellent business, and the court confirmed that part of the pitch from primary filings: revenue grew about 28% year over year, pretax margins run near 77%, accounts grew about 34%, and the automated-broker cost advantage is real. We also believe our own screening tools systematically misread this company — the negative enterprise value (−$43.4B) and the ~6.1× price-to-sales figure are artifacts of customer cash sitting on the balance sheet and the holding-company structure, not signals. That insight is worth keeping.

But we do not believe the stock is a buy here, for four reasons the court sustained:

1. **The current earnings sit on a leverage peak.** Customer margin loans are up 67% year over year to $108.5B, and up 20% in just the first half of 2026 — accelerating. Margin balances are among the most violently mean-reverting lines in brokerage. Today's profits are partly a bet that clients stay this levered.
2. **Outside shareholders get taxed economics.** Public Class A holders own only ~26.6% of the company, the founder's holding company controls ~73.4% of the vote, and the company has agreed to pay away 85% of certain tax savings to the pre-IPO holders. Minority shareholders can force nothing.
3. **We custody our own assets at IBKR.** Owning the equity of our own broker means a bad operational outcome (outage, failure) would hit our position and our custody access at the same time — a correlated tail we'd have to size around.
4. **We could not verify the price is fair** — see the pricing section below.

## What the market believes

The market treats IBKR as a discovered, high-quality growth compounder and has paid up accordingly: the stock is $91.42, only 6.6% below its 52-week high of $97.84 and 55% above its 52-week low, at roughly 36× trailing earnings and 7× book. The nearest comparable, Schwab, trades at ~20× trailing earnings (15.4× forward) and 4.4× book — so the market is paying IBKR an ~80% premium, partly justified by its faster growth (28% revenue growth vs. Schwab's slower pace). Consensus also implicitly believes the growth engine (client leverage, trading activity, high rates on client cash) persists.

## Why we might have edge

Stated honestly, our edges here are knowledge, not entry signals:

- **We use the product daily.** As a customer we understand the platform's stickiness and cost advantage first-hand. But the court noted the flip side (custody concentration), and familiarity is not mispricing.
- **We caught our own tools lying.** The screen-defect finding (customer cash breaking EV and P/S for brokers) is genuinely useful — it will stop future false positives and false negatives across the broker sector. It just doesn't make this stock cheap.
- **We identified the tax-sharing structure** (85% of certain tax savings paid to insiders) before the market narrative discusses it much. But it argues *against* the stock, not for it.

## Why it might be priced in

Almost certainly it is. This is a widely covered, 19-year-listed mega-cap with no dislocation: no drawdown, no forced seller, no misunderstood filing. The efficient-pricing presumption is legitimate for a name like this. The 80% premium to Schwab is at least partly earned by faster growth, and everything good we confirmed (growth, margins, account adds) was in the press release everyone read. Both benches agreed: the market fully knows.

## PROPOSED ENTRY BANDS

**No entry at any price today.** The courts' finding is a valuation objection against a confirmed-good business, so the disposition is a price-gated watch with reopen conditions, not a permanent kill:

- **Reopen band 1 — price:** below **~$73.40** (a 25% drawdown from the 52-week high of $97.84 — the red bench's own stated flip level, where it moves to "accept with size cuts"). Hitting this level **reopens the case for a fresh court; it is not an automatic buy.** At that level, watch specifically whether margin balances are compressing (which would mean earnings are falling with the price and the stock isn't actually getting cheaper).
- **Reopen band 2 — fairness verified, any price:** (a) the $81M/25bp rate-sensitivity sentence quoted verbatim in context from the 10-Q's market-risk section, **and** (b) forward whole-company earnings multiple converging toward Schwab's (~15–20× forward vs. ~36× trailing today).

- **Sizing if a future court accepts:** must price the custody overlap (we hold our assets at this broker) and the insider tax-sharing drag — i.e., start below a normal full position.
