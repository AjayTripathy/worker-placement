## What we believe

Blue Moon Metals (NYSE American: BMM, ~$507M market cap at $4.82) is building the Nussir copper mine in Kvalsund, Norway — a fully permitted (under Norwegian law) project with a completed feasibility study, C$159.1M of cash on the balance sheet as of June 30, 2026, and a stated path to production in 2027. The bull story is real in outline: Europe wants domestic copper (the EU has designated Nussir a Strategic Project under its Critical Raw Materials Act), the study's economics improve sharply with the copper price, and copper on the London Metal Exchange closed August 14 at $14,545/tonne (~$6.60/lb) — above even the "spot" scenario the company's own study used.

But we believe the stock is not buyable today, for three stacked reasons:

1. **The permits are legally contested at the European level.** The EFTA Surveillance Authority — the watchdog that checks whether Norway follows EU-derived environmental law — has issued a formal notice finding Norway in breach of the Water Framework Directive *by authorising, and maintaining in force*, Nussir's permit to deposit mine tailings in a fjord ([primary document, ESA Case 86194](https://www.eftasurv.int/cms/sites/default/files/documents/gopro/Case%2086194%20College%20Decision%2008126COL%20-%20Letter%20of%20formal%20notice%20-%20Norway%20-%20Incorrect%20and%20incomplete%20implementation%20a.pdf)). The company's August 13 statement that "all permits at Nussir are in good standing" is true under Norwegian law and beside the point: the challenge is to the Norwegian framework itself. If it goes badly, capital already sunk into construction could be stranded.
2. **The money to finish the mine is not in hand.** Initial capital cost is US$184M. A precious-metals streaming deal (selling the future gold and silver output for cash up front) was announced as a memorandum roughly eight months ago and, per the company's own August 13 statement, "no precious metals streaming agreement has been closed or drawn." The funding hole is at least ~US$70M, and the share count already doubled in a year (51.3M → 98.4M weighted average). More share issuance is the predictable path — and we should not be buying just ahead of a stock sale we ourselves predict.
3. **At the study's own long-term copper price, a finished, funded, unchallenged Nussir is worth less than today's stock price.** Roughly $3.21/share (see math below). Everything above that is a bet that copper stays well above $4.78/lb for the 13-year mine life.

So: the upside is genuinely large if copper stays here, but at today's price you are paying up front for three things that haven't happened yet — high copper forever, a legal cure, and financing on decent terms.

## What the market believes

At $4.82 the market is pricing in something close to: the permit challenge resolves (or never bites), the financing closes without severe dilution, and copper stays meaningfully above the study's $4.78/lb long-term assumption — roughly $5.50/lb held flat gets you to today's price on funded-project math. That is not a crazy set of beliefs given LME copper is at $6.60/lb today, which is why this is a "fairly contested" name rather than an obvious short.

The name is also no longer undiscovered: market cap is up ~287% year over year, Scotiabank (C$14 target) and Maxim ($15) have initiated coverage, a short-seller (Viceroy) has published against it, and a fresh 13G/A ownership filing landed August 14. Bulls and bears are both already here.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the primary legal document; most commentary hasn't.** The ESA letter (pages 18–27, read directly) does two things the company's reassuring press release does not address: it finds the breach is ongoing ("maintaining in force" the permit), and it confirms — citing the Ministry's own January 2024 doctrine — that the June 2026 permit amendments were processed as "non-deteriorating" changes, meaning they did *not* re-run the environmental review. The company's "permits in good standing" line is accurate and irrelevant to the actual risk.
- **We rebuilt the valuation from the company's own feasibility study**, including the sensitivity figure the first pass on our own desk missed: NPV of US$235M at $4.78/lb copper and US$559M at the March 4, 2026 spot price (~$5.88/lb), per the [company's feasibility study press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/blue-moon-announces-results-of-nussir-project-feasibility-study-302743955.html). That gives a disclosed, not invented, slope of roughly US$295M of project value per $1/lb of copper — which cuts both ways (see next section).
- **We caught the timing trap.** The most likely near-term corporate action is an equity raise or a stream on tough terms. Waiting for that event is free option value; buying before it is paying for someone else's exit.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the symmetric case, and our challenging bench made it well:

- **At today's actual copper price, the market may be *under*paying.** Extending the study's own disclosed sensitivity to the current LME price of ~$6.60/lb implies a funded project plus cash worth ~$8/share — roughly 70% above the tape. The prosecution's claim that the stock trades above any plausible funded value was **wrong**, an artifact of its own hand-built math (detailed below). If copper simply stays where it is and the two contingencies clear, today's buyer does very well.
- **The permit risk may resolve quietly.** The ESA letter itself signposts the cure path (a formal "scarcity of supply / overriding public interest" justification), and Norway and the EU both have strong political reasons — Critical Raw Materials Act status — to want this mine built.
- **The crowding cuts both ways**: two banks with targets near triple the current price mean any good news has ready buyers.

Our answer to all three: probably some of it is priced in, which is exactly why this is a "wrong price, live re-open" call rather than a short. We are explicitly **not** recommending a short — borrow availability was never checked, the company has every incentive and a visible path to cure, and the copper tape is against a short.
