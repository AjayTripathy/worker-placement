# PITCH — VNCE — VERDICT
**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION** — Take a token starter now (≤$35k / ~0.15% of book) at ~$4.96 under the already-tripped legacy price alert, with the real decision deferred to the Q2 FY26 10-Q landing this week; full starter to ≤0.4% only if the redacted royalty minimum is ≤$3M/yr and H1 cash flow is positive ex-refund.

## What we believe

Vince Holding is a ~$64M market-cap apparel company (12.85M shares at the pack print of $4.96) that just did three things at once, and the market has only priced one of them.

1. **The Q2 print was mostly a tariff refund, and we accept that.** Adjusted operating income of $16.4M versus $5.5M last year contained a **$10.4M IEEPA tariff-refund benefit** ($7.2M wholesale, $3.2M direct-to-consumer). Strip it and you get roughly **$6.0M versus $5.5M on 11.7% more sales** — an ex-refund adjusted operating margin of ~7.3% against 7.5% last year. There is no operating leverage in this business. We do not capitalize $1.02 of adjusted EPS or a 20% margin. Primary source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001579157/000119312526387188/vnce-ex99_1.htm
2. **The balance sheet quietly got much better, and almost nobody said so.** Borrowings fell to **$12.3M from $31.1M** a year ago; excess availability rose to **$63.6M from $42.6M**. Inventory **fell** to $73.4M from $76.7M. The at-the-market equity program sold **zero** shares in the quarter with only $0.9M left on it. Every "the balance sheet is deteriorating" leg in the original brief is factually backwards.
3. **The structure changed in a way that is not yet quantified anywhere.** On 8/27/26 VNCE bought the OVO (October's Very Own) operating companies for **$3.00 total** while Authentic Brands paid **$117,647,058.82** for the intellectual property; VNCE kept 5% of the IP subsidiary and took a worldwide exclusive apparel license at "a single digit percentage" on retail/e-com and "ten percent (10%) or lower" on wholesale. **The annual guaranteed minimum royalty is redacted.** The deal was funded by amending the existing asset-based loan, not with new capital. Primary source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579157/000119312526369683/vnce-20260827.htm

So our belief is narrow and cheap: at ~5.0x enterprise value to FY25 adjusted EBITDA ($15.1M), we are being asked to pay very little for a de-levered balance sheet plus a ~$50M-sales brand with a stated path to $100M+ sales and $10–12M of EBITDA by FY2030 — while the one number that could break it (the guaranteed minimum) is hidden behind a redaction that resolves in days, not months. That is a bounded, dated, small-size bet on a disclosure event.

## What the market believes

The tape took the stock down roughly 17% intraday on print day and closed near –24% from the ~$6.74 pre-print level, into a pack print of $4.96 — **against a raised full-year guide and a $0.76 headline EPS beat**. The marginal holder appears to believe: (a) the beat is fake because it is a refund, (b) the Q3 Vince-only guide of +5–8% is a step down from +11.7% and *still* leans on ~$2.6M more refunds, and (c) OVO is a cost and a distraction — management itself calls it earnings-neutral in FY26.

The market is right on (a). It is probably wrong on (b) in two-year-stack terms: H2 FY25 grew +5.4% against H1 declines of –2%, so Q3 laps the hardest comp of the year; +5–8% on that lap is not deceleration. And on (c), nobody has published a number for the brand at all.

## Why we might have edge

- **The sell-off cannot be discovery.** On 8/27 — ten trading days before the print — the company stated in the OVO release that Q2 would land at the high end of prior guidance **excluding any benefit from tariff refunds** (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260827526062/en/). The "the beat was one-off" fact was public before the print. So the –24% is either structural (the redacted minimum, OVO reinvestment) or it is positioning exhaustion in a stock that had run enormously off its $1.95 low. Knowing which one it is *is* the trade, and neither bench answered it.
- **The binding document lands this week, not in December.** VNCE files its 10-Q the same day as its results 8-K (Q1: 8-K 0001193125-26-271795 and 10-Q 0001193125-26-271897, both 6/16/26). The 9/10/26 8-K arrived with **no companion 10-Q**, and the statutory due date for a period ended 8/1/26 is ~9/15/26. That document carries purchase accounting for OVO, the assumed liabilities, the ABL Third Amendment, H1 operating cash flow, possibly the license exhibit. The red bench gated its entire reopen on a December filing and never checked the clock.
- **The redaction is itself the signal.** A guaranteed minimum a licensee can comfortably cover is usually disclosed. One that is redacted is more often one that is uncomfortable, escalating, or tied to the $100M-by-FY2030 target rather than to today's $50M. We are structuring size around that asymmetry rather than assuming it away.
- **Nobody has valued the asset we are paying royalties on.** The bear case litigated the royalty cost in detail and never once priced OVO's $10–12M of target EBITDA against a $64M market cap.

## Why it might be priced in

Honestly: most of it probably is.

- The refund-loaded quarter, the flat ex-refund margin, and the Q3 step-down are all in the press release the whole market read. Our kill on the print is **consensus-class and already transacted** — a –24% day is not a market that missed it.
- The balance-sheet improvement is also in the same release. It is not proprietary; it is merely under-discussed in a name with essentially no coverage.
- +154% off the 52-week low is not a dislocation. A 39.5% drawdown from an $8.20 high in a stock that tripled is a retracement in a winner, and "cheap versus the high" is the weakest form of cheap.
- The OVO prize is a FY2030 number from management, on a brand that just sold its IP to a licensing firm. Brand-management transactions frequently mark the *top* of a brand's cultural arc, not the base of a growth ramp. Paying $3.00 for the opcos means the seller thought the operating business was worth approximately nothing.
- We cannot verify OVO demand direction at all. Both benches' consumer detectors came back UNCHECKABLE (baseline-zero heat feed, bot-walled retail channel). We are long a consumer trend we cannot measure.

## What could go wrong

**Failure narrative 1 — The redacted minimum is bigger than either bench modeled, because a minimum is not a rate.** Both benches sized the royalty by multiplying a rate band by today's ~$50M of OVO sales and got $2.5–5.0M/yr. That is an arithmetic error of kind, not of degree: a *guaranteed minimum* in a brand license is typically a floor keyed to the licensor's growth expectations — here, an explicit $100M+ target by FY2030 — and it usually escalates on a schedule. A minimum that starts at $4M and steps to $8–10M by FY29 is entirely ordinary structure and would exceed half of group FY25 adjusted EBITDA as a *fixed* charge in the years the equity is supposed to re-rate. Tripwire: the 10-Q purchase-accounting and commitments notes. Monitorable.

**Failure narrative 2 — The refund faucet closes and there is nothing behind it.** Q2 carried $10.4M; Q3 guidance carries another ~$2.6M. Ex-refund the business earns ~$6M a quarter of adjusted operating income against a roughly $76M enterprise value, before any royalty. If the refund stream ends and the core simply holds flat while OVO consumes working capital, FY27 EBITDA lands near $12–15M and the stock is fairly valued at $4, not cheap. Tripwire: the tariff-refund line item in the Q3 release; monitorable but coarse.

**Failure narrative 3 — NO MONITORABLE TRIPWIRE: the brand goes cold.** OVO is a culture-dependent label whose equity is tied to a single artist's public standing. The thing that kills it is not a filing, a covenant, or a comp — it is a reputational event, a cultural rotation, or a slow fade in relevance that shows up in the P&L four to six quarters after it shows up in the culture. We have **no instrument** for this: `consumer_product_heat` returned BASELINE_ZERO with heat, rank, and social all null; `consumer_product_reviews` returned a bot wall on the retail channel and a baseline-only cohort delta; `app_review_velocity` has no first-party SKU to poll. There is no price, filing, or dated disclosure that fires before the damage is done. **Unknown-unknown class: celebrity/culture-linked intangible impairment — a reputational-beta exposure with no financial-statement leading indicator and no public telemetry we can lawfully or technically acquire.** We name it because we cannot sense it, and size accordingly.

**The reflexive / structural leg.** P180 owns ~7.22M of 12.85M shares, leaving a float of roughly **4.4M shares (~$22M)**. Our own ADV estimate is unverified — call it 200–400k shares on a normal day, several multiples of that in the week after a print. A $35k starter is ~7,000 shares: under 4% of a normal day, invisible. A full 0.4% sleeve on a $25M book is ~$100k, ~20,000 shares, still under 10% of ADV *if* our ADV estimate is right — and that estimate is the single most fragile number in this deck, because in a 4.4M-share float the bid can vanish entirely on a quiet day and our exit becomes a multi-day event exactly when we most want a single-day one. Predicted flow is adverse in one specific way: any P180 or insider sale into this float moves the stock more than the news it represents, and any positive re-rating draws momentum flow into a float too small to absorb it, which means the upside branch overshoots and the downside branch gaps. Doctrine interaction: our consensus-kill rule pushes toward a starter precisely *because* the bear case is public, while our thin-float rule pushes toward zero — the resolution is that the position is capped by liquidity before it is capped by conviction, and the legacy price sensor (below) independently caps it at speculative-tag size. Every one of those constraints binds at a smaller number than our thesis would justify. That is the correct outcome and we are not overriding it.

If this position loses money, the most likely reason will be ___ that the redacted guaranteed minimum royalty turns out to be a large, escalating fixed charge on an earnings base that never had operating leverage once the tariff refunds stopped.
