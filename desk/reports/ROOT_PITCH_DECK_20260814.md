# PITCH — ROOT — PROPOSED VERDICT: REJECT / NO POSITION — reopen at the Q3'26 print on pre-registered reserve gates, or below ~$34 (**PROPOSED — PENDING ADJUDICATION**)

## What we believe

Root, Inc. (auto insurer, ~$794M market cap at ~$51 on 15.49M shares) is showing headline results that are better than the underlying business. Second-quarter net income of ~$25M and a 92.1% combined ratio look strong, but both are propped up by two levers that don't repeat: a deliberate cut to marketing spend, and — the decisive court finding — accelerating releases of loss reserves set aside in prior years. In the first half of 2026, $28.5M of favorable prior-year reserve development funded roughly 47% of the $61.3M in net income. Strip that out and the company earns about $66M a year, which puts the stock at roughly 10–12x earnings — not the ~7.9x the original brief advertised. Meanwhile the current-year (accident-year) loss ratio is drifting *worse* (+0.9 points year over year), the company is giving back rate to customers that hasn't yet flowed through results, retention of its own risk jumped from 94.2% to 98.5% (less reinsurance cushion), and earnings are effectively untaxed today only because of a valuation allowance. At 2.4x book value, the price does not pay us for those risks. Both benches independently landed on no-entry: the red (bear) bench at conviction 8/10, the blue (bull-check) bench at 7/10.

## What the market believes

The stock trades around $51, down about 51% from its 52-week high of $104.47 but already bounced ~25% off its $40.91 low — so the market has partially repriced but stabilized. The visible bull case is the surface arithmetic: annualize Q2's $25M net income and you get ~$102M against a ~$794M cap (≈7.9x earnings, ~31% return on equity), a shrinking share count from buybacks, and a 51%-of-new-business distribution partnership with Carvana. The market is treating reported earnings as clean run-rate earnings. That is the specific belief the court refuted.

## Why we might have edge

- **We read the reserve rollforward; the headline multiple didn't.** The finding that ~47% of first-half net income came from prior-year reserve releases comes straight from Note 5 of the 10-Q filed 2026-08-05 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1788882/000178888226000061/root-20260630.htm). Reserve releases are a depleting asset, not a business.
- **We caught the masking direction.** The calendar-year loss ratio improved ~1 point while the accident-year ratio worsened ~0.9 points — the improvement is entirely funded by releases. That is the classic pattern of an insurer's headline flattering deteriorating current-period underwriting, and it is happening just as a written rate give-back has yet to earn into results.
- **We verified the cap structure and tax situation from the primary filing**, not summaries: Carvana warrants (7.2M shares max) are all struck at $180–$540 — no dilution risk near $51, but also no equity incentive left for the partner sourcing 51% of new business, with expiry 2027-09-01.
- **The court corrected its own errors both directions** (see the table below), so the surviving numbers have been adversarially stress-tested rather than advocated.

## Why it might be priced in

Honesty requires the symmetric case:

- The stock is already down half from its high. A market that has cut the price 51% has priced in *something*; this is not an undiscovered problem.
- The blue bench softened the bear math meaningfully: the red bench's claim of +2.7 points of accident-year deterioration was a calculation error (real figure +0.9 points), and a zero-release assumption is too harsh — well-reserved insurers release reserves routinely, so the honest ex-release multiple is 10–12x, not a disaster.
- There is a real, un-credited bull catalyst: the tax valuation allowance. The 10-Q says it may be released "in the foreseeable future," which would be a one-time boost to book value and reported earnings.
- The buyback is real (just half the advertised pace), and the Q2 one-time $4.4M private-equity impairment modestly understated investment income.

Net: the bear case survives, but at reduced severity — this is "not cheap enough for the risk," not "proven value trap."
