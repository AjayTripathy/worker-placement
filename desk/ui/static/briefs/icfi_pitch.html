01The situation

A −25% federal shock, taken as if it were the whole company

In 2025, DOGE terminations-for-convenience and the Oct–Nov shutdown hit ICF's civilian book directly: ~$418M of backlog terminated (~6.4% of revenue), federal revenue fell ~25% to ~$855M, and federal dropped from ~54% to ~43% of the mix. The stock fell a third from its ~$101 high. The market extrapolated the shock forward — that a shrinking-government thesis had permanently impaired the business.

The question this deck answers: is the federal cut a step-down that's now stabilizing, or a slide that keeps sliding? The award tape has an answer the price hasn't caught up to.

…but honest about the strength

the temper the filings alone wouldn't give you

On a like-for-like 9-month basis, FY26 award flow ($198M) is essentially flat vs FY25 ($192M) and still ~40% below FY24's $331M. Federal has stopped falling but is not re-accelerating.

So we do not underwrite federal growth. Federal just has to not roll over — a floor, a removed down-signal. Everything above the floor comes from commercial.

The commercial engine

where the thesis actually lives

The energy/utilities commercial book is >60% of 2026 revenue and guided to double-digit growth — a secular grid-modernization / energy-transition tailwind that has nothing to do with federal budgets.

Total-company book-to-bill of 1.21× ($2.2B of awards) confirms the aggregate order book is growing — the non-federal books are more than filling the federal hole.

06What makes us wrong

The honest kill-list

A fresh federal termination round
The tape floor breaks — new de-obligations spike back toward the 2025 crisis levels. The "cut is behind it" leg fails; back to bear.
Commercial doesn't reaccelerate
Q2 commercial stays soft (the Q1 dip wasn't timing). With federal flat and commercial stalled, there's no growth engine — it's just cheap-for-a-reason.
The award tape misleads
Prime-only obligations are lumpy (FY-end option spikes) and lead/lag revenue; two up-quarters is a thin base. It could be noise, not a turn.
Leverage bites in a slower recovery
The health-M&A debt is fine while booking; a longer federal drought + a soft commercial year would pressure coverage. Watch net debt / EBITDA at the print.
