On The Beach Group

The cheapness was never there — it was other people's holiday money

Ticker OTB (London) Price ~176p Market cap ~£255m / ~$343m Verdict PASS Screen said 6.9× EV/EBIT As of 21 Jul 2026
What this security is

The common stock of On The Beach Group plc, a UK online travel agent that sells package beach holidays — flight-plus-hotel to the Mediterranean, Canaries, Egypt and Turkey — primarily to British consumers. It surfaced on our European value screen as a cheap, under-covered UK small-cap (a "research-desert orphan"); it reached full diligence, and we declined it.

PASS · diligence 3.5 / 10 Screen 6.9× EV/EBIT → honest 9–14× Guide cut ~45% on a demand shock Buyback stepped down 5×
The bottom line

On The Beach looked like the ideal find: a neglected UK small-cap trading at under 7× operating earnings with a big buyback. Every one of those facts fell apart on inspection. The "cheapness" was an accounting mirage — nearly all the company's cash is ring-fenced customer money it is not allowed to keep, and once you strip it out the multiple is a fair-to-full 9–14×, not 7×. The disruption behind the recent slide is a demand shock, not a one-off glitch. The guidance wasn't merely trimmed — it was cut roughly 45%. And the celebrated buyback was last year's program; this year's is one-fifth the size, with the founder selling. We pass. It re-enters the funnel only if the honest multiple falls below 7× and the autumn trading update confirms the recovery.

What the market believes

A quality compounder oversold on a temporary wobble. The bull case, and the screen that surfaced it, reads OTB as a capital-light, cash-generative travel platform gaining share against Jet2 and TUI, run by owners who returned 14% of the share count in buybacks last year. On that view the 2026 setback is a passing disruption, the balance sheet is stuffed with net cash, summer bookings are up double digits, and at ~7× operating earnings a patient buyer is paid to wait for the rebound. It is the textbook "good orphan": cheap, buying back stock, temporarily marked down.

What we found — and why it's a pass

Four load-bearing facts, four failures. This is not a close call dressed as a pass; the thesis dissolved on primary sources.

  • The cash is not the company's. At the March-2026 half-year, OTB held £210m of cash — but £210m of it is ring-fenced trust money (customer prepayments held under UK travel-protection rules), matched by an equal customer liability and legally unable to fund the business. Free corporate cash was negative £0.5m; the company runs net debt of ~£27.5m. Netting the trust cash — as the screen did — invents a phantom enterprise value of ~£72m and a fake 6.9× multiple. The honest enterprise value is ~£282m, so the real multiple is ~9.4× last year's operating earnings and 10–14× this year's. Not cheap.
  • The "disruption" is demand, not a glitch. Management's own words: the Middle-East conflict "has impacted consumer demand since 1 March," prompting them to pull guidance. Egypt and the Eastern Mediterranean are core destinations. Half-year operating profit roughly halved. This is a demand shock to the product, not a fixable internal problem.
  • The guidance was cut ~45%, not trimmed. The number that matters is not "to £18–25m" — it is from £39–43m, the company's own earlier guide for this year. That is a trend break, and the current-year multiple is computed against the reduced figure.
  • The buyback shrank 5×, and the founder is selling. The £50m / 14%-of-capital program the bull case cites was last year's. This year's authorization is £10m — a five-fold step-down consistent with the cash squeeze — and it has been buying at ~216p while the stock now trades ~176p. Founder Simon Cooper has been a net seller. The capital-return signal is retreating, not advancing.

Strip the mirage and OTB is a fair-to-fully-valued online travel agent whose guide was cut on a live demand shock, whose returns are pulling back, and whose recovery rests on a summer that the last update showed up only single digits. There is no discount to own.

Why this one is worth a written note

Most declines get a line in the ledger. This one earns a report because it exposed a whole category of false signal. Any business that holds large customer floats — travel agents, betting operators, payment processors, insurance brokers, estate agents — will screen artificially cheap, because the screen nets client money it can never touch into enterprise value. OTB is the cleanest example: a genuine 9–14× business that printed as 6.9×. We have since wired a permanent guard into the European screen that flags these models and re-computes their multiples with the customer cash excluded — so the next OTB is caught before it ever reaches diligence, not after.

The court record

OTB was killed at the diligence stage, so it did not need a full adversarial bench — but the reasoning was argued both ways before the verdict landed.

The bear (which won)

The cheapness is an accounting illusion: the entire cash balance is ATOL trust money, free cash is negative, and the honest multiple is 9–14×. The guide cut is a demand trend, not a one-off; the buyback fell five-fold; the founder is selling. There is no discount and no improving capital-return signal — decline.

The bull (which lost, honestly heard)

Record half-year volumes (up ~7%), share gains against a flat-to-down market, a premiumized mix (four- and five-star >80% of bookings), and a genuinely temporary geopolitical shock that could stabilize into the summer. If the conflict de-escalates and bookings convert to margin, ~£25m of profit on ~9× is a fair recovery entry.

Presiding ruling

The bull case requires two conditional legs to both come true — the conflict resolving and margin (not just volume) recovering — neither of which is priced or in our control. The load-bearing fact is dispositive and needs no forecast: the 6.9× that made this interesting is a trust-cash artifact, and on the true ~9–14× there is simply nothing to own. Verdict: PASS. Revisit only if the honest multiple falls below 7× and the autumn pre-close confirms ≥£25m of profit and the buyback is re-upped — three gates, not one.

What it would take to change our mind

  • Honest multiple below 7× — i.e. a materially lower price on the true ~£282m enterprise value, not the phantom £72m.
  • Autumn pre-close (Sept/Oct) confirming ≥£25m profit — the summer converting to margin, not just booking volume.
  • The buyback re-upped beyond the shrunken £10m — a capital-return signal turning back up rather than retreating.
  • All three, not one. A single leg is a value trap with a story.
