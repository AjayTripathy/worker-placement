What survived, and what the court corrected

The business is real. Revenue +128% YoY (US$43.6M FY25), driven by producing royalties (Karlawinda, Timok, Caserones, Laverton) — GEO-driven, not land-sale-padded like the trap names. Net cash US$53M, undrawn US$150M facility. Four cornerstone assets ≈ 47% of NAV — diversified, no single-asset dominance.
"Discount to NAV" was a misframe — corrected. It trades ~1.02x P/NAV — roughly at NAV, not below. The real edge is a discount to the peer multiple (juniors 1.22x, intermediates 1.56x): a re-rating bet as it scales, gets covered, and enters indexes — not a value bet. The pullback from ~$20.9 to $15.65 may have opened a genuine ~0.85–0.95x window.
No hidden dilution. The scary "63.8M vs 78.4M shares" is a 10:1 consolidation artifact, not a GROY-style dilution machine. The merger was roughly NAV-neutral. Anyone quoting a per-share figure without the 10:1 will be off by an order of magnitude — the trap here.

The crux — we diligenced the shareholder, and the tail was mostly stale

The prior read treated Tether as an unhedgeable black box: "37.8% economic, board-blessed path to 51.8%, no standstill — a forced-seller air-pocket waiting to happen." A deep DD on Tether itself took that apart on three axes:

The control figure was stale — and it's running in reverse. The latest primary filing (Schedule 13D, Dec 31 2025) shows Tether at 31.9% of the merged Elemental Royalty — not 37.8%, not a majority. It briefly hit ~51% of the old Elemental Altus via an option in Oct 2025, then the EMX merger diluted it back to 31.9%, and the pending Vizsla deal takes it toward ~27–28%. Tether is board-supporting its own dilution to fund ELE's growth — the opposite of a creeping squeeze-out.
It has no solvency reason to sell. Tether's Q1-2026 BDO report shows $191.8B reserves vs $183.5B liabilities → an $8.2B excess buffer and +$1.0B profit in the quarter off ~$117B of T-bills. An entity generating ~$1B a quarter has no liquidity motive to dump a ~$300M ELE block. Caveat, stated plainly: this is an attestation, not an audit — management-scoped, point-in-time (a single date), with no opinion on liability completeness or what happens between snapshots, and Tether's promised full audit has never arrived. So read the buffer as a credible floor on solvency, not a settled fact — it refutes the *forced*-seller tail, it doesn't certify the balance sheet.
It's a sticky, aligned holder. Tether carries ~$19.8B of physical gold (its largest non-Treasury reserve line, ~80 tonnes) plus its own XAU₮ token — and ELE now pays its dividend with an option to receive Tether Gold. Tether has wired ELE into its own gold-token rails; you don't build that and flip the equity. Its control-deal record elsewhere is friendly-premium (Adecoagro tender at +12.4% to the unaffected price, unanimous board; Rumble with no board rights) — no lowball freeze-out precedent.

What genuinely remains — and it's why this is fair pay, not free: (1) there is no contractual standstill, so Tether is legally free to creep back up (though any related-party or going-private deal trips Canadian MI 61-101 — a majority-of-the-minority vote Tether's own shares are excluded from, a real procedural guardrail); (2) a 2024 DOJ probe into whether Tether's backers committed bank fraud — no public charges, status unverifiable, the true residual binary; (3) crypto-sentiment beta bleeding into a gold-royalty name on any USDT headline.

What the full court corrected

Correction 1 — it's levered gold-beta, NOT crash-convexity. The scorecard's "+2.9% in the 2020 crash / +10% in the 2025 tariff shock" was refuted: IDR fell with the mining complex in the April-2025 tariff risk-off ($12.78 → $11.74), then recovered as gold rose. Its "convexity" is levered gold upside — ~2–3× bullion on a spike via operating leverage — not insurance against a liquidity crash. In the bucket it amplifies the gold-royalty factor; it does not diversify it.
Correction 2 — the Lemhi Pass REE "option" is story-stage, yet already priced. The 10-K states verbatim it has "not established any known reserves," carries the project at $0, and expenses the costs — drilling is only planned for 2026. The mineral is monazite: thorium-encumbered, with no US processing route. Yet a ~$100M+ premium is embedded in the multiple (~22× on peak gold vs ~20× for producing peers). You're paying today for a call the company hasn't yet drilled.
The revenue ramp is ~95% gold PRICE, not volume. Golden Chest production is roughly flat (~12,500 oz); the $7.3M → $14.5M/qtr climb is almost entirely the gold price. So the P/E capitalizes peak gold: ~22× at $4,090, but ~43× at $3,000 and ~79× at $2,500 — brutal downside operating leverage, with AISC (~$1,892) rising.
What holds up. Genuinely profitable and self-funded (~$8.8M/qtr operating cash flow, net cash ~$18.8M, clean unqualified audit — the going-concern text is expansion-capital boilerplate). Dilution is disciplined — the $52M raise was into strength and per-share value rose. Golden Chest reserve life (~6yr reserve / ~19yr resource, growing) makes the run-rate real, not a high-grade-pod spike. The single-asset tail is still real, but it's a real mine.

The crux — you're paying peak-gold + a free-option premium at $29.71

Split the ~$470M cap: the producing gold mine at a normalized ~$3,000 gold is worth roughly half the current price; the rest is peak-gold multiple + the ~$100M REE premium. That's not fair convexity — it's paying up for both the gold cycle's top and a rare-earth lottery ticket. The mild tell in management's own behavior: raising equity into strength is what you do when you think the stock is fully valued.
