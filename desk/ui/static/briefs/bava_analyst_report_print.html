Bavarian Nordic

A rejected buyout, a cheaper company, and a floor the register drew itself

Ticker BAVA (Copenhagen) Price DKK 181 Market cap ~DKK 15.5bn Verdict STARTER — half size Class Fairly-paid risk + dormant M&A call As of 21 Jul 2026
What this security is

The common stock of Bavarian Nordic A/S, a Danish vaccine maker with two engines: a fast-growing travel-health business (rabies, tick-borne encephalitis, typhoid, cholera, chikungunya) and a lumpy but high-margin public-preparedness business selling smallpox and mpox vaccines to governments, chiefly the United States. It carries roughly DKK 3bn of net cash and pays no dividend.

The bottom line

Ten months ago private-equity buyers offered DKK 250 a share and were turned away by their own target's largest owners as too low. The stock now trades at DKK 181 — 28% below that offer — but this is not simply a busted deal on sale. In the months since the offer lapsed the company raised its guidance twice, so the buyers walked away from an asset that has since gotten cheaper on its numbers, not just its price. We are buying a half-sized starter: a fairly-priced, cash-rich vaccine business at the low end of an eight-month trading range, with a dormant takeover option on top that the market is pricing at close to nothing. We size it small because there is no near-term catalyst, the cash currently earns its keep for no one, and the next real information arrives around November 2026.

What the market believes

The deal is dead, and so is the story. The consensus read is straightforward: a credible pair of buyers ran a full process, reached 60% acceptance, fell short of the two-thirds they needed, and left. Under Danish takeover rules the same bidders are broadly barred from returning for about a year, and they hold no stake to build on. With the takeover premium gone, the market has re-priced BAVA as what it is underneath — a specialty vaccine company whose headline growth was a one-off mpox cycle now fading, whose preparedness revenue depends on a single government customer at a moment of budget pressure and vaccine skepticism in Washington, and whose pipeline just lost its RSV program in a failed trial. On that view DKK 181 is not a discount to DKK 250; it is fair value for a de-rated, ex-takeover, no-catalyst business, and the money is better left in cash earning ~4% while the year runs out.

What we believe

The two pillars of that bear case are factually out of date. The market is pricing a company that stopped existing in February.

  • The mpox "cliff" is not ahead of the numbers — it is inside them, and the guidance went up anyway. On 13 May 2026 the company raised 2026 revenue guidance to DKK 5.5–5.7bn and its margin target to ~28% (from DKK 5.0–5.2bn and 25%), after the US preparedness agency exercised $97m of freeze-dried-vaccine options. The preparedness line is a DKK 1.5–2.0bn base plus government options layered on top, reaching ~DKK 2.3–2.5bn — the options are the swing, and they landed. Government orders land rolling, in the second half for the following year — the "zero secured for 2027" that frightens the bear is the normal cadence of the order book, not evidence of a cliff.
  • Travel health is a real, organic compounder, not an accounting illusion. It grew roughly 30% in 2025 to ~DKK 3bn and beat its own guidance; first-quarter 2026 travel revenue was DKK 721m. This is the recurring engine SIGA-style stockpile names simply do not have — and it is what turns a preparedness normalization into a de-rating rather than an impairment.
  • The floor is empirical, not rhetorical. We do not claim the rejected DKK 250 is a price anyone must pay — a control-transaction reservation price binds acquirers, not the day-to-day marginal seller, and the tape proved it (the stock fell 21% the week the offer lapsed). But in the eight months since, it has built a stable DKK 172–203 band, with the ~DKK 172 low tested twice and bought both times. That is revealed demand under the price, independent of any bidder.

On the raised guidance the enterprise value is roughly 8× EBITDA for a government-backed countermeasure franchise wrapped around a 30% grower — cheap-ish, not merely fair. Underneath sits ~DKK 3bn of net cash and a completed buyback. And on top sits a dormant takeover option: buyers who wanted it at 250, a register that priced itself above 250, and an asset that has since improved. We think that option is worth far more than the ~nothing embedded at the band low.

Where the edge is — and isn't

This is not an information edge; the facts above are public. It is a willingness edge: owning a fairly-priced compounder at a demonstrated floor, sized to survive a year of dead money, in exchange for a cheap call on a re-approach we did not have to pay up for. The honest framing is fairly-paid risk with an option kicker, not alpha. We size it accordingly.

The court record

Every thesis on this desk is argued before it is owned: an independent diligence pass, then an adversarial red team paid to kill it, then a blue team paid to rescue what survives, then a presiding ruling. The BAVA case turned on a fact that only surfaced under pressure.

Diligence · 6.5 / 10 · Starter (small)

Corrected the original framing in two directions. The feared "stockpile-spike" business is genuinely diversified — travel health is a recurring ~DKK 3bn base — so a preparedness normalization is a de-rating, not a collapse. But the DKK 250 mark is dead history, not an enforceable floor, and there is no fireable catalyst inside twelve months. Verdict: a small starter on the business quality plus a soft ownership floor.

Red team · 7.5 / 10 · Wait

Two structurally sound blows. First, the "willing-holder floor" is a category error — a reservation price in a control deal says nothing about where the marginal seller trades the stock day to day; and the tape had, in fact, already traded 28% through it. Second, the November-2026 re-offer window is itself the information event: waiting means deploying later with knowledge, so buying now pays four months of negative carry for nothing. Notably, red declined to kill — the cash and travel-health floor are real — but argued the starter had no reason to exist today.

Blue team · 7.0 / 10 · Starter now, at the band low

Refused to converge, and won on verified facts rather than argument. Red's two load-bearing pillars — a 2027 preparedness cliff and inorganic travel growth — were shown to be wrong: a $97m order and a >DKK 700m contract already cover 2026–27, and travel health grew ~30% organically. Crucially, red and the diligence pass had both run the stale February guidance; the May raise moves the multiple from ~10× to ~8× and flips "fair" to "cheap-ish." On the re-offer option, blue priced it directly: waiting means chasing the gap when an approach prints, and the break-even probability of a re-approach that justifies buying now over waiting is only ~4–5% — any plausible odds clear it.

Presiding ruling

Blue wins the overturn — because it won on facts we verified, not rhetoric. We independently confirmed the raised guidance (13 May 2026) and pulled the actual price history: pre-lapse ~230–245, a 21% drop the lapse week, then an eight-month 172–203 band with the low twice-bought. Red's mechanism was correct — the rejected bid is not a floor — but its two fundamental pillars were factually stale, and the corrected multiple is a tailwind, not a neutral. We concede red's real points by sizing: half a normal starter, because the ~DKK 3bn cash has no stated return path, carry is ~zero, and the name is dead money until a catalyst fires. Verdict: half-starter, 190 shares at DKK 181 (~0.5% of the deployable book).

The concessions — featured, not buried

  • No return path for the cash. Bavarian Nordic has never paid a dividend; its stated priority is pipeline, then reinvestment, then M&A, with returns "eventually." The DKK 500m buyback completed on 13 July with no successor program announced. Roughly DKK 3bn of net cash currently works for no one — a floor, not a catalyst.
  • Dead money, honestly. No fireable catalyst inside twelve months (RSV was killed; the next pipeline entries slip to 2027), and ~zero carry against a ~4% cash floor and our live print-gated ideas elsewhere. This is why the position is half-sized.
  • The governance scar. Management recommended and campaigned for the DKK 250 deal that 40% of its own owners called too cheap. Whose interest it carries into the next approach is a fair question.
  • One-customer preparedness. Half the business is effectively US-government demand, under an administration cutting health budgets and skeptical of vaccines. Our rebuttal (orders already cover 2027) addresses the near term, not the structural concentration.
  • The re-offer bar is source-conflicted. The ~12-month standstill that dates the November-2026 window is standard under the Danish takeover order but not independently confirmed for this case — flagged, not closed, and it would need counsel before any takeover-arbitrage sizing.

Position & how it's graded

Half-starter: 190 shares at DKK 181 (~DKK 34k / ~US$5k, ≈0.5% of the deployable book), staged for submission on Copenhagen. Adds are gated on any one of: a re-approach tape-tell, a retest of the DKK 172–175 band low, or a new capital-return program that gives the cash a destination.

The dormant option is tracked as a public, dated forecast so the thesis grades itself:

Frozen forecast · BAVA-REOFFER

30% probability that a fresh takeover approach at ≥DKK 250 is announced by 5 November 2027. Against the blue team's ~4–5% break-even, the option is cheap even if our estimate is twice too high. If it resolves yes, the position pays the re-rate; if no, we have owned a cash-rich compounder at ~8× through a dead year — the fairly-paid-risk floor doing its job.
