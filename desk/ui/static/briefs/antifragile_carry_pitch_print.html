The allocator's problem

Every crisis hedge on the shelf makes you choose.

You want a book that is geared to disorder. But the products that offer it each force a bad trade — and you already know which.

The trade underneath all four: carry versus convexity. You either pay to hold the payoff, or you take regime risk that the crisis cooperates, or you buy the crowded theme. We claim those can be partly decoupled — and we bring the evidence, including where it fails.

The insight

Convexity doesn't have to decay.

The old way — buy the option

An out-of-the-money put overlay decays every day. You rent convexity, renew the premium, and must time the monetization perfectly. The carry is negative by construction.

Ours — own the business

Select cash-flowing companies whose fundamentals improve when the world breaks — a royalty stream that bids in a debasement, a utility that decouples, a supplier that re-rates on a supply shock. The convexity is in the equity; the carry is positive.

The catch, said plainly: this works for realized structural disorder — a supply rupture, a monetary debasement, a conflict that re-rates earnings over quarters. It is not a three-week VIX-spike hedge. If you need the liquidity-flush payoff in that window, buy puts. We'll say so in the room.

Elemental Royalty (ELE)  ·  now ownable at fair value  ·  $14.57 as of 20 Jul 2026

A real, net-cash gold-royalty junior trading below its peers' multiple. Its dominant risk is its cornerstone shareholder — so we diligenced Tether, not just the company. Claim: "Tether is a forced seller creeping to a majority squeeze-out." Method: we read the primary filings. Finding: the control figure was stale — the December 2025 ownership filing shows Tether at 31.9% and diluting (via successive mergers), not a majority, and it is supporting its own dilution to fund the company's growth. Tether's own reserve report shows an $8.2B surplus and roughly $1B of quarterly profit, so it has no solvency reason to dump the position. Important caveat, stated plainly: that reserve report is an attestation, not an audit — a single-date, management-scoped snapshot with no opinion on completeness, and the promised full audit has never arrived. Read the buffer as a credible floor on solvency, not a settled fact. The residual, genuinely fair-paid risks: no contractual standstill on Tether, an unresolved 2024 DOJ probe of Tether's backers, and crypto-sentiment spillover. Net: reclassified from speculative to fairly-paid risk you can own now, small, at the money.

Idaho Strategic (IDR)  ·  keep on the watchlist — wait for a lower price  ·  $27.64 as of 20 Jul 2026

A real, profitable single-mine Idaho gold producer — but the court corrected the scorecard on two counts. First: it is levered gold upside, not crash insurance. Claim: "up in the 2020 and 2025 crashes." Method: we checked the actual tape. Finding: it fell with the mining complex in the April-2025 risk-off, then recovered as gold rose — so in the sleeve it amplifies the gold factor rather than diversifying it. Second: its rare-earth "option" is story-stage — the company's own annual report carries the project at zero and states it has established no reserves, yet the market has already paid a premium for it. Its revenue climb is roughly 95% gold price, not volume, so the earnings multiple capitalizes peak gold. Verdict: own it cheaper — a fair band well below today's price — not a chase at $27.64. No carry.

What you'll ask — and what we concede

We led our own diligence with the attacks.

Concede — "convexity is just beta in a panic"

In a pure liquidity flush, correlations go to one for weeks and small names gap hardest. True. We hedge structural disorder, not the three-week spike — and the carry buys the time to survive the air-pocket. For the spike, buy puts.

Concede — "capacity is too small"

Correct, and it's the moat. The paper is mispriced because no large fund can cover a $100–500M name at size. If we could take your $500M ticket, the edge would already be gone.

Rebut — "why not replicate with ETFs?"

You can buy the theme. You can't go below the index's cap floor, you can't read the filings, and you can't exclude the traps — an index rebalances on a rule; we diligence on primary sources.

Rebut — "thematic basket dressed up"

A thematic ETF screens a keyword and buys the basket — including the four verticals our crisis data says get crushed. We hold only what's proven convex, name by name, court by court.

What could make us wrong

The risks, on the page — not in the footnotes.

  • It's resilience and carry, not explosive convexity. We draw roughly two-thirds of the market's crash and pay a carry. We do not print +3,000% in a quarter. If that's what you need, this is the wrong fund.
  • Miners are not the metal. The equity carry costs about half the crash protection of gold itself. The royalty layer is the best compromise, not a free lunch.
  • No live track record yet. We have the principal's seeded book and a backtest — and our standing rule is that backtests falsify, they don't confirm. We pitch on process and an honestly-caveated test, and build the audited record over 12–24 months.
  • The convexity vectors may correlate. Reshoring, supply-chain, and infrastructure can all fire on one industrial-policy regime — less diversified across types of disorder than the pitch implies. And the three gold-royalty names are about 0.85 correlated: one gold factor, not three units of diversification. We decompose it explicitly.
  • Positive carry in calm is an alpha claim. It needs live controls, not just history, before we call it proven rather than hoped-for.
