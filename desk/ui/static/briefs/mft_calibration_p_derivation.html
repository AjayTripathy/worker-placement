The method — and an honest disclosure

The nowcast method. For each monthly print we find a public leading indicator that precedes it (a peer's print, a weekly sales tracker, an agency data series, or the name's own trailing months), read the latest values, and map them to a probability the gate resolves favorable. The MFT edge is reading the operating trajectory before the print — so the P is only ever as good as the leading indicator behind it. Every P below is grounded in specific, dated numbers, listed per call.

What we DID use: primary external data — company IR (Costco, Toyota, TSMC), government agencies (Macau DICJ, Illinois Gaming Board, state cannabis departments), peer monthly prints (UMC, ASE), and standard trackers (Johnson Redbook, EIA gasoline, SIA/WSTS billings). Print dates verified against IR calendars. Three parallel research agents did the reads; the freeze_call discipline (reasoning + a plain what/how/conclusion + a closed event-type enum) enforced the write.

What we did NOT use — the honest part. We did not run the desk's registered SignalOS connectors/detectors to generate these P's programmatically. This was analyst/agent research reading external sources — reproducible in its reasoning, but not yet a pipeline. The one SignalOS connector we invoked, the conditioning layer (discovery_state, the "is-it-already-priced?" regime tagger), failed on this run (cloud-IP-blocked sources), so even the regime overlay is incomplete. So these are defensible, data-grounded, but hand-read probabilities — not connector-generated ones. That gap is the point of the fix at the bottom.

Below: the FOR / AGAINST behind the two most and two least confident calls (the full reasoning for all six is in the calibration ledger).

TSM — June revenue ≥ +30% YoY · 0.90 HARD

gate: reported June revenue ≥ ~NT$342.8bn (=+30% vs the NT$263.7bn base)
Why 0.90 — the leading indicator is nearly deterministic
  • FOR: Taiwanese peers report monthly by law, and June is already on the tape — UMC +22.9%, ASE +32.9%. TSMC guided +10% QoQ in USD; May already ran +30.1%. Consensus NT$400–440bn sits 15–28% above the +30% threshold.
  • AGAINST: the residual 0.10 is almost entirely FX — a strong NT$ could compress the NT$-terms print. The Jun-2025 base is wire-sourced (the 6-K 403'd), but the gate clears at any plausible base.
  • Read: this is a deliberate high-confidence tail call. Its job isn't to be a hard prediction — it's to test whether we're over-confident at 0.9 (a well-calibrated 0.90 should resolve TRUE ~90% of the time).

Macau GGR — MoM ≥ June · 0.68  /  YoY positive · 0.30 JUDGMENT

two gates, one event — to test threshold discrimination
Why these are judgment, not extrapolation
  • The driver is a readable structural event, not a data trend: the FIFA World Cup diverted Macau demand (June −12.1% YoY), and the final is Jul-19 — so the back third of July is post-tournament.
  • MoM ≥ June (0.68): June was a depressed base; recovery off it as the WC ends is the base case — but the tournament still covers ~⅔ of the month, so it's a genuine mid-range call, not a lock.
  • YoY positive (0.30): a swing all the way back to growth inside the same headwind, against a tough comp, is a low base-rate — Macquarie cut Q3 growth to +2%.
  • Read: logging both gates on one event is the point — it tests whether our P's discriminate across thresholds (a good forecaster should separate a 0.68 from a 0.30 on the same print).

Honest grading of the P-quality

The slate deliberately spans 0.30 → 0.90. A calibration set clustered at 0.7 teaches you nothing about the tails; a spread tests over/under-confidence across the whole range.

But note the construction bias: several of the high-P calls picked easy-to-clear gates — TSM "+30%" when consensus implies +30–50%; COST "+5%" when it's running +7–8%. Those aren't hard predictions; they're tail-calibration probes (does a 0.90 come true 90% of the time?). The genuine forecasting skill is tested by the judgment calls in the murky middle (Macau, and the contracting-cannabis operators at ~0.45). Read the eventual scorecard with that split in mind: hard-grounded calls grade our discipline, judgment calls grade our edge.

The fix — BUILT: pipeline P vs hand P

Done. Each leading-indicator reader is now an encoded connector (desk/leading_indicators.py): cached dated inputs + a live monthly fetch + an a-priori nowcast model (a normal-CDF map from the leading data to P, specified before looking at the hand P — not tuned to match). How close did we get? mean |Δ| = 0.078, median 0.081; 3 of 6 within 0.05. The three mechanical calls (TSM/COST/SIA) reproduce to ±0.03 — the reasoning was straightforward and is now faithfully a program. The three judgment calls diverge, and the divergence is the finding: on TM the human added caution the model lacks (encode a comp-difficulty feature next); on MACAU YoY the human anchored toward 0.3 while the model, refusing to compress the tail, says 0.09 — a case where the pipeline is probably the better forecaster.

Important: this compares the two estimates, not accuracy — which is right is only settled when the prints resolve (Jul-13 → Aug-5). The value already banked: the P is now reproducible, auditable, tail-disciplined, and re-runnable monthly. The frozen calls are NOT revised — they stand at the hand P; the pipeline P is the shadow the next batch will be generated from.
