Read

Liquidity is not the risk — concentration is. With no margin/leverage in the book, even a -27% crash leaves $9.14M raisable from cash + marketable holdings alone — many multiples of the $2.28M in claims (mortgage + the Sept tax reserve). You are a forced BUYER, not a forced seller: drawdowns are discounts.
The real tail is magnitude & concentration: Parametric (~40% of assets, tech-tilted) drives most of the worst-case loss, and the tech-wipeout / equity-crash scenarios cluster the damage in one correlated block. The natural hedges — munis, bonds, cash — are small. But the tax reserve is not a fixed claim: a selloff spikes harvesting and shrinks it — in the worst case by ~$803k, an automatic cushion (see Microstructure below).

Microstructure & second-order effects — encoded as programs

5 effects registered in desk/effects.py, dispatched per scenario (not hardcoded). ENCODED effects move the drawdown numbers above; NOTED effects compute an unbooked shadow the linear model misses. Each is a tested function — a new insight is a new registered effect, never an engine edit.

🌾 Tax-loss-harvest hedge ENCODEDtax · within tax-year peak ~$803k cushion · Tech / AI wipeout (Nasdaq −45%)
The incoming gain's tax reserve is ANTI-correlated with the market: a selloff pushes the harvester's lots underwater, spiking realized losses that offset the gain and shrink the reserve. Convex (bigger crash → more harvest), capped at the gain, one tax-year only — the tax code pays part of a crash's bill. → worst case: Parametric -46% → +$2.16M harvest → reserve shrinks $803k.
🏠 Fixed-rate mortgage = a rates/inflation short NOTEDrates · permanent peak ~$98k shadow · Rate shock (+200bp)
You owe the principal nominally regardless, but the ECONOMIC value of below-market fixed debt FALLS as rates rise — you hold cheap fixed debt while the world reprices. A real hedge in rate-shock / stagflation, not booked to net worth (it's your residence, not a position you'd sell), and a reason never to prepay while rates are higher. → worst case: +200bp → below-market 2.5% loan worth ~$98k more to you (unbooked).
🕰️ Illiquid marks LAG — near-term drawdown understated NOTEDliquidity · 1-2 quarters peak ~$520k shadow · Venture winter / down-round wave
Private marks smooth and reset on a delay, so illiquid sleeves show FALSE stability in the first weeks of a crash while public sleeves reprice instantly. The planner books the hit immediately (conservative on timing) — reality is a lagged, lumpy catch-up. The danger is behavioral: smoothing understates the true hit and overstates diversification when it matters. → worst case: ~$520k of venture loss is real but marks lag — reported DD understates it near-term.
⚖️ 131/31 short book cuts both ways NOTEDequity-factor · intra-crash peak ~$226k shadow · Tech / AI wipeout (Nasdaq −45%)
The factor-short book (utilities / REITs / mREITs / regional banks) cushions a broad selloff — but in a FLIGHT-TO-QUALITY crash those defensives RALLY, so the short drags and the sleeve's effective beta rises above its net average. A single net beta understates the tail in a defensives-led rotation. → worst case: if defensives rally ~8%, the $2.82M short adds ~$226k of drag (not in the net beta).
🔗 Correlations compress toward 1 in a crisis NOTEDcorrelation · intra-crisis peak ~$6k shadow · Equity crash (−30% SPX, 2008-style)
The cross-asset hedges (munis/bonds rallying, alpha flat) rely on calm-market correlations. In a genuine liquidity freeze those COMPRESS — everything sells to raise cash and munis can gap WIDER even as Treasuries rally. Treat the diversifying hedges as weaker than modeled when volatility spikes: the hedge P&L below is what's at risk of evaporating. → worst case: the ~$6k muni/bond hedge can evaporate (or flip) if correlations spike.
