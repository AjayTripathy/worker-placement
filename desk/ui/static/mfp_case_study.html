The play

Midera Food Processing (MFP) — spun off Middleby, regular-way trading began July 7.

JUL 7 · ENTRY
Bought 150 @ $35.50 into the distribution-day open, near the $34.65 low — the moment mandate-constrained holders were dumping.
JUL 9 · EXIT
Sold 75 @ $39.47 (the $39.25 rung price-improved on a gap-up) + 75 @ $39.75, into the recovery and a fresh S&P-600-inclusion bid.
DISCIPLINE
It spiked to $42.99 intraday after we sold. We left that on the table by design — the exit is the trade; never marry the flow.

Why it was ours

When a company spins off a subsidiary, index funds holding the parent receive the new spinco — and if it doesn't fit their mandate, they sell it mechanically, on a schedule, without regard to what it's worth. That forced supply, hitting a market with no natural buyer base yet, creates a temporary discount. We didn't need to know anything about Midera that its holders didn't. We were paid to provide liquidity to a motivated seller on a known date.

The forced-sell day traded ~$83M; the exit window (Jul 8–9) another ~$59M. Working the real constraint — you can't buy more cheap shares than are being dumped, and the exit is the tighter side — the honest capacity is:

~$5–10M could have gone through this single event before slippage started eating the ~11% edge; ~$12M is the aggressive ceiling. That's 1,000–1,900× the size we actually ran. We used a sliver, so we captured the clean mid-price with zero market impact.

Two things matter for the strategy, not just this name. Capacity aggregates across events — index reconstitutions are quarterly, spin distributions land roughly monthly — so the sleeve is $5–10M × dozens of events a year. And it's capacity-ceilinged by design: $5–10M per event is a rounding error to a billion-dollar fund, which is precisely why the dislocation is left lying there for a small book to harvest. It compounds ~$1M beautifully and would starve at scale. The ceiling is the moat.

The honest asterisks

  • n = 1. One clean win is a datapoint, not a track record. The cohort has to build before it means anything.
  • The edge is real but small and ceilinged — it compounds a small book; it does not scale to a fund. That's the design, not a flaw.
  • We caught the middle, ~11.5% of a possible ~24% low-to-high. Disciplined, not optimized — don't mistake a clean exit for having nailed it.
