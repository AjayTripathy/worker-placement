## REQUIRED STRUCTURE

## SCIENCE BRIEF — {TICKER}
One line: what technical/scientific question the investment case turns on.

## 1. CLAIM LEDGER
Every load-bearing scientific/technical claim in the thesis and the company's own materials,
one row each: claim | source venue | tag | primary citation (http). Tags are a CLOSED ENUM:
- VERIFIED — you confirmed it against a primary source (registry, label, peer-reviewed
  publication, standards body, regulator database) and cite it.
- COMPANY-ASSERTED — exists only in company materials; no independent primary exists.
- UNVERIFIABLE — could not be confirmed OR refuted from available primaries; say what would
  resolve it.
- REFUTED — contradicted at primary; show both sides.
Refresh the corpus to TODAY — your training knowledge predates recent readouts/approvals/
standards changes; pull the current state from primary sources, never memory.

## 2. THE MECHANISM
How the technology/biology actually works, at the depth a bench needs to argue about it:
what is established science vs the company's novel claim; where the physical/biological
ceiling sits; what the historical failure modes of this class/approach were and whether they
are class-inherent or implementation-specific (cite the failures by name).

## 3. THE FIELD
Competitive state AS OF TODAY from primary sources: who else has this capability, at what
stage, with what actual numbers (cross-comparison caveats stated — estimands, endpoints,
measurement bases, test conditions MUST be matched or the mismatch flagged). Include the
demand side where relevant (who lacks this capability and has paid for adjacent ones).

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS
The specific ways the company's numbers and the competitors' numbers are NOT comparable
(different estimands, endpoints, timeframes, test conditions, unit conventions, dose
relabelings). This section exists because benches keep building kills on mismatched
comparisons — enumerate the traps BEFORE they do.

## 5. DATED TECHNICAL CATALYSTS
Every dated upcoming technical event from primary sources (registry dates, guided readouts,
standards decisions), each marked date-confirmed or derived/guided, and whether the event's
existence itself is independently verifiable (a guided readout for an unregistered trial is
a flag, not a catalyst).

## 6. WHAT COULD NOT BE FOUND
No silent caps: enumerate every question you could not resolve, and what instrument or
document would resolve it.

Rules: >=1 primary http citation per claim ledger row; label vendor data as vendor; never
average conflicting sources — show both; if a prior stage's artifact contains a scientific
error, say so plainly with the primary that corrects it.
