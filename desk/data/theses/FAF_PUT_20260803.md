## The four ideas

**1. What the market believes.**
First American is a fairly-valued title insurer in a cyclically depressed mortgage market — no strong view, thin coverage, and *no options market at all* in its long-dated puts: the Jan-2027 chain traded at ~8%-wide quotes with near-zero open interest before our offer. Nobody quotes it because no flow justifies the market-maker's attention. The absence of a price IS the market's belief.

**2. What we believe.**
Two independent legs, courted 2026-07 (WATCH 6/10, dual-leg):
- **Price leg:** FAF is worth owning at 66–70 — a title duopoly franchise (FAF/FNF) with escrow-float economics, priced for mortgage-volume pessimism that mean-reverts with any rate relief.
- **Operations leg:** title insurance is a top-tier AI-adopter candidate (search, examination, and curative work are labor that automates). The falsifiable gate: the Oct-21 print shows personnel ratio ≤ 44.0% — evidence the margin inflection is real, not narrated. (Sibling evidence: STC has the best filed intent language in the sector; the AI-adopter screen ranks the thesis structure, not the story.)

We do not need either leg to be right to be paid: the put was sold at *our* reservation price on a chain where no competing seller exists.

**3. Why this is edge.**
Three structural edges, none of which depend on forecasting:
- **Spread edge:** on a dead chain there is no mid to be arbitraged against. We rest at our price; a buyer either pays it or there is no trade.
- **Capacity edge:** no market maker will commit capital to quote size in FAF LEAPs — the flow doesn't fund the attention. Our position is 2 contracts. The trade exists *because* it is too small for anyone structured to compete for it. This is the aggregation-cost moat in miniature.
- **Bounded downside by construction:** the worst case is buying FAF at 66.60 — the entry the court already approved. There is no scenario where this position produces an outcome we didn't underwrite. (Unlevered, sleeve-capped, the RP_FAIR bar met.)

The counterparty analysis (demand map v2) says dead-chain put buyers are borrow-impaired bears or mandate-constrained hedgers — buyers who *cannot* express the view any other way and pay a captivity premium. Regulation excludes the informed-insider class: lockup agreements and Dodd-Frank anti-hedging policies bar concentrated holders from buying this protection.

**4. Why it isn't priced in / why does this trade exist.**
Coverage economics, same as the equity side of the book: the chain is unpriced because pricing it earns no one a living. Retail can't rest institutional-quality offers; institutions can't bother with 2-lot flow. An AI-underwritten book with zero marginal research cost and no minimum-ticket constraint is the only structure that can profitably write it. If this works, it scales to the census (TRU next; 17 eligible dead chains) — but only after forensics.

## Risks and kills
- **Assignment in a housing/credit break:** we own FAF at 66.60 into weakness. Accepted — it is the courted entry; the tail is bounded and unlevered. Sleeve context: total book assignment exposure across all short puts ~$44k.
- **Oct-21 gate fails** (personnel ratio > 44%): the operations leg dies; the position stands on the price leg alone — re-court, don't reflexively close (premium already banked; basis still inside band).
- **Adverse-selection finding at +20d:** program-level kill for scaling; this position is held to term regardless (2 lots, bounded).
- **Tax honesty:** premium is short-term ordinary income if it expires — anti-TLH by nature. Accepted as the template-2 exception: the experiment's information value, not the premium, is the point.

## How dead is the chain — quantified (tape of 2026-08-03, Yahoo chain data)

The entire FAF options market, all four expirations and every strike, traded **65 contracts today** — roughly **$490k of notional against ~$75M of daily stock volume (≈0.6%)**. Total open interest across the whole name is **1,230 contracts**, and over 80% of it sits in one October pocket (OI 1,037 — a single position, likely a covered-call or collar program; the only living corner of the chain).

Our expiry, January 2027, is the desert the program was designed for:
- **112 contracts of open interest across 15 strikes** — a median of a few lots per strike.
- Put quotes are not markets: **bids of 0.00 against placeholder asks near 4.80** at most strikes — literally no one willing to pay anything, against asks that are noise. The 65-strike "market" was 0.10 × 5.00.
- Today's Jan-2027 volume was 31 contracts — **our 2-lot fill was ~6% of the expiry's busiest day** and, at the 70 strike, effectively the entire market. (The strike doesn't yet appear in consolidated chain data — open interest posts T+1; as of this writing the public tape hasn't caught up to a trade we are one side of.)

This is what the capacity edge looks like in numbers: a market that trades 65 contracts a day cannot fund a market maker, an institution cannot deploy into 112 contracts of OI, and a 2-lot resting offer is invisible to everyone except the one buyer who needs exactly this instrument. The program's ceiling is real and low — and that ceiling is the moat.

## Pricing post-mortem (rev 2 — rev 1 was itself wrong, and the fix is a program)

Rev 1 of this section claimed we sold at ~half of theoretical (17.8% implied vs 29.2% realized). **That analysis used a 1.45-year tenor for a 0.45-year option** — Jan-15-2027 is 165 days out, not 17 months. The error was caught on the first run of `desk/dead_chain_pricer.py`, the tool built in response to it. Corrected numbers:

- **Theoretical at realized vol (29.2%): 3.29. Our fill at 3.4048 implies 29.8% — at actuarial.** The sale was priced at fair volatility, not below it.
- **The reservation ask under the captivity rule (RV+4 pts) is 4.01** — we conceded ~$120 of captivity premium across the 2 lots by pricing from the chain's marks instead of the model. Mild, not the claimed ~$700 — but the rule stands: on a dead chain, quote from the model, and the pricer is now the mandatory staging step.
- **Carry, honestly stated (metric named):** ~10.7%/yr on strike pre-tax; ~15% stacked with SGOV; **~7.5% after-tax versus the CA-muni hurdle of ~3.9% tax-exempt — CLEARS**, roughly 2× the muni with a courted-entry tail rather than a credit tail.
- The same-day **3.50 print on the 65-strike** (theoretical ~1.8) now reads as a captive buyer paying ~2× fair for a *worse* strike — evidence the captivity premium is real and collectible. Our 70s at 3.40 were the cheapest protection on the board that day; future legs price at reservation.

Two lessons, both encoded: **tenor is checked by machine now, not by eye** (the pricer computes T from the calendar); and corrections themselves get verified before they ship — rev 1 went to print without its own numbers being re-derived, which is exactly the class of error the house catches in others' filings.
