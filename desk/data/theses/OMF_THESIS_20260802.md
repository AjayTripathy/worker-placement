## 1 · The four-idea frame

### What the market believes

OneMain is a subprime consumer lender at the wrong point in the cycle, and the low multiple is the
market correctly refusing to capitalise peak-cycle earnings. Charge-offs are running above 8% and
rising year over year. Tangible equity is thin against a $25B receivable book, so a genuine
consumer recession does not compress the multiple — it impairs the equity. The dividend yield near
7% is not a reward, it is a warning: the market is telling you the payout is at risk. And the
company is growing into this — receivables up, originations up, credit cards up sharply — which is
what lenders do right before they find out what they underwrote. A holder of this view says: you
are being paid a fair coupon for a real 2008-lite tail, and "fair" is the ceiling, not the floor.

We think that is close to correct, and we say so in the edge section. This is not an EDGE name.

### What we believe

Two specific things, both numerical:

1. **The credit deterioration is substantially a mix effect, not a core deterioration.** The
   headline that scares people is the total-company net charge-off ratio: **8.20% in Q2 2026
   against 7.57% a year earlier**. But the core personal/consumer loan book charged off **7.77%
   against 7.19%**, and the gap between those two series is widening because **credit-card
   receivables grew 52% year over year, from $752M to $1,144M**. Cards run structurally worse
   credit (30–89 day delinquency 4.66% versus 3.02% on personal loans; 90+ at 5.53% versus 2.15%).
   A fast-growing, structurally-higher-loss segment mechanically lifts the blended ratio. That is
   mix, and it is a choice management is making, not a signal that the core borrower is failing.

2. **Delinquencies are improving while charge-offs rise — which is the shape of a book working
   through a bad vintage, not entering one.** Personal-loan 30–89 day delinquency improved from
   **3.36% to 3.02%** year over year and 90+ improved from **2.49% to 2.15%**. Credit-card
   delinquencies also improved (30–89: 5.38% → 4.66%; 90+: 7.05% → 5.53%). Delinquency leads
   charge-offs by roughly two quarters. Rising net charge-offs on falling delinquencies is the
   lagged consequence of the *older* cohort, and it says forward losses fall. This is the single
   strongest piece of evidence for the second-half-improvement thesis, and it is stronger than the
   argument our own record made.

At $62.58 the stock is **~9.4x trailing-twelve-month earnings of $6.64**, **2.13x book value of
$29.40 a share**, and pays a **6.7% dividend yield** ($1.05 quarterly, held). We are being paid
roughly 6.7% in cash to wait on a book whose leading indicator is improving.

### Why we might have edge

**Honestly: not much, and the label says so.** This is a RISK_PREMIUM position, not an EDGE
position. Nobody is grossly mispricing OneMain — it is a liquid, heavily-covered, widely-understood
consumer lender, and the discount to the market is a fair price for genuine cyclical and capital
risk. The doctrine on this is explicit: fairly-paid risk is ownable, and edge is an overlay, not a
requirement.

To the extent there is *any* overlay, it is the mix-versus-core decomposition above. The
sell-side and the tape quote the blended ratio; the decomposition that separates card-growth mix
from core-borrower behaviour sits in the 10-Q selected-statistics tables, not in the release
headline. That is a small, real, and short-lived asymmetry — worth a low-end scale, not a full one.

What we are actually buying is **carry with a bounded, unlevered tail**, sized so that the tail
cannot hurt the book.

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

The strongest bear case, stated properly:

- **Q2 earnings actually fell.** Net income was **$152M versus $167M** a year earlier; diluted EPS
  **$1.32 versus $1.40**, and down hard from Q1's $1.93. The provision rose to **$610M from $511M**,
  including a **$104M allowance build**. Our record files that build under "growth, not
  deterioration" — that is a judgement call, not a fact, and the bear reads the same $104M as an
  early admission.
- **The net ratio is being flattered by recoveries.** On the total book, gross charge-offs rose
  **105 basis points** (9.05% → 10.10%) while the net ratio rose only **63 basis points**, because
  the recovery ratio improved from 1.48% to 1.90%. Recoveries on charged-off consumer paper are
  partly a function of the price third parties will pay for it. That is a market-dependent input.
  If recovery pricing normalises, ~40bp of the "improvement" in the net series evaporates without
  a single borrower behaving differently. **This is the number I would watch most closely and it
  is not in our kill list.**
- **The equity is thin and shrinking.** Total shareholders' equity **$3,383M against $25.1B of net
  receivables** — roughly 13.5x levered — and equity *declined* sequentially ($3,401M → $3,383M) as
  $122M of dividends and $32M of buybacks exceeded $152M of earnings net of other movements. In a
  real recession, this is the mechanism by which a cheap stock becomes a broken one.
- **Higher-for-longer is a slow-roll squeeze.** The asset book is fixed-rate; the funding is
  staggered term ABS and unsecured. With ~20bp of 2027 hikes now priced into the futures strip,
  each maturing tranche refinances higher against a book that cannot reprice. This does not show up
  in one quarter; it shows up over eight. Our response was not to re-court the name but to express
  it through **low-end scaling** — 1.17% rather than a full proportional weight.

### Mechanism falsifier

**Dated and checkable:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, ~28 October 2026. The thesis is falsified if
**personal-loan 30–89 day delinquency stops improving year over year** while net charge-offs
continue to rise — that combination breaks the lead-lag argument that is doing all the work above,
and converts "working through a vintage" into "entering one".

Secondary falsifier, same print: the full-year net charge-off guide of **7.4%–7.9%** being raised.
The first half ran 7.89–7.90% on the guided basis, so the second half must decline for the guide to
hold. A raise says management no longer believes it.

### Edge type

**RISK_PREMIUM.** Fairly-paid, bounded, unlevered, sleeve-capped carry with a small decomposition
overlay. The label is what authorises the *low-end* 1.17% scale rather than a full proportional
weight.

### The basis note — the material process finding

The kill trigger in our record reads **"NCO > 8.7%"** and **does not name a basis**. The 10-Q
publishes two different net charge-off ratios for the same quarter:

- **Total finance receivables: 8.20%** — headroom to the bar: **50 basis points**
- **Consumer loans: 7.77%** — headroom to the bar: **93 basis points**

The court graded against 7.77%, and on the evidence that is the *correct* basis: the company's own
7.4%–7.9% full-year guide can only be reconciled against the consumer series (H1 7.89%), not the
total series (H1 8.30%). So **the pass stands.** But the trigger as written is ambiguous, and with
credit-card mix pushing the two series apart quarter by quarter, an unspecified basis is a latent
false-fire (or false-clear) waiting to happen at the October grade. **Recorded as a diligence item,
not a sizing change:** the bar should be restated as "consumer-loans net charge-off ratio > 8.7%"
before the Q3 print is graded.

**Discrepancies printed, including the unfavourable ones:** items 3, 4, 9, 13 and 14 are all worse
than the record states. Items 5, 6 and 12 are better or more informative than the record states.
The record's "cleared 4/4" verdict survives all of it — but the framing "growth not deterioration"
was doing more work than the numbers support.

The honest summary: at 12x the dollars, OMF's worst court-modelled outcome costs about a third of
one percent of capital, and it is bounded because the position is unlevered, the tail is a price
tail rather than a permanent-impairment tail, and the sleeve is capped by a number that was set
