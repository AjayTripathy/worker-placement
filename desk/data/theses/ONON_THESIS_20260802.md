## 1 · The four-idea frame

### Market believes
Premium sportswear is cooling with the consumer. On is a high-multiple growth story whose growth is
decelerating on its own guidance, and the whole premium-athletic complex should be sold. The stock is
−20% year-to-date and 30% off its high, which is the market saying it already believes this.

### We believe
The channel data contradicts the cooling narrative **today**, and it does so on the balance sheet rather
than in management commentary.

The sell-in bear — "they are stuffing wholesale" — is refuted by the inventory line. Inventories are
**CHF 406.0M, +1.7% year-over-year**, against net sales growing **+26.4% constant-currency**. Days of
inventory are falling hard. A company channel-filling does not shrink inventory into accelerating sales.
And **DTC is out-growing wholesale** (+28.7% cc versus +25.1% cc) — the opposite of the sell-in pattern.
Gross margin is **64.2%, +430bps**. Adjusted EBITDA margin is **21.0%**, from 16.5%. The company
**reiterated** ≥23% constant-currency full-year growth and **raised** both the gross-margin guide (≥64.5%)
and the adjusted-EBITDA guide (19.5–20.0%) — while embedding a **20% incremental tariff** on US imports
from Vietnam in that guidance.

Roughly **12 percentage points** of the reported-versus-constant-currency gap is FX translation optics —
the reported +14.5% and the underlying +26.4% are the same business seen through different currency
lenses.

**And we hold this claim with two corrections our own red team forced, both of which stay in force:**
1. **The fair value is half a re-rating bet.** The original FV of $48 was exposed as a 28x re-rating
   assumption; it was blended to **$44** (50/50 an earnings-only $40 and a re-rated $48). We are not
   underwriting the multiple.
2. **The margin expansion is guide-cushioned, not proven.** Decomposing the +430bps of gross margin: only
   about **150bps is structural price**; roughly 100bps is FX and 280bps is freight — **both reversible**.
   And the CHF-heavy operating-expense drag is real and management-named, which the "FX is just optics"
   framing understates.

### Why we might have edge
**A genuine takeaway-versus-data divergence, held honestly.** The marketed bear story is that premium
consumer demand is rolling over; On's own inventory, DTC mix and order books say otherwise right now —
run-specialty order books are **+25%**, and a supply-strained brand cuts allocations rather than growing
them. That divergence is the edge, and it is the reason this is classified **EDGE** rather than carry.

But the edge has a named counterweight that we armed ourselves: **a premium brand ran its deepest-ever
public discount six weeks before its last print while preaching full-price discipline.** The honesty
channel stays armed precisely because the one observed *behaviour* siding with the bear narrative is
promotional. Carry-vs-edge label: **EDGE, with the promotional tripwire as the live disconfirming
instrument.**

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong
1. **The guide itself embeds the deceleration.** Full-year ≥23% cc with Q1 at 26.4% implies a **Q4 exit
   around 19–20%**. The market may simply be early on the same curve we are riding. "2025 re-acceleration"
   is the rear-view mirror.
2. **The margin legs are reversible.** Strip FX and freight and the structural gain is ~150bps, not 430.
3. **The promo is now a pattern, not an incident** — see §2. A second discount event, in a window where
   there was none a year ago, nine days before a print, is exactly the evidence the falsifier was written
   to catch.
4. **Multiple risk is the dominant risk.** Even after −20% year-to-date this is a growth multiple. The
   bear FV of $24 is **−34% from spot**, and it does not require the business to break — only the
   re-rating leg to reverse and growth to normalise.
5. **An unexplained tape event.** On 2026-07-23 the stock fell **−4.9%** ($36.65 → $34.84) with no company
   filing or release that day. Something moved it; we do not know what, and "we do not know" on a name
   with a live promotional tripwire is not comfortable.

### Mechanism falsifier
**A second deep broad-assortment promo event pre-print, or any quarter below 20% constant-currency
growth.** Secondary: inventory growth flipping faster than sales (the channel-fill tell); wholesale
out-pulling DTC for two consecutive quarters; a guide cut below 23% cc.

### Edge type
**EDGE** (three-tranche entry, dislocated-quality). This is the only name in the tier carrying that label,
and it is why the band is written as an **edge-floor** rather than a bid: EDGE names get *taken* at market
up to the price at which the thesis loses its class; FAIR names get *bid*.

### Adjudicating the tripwire — the honest reading

The court's kill trigger reads: *"a **SECOND deep broad-assortment** promo event pre-print CANCELS the T2
auto-add."* Applying it mechanically to this evidence gives the wrong answer in **both** directions, so it
needs stating carefully:

- **As literally written, it does NOT fire.** This is not a broad-assortment event. It is confined to the
  pre-existing "Last Season" clearance shelf, contains no site-wide sale, and does not touch current-season
  product. The June event was assessed the same way — *"prior-season outlet clearance on best evidence."*
  Neither event is what the trigger describes.
- **But the substance is more adverse than "it didn't technically fire" implies.** There was **no
  equivalent promotion in the same weeks of 2025**. Whatever the mechanic, On is discounting into a window
  it did not discount into a year ago, nine days before a print, in **North America only** — which is
  precisely where the demand question sits.
- **The strongest counterweight is quantitative, and it is the reason this is not a kill.** Inventory is
  **+1.7% year-over-year against +26.4% cc sales**. Days of inventory are collapsing. **This is not a book
  that needs clearing.** A distressed retailer discounts because it must; a brand with falling days of
  inventory discounting prior-season product on a permanent clearance shelf is running a calendar, not a
  fire sale.

**Recommended adjudication (for the court lane, not decided here): RECLASSIFY the trigger rather than fire
it.** Amend the wording from "deep broad-assortment promo event" to a two-part test — *(a) any discount
event touching **current-season** product, or (b) a **third** prior-season clearance event within twelve
months in a window with no year-ago analogue* — and treat today's evidence as **strike two under (b)**.
Consequence for sizing: **the T2 auto-add is downgraded from automatic to discretionary-on-the-gate.** It
must be earned by the Aug-11 compound gate rather than fired by it.

**Honesty grade: CLEAN.** Everything above comes from On's own filings and its own public storefront.
Nothing is concealed. The finding is about our *tripwire's* wording, not about On's disclosure.
