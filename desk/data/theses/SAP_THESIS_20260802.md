## 1 · The four-idea frame

### Market believes
Enterprise application software is the line item AI eats. Agents replace the seats SAP bills, the
renewal wall is coming, and a 35x multiple deserved to become 19x. Sell the SaaS-pocalypse.

### We believe
The court's one-line framing still holds and is worth restating exactly: **"a conceded fear, over-charged
by half — owned small until the renewal wall testifies."**

The renewal wall has now testified once, and the testimony was good. **Current cloud backlog grew 26%
constant-currency, and ≥25% of that is organic** — Reltio contributed under one percentage point and
management **volunteered** the figure without being asked. That matters twice over: it clears our ≥24%
gate with room, and it is the honesty contrast with the other vendor in this sleeve, which stopped
quantifying its inorganic contribution the moment the contribution became a beat.

The de-rate was never an earnings break. It was a guidance-driven multiple compression on a business
whose cash generation kept compounding. And management has now told us what it thinks the AI transition
does to pricing power — the opposite of the market's assumption. Klein, on the call: *"For 50 years this
company has sold systems of record… now with AI, **we can completely reset the price level**… it's not
your end user, it's the agents doing this autonomously. And here is **outcome-based pricing**."* And to
the sales force: *"don't even go to the price levels you have given."* The word "seat" appears **nowhere**
in either transcript. If that is right, agents are a pricing *reset upward*, not a seat-count collapse.

**But we are honest about what that claim is: a dated, unquantified promise.** Management put a ~12-month
clock on it (*"will hopefully end up in the next 12 months in an acceleration of our AI cloud revenue"*)
with **zero** revenue, ARR, uplift or attach-rate quantification behind it. The only penetration figure
offered — AI and Business Data Cloud "embedded as key pillars in more than 90% of our 50 largest deals" —
appears in one transcript rendering and not the other. That is a checkable promise with a date on it, and
it should be graded, not believed.

### Why we might have edge
**Less than we did three weeks ago, and the reason is price.** The court's own quant note reads: *"spot
EUR 136.72 is ~16–20% ABOVE bear FV ~EUR 114."* At EUR 157.4 the stock is now **~38% above that bear**.
Upside to base FV EUR 175 is **~+11%**; downside to the bear is **~−28%**. The asymmetry the court bought
has inverted. What remains is a **quality compounder at fair value**, which per the TINA doctrine is a
legitimate thing to own — fair value *is* the entry for quality, and "fairly valued" is not "expensive."
Carry-vs-edge label: **carry, with the valuation edge now largely realised.**

The one place a live edge may persist is the **pricing-model claim**, because it is genuinely
non-consensus and genuinely unpriced: the market models AI as a deflationary force on application
software, and SAP's CEO is telling the sales force to reprice upward on outcomes. Nobody can underwrite
that yet — which is precisely why it should be frozen as a call rather than capitalised into a target.

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong
Four strong bears, three of them new this quarter:

1. **A regulator just handed SAP's customers the right to spend less.** On 2026-07-09 the European
   Commission concluded its competition investigation into SAP's on-premise maintenance and support
   practices **through a commitment decision** — one buried line in the quarterly statement. The call
   unpacked it: Asam conceded that customer flexibility to adjust software spend has *"been to some
   degree now formalized in this agreement and in some degrees **some more flexibility has been granted
   in limited scenarios**"*, and that for customers prioritising lower spend *"**there might be an impact,
   but we think we can manage it**."* This lands on software support revenue that is **already −8%
   (−7% cc)** and which SAP separately guides to decline at an **accelerating** rate. It is a structural
   change to the legacy annuity, mandated by a regulator, and it is not in anyone's model.
2. **A third of first-half operating-profit growth was a share-price accident that is now reversing.**
   The **EUR 196M** H1 stock-compensation relief was driven by SAP's own share price falling — Asam named
   it as the *first* item in the profit bridge: *"a very big strong positive from lower stock-based
   compensation because a around about EUR 60 share price drop in Q1 that didn't reoccur in Q2."* It is
   **Q1-weighted** (Q1 ≈ −EUR 135M, Q2 ≈ −EUR 61M). Shares have since recovered from EUR 127.50 to
   ~EUR 157. **The tailwind becomes a headwind in H2, and management did not address the reversal.**
3. **The statutory earnings line is flattered by marks.** IFRS EPS **+30%** is carried in part by
   **EUR 463M of venture/strategic-investment marks** — non-operating, non-recurring, and mark-to-market.
   The mirror image appeared at ServiceNow the same quarter ($273M of $438M pretax). **Two enterprise-
   software vendors' statutory lines carried by investment marks in the same quarter** is a sector-level
   quality-of-earnings fact, not a coincidence.
4. **The valuation cushion is gone.** At the EUR 156 ceiling the forward return was "adequate." Above it,
   by the court's own construction, it is not.

### Mechanism falsifier
**Current cloud backlog growth falling below ~24% constant-currency on an organic basis**, or any
disclosure that the maintenance-flexibility commitments are widening beyond "limited scenarios" into a
broad cohort. Either means the renewal wall is real and arriving. Secondary: FY free-cash-flow guidance
cut for a **non-elective** reason (see §2 — this quarter's cut was elective and does not count).

### Edge type
**RISK_PREMIUM / RP_FAIR carry** at spot, with a residual VALUE claim that is only live **below EUR 156**.
Sizing reads this label, and the **price gate** does the rest.

### Gate verdict: **PASSES ON SUBSTANCE, FAILS AS LITERALLY WRITTEN**

| Leg | Bar | Actual | Result |
|---|---|---|---|

**This is the mechanical-gate failure mode worth encoding.** The gate was built to catch *deterioration*.
It fired on a printed number that moved for the **opposite** reason — management booking optionality
rather than losing it. A grader reading the guidance table alone records a fail; a grader reading the call
records declined upside. **The lesson generalises: any "all guides reaffirmed" leg needs an elective-versus-
forced test attached, or it will manufacture false negatives on every M&A-dilution quarter.**

**Honesty grade: CLEAN, and notably so.** SAP volunteered the Reltio inorganic contribution unprompted,
named the SBC windfall as the *first* item in its own profit bridge, disclosed the EC commitment decision
in the quarterly statement, and explained the guidance cut as an elective choice rather than dressing it
up. The EC line was buried and the venture marks flatter the statutory EPS — both are real criticisms of
*emphasis* — but every number is on the page. There is no liar here to exclude.

**A wash-sale trap specific to this name, stated because it is non-obvious and would be easy to walk
into.** The ADR line "SAP" and the Xetra ordinary line "SAP.DE" are **the same issuer's equity**. An ADR
and its underlying ordinary share are **substantially identical** for §1091 purposes. **Selling the ADR at
a loss and buying SAP.DE (or vice versa) within the 61-day window does NOT work as a rotation — the loss
is disallowed.** The core table's note that both lines are "the same underlying" is correct and is exactly
why this must not be used as a workaround.

### Liquidity note
The **ADR** line trades ~**$599M/day** (90-day average dollar volume) and is the position we actually hold
at IBKR; the Xetra line is deeper still. Combined depth is a complete non-constraint at $100k. Practical
points:
- **Execute on the ADR line only.** It avoids the EUR settlement leg, the Xetra session mismatch and the
  1:1 conversion mechanics entirely.
- **German dividend withholding** applies to the underlying (statutory 26.375%, reduced by treaty for US
  holders, typically to 15%, with the reduction usually applied at source by the depositary). At a 1.63%

  not VERIFIED** — confirm with the broker before relying on them.
- **The ADR carries a depositary fee** (typically a few cents per share annually, netted from
  distributions). Immaterial at this size, but it is a small permanent drag the Xetra line does not have.
