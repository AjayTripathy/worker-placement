## 1 · The four-idea frame

### Market believes
The safest way to own drug-price inflation. A three-company oligopoly toll road that clips a fee on
every branded pill in America, compounding earnings 13–16% a year with a shrinking share count — worth a
premium multiple, and it has had one.

### We believe
Mostly the same thing, and we say so plainly: this is a **toll-road quality compounder** and the numbers
hold up under audit. FY26 revenue **$403.4B**, operating income **$6,212M**, diluted EPS **$38.38**,
FY27 adjusted-EPS guide **$43.80–$44.60 (+12–14%)** with the 13–16% long-term algorithm reaffirmed. Cash
return is real, not rhetorical: **$4,750M of buybacks plus $381M of dividends = ~95% of free cash flow**,
with diluted shares down 134.1 → 128.1 → **124.1M** (~3%/year), a new **$2.25B accelerated share
repurchase**, and the dividend just raised **15% to $0.94**.

Two things we believe that the "safe compounder" framing does not say:

1. **The GAAP operating-income jump is ~40% one-time, and McKesson says so.** Reported OI went
   $4,422M → $6,212M (+$1,790M), but that includes a **+$480M Norway divestiture gain** in FY26 against a
   **−$667M Rexall/Canada charge** in FY25 — a ~$1.15B one-time swing — partly offset by antitrust gains
   rolling off ($444M → $23M). The honest run-rate is management's **adjusted EPS +18% ($39.11)**.
   McKesson leads with adjusted, does not forecast GAAP, and reconciles both. **Honestly disclosed, not
   masked — CLEAN on takeaway-versus-data.**
2. **Negative book equity is a buyback artifact, not distress.** Equity is −$2,172M while **retained
   earnings are +$22,291M and growing**; treasury stock of −$32,005M is the mechanical cause. ROE is
   meaningless here; use ROIC and cash return. Leverage is trivial: funded debt ~$7,982M less $3,975M
   cash = **~$4.0B net, ~0.6x EBITDA**, investment grade, in compliance with all covenants, with a new
   $5.0B revolver to April 2031.

**And two things that are genuinely unresolved, both of which the company itself declines to bound:**
the **opioid residual** outside the settled $7.9B core, and the **CVS contract**.

### Why we might have edge
**We do not, and the record says so in numbers.** Expected price return **+1.3%**, expected total return
**+1.7%**, against a symmetric cone — the EC record's own words: *"essentially FAIR… Symmetric — no alpha
edge, this is a carry name."* Classification: **RP_FAIR quality carry (85%) plus a small
flight-to-quality-bid overlay (15%).** Carry-vs-edge label: **carry, full stop.**

At $856.19 even that thin edge is gone: the stock now trades **above** the probability-weighted FV that
produced the +1.3%. On the court's own arithmetic, the expected return from here is **negative on price**.
That is not a reason to short it or to dislike the business; it is a reason not to pay this price.

The one place a defensible information advantage exists is **the opioid residual**, and it is a
*negative* finding rather than an edge: McKesson expressly declines to estimate the private and
non-participating-subdivision exposure, so the mandate gate "settlement run-offs truly capped" is **NOT
met** — it is **UNVERIFIABLE-as-bounded by the company's own words**, and unverifiable is not clean.

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong
1. **Customer concentration is material and the biggest contract expires in ~11 months.** **CVS is ~24%
   of consolidated revenue and 21% of total trade accounts receivable**; the distribution contract runs to
   **June 2027**. The next two customers are 11% and 10% — **three customers each ≥10%, ~45% combined; the
   top ten are 73%.** Neither McKesson nor CVS has said anything about renewal. This is the largest
   enterprise-value risk in the name and it sits squarely inside the holding horizon.
2. **The multiple is not cheap any more.** The record's case rested on MCK trading **below** its own
   3-and-5-year average of ~24x and being the cheapest of the big three. At $856 that argument has
   weakened by ~5%.
3. **The Medical-Surgical carve-out is being levered, and that is new.** Apollo's $1.25B for ~13% closed
   2026-06-02 **as convertible preferred**, and on 2026-06-09 McKesson Medical-Surgical Top Holdings
   entered a **$2,250M senior secured Term B facility due 2032** (SOFR+225bps), secured on substantially
   all assets, with **net-leverage and interest-coverage maintenance covenants**. A ring-fenced,
   covenanted, secured structure with a convertible preferred above the common changes the SOTP arithmetic
   and adds real GAAP noise.
4. **The one truly un-hedgeable item.** The opioid residual is un-reservable by construction and the
   Cabell/Huntington remand is live, with plaintiffs seeking a **15-year, $2.5B abatement plan**.

### Mechanism falsifier
**The CVS distribution contract non-renewed, the specialty book lost, or a punitive re-price** — that is
the kill, and it should resolve between H2-2026 and H1-2027. Secondary: an adverse re-trial verdict in the
remanded Cabell/Huntington case forcing a material accrual increase beyond the $5.7B schedule; or an
adjusted-EPS guide cut below the reaffirmed 13–16% algorithm for structural rather than one-time reasons.

### Edge type
**RISK_PREMIUM (RP_FAIR quality carry).** Not EDGE. Sizing reads this label; the **price gate** does the
rest, and at $856 the price gate is shut.

### The finding that matters most, stated plainly

The record's `decisive` block rests on two Mode-B findings. **One of them has the sign backwards.** It
lists "MD vacated a $109M Baltimore judgment Apr-2026" among the evidence that the opioid residual
"trended ADVERSE in the last 12mo." The Maryland Supreme Court vacated a judgment **against** McKesson,
and the plaintiff then abandoned the case. That is a **favourable** development.

The correct statement is narrower and still uncomfortable: **the residual remains un-bounded because
McKesson declines to estimate it** — that part stands, and it is the part that fails the mandate gate.
But the *trend* over the last twelve months is **mixed, not adverse**: one live adverse remand
(Cabell/Huntington, 4th Circuit) and one resolved-favourably case (Baltimore).

**Process consequence.** The EC record carries a `no_red_team_rationale` explaining that no adversarial
court was run because *"findings are primary-sourced and consistent across two independent 10-K reads…
Verdict is RP_FAIR/STAGE (not a high-conviction EDGE claim requiring adversarial court)."* A directional
error survived two 10-K reads and a self-review. **RP_FAIR is not a licence to skip the red team** — the
error class here is direction-of-effect, which is exactly what an adversary catches and a re-read does
not. This is the same failure mode already logged once on a tax-change direction.

**Honesty grade on McKesson: CLEAN.** Adjusted-versus-GAAP is fully reconciled, the one-time gains are
named, the customer concentration is stated in plain language, and the company says out loud that it
cannot estimate the residual. Nothing is being masked.

The execution nuance here is **price per share, not depth**: at ~$856 a share, a $110k line is only ~128

- **Lot granularity is coarse.** With so few shares, specific-ID lot selection is *easier* to manage but

  stock. Plan the rung sizes with the harvest in mind.

### Harvest rule for this name
- **Harvest window:** any lot ≥ **$2,500** and ≥ **7%** below basis. Against a $785 rung that is ~$730 —
  reachable, since the 13-week low is $724.
- **Mechanics:** specific-ID lot sale; rotate to **COR** for the 31-day window; re-enter MCK on day 32.
- **Do not harvest across the FY27-Q1 print (2026-08-05)** or across any window in which a **CVS renewal
  announcement** is plausible. The renewal is the single largest binary in the name and it can arrive
  unannounced from *either* counterparty; being flat for 31 days when it lands would be an expensive way
  to save tax. Because the renewal has no known date, prefer harvesting **immediately after** a quarterly
  print, when the odds of an intervening disclosure are lowest.
- **Low-basis-winner discipline.** If the ladder fills at $720–$785 and the stock recovers toward $900,
  those become low-basis winners in a taxable book — **defer them**, do not trim, per the tax-asymmetry
  doctrine. The harvest engine wants the losers; the winners want to be left alone.
- **Cross-book check:** MCK is **not** on the core table's Parametric wash-sale coordination list (only CI

  304-name direct-indexing sleeve. **Verify against `msprime.db` before the first harvest**; do not rely
