## 1 · The four-idea frame

### What the market believes

Kalmar is a good business at a fair price with nothing left to discover. It earns 24% on capital
employed with essentially no net debt, which is genuinely excellent — and that is exactly why it
trades where it does. The problem is the cycle: container throughput growth is decelerating from
6.5% toward 3%, orders are flat to down, the order book has shrunk 4% and the services book 9%, and
book-to-bill has slipped below 1.0. Sales grew 14% in the quarter by *drawing down* the order book,
which makes the second half's comparisons harder, not easier. The self-help programme is real but
finite — €49m secured against a ~€50m target, so it is nearly spent. And the Herlin family controls
69% of the votes, so there is no takeout, no activist, and no structural catalyst. A holder of this
view says: you are paying a fair price for a quality asset heading into a downcycle, and fair is
what you will get.

We largely agree. This is a fair-price position and the label says so.

### What we believe

The specific claim is narrow: **Kalmar's balance sheet lets it cross a decelerating cycle without
impairing the equity, and the market is pricing the deceleration without pricing the durability.**

- **Net debt is €13.1m against €723.2m of equity — gearing 1.8%, net debt to EBITDA 0.0x — and that
  is *inclusive* of IFRS-16 leases**, which was the owed diligence item from the court and is now
  verified from the note itself. Liquidity reserves of €480m plus a €150m commercial paper programme.
  A company with this balance sheet does not issue equity at the bottom, does not cut into the
  downturn, and can buy assets from people who must sell.
- **Return on capital employed is 24.1% on a twelve-month basis, up from 20.7%** — this is not a
  cycle-peak artefact of a levered balance sheet; it is unlevered.
- **The self-help is banked, not promised.** €49m of annualised gross efficiency improvements
  secured by the end of Q2 against a ~€50m end-2026 target, the majority from sourcing.
- **The intake worry has a positive cross-read.** The adjacent warehouse and intralogistics capex
  complex turned up — Jungheinrich orders +10.8% with automation +18%, KION raising full-year on
  record intake. That cuts against the book-to-bill strike.

At €38.66 the stock sits roughly **25% below its 52-week high of €51.15**, against a base fair value
of €44 and a bull of €53.50.

### Why we might have edge

**Very little, and the label is honest about it.** RP_FAIR is the deploying-book default class: a
quality business at a fair price, owned because fair value *is* the entry for quality, not because
anyone is mispricing it. The doctrine is explicit that "fairly valued" is not "expensive" and that
WAIT should be reserved for deteriorating businesses.

The one genuine overlay is **forward instrumentation on the operating variable that actually
decides this**. Order intake is the load-bearing number behind both armed tripwires, and Kalmar does
not report it until the quarter closes. We run a nowcast off free channels — EU cargo-handling
tender notices (CPV 42414*, which lead awards by months), Kalmar's own order press releases as a
quarter-to-date floor, throughput anchors, and plant hiring velocity. This does not tell us anything
the company is hiding; it tells us the same thing earlier. That is a **latency** edge of a few weeks,
worth staging around, not worth a full weight.

Note also what this document's sweep demonstrates: the nowcast is not a substitute for reading the
release wire. The single most decision-relevant datum found today — a divisional president resigning
— came from a stock-exchange release, not from a detector.

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

The strongest bear case, with two legs sharpened by this sweep:

- **The full-year margin guide is already stretched, and our record did not say so.** Kalmar guides
  comparable operating profit margin "above 12.5 percent" for 2026. **H1 actual is 12.4%. Q2 is also
  12.4%, against 13.1% a year earlier.** The guide is not merely un-raised — **it requires
  second-half acceleration from a first half that came in below it**, in a decelerating market,
  while a cost programme that has already banked €49m of a €50m target runs out of room. Our kill
  trigger fires at "guide cut below 12.5%"; the more likely path is a guide *missed* rather than
  formally cut.
- **The eco-portfolio direction is worse than the level suggests.** Eco orders were 40% of the mix
  in Q2 against 44% a year earlier, and 39% for the half against 44%. Fully-electric equipment was
  12% of equipment orders in Q2 — but **11% for the half, down from 13%**. Our record cited the 12%
  Q2 figure as refuting Chinese electric share-loss; the half-year trend does not support that
  reading. The 35% trigger is not close, but the slope is wrong.
- **Sales growth is order-book drawdown.** +14% in Q2 with a book down 4% year over year means the
  second half faces harder comparisons against a thinner backlog.
- **Three Leadership Team seats turn over inside one year, all exiting by 31 December** — CFO,
  President of Services, and now President of the Counterbalanced Division. Individually each is
  explicable; two were pre-announced with named successors and overlap periods. Collectively, in the
  H2 delivery window, it is a concentrated continuity risk that no single release frames as one.
- **The margin cross-read from the capex complex is mixed, not positive.** Jungheinrich raised its
  order and revenue ranges while *cutting* its margin guidance on price competition. The read-through
  survives on the orders half and argues *against* us on the margin half — which is the half where
  Kalmar's guide is already tight.
- **A true recession is a €24–27 stock**, on trough comparable EBIT of €160–180m, 25–30%
  decrementals, with the Driving Excellence savings offsetting about half.

### Mechanism falsifier

**Dated and checkable:** the Q3 2026 interim report, **29 October 2026 at approximately 09:00 EET**.
The thesis is falsified — and specifically, the *remaining* size is forfeited — if group book-to-bill
prints **below 0.95 for a second consecutive quarter** (Q2 at 0.94 was strike one), or if comparable
operating profit margin prints **below 12.0%**.

The instrument is `kalmar_order_nowcast`, running EU tender notices, Kalmar's order press-release
flow as a quarter-to-date floor, throughput anchors, and plant hiring velocity at Ottawa, Stargard
and Tampere. Its most recent read was **normal on deliveries, with intake still the open question** —
which is the correct answer, and the honest limitation of the instrument.

### Edge type

**RISK_PREMIUM (RP_FAIR).** Quality at fair value with a small latency overlay on order intake.
The label authorises a 1.0% weight; the armed tripwire is what restricts it to ~60% staged.

impairment — is the whole basis for tolerating it unhedged.
