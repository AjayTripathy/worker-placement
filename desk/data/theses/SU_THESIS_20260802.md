## 1 · The four-idea frame

### Market believes
Oil sands is a cyclical top after a huge run, and the refining super-cycle is done. Canadian heavy crude
carries structural discount risk, the assets are long-lived but carbon-exposed, and the +48.7%
year-to-date move already paid for the good news.

### We believe
**Fair carry, and we say so in those words.** The record's own framing: *"SU at ~$65 is fair carry — the
lowest-breakeven oil-sands FCF machine, ~9% total yield, survives $55 WTI with a refining book that hedges
the crude drawdown. We ladder into weakness rather than chase green; fair value is the entry for
quality."*

The operating case is real and it is verified. Q1-2026 delivered **record first-quarter upstream
production of 875.2 mbbls/d**, **record first-quarter refinery throughput of 497.8 mbbls/d at 97%
utilisation**, and an **all-time quarterly record for refined product sales of 681,000 bbls/d**. That
produced **CAD $4,030M of adjusted funds from operations** and **CAD $2,913M of free funds flow**, of which
**CAD $1,537M went back to shareholders** ($825M of buybacks plus over $700M of dividends). **Net debt is
CAD $6,842M**, down from $7,559M a year earlier — the cleanest balance sheet of the oil-sands trio. The
CFO's own note is that the company **raised its monthly repurchase pace for the second time in four
months.**

The strategic case has dates on it. At the March-31 Investor Day Suncor committed to a **US$5/bbl
reduction in corporate WTI breakeven to US$38 by 2028**, **100,000 bbls/d of upstream production growth by
2028**, a **$2B increase in free funds flow at US$65 WTI by 2028**, and a **10% increase in refining
nameplate capacity to 511,000 bbls/d** — which has already happened.

**And the differentiation versus the other energy holding is the point of owning it:** the integrated
refining book is a natural hedge on crude weakness. When the crude leg suffers, the refining leg captures
wider crack spreads. That is a different quality from a pure producer, and it is why the court chose SU as
the second energy starter rather than a royalty name.

### Why we might have edge
**None. The record states `edge_pp = 0` explicitly.** This is **RP_FAIR carry** — the deploying-book
default class. The record's own answer to "why we might have edge" is *"None — edge_pp=0. RP_FAIR carry.
The differentiation vs XOM is the integrated-refining hedge (a different quality) that diversifies the
sleeve's drawdown behaviour."* And to "why it might be priced in": *"It IS — +48.7% YTD. The refining
tailwind + the TMX diff tightening are in the price."*

This document does not manufacture an edge that the court declined to claim. Carry-vs-edge label:
**carry, zero edge overlay.** The bar this position must clear is the RP_FAIR bar — fairness verified,
tails bounded and unlevered, sleeve-capped, label stated — and §5 and §6 test exactly that.

The only genuine finding this pass produced is a **negative** one: the differential input is materially
stale, and one kill trigger is mis-calibrated (see §2).

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong
1. **The differential moved against us and our record did not notice.** Verified from the Government of
   Alberta's primary series: WCS-WTI averaged **US$14.18 in Q1-2026** and **US$17.58 in Q2-2026**, with
   **May at $18.99 and June at $18.91** — the widest sustained level since March 2024, versus **$11.76 for
   calendar 2025**. Our record's "~US$12 post-TMX" is stale by **$5–7**, not by a rounding error. The
   `$(20)` kill trigger is **not tripped**, but it came within about a dollar twice.
2. **Refining-crack reversion is the named risk and it is the same leg we call a hedge.** Record cracks
   mean-revert; in a demand-soft recession the refining bonus is given back. The court accepted this
   openly.
3. **Expected value from here is fractionally negative on price.** Probability-weighting the record's own
   cone (bear $50 at 0.32 / base $67 at 0.43 / bull $82 at 0.25) gives **e_fv ≈ $65.31** against a $67.28
   spot — about **−2.9%** before distributions. The ~9% total shareholder yield is what makes it positive.
   That is the definition of a carry position and it should be stated in numbers.
4. **The emissions-cap relief is real but not locked.** See §2 — the regulation is **legally dormant, not
   extinguished**, and the commitment not to implement it sits in an intergovernmental memorandum whose
   consideration is still being performed.
5. **It is 4.3% below a 52-week high**, having run from $37.10. Very little of the cycle's good news is
   still unpaid for.

### Mechanism falsifier
**2026 buyback pace cut below ~CAD 3.5B annualised**, **corporate WTI breakeven stalling above the
Investor-Day glide path**, or **the WCS-WTI differential blowing back past US$(20)/bbl**. Any of the three
means the low-cost, high-return story broke.
