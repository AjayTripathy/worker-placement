## 1 · The four-idea frame

### What the market believes

FS Bancorp is a small, thinly-traded Washington-state community bank earning a trough return on
equity in a hard operating environment, and roughly one times book is the right price for it.
Return on equity is running near 10% — respectable, not exciting — and the earnings mix leans on
mortgage gain-on-sale and indirect consumer lending, both of which are cyclical and neither of
which deserves a premium multiple. Commercial real estate is 37.8% of the book with construction
and development running near the regulatory heightened-attention threshold, in a market where
office collateral is impaired. Deposits fell 7.2% in a single quarter. At a $320M market
capitalisation with a 7.4M share count, no institution can build a position that matters, so there
is no marginal buyer to re-rate it. A holder of this view says: fair is fair, and there is nothing
here to arbitrage.

That is very close to our own view. This is a fair-price position, and the label says so.

### What we believe

Three claims, none of them heroic:

1. **The deposit "wobble" is management, not stress — verified.** Deposits fell $188.7M (7.2%)
   sequentially, but **$201.1M of that was brokered runoff, deliberately executed, while retail
   deposits grew $12.1M**. The cost of deposits *fell* from 2.24% to 2.18% as a direct result.
   A bank losing depositors' confidence does not simultaneously shed its most expensive funding and
   lower its funding cost. The original kill trigger false-fired on this and was re-specified by the
   red team to key on **retail** runoff.
2. **The return on equity is cycle-trough, not structural.** Q2 net income was **$7.9M, versus $7.8M
   the prior quarter and $7.7M a year earlier** — flat, unglamorous, and consistently profitable
   through the worst of the rate cycle. Gain-on-sale is only ~18% of net income, better than feared.
   At roughly one times book, we are paying nothing for the mean reversion.
3. **The constraint is liquidity, not conviction.** With 7,423,772 shares outstanding, position size
   is capped by what we can buy and sell, not by what we believe. That is why the ruled scale is
   **0.4% and not 2.0%** — the single deviation from proportional scaling in this round, made on the
   court's own grounds.

### Why we might have edge

**Almost none, and the label is honest about it.** RP_FAIR is the deploying-book default class:
we are being paid a fair price for a fairly-disclosed community bank, and edge is an overlay we do
not claim here. Nobody is grossly mispricing FS Bancorp.

The one genuine informational asymmetry available is the *structural* one: at ~7.4M shares and
minimal institutional coverage, the marginal price-setter is a retail or small-fund holder who is
unlikely to read a 424B3 merger prospectus. That is a real neglect channel — but it cuts both ways,
and this sweep it cut **against** us: the neglected document contained a fact we ourselves missed
(below). The honest conclusion is that the neglect is real and we have not yet demonstrated we
exploit it better than the market does.

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

The strongest bear case, and it now has a fourth leg the court never saw:

- **Construction concentration sits in the regulatory attention zone.** Construction and
  development is **~105% of Bank risk-based capital**, above the 100% supervisory guidance level;
  total CRE is ~219% against the 300% threshold. Regulators do not require an enforcement action to
  make this expensive — they can simply slow growth.
- **The first construction charge-off has already happened.** $2.3M in the quarter, and the
  remaining office-construction exposure of **$7.164M is 100% non-performing and classified**. One
  charge-off is a credit event; the red team's added trigger asks whether it is a *sequence*.
- **The funding shift replaced deposits with borrowings, not with retail growth.** Borrowings went
  from **$167.3M to $324.5M, up 94% sequentially** — a $157M increase against a $188.7M deposit
  decline. Loan-to-deposit moved from 100.7% to 108.6%. The red team is right that this was
  deliberate and cheaper; the bear is also right that it is a less stable liability structure, and
  both can be true.
- **The Pacific West acquisition imports impaired credit into a bank whose credit is the worry.**
  This is the new leg. Pacific West is a $403M-asset Portland, Oregon bank with **non-performing
  loans at 2.92% of loans** against FS Bancorp's own ~0.69%, **allowance coverage of
  non-performing loans of just 41.81%**, and **non-performing assets at 1.95% of total assets**
  versus FS Bancorp's 0.57%. It earned **$155,000** in Q1 2026. FS Bancorp is paying ~$35.5M —
  $16.8M in cash and 430,176 shares — for a bank with materially worse asset quality than its own.
  Purchase accounting will mark that credit at close, so it will not run through the provision line
  as charge-offs; but it will consume $16.8M of cash, add ~5.8% to the share count, and inject
  Oregon CRE into a balance sheet already at 105% construction-to-capital.

If the bear is right, we own a fairly-priced small bank that stays fairly priced, absorbs a credit-
impaired target, and never mean-reverts. That is the $38 bear.

### Mechanism falsifier

**Dated and checkable:** the FDIC call report for cert 57633, quarter ended 30 June 2026, published
~late August 2026. The thesis is falsified if **construction and development exceeds ~115% of Bank
risk-based capital**, or if any regulatory agreement is disclosed. Last mechanical read: **102.9%
against the 115% bar**, with retail deposits stable and allowance at 1.21%.

**Second, and newly dated by this sweep:** the Pacific West shareholder vote on **13 August 2026**,
and the merger's completion inside Q3. If the deal closes as anticipated, the Q3 print that grades
our October call will contain merger charges, a day-one allowance establishment on acquired loans,
and a restated deposit and loan mix. See the frozen-call staleness note — this is the most
important thing in this document.

### Edge type

**RISK_PREMIUM (RP_FAIR).** Fairly-priced, bounded, unlevered, and float-capped. The label is what
authorises the 0.4% scale and, equally, what forbids anything larger.

  ratio either way depending on the PCD/non-PCD split.
- *"C&D concentration above ~115% of Bank risk-based capital"* — the denominator changes.

  reasons that are not the thesis failing.

**I am not re-freezing anything** — the frozen number stays 0.65 and later evidence adjusts sizing
only, never the frozen number. What I am recording is that whoever grades this call in October must
grade it on **merger-adjusted, like-for-like figures**, and that the bar as written does not say so.
This is the same failure mode as binding a catalyst to the wrong trial: the number will resolve, but
against the wrong quantity, and a false clear here is worse than a false fire.

### What changes at 12x the dollars

**Nothing about the thesis. Everything about the tail math** — and here, less than anywhere else,
because FS Bancorp was scaled **2x, not 6–12x**. The court's finding was that the cap is
float-gated, and **liquidity caps scale with float and average daily volume, not with book size.**
A $6M book cannot buy more of a 7.4M-share bank than a $1M book can. This is the one name in the
round where the forward base does *not* license proportional scaling, and the ruled size reflects it.

**Loss at the court's bear case ($38 fair value), at the full ruled size:**

> **560 shares × ($40.84 − $38.00) ≈ −$1,590, or −7.0% of the position.**

That is **0.027% of the $6.0M forward base** — under three basis points. Even a −40% stress to ~$25
costs about **−$8,900, or 0.15% of the base.**

The real risk on this name is not the loss; it is the **exit**. At 560 shares against a 7.4M share
float, the position is a fraction of a day's volume and exits cleanly. That is the whole reason the
cap exists, and it is why the merger's 430,176 new shares are a mild positive for us: a 5.8% larger
float loosens, very slightly, the constraint that is actually binding.

**Why this is acceptable within the axis budget.** FS Bancorp is the smallest line in the
consumer-credit / financials-funding axis, which carries a **$360,000 review tripwire (6.0% of the
forward base)**. The ruled program is WAL $120k + OMF $70k + FSBW $24k + NPB ~$5k staged + TRU $60k
reserve = **~$279k, or 4.65% of the forward base**, with ~$81k of headroom. FS Bancorp consumes 6.7%
of the axis budget and contributes ~5% of the axis's bear-case loss. If all three US names print
their court bear cases together, the combined loss is **~$34,300, or 0.57% of the forward base**.

The honest answer to "what changes at 12x": for this name, almost nothing, by design. It is fully
staged now with nothing scheduled against the September landing, because the constraint is the
market's, not ours.
