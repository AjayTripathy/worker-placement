## 1 · The four-idea frame

### Market believes
Leidos's most profitable business is being dismantled by its own biggest customers on a published
calendar. The VA is re-competing the compensation-and-pension exam contract that carries the margin;
the Defense Health Agency is taking MHS GENESIS integration in-house. Cut the stock and keep cutting —
which the market has, by **37.5% year-to-date** and 44% from the 52-week high.

### We believe
The dismantling is **real, dated and partial** — and the near-term P&L is holding, but *only for a reason
that is itself the bear case*.

The reported numbers say Health is fine: Q1-26 segment operating income **$284M on a 23.9% margin**
(versus $288M, −1.4%), revenue flat, funded backlog **more than doubled** ($1,760M versus $832M). The
marketed fear — Health being cut in half *now* — diverges from that data.

But the court corrected our framing too, and this is the honest core of the thesis: **FY2026 exam volume
is already bought and paid for.** USASpending shows the VA obligated **$2.24B to the QTC family in
Oct–Dec 2025 — 100% of the fiscal year, front-loaded** — followed by seven straight months of zero or
negative new obligations. That is why funded backlog jumped and why the near-term P&L looks healthy. It
tells you nothing about FY2027.

Meanwhile Health's *total* backlog fell **−20.6%** to $6.56B, unfunded fell **−35%**, trailing book-to-bill
is **~0.64x** and coverage slipped from 1.74x to 1.38x. So: revenue and margin holding, order book
eroding. The FY2027 reset — VA recompete price and share, plus the DHA integrator unwind — is genuinely
**two-sided and unresolved**. At **8.7x forward adjusted EPS / ~8.0x EV-EBITDA / ~11% free-cash-flow
yield**, the risk is charged at more than its weight. But the edge is **a fragile probability vector, not
a lay-up**, and the court said so in those words.

**What changed since the court, and it cuts both ways.**
- *Positive:* the sector read-through was good. Booz Allen printed 2026-07-24 with adjusted EBITDA margin
  **+130bps to 11.9%**, adjusted EPS **+22.3%**, free cash flow **+172%**, **book-to-bill 1.5x** in the
  quarter, and FY2027 guidance **held word-for-word** — and the stock rose **+10.1%**. The market paid for
  margin, cash and bookings *despite* revenue −4.2%. LDOS rallied with it, $110.33 → $118.36 across the
  week. Leidos's own news flow in the window was neutral-to-positive: a **USAF ISR task order worth up to
  $717M** (7/28), an AI cyber platform (7/29), a CoreWeave collaboration (7/30), and a dividend
  declaration plus a **20M-share repurchase authorisation** (7/31) — which matters, because the prior
  authorisation was nearly exhausted.
- *Negative, and it is the one that counts:* **Booz Allen's Civil and Commercial segment fell −16.4%**,
  with management's own words *"Civil remains challenged."* Federal-civilian volume — which is exactly
  where Leidos Health sits — is deteriorating in the peer's book while margin and bookings carry the
  story.

### Why we might have edge
**A genuine one, and it is narrow and mechanical.** The USASpending obligation trail is a *free, leading,
primary-source* read on whether FY2027 exam volume is being funded — visible months before it reaches the
P&L, at roughly zero lag for civilian agencies. Almost nobody models federal services companies off the
obligation tape. That is the edge: **not a view about Leidos, but an instrument for grading the view.**

Beyond that, the honest answer is that this is **over-charged risk, court-tempered** — a dated
probability disagreement, not a liar-exclusion catch. The honesty axis reads: the marketed *fear* diverges
from the reported data, but the reason is pre-funding, which the company discloses. Nobody is lying.
Carry-vs-edge label: **a small, dated, free-to-resolve edge sitting on top of a deep-value multiple.**

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong
The court's red team already dismantled three of our own arguments, and they stay dismantled:
1. **"Beating and raising" was misleading.** The May-5 guidance raise was **Entrust-mechanical** (+$500M
   revenue = the acquisition's remaining-year contribution; adjusted-EPS raise only +$0.05 on $12.30 =
   +0.4%, effectively flat organically after ~$65M of new interest). **The beat-and-raise week closed
   −12.5% on record volume**, and GAAP EPS fell year-over-year.
2. **"Below the street-low price target" is a decaying anchor.** Jefferies' $110 low is chasing the tape —
   six downgrades since January.
3. **The probability vector is fragile.** Shade base to $115 and bull to $140 and the implied severe-bear
   probability falls to ~30% **and the edge mostly evaporates**. This is not a robust valuation.

Add the structural bear: backlog composition (Intel 40.0% / Health 13.6% / Homeland 20.4% / Defense 26.0%)
at current segment margins implies a blended segment margin of **~9.8% versus 11.9% today** — a
permanently *lower* base multiple (10.5–11.5x, not the defunct 13–15x). And Homeland's optics will look
crushed all year because ~$25M/quarter of Entrust intangible amortisation sits **inside** segment
operating income from Q2 onward; net debt is ~$5.88B (~2.4x EBITDA).

### Mechanism falsifier
**Aug-4 shows Health backlog erosion accelerating (funded rolling over), OR management cannot quantify a
bounded DHA exposure, OR the September-window VA FY2027 obligation fails to appear** — then the reset is
unbounded and the existing starter exits.

### Edge type
**RISK_PREMIUM (over-charged risk), court-tempered to a gated, probability-vector add.** Sizing reads
this label and the gate reads the date.
