## 1 · The four-idea frame

### Market believes
Luxury's engine is broken. LVMH's own flagship division — Fashion & Leather Goods, 75% of group operating
profit — is still shrinking after two years of decline. The China recovery is a jewelry-and-gold artifact.
Wait for the numbers to turn before paying 22x for a compounder that has stopped compounding.

### We believe
This is the version the court adjudicated, and it needs restating honestly in light of the print:

*Pre-print:* the engine is troughing rather than broken; the fortress balance sheet pays ~2.7% while it
turns; a Middle-East reversal is a free option; and the Jul-28 gate would settle whether the improvement
was real demand or comp arithmetic.

*Post-print:* **it was comp arithmetic, and we said in advance that this was the thing to test.** F&LG
organic was **−1% in H1 and +1% in Q2** against a **−9% prior-year comparison** — the easiest comp of the
downturn. On a two-year stack that is still deeply negative. Asia ex-Japan, the region our gate keyed on,
**decelerated sequentially +7% → +4%** while the press release describes it as "confirming the improvement
in trends." Japan's +14%, the single largest contributor to the sequential acceleration, sits on a −28%
base and remains ~18% below Q2-2024. **The United States is the only region with genuine two-year
improvement (+6% on a flat comp).**

And the acceleration is not in the profit engine. The divisions that accelerated — Watches & Jewelry +11%,
Selective Retailing +6% — are **19.8% of group recurring profit** (€1,724M of €8,691M). F&LG is **71.3%**
(€6,195M), and its profit fell **−7%** on revenue −5%: negative operating leverage, with margin down 60bps
to 34.1% (FY25: 35.0%).

So: we believe the business is stable, fairly valued, financially unimpeachable (net debt €8,245M =
11.8% of equity, down 19% year-on-year), and **not yet inflecting**. That is a "wait" conclusion, and the
court's own structure already encoded it.

### Why we might have edge
**We may not — and after the print, the honest answer is that we almost certainly do not at this price.**
The EC record said so before the print in plain words: *"Thin and stated: ~+14% to fair value plus the
dividend — a fairly-paid quality anchor with a free Mideast option, not a mispricing,"* and *"the Street's
average target sits exactly on our base case — we're with consensus, sized accordingly."* That is
**RP_FAIR carry**, labelled as such.

The one place a genuine edge existed was in the *test design*, and it paid: our red team refuted our own
memo's "−7 → −3 → −2 improving trajectory" as a splice of half-years onto a quarter (the real quarterly
series was −5/−9/−2/−3/−2, a plateau) and hardened the gate from "≥0%" to "≥+3% on a real two-year stack."
The original gate **would have flashed green** on the weakest underlying reading of the downturn. The
hardened gate correctly said no. That is the framework catching a takeaway-vs-data divergence **inside our
own memo** — which is the product working, even though it produced no trade.

Carry-vs-edge label: **carry, and the carry is not currently available at an authorised price.**

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong
The strongest bear:
1. **Mix degradation is structural, not cyclical.** Growth is coming from divisions earning 9–16% margins
   (W&J, Selective Retailing) while the 35%-margin engine shrinks. Every point of mix shift costs
   ~20–25bps of group margin. That justifies 20–22x, not 23x — which is exactly why the red team cut base
   FV from €572 to **€525**.
2. **22x is on trough EPS.** Normalised EPS ~€26–27 puts it at 18–19x — *fair for quality*, not deep value.
   There is no valuation cushion to be wrong in.
3. **The tape already disagrees with our gate.** Hermès **passed** the Asia bar (+2.4% cc) and fell
   **−11.0%** on its print; LVMH **failed** the F&LG bar and rose **+0.75%**. Over Jul-27 → Jul-31 MC.PA
   is **+1.79%**. Whatever the market traded that week, it was not the variable our gate selected. That is
   a genuine criticism of the gate, not of the stock — and it is the kind of thing that should lower our
   confidence that F&LG organic is the *priced* line at all.
4. **Governance.** Arnault is 77, the age cap was raised to 85, and there is no named successor. The court
   assessed this as a modest permanent discount, not a catalyst. It is permanent, and it is a discount we
   pay every day we own it.

### Mechanism falsifier
**F&LG still negative *and* Asia decelerating = restocking, not demand — stop adding; the knife resumes.**
Q2 delivered *half* of that (Asia decelerating, F&LG barely positive), which is why the gate failed
without the hard kill firing. The hard kill remains: **F&LG organic below zero against the −9% comp = the
thesis is dead.** Q2 printed **+1%**, so the kill did **not** fire.

### Edge type
**RISK_PREMIUM (RP_FAIR quality anchor).** Not EDGE. Sizing reads this label — and the price gate reads
the failed test.

### Liquidity note
Mega-cap CAC-40. **~€325M/day** 90-day average dollar-equivalent volume on the SBF line. A $120k
(≈€104k) position is ~0.03% of one day — trivially harvestable at any lot size, no staging constraint.

**Three frictions that do not apply to the US names in this book:**
1. **French financial transaction tax.** France levies an FTT on acquisitions of shares in French-
   incorporated companies above €1bn market cap. The standing rate is **0.3%** — but a rate increase was
   under active debate in the 2025–26 French budget cycle, so **treat the current rate as CORROBORATED,
   NOT VERIFIED, and confirm it with the broker before the first harvest round-trip.** At 0.3%, a full
   harvest-and-repurchase cycle on a $120k line costs **~$360 per round trip in tax alone**, before spread.
   That is a genuine drag on a name whose whole purpose in this sleeve is lot generation.
2. **FX is part of the tax lot.** For US tax the basis and the proceeds are each translated at their own
   spot rate, so EURUSD moves flow straight into the realised capital gain or loss. This *helps* the
   harvest engine — a EUR position can show a harvestable USD loss with the euro price flat — but it also
   means the "loss" being banked is partly a currency loss the principal may not have intended to take.
   State it on the ticket rather than discovering it at year-end.
3. **Withholding.** French dividend withholding applies to the €5.50 interim (payable 2026-12-03) and is
   creditable, not lost — but it is cash out and credit back, and it is one more reason not to churn the
   position across the ex-date.
