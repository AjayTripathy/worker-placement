## 1 · The four-idea frame

### What the market believes

Western Alliance is the bank that nearly died in March 2023. It is a concentrated,
deposit-flighty, venture-and-CRE-levered regional whose funding base proved it can run, and
which therefore deserves to trade at a structural discount to clean regionals no matter what the
current quarter prints. The 2026 numbers do not settle that argument, because the fragility was
never about earnings — it was about the liability side, and the liability side is still
wholesale-flavoured: a third of deposits sit in escrow/ECR balances the bank pays for through
non-interest expense, uninsured balances are climbing again, and the bank is growing loans into a
late credit cycle. On top of that, Q1 2026 handed the bears a gift: $152.5M of fraud-related
charge-offs in fund-finance/NDFI, which is precisely the "you don't know what you own" risk the
discount is supposed to compensate. A holder of this view would say: the multiple is not a
mistake, it is the price of a balance sheet you cannot fully underwrite from the outside.

That is a serious position and it is roughly a third right. We say so below.

### What we believe

The discount is priced for a franchise that no longer exists, and roughly two thirds of it is
unearned. Specifically:

- The bank now earns a **15.3% return on tangible common equity** in a clean quarter (Q2 2026),
  against a **1.27x price-to-tangible-book** at the current $80.49. That is the cheapest
  price-to-book in its comparable set on the *best* return in that set — ZION trades ~1.55x on a
  lower ROTCE, EWBC ~2.05x on a similar ROTCE. Our base fair value of $98 is not a re-rating to a
  premium; it is **mere parity with ZION**, a bank that earns less.
- The deposit franchise is not the 2023 franchise. Average deposits grew **17.5% year over year
  ($71.1B → $83.5B)** while the **average cost of those deposits fell 35 basis points (2.13% →
  1.78%)**. A bank that has to pay up to keep money does not post that combination.
- The insurance overlay is *growing*, not shrinking: CDARS + ICS reciprocal balances stood at
  **$13.9B at 30 June 2026 ($2.1B + $11.8B)**, up from the $13.3B our court recorded a quarter
  earlier. This matters because the red team's own added kill-trigger was "reciprocal shrinking
  while uninsured grows" — the reciprocal leg is moving the right way.
- Credit is normalising with one identifiable bad quarter inside it, not breaking. Net charge-offs
  ran **0.37% of average loans in the first half of 2026 (vs 0.24% prior year)**; non-accrual loans
  are **0.92% of funded loans, up from 0.85% at year-end**. Those are deterioration, and we say so;
  they are not distress.

The trade is that the market is still pricing the *story* while the disclosure prices the
*franchise*, and there is a two-to-three-quarter lag before the gap closes.

### Why we might have edge

The mechanism is **narrative persistence on a name with a memorable scar**. Three things have to
be true for this to be a real edge rather than a value trap, and each is independently checkable:

1. **The masking channel is framing, not accounting.** Western Alliance discloses everything —
   the fraud charge-offs, the ECR structure, the uninsured mix. Nothing is hidden. What persists is
   the *interpretive frame* investors apply to a bank whose ticker is attached to a bank run. That
   frame decays with distance from the event and with each clean call report, not with a
   restatement.
2. **The signal channel is the quarterly credit and funding print** — the earnings release, then
   the FDIC call report for cert 57512 roughly a month later. Both are free, both are mechanical,
   and neither requires us to be smarter than anyone else, only more patient about grading them.
3. **The latency is 2–3 quarters**, because the re-rate is a multiple move that requires a
   sequence of uneventful quarters, not a single catalyst. A name that re-rates on one print does
   not carry a scar discount in the first place.

Honest qualifier: this is a *crowded-analyst* name with full sell-side coverage. Our edge is not
information. It is that we have written down, in advance and with dated bars, what would make us
wrong — and most holders of the bull case have not.

### Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong

The strongest bear case is not the 2023 replay. It is this:

**About a third of the discount is earned, and we can name the three components.**

- **Fraud is a control finding, not a credit finding.** The Q1 2026 charge-offs — $126.4M
  (Leucadia) + $26.1M (Cantor), $152.5M total in fund-finance/NDFI — are not a loss-given-default
  story. They are evidence that the bank underwrote counterparties it did not understand in a
  business it grew fast. One fraud is an event. The bear's claim is that it is a *sample*. Note
  what this does to the headline: the clean-quarter ROTCE is 15.3%, but the **first-half 2026 ROTCE
  is 12.9%, below the prior year's 14.2%** — because the fraud quarter is in it. Our own thesis
  leans on the 15.3%; the honest run-rate is between the two.
- **The funding mix is moving the wrong way even if the level is fine.** Uninsured deposits went
  from 28.2% to 36.5% of the book across five quarters — a peer-normal *level* but the wrong
  *direction*, on a bank with a **7.0% tangible common equity ratio**, which is thin. And 37% of
  deposits are ECR balances costing **$179.2M a quarter routed through non-interest expense**,
  which means the reported 3.53% net interest margin and the reported efficiency ratio are simply
  not comparable to a peer's. Anyone marking WAL against peer NIM is marking it wrong, in our
  favour on NIM and against us on efficiency.
- **Non-performing loans are rising, not normalising.** 0.76% → 0.83% → 0.92%, with allowance
  coverage of non-accruals falling from 105% to 96%. The correct sentence is "criticized assets
  improving, non-performing loans rising" — not "credit normalising". Our record's original wording
  was too kind and the red team corrected it.

If the bear is right, we do not lose 2023-style; we lose by owning a permanently 1.2x bank that
earns 13% through the cycle instead of 15%, and the $98 base never arrives. That is the $70 bear.

### Mechanism falsifier

**Dated and checkable:** the FDIC call report for cert 57512, quarter ended 30 June 2026, published
~late August 2026. If uninsured deposits print **above 40%**, or if reciprocal CDARS/ICS balances
*shrink* while uninsured balances grow, the funding-scar leg of the thesis is falsified and the
position goes to exit-review regardless of the earnings line. Last mechanical read: **36.48% and
rising**. This is the single most informative number we will see before the October call resolves.

Second falsifier, same window: any new fraud- or NDFI-related charge-off **above $25M**.

### Edge type

**EDGE.** Verified divergence between the marketed takeaway (fragile venture bank) and the
disclosed data (diversified $82B franchise earning 15.3% at 1.27x book), with a named masking
channel, a named signal channel, and a named latency. This label is what authorises the full
proportional 2.0% scale — it is the strongest conviction of the five names.

**Why this is acceptable within the axis budget.** WAL's $120k is the largest single line in the
consumer-credit / financials-funding axis, which carries a **$360,000 review tripwire (6.0% of the
forward base)**. The ruled program across the axis is WAL $120k + OMF $70k + FSBW $24k + NPB ~$5k

~$81k of headroom.** Crossing $360k in aggregate (fills, marks, or new staging), or adding a sixth
name to the axis, forces a sizing court before the next add. It is a review trigger, not a hard
cap; the no-cap doctrine of 29 July stands as doctrine, but no-cap is not the same as no-number.

If all three US consumer-credit names hit their court bear cases simultaneously — the correlated
hard-landing scenario, which is the only way this axis actually hurts — the combined loss is
**~$34,300 on $214k deployed, or 0.57% of the forward base.** Across all five names in this
scaling round, an all-bear outcome is **~$50,800, or 0.85%.**

That is the honest answer to "what changes at 12x": the worst plausible outcome on this position
goes from a rounding error to about a quarter of one percent of capital, and the axis as a whole
risks under one percent. Both are inside a budget that was set before this doc was written.
