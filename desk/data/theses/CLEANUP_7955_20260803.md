# Cleanup Corporation (7955.T) — full court, v1.6

**Verdict: WATCH 5/10.** Risk-premium class, not edge. Entry band ¥780–830, gated on the August 7 print. Spot ¥906 (IBKR, TSEJ). Market cap ¥32.1B / ~$205M.

**What changed during the court:** it opened as a STARTER 6/10 on the strength of a cheap-asset case plus a currency tailwind. The red team took away both the dislocation framing and the currency leg. What survives is narrower and more honest: a fairly-priced low-return manufacturer whose asset backing is real and whose management has asserted a margin path it has not yet delivered.

---

## The four ideas

**1. What the market believes.** A Japanese system-kitchen maker earning a 2.9% operating margin on ¥134.5B of revenue is a low-return manufacturer in a market with structurally declining housing starts, and 0.53× book is the correct price for that. This view is *better supported than value screens admit*: at a 5.6–6.0% return on equity, residual-income maths puts justified price-to-book at 0.57–0.66× depending on the cost of equity you use. **Spot is roughly fair on current earnings.** The market is not making a mistake about what this business earns today.

**2. What we believe.** Two things, neither of which requires the market to be wrong about today:

- **The asset floor is unusually hard.** Net cash of ¥11.94B covers 37% of the market capitalisation, and current assets net of *all* liabilities cover 74%. This is the only name in the ten-name EDINET batch whose sub-book status survives the strict denominator — 0.53× on net assets and 0.57× on shareholders' equity. Six other names in the batch stopped being cheap once the honest denominator was used; this one didn't need the flattery.
- **Management has asserted the margin path and, once, acted on it.** FY3/27 guidance is revenue +5.6% with recurring profit +20.1%. If it lands, return on equity moves to roughly 7.2% and justified price-to-book to ~0.89× — about ¥1,520. In February 2026 the company executed a buyback **with cancellation**, which is an action rather than an intention, and it raised the dividend from 31 to 33 before holding it.

**3. Why this might be edge — honestly, it is thin.** The classification is RISK_PREMIUM, not EDGE. The one genuine informational asymmetry is the denominator test: most screens carry this at a looser book basis, and most people who look at Japanese small caps do not distinguish between a discount that survives treasury-share and equity-basis adjustment and one that does not. That is a small, real, and shrinking edge. Everything else here is a *fairly-paid* proposition: you are paid a 3.63% dividend and a 6% free-cash-flow yield to wait on a call option over management's own guidance.

**4. Why it may be priced in / why we may be wrong.** The market is pricing the base case at near-certainty. Solving spot against our own scenario prices implies roughly a 0.94 probability on the base case and a *negative* weight on the bull — which means our entire modelled edge comes from assigning a **lower** probability to the bear case than the market does, not from any optimism about the upside. That is a fragile place for an edge to live.

## Entry plan

- **Band ¥780–830** — 0.45–0.48× strict book, deliberately *below* the 0.57× justified price-to-book at today's earnings, so the entry pays for the bear case rather than the base case. That is 9–14% below spot.
- **Size 0.35% starter**, scaling to 0.7% only after one clean quarter of the guided margin *and* verification of the buyback's size.
- **Gate:** the August 7 print first. No pre-positioning — the margin-leverage argument is the entire case and the guide is its only evidence.
- Limit orders only, TSEJ, 100-share board lot. Liquidity is the best in the batch relative to position size.

## Kill triggers

- Revenue declines more than 5% year-on-year in any quarter — a 2.9% margin cannot absorb it.
- FY3/27 guidance withdrawn or cut.
- No further buyback or dividend action within twelve months of the February cancellation — the register argument dies without management choosing return.
- USD/JPY back above 162 (removes what remains of the FX optionality).
