# COMMODITY COURT — URANIUM
**2026-08-14 · SignalOS · question posed: thesis or ballast for this book?**

**VERDICT: 3/10 — NO POSITION. Ballast claim REFUTED on data; thesis claim UNPROVEN at current prices.**
Uranium does not hedge this book's AI exposure — it *doubles* it, including in physical form.
The bull mechanism everyone cites (financial buying squeezes spot) ran at **record size this year
and produced almost nothing**. The one genuinely healthy fact in the complex — a term price up
twelve months straight — is **not accessible through any instrument we can buy**.

---

## 1. Lead finding (Mode B, no promoter framing)

**The tradeable instruments are levered to the wrong price.**

The uranium fundamental, to the extent it is real, lives in the **long-term (term) contract price** —
what utilities actually pay under multi-year contracts. That price has risen every single month for
twelve months. But:

- **SPUT / physical trusts hold *spot*** — a thin residual market that has gone sideways for two years.
- **Cameco equity** carries the term price *plus* an AI-narrative multiple, a deteriorating
  Westinghouse stake, and an ~8 Mlb/yr purchase obligation that makes it partly *short* uranium.
- **Centrus (LEU)** is not a uranium trade at all — it is enrichment plus a government program.

There is no clean retail expression of the only price series that is actually working. That is the
whole finding, and it is why "the uranium bull case is right" and "we should own uranium" are
different statements.

## 2. The reflexivity test — and a hypothesis of mine that got REFUTED

I went in expecting to find that SPUT's at-the-market program was **switched off** at a discount to
NAV (the widely-repeated claim that the trust can only issue accretively, at a premium). **The
trust's own filing refutes that.** Recorded here because the refutation is the more useful result.

From the Sprott Physical Uranium Trust **Q2 2026 Report & MRFP** (primary):

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Units issued via ATM, H1-2026 | **33,085,763 units, $703.0M gross** |
| Units issued via ATM, H1-2025 | 13,506,362 units, $232.8M gross |
| ATM scale-up year over year | **3.02×** |
| Pounds U3O8 held, Dec-31-2025 → Jun-30-2026 | 74,789,404 → **81,447,348** (+6,657,944 lbs) |
| Average discount to NAV during H1-2026 | **2.7%** (vs 8.0% in H1-2025) |
| Spot U3O8, Dec-31-2025 → Jun-30-2026 | $81.54 → **$85.03 (+4.3%)** |

So the ATM is not off. It is running at **three times last year's pace, and issuing at a discount.**

**And that is exactly why the bull case is in trouble.** The single largest price-insensitive
physical bid in the market bought 6.66 million pounds with $703 million in six months, and spot
moved **+4.3%**. That is roughly **$164 million of dedicated buying per 1% of spot price.**

In the 2021 episode the same machine moved spot far more on far less. **The reflexive channel is
live, at record throttle, and impotent.** A market that absorbs $703M of non-economic buying with a
4% move is a well-supplied market. This is disconfirming evidence of the highest quality, because it
tests the bull mechanism on the bulls' own terms.

**Second-order note:** the ATM now works *against* holders. Sprott issues into any strength, so the
program caps the premium while the discount is free to widen. It has flipped from a 2021-style
upside accelerant into a structural ceiling. Discount was **-10.3% at Jun-30**, averaged -2.7%
across H1, and sits at **-7.5% today** (NAV $20.91 vs market $19.34, Aug-13-2026) — wider than the
H1 average, which implies issuance is throttling back in H2.

## 3. The supply natural experiment — two tier-1 outages, no price response

Cameco suffered **two separate interruptions at the world's best uranium mines in 2026**, and I can
date both from company releases:

1. **May 2026** — flooding collapsed the **Smoothstone River Bridge**, the primary supply route.
   Key Lake mill *halted*, McArthur River *reduced*. Resumed 2026-05-27.
2. **July 1, 2026** — Orano's **McClean Lake mill sulfuric acid plant** went down; **Cigar Lake
   suspended**. Resumed 2026-07-14.

Monthly average spot across that window (Cameco/UxC): **Apr $86.35 → May $84.18 → Jun $85.00 →
Jul $86.38.**

Spot **fell** during the McArthur outage and finished the two-outage period **$0.03 above where it
started.** If this market were structurally tight, simultaneous disruption at McArthur River and
Cigar Lake is precisely the event that produces a squeeze. It produced nothing.

Cameco held 2026 guidance at **19.5–21.5 Mlbs (its share)** through both events.

**The buried structural fact:** Cameco has contracted **>28 Mlbs/yr of average deliveries over the
next five years** against ~20 Mlbs of production. It must **buy the difference**. Cameco is a net
purchaser of roughly 8 Mlbs/yr — so a spot spike *compresses* its near-term margin. CCJ is not clean
long-spot leverage, and anyone treating it as a uranium proxy has the sign partly wrong.

Also worth flagging as takeaway-vs-data divergence: **Westinghouse — the segment carrying the
newbuild/AI story — posted a Q2-2026 net LOSS of $10M (Cameco's share), down from +$126M.** The
narrative leg deteriorated while the narrative got louder. JV Inkai delivered only **0.8 Mlbs in
H1-2026 against a 4.2 Mlb expected annual allocation.**

## 5. BALLAST TEST — the decisive result

Computed from IBKR weekly closes, **104 observations, 2024-08-15 → 2026-08-10**. This is the
question the desk actually asked, and the data answers it cleanly.

### Return vs risk, two years

| Asset | 2y total return | Annualised vol | Return/vol |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCJ | **+143.5%** | 45.0% | 1.25 |
| **SPUT (physical U3O8)** | **+13.5%** | **31.5%** | **0.21** |
| PHYS (gold — book's ballast) | +71.1% | 21.1% | 1.46 |
| SPY | +40.0% | 15.5% | 1.18 |
| NVDA | +80.8% | 42.5% | 0.81 |
| T-bills (reference) | ~+9.6% | ~0 | — |

**Physical uranium returned +13.5% over two years against ~+9.6% for T-bills — while carrying 31.5%
annualised volatility.** Four points of excess return over cash for a third of the volatility of the
entire equity book. The commodity itself has been dead money. CCJ's +143.5% was a *multiple*
re-rating, not a commodity move: spot rose ~7% over the same window.

### Behaviour in AI stress — worst-decile NVDA weeks (n=11, NVDA mean −10.2%)

| Asset | Mean return | Weeks positive |
|---|---|---|
| CCJ | **−5.51%** | 18% |
| **SPUT (physical)** | **−4.00%** | **9% (1 of 11)** |
| SPY | −2.36% | 9% |
| **PHYS (gold)** | **+0.34%** | **55%** |

**This is the answer.** In the exact scenario the frozen AI-break call (p=0.45) is about, **physical
uranium falls 1.7× the S&P and is positive in one week out of eleven.** It is not a diversifier. It
is high-volatility AI-narrative beta wearing a hard-asset costume. Gold, which this book already
owns, does the ballast job properly: positive on average, positive more often than not, beta to
NVDA of **0.05**.

Correlations: SPUT/NVDA **0.33**, SPUT/SPY 0.34, CCJ/NVDA **0.48**, CCJ/SPY 0.48 — versus PHYS/NVDA
**0.11**. Betas: CCJ/SPY **1.41**, SPUT/SPY **0.70**, PHYS/SPY 0.25.

**Adding uranium as a hedge against an AI break would add exposure to the thing being hedged.** The
demand story *is* the AI story — the datacenter-PPA narrative is the marginal buyer of the
narrative, so the asset inherits the correlation. Confirms the existing desk finding that the power
layer is an AI-break *transmission channel*, not a refuge.

## 6. What is genuinely bullish, stated fairly

One thing in this complex is unambiguously working, and it deserves to be stated without hedging:

**The long-term (term) contract price has risen twelve months out of twelve.**

| | Aug-25 | Nov-25 | Feb-26 | May-26 | Jul-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Term | $81.00 | $86.00 | $90.00 | $94.00 | **$95.50** |
| Spot | $75.13 | $75.80 | $86.95 | $84.18 | $86.38 |

Term is **$9.12 above spot (+10.6%)** and has never once declined month over month. Spot spiked to
$94.28 in Jan-26 and gave it all back; term just kept grinding up. Utilities — the only buyers who
matter — are contracting at steadily rising prices. And Cameco's own book is **heavier 2026-28 and
lighter 2029-30**, meaning the contracting cycle for the back years is still ahead.

That is a real bull case. It is simply **not one we can buy**: spot instruments miss it, and the
equity wraps it in a 1.41 market beta plus Westinghouse.

## 9. Verdict and sizing

**3/10. NO POSITION. Size 0%.**

- **As ballast: REFUTED, not merely unproven.** Physical uranium loses 4.0% in the average
  worst-decile AI week and is positive 1 time in 11. It would concentrate the exposure the frozen
  p=0.45 AI-break call is meant to be defended against. PHYS already holds that slot and does it
  properly.
- **As thesis: UNPROVEN and currently contradicted.** Record ATM buying moved spot 4%. Two tier-1
  mine outages moved spot $0. The commodity beat T-bills by ~4pp over two years with 31.5% vol.
- **Not a lie, and not a trap** — this complex discloses its own weakness honestly in the filings.
  There is no honesty-alpha short here either. It is simply a fully-priced narrative asset whose
  marketed mechanism does not survive its own data.

**This is a valid no-edge outcome.** No capital, and none of the SGOV tax reserve — the Q3 estimate
is due 2026-09-15.

### Tripwires that would reopen this (dated, falsifiable)

1. **SPUT discount wider than −12%** on a sustained basis. Wider than anything in the five-period
   record (worst: −10.3%), and it would force the ATM off — removing the paper supply that currently
   caps the instrument. *This is the cleanest re-entry trigger and it is mechanical.*
2. **Term price ≥ $100/lb while spot lags below $90** — confirms the utility cycle without the
   financial froth. Check monthly at Cameco's price page.
3. **Cameco 2029-30 contract book filling at disclosed higher floors** — Q3-2026 report, watch the
   commitment profile, not the headline.
4. **Demand leg closed with binding, dated PPAs on NEW reactors** — not re-contracted existing units.
   Until that is verified, the incremental-demand claim stays unquantified.

**Re-open condition is CONJUNCTIVE for a thesis upgrade: (1) or (2), AND the demand leg from §7
actually closed.** A discount alone buys a cheap instrument, not a thesis.
