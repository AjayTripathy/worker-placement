## 2. THE ONE PLACE THE RED IS WRONG — AND WHY IT DOESN'T RESCUE THE LONG

The red presents the 21.6% one-quarter US cut as evidence of impairment. **Management's own bridge contains no impairment at all.** Verbatim, the US walk from $970m to $760m:

| Item | $m |
|---|---|
| Q2 trading ahead of guidance | **+15** |
| Market-making revenue/EBITDA benefit | **+50** |
| Additional operating cost savings | **+45** |
| NFL season one-week delay (phasing) | **−50** |
| Net investment to accelerate FanDuel's sportsbook momentum | **−270** |
| "A measured view of current market trends" | **0** |
| **Total** | **−210** |

It reconciles **exactly**. The residual "market trends" line carries no number. So the cut is **elective investment plus a calendar shift**, and the underlying US outlook went *up* $110m. Normalizing the two items the court asked about ($270m step-up, $50m NFL delay) takes FanDuel from **15.2x to 10.7x** guided EBITDA (International at 8x). Adding back the ~$300m FanDuel Predicts loss and ~$105m of new-state launch losses takes it to **~7.8x**.

**Why this does not carry the day — three reasons, all primary:**

1. **The guide is low-quality evidence.** FY25 was guided to **$3.295bn on 2025-08-07** and delivered **$2,845m — a 13.7% miss**. FY26 has gone $2.97bn → $2.865bn → $2.655bn. Four consecutive prints have cut. A bridge that reconciles is not the same as a bridge that happens ([Q2'25 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1635327/000119312525175427/d43893dex991.htm)).
2. **The multiple never broke.** EV/EBITDA was ~10.9x at the FY25 print (2026-02-26) and is **10.06x today**. The stock fell from $125 to $93.50 over six months at an essentially **constant multiple** — that is re-forecasting, not a sentiment dislocation. A dislocation requires the multiple to break. It hasn't.
3. **The price already equals the guide.** Holding 10.06x, the Q4 scenarios price out as:

| Q4'26 US margin | Q4 EBITDA | FY26 group | Implied price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14.5% (= Q4'25 actual) | $359m | $2,492m | **$84.05** |
| 18.0% | $445m | $2,578m | $89.07 |
| **21.1% (= the guide)** | **$522m** | **$2,655m** | **$93.52** ← live $93.50 |

**The market is paying full freight for the guidance, with zero cushion.** That is the arithmetic answer to "is $93.65 a dislocation." It is not. It is the guide, marked.

## 3. TWO FINDINGS NEITHER BENCH HAD

**The Fox Option is an independent, auditor-reviewed valuation of FanDuel — and it collapsed.** Fox holds an option on **18.6% of FanDuel** struck at **$4.9bn** (arbitration-set at $3.7bn as of Dec-2020, escalating 5%/yr), exercisable to Dec-2030, cash, in full only. That strike implies **$26.3bn for 100% of FanDuel**. The option was marked **$560m → $220m** in six months — **while management cut the DCF discount rate from 18.0% to 16.5%**, a change that *raises* value. The underlying FanDuel enterprise-value input therefore fell by more than the headline. The $333m H1 fair-value gain on that liability is the entire "other income, net" of $318m; ex it, the H1 net loss is roughly **−$420m** rather than −$87m ([10-Q Note 15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1635327/000163532726000056/flut-20260630.htm)).

**Flutter paid $1,553m for 5% of FanDuel in July 2025 — implying $31.1bn.** The market now implies ~$11.5bn. A −63% mark on the crown jewel in thirteen months, by the buyer's own transaction ([FY25 release, fn 15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1635327/000119312526076569/d763070dex991.htm)).

**A third, smaller one, on the red's "rented countermove":** the disclosed CME term is a **~50% share of FanDuel Predicts gross revenue before promotional spend**, with FanDuel bearing 100% of app promo, sales and marketing and non-exchange cost of sales. The red flagged the tension honestly; the mechanism is a revenue share, not an equity split, and it is worse economics than the red assumed (FY25 release, fn 10).

## 4. WHERE I CONCEDE, AND WHERE I HOLD

**Conceded.** My best three attacks all failed on primary evidence: the Q4 base was flattered not depressed; International is not at trough because a second duty step lands in April 2027; and "disposal optionality" is **unsupported** — there is no sale process disclosed for Sisal, Snai, PokerStars, Sportsbet or any other asset, and the direction of travel is buy-in (Boyd), not sale.

**Held.** Three things genuinely improve on the red's telling and belong on the record: the guidance cut contains no disclosed operating deterioration; the balance sheet has no near-term test (\$78m of maturities through 2027 against \$2.6bn of liquidity); and US share, having fallen 41%→39%, has been **stable at 39%/27% for two consecutive quarters** — the erosion is bounded, not compounding. Also, 17pp of the group's AMP decline is the **statutory India exit**, not customer attrition.

**On honesty — which is our actual product — Flutter is CLEAN.** The guidance bridge reconciles to the dollar; the share loss was disclosed by the company itself in February; the sports-results impact is quantified in basis points; the India exit is quantified three years forward; the second UK duty step is disclosed with before- and after-mitigation numbers. This is an honestly-reported earnings trough, not a masked one. The single divergence worth noting is presentational: the Q2 release states "Continued #1 sportsbook and iGaming positions; 39% and 27%" without noting these are down from 41%/28% two quarters earlier. That is a *review flag*, not masking — the underlying data is in the state regulator files. **And under our own doctrine, an honestly impaired business is CLEAN, and CLEAN is not BUY.**
