## SCIENCE BRIEF — ATEC (Alphatec Holdings)

**The question:** ATEC's differentiation is a *procedural* claim — that the prone transpsoas (PTP) approach, wrapped in SafeOp neuromonitoring, EOS imaging and Valence navigation, makes lateral spine surgery reproducible enough to convert surgeons. The investment case turns on whether that claim is demonstrated at a level that commands **price per case**, or whether it is a workflow/efficiency advantage that buys **case volume** while the consumable that actually carries price — biologics — remains commodity tissue. Revenue per case is the financial readout of exactly that scientific question.

## 2. THE MECHANISM

**What is established.** Lateral transpsoas access reaches the L1–L5 discs through the retroperitoneum by splitting the psoas major. The lumbar plexus runs within and posterior to the psoas and migrates ventrally as you descend, so the safe working corridor narrows caudally and is at its worst at L4–5. Retractor dwell time on the plexus, not the discectomy, is what injures nerves. This is settled anatomy and it is the reason every player in this space sells neuromonitoring alongside the retractor.

**What is ATEC's novel claim.** Two things, and they are different in kind:

1. *Positioning.* Performing the transpsoas approach **prone** rather than in lateral decubitus. The published rationale is that prone leaves the psoas and plexus relatively more posterior, lets hip extension supply natural lordosis, and — the real driver — permits simultaneous anterior-column and posterior-instrumentation work without repositioning ([J Spine Surg review](https://jss.amegroups.org/article/view/6203/html)). The pooled evidence supports **workflow** gains (OR time, LOS) and is **null on complications and on segmental lordosis** ([PMC11483458](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11483458/)).
2. *Instrumented reproducibility.* Neuromonitoring (SafeOp, saphenous SSEP), navigation (Valence), alignment planning (EOS/spineEOS/VEA Align, IntraOp Alignment/CONTOUR3D). The claim is that instrumentation converts an expert-dependent approach into a teachable one — which is a claim about **adoption**, not about outcomes.

**Where the ceiling sits.** Three hard limits, all class-inherent:

- **L5–S1 is unreachable transpsoas** — the iliac crest and the plexus close the corridor. PTP can never be a whole-lumbar solution; it competes for a subset of levels, and ALIF/TLIF/posterior work must carry the rest. This caps the share of any spine case that PTP-specific product can capture.
- **SSEP's sensitivity ceiling.** At **44% sensitivity**, saphenous-SSEP misses more than half of the deficits it is deployed to catch; its value is its 97% specificity (a *stable* trace is informative, a *changed* trace is highly informative). No amount of engineering on the amplifier changes the physiology of a modality that is late by design — evoked-potential loss follows ischemia, it does not anticipate it. SafeOp 3's January 2026 clearance is a substantial-equivalence clearance, not new evidence.
- **Distraction mechanics.** ALL rupture at ~2.2% pooled is the price of the lordosis the approach is sold on. Correction and rupture are the same lever.

**Historical failure modes, by name.**

- **XLIF (NuVasive, 2000s).** Built the lateral market and carried thigh pain, groin numbness and hip-flexor weakness as its signature morbidity. ATEC's leadership is the NuVasive alumni cohort and PTP is explicitly the answer to that morbidity — but the meta-analysis shows the answer is **not yet demonstrated**: postoperative sensory symptoms 8.2% and lower-extremity weakness 5.8% in the largest PTP series ([PMID 37272706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37272706/)). *Class-inherent, not implementation-specific.*
- **INFUSE / rhBMP-2 (Medtronic).** The definitive cautionary tale for spine biologics: independent reanalysis of individual participant data found little or no benefit over bone graft, possible added harm including a cancer signal, and that only ~18.5% of adverse events in the trial data reached publication ([Ann Intern Med 2013, two papers](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/0003-4819-158-12-201306180-00006)). ATEC's stated remedy for its biologics gap is **another rhBMP-2 derivative**. Whether the AMP2 binding-peptide/lower-dose design escapes the class problem is precisely what an unpublished ~100-patient Phase I/II and a withheld pivotal cannot tell you. *Class-inherent; the dose-targeting design is the implementation-specific bet.*
- **PureGen (Alphatec's own, 2011–2013).** ATEC previously tried to differentiate in biologics with a stem-cell allograft and registered three studies for it. All three are **TERMINATED in the registry with token enrollment**: NCT01294007 (n=1), NCT01293981 (n=6), NCT01291134 (n=30). A fourth ATEC study, OsseoFix (NCT00961714), is also TERMINATED at n=15. *Implementation-specific, and it is ATEC's own precedent in the exact category the current thesis hinges on* ([registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.spons=Alphatec)).

## 3. THE FIELD (as of 2026-09-04)

**The approach is portable; the instrument set is not.** Published single-position prone-lateral series are being run on **competitor** platforms — Medtronic StealthStation S8 + O-arm for navigation, NuVasive NV5 free-running neuromonitoring for plexus mapping, with Globus supplying fellowship funding for prone-lateral research ([IJSS 19(1):70 / PMC12053097](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12053097/)). PTP is an approach, not a patent; ATEC evangelized a category that its two larger competitors can serve with their own hardware. That is the central competitive fact of this file.

**Biologics.** INFUSE remains the only PMA-approved rhBMP-2 in U.S. spine. Theradaptive/OsteoAdapt would be the second if approved — which is why ATEC bought U.S. rights rather than building. Everything else in ATEC's biologics bag (DBM, DBM fibers, CBM) sits in a $3.5B commodity category growing ~5% where hospitals push back on premium pricing ([market vendor data — Mordor/Fortune, labeled vendor, not primary](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/spine-biologics-market)). **Demand side:** the buyer that lacks a differentiated biologic and has paid up for adjacent capability is ATEC itself — it is the entity that just paid upfront + milestones + a perpetual royalty for a Phase-pivotal asset.

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS

Enumerated before a bench builds a kill on one.

1. **Revenue per case has no external comparator.** Neither Globus, Medtronic nor Stryker discloses a revenue-per-case metric. Any statement that ATEC's RPC is worse/better than peers' is unbuildable from primaries. Worse: RPC appears **only in ATEC's earnings call** — not in the 8-K press release and not in the 10-Q. It is an unaudited, company-defined metric with an undisclosed denominator.
2. **Surgical vs total vs U.S. spine.** ATEC's +17% is surgical-only; its +15% includes EOS; Globus's +7% is U.S. spine only and its +5.9% includes neuromodulation and trauma. Pick one perimeter or flag the mismatch.
3. **Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock comp, and ATEC's SBC is structurally large.** The evidence pack's SBC series is mis-stamped and cannot be used; take SBC from the 10-Q cash-flow statement before comparing ATEC's 16.8% "adjusted" margin to anyone's GAAP margin.
4. **Segmental vs global lumbar lordosis.** Marketing and single-arm series quote *segmental* gains (5.1° vs 2.5°; 9.9° vs 0.5°). The pooled meta-analysis finds **no segmental difference (P=0.28)** and only marginal global LL benefit (P=0.05). Different estimands, opposite impressions.
5. **Single-position vs prone.** The 103 vs 306 minute figure compares *single-position* to *dual-position* workflow — it confounds patient repositioning with the approach itself. It is not evidence that prone transpsoas is a better dissection.
6. **"Transient" is a timeframe, not a finding.** Sensory/motor deficit rates differ by an order of magnitude depending on whether they are measured at discharge, 6 weeks, or 12 months. The 8.2% / 5.8% figures are postoperative; the 0.6% figure is *permanent*. Do not compare across those windows.
7. **Radiation dose units.** Entrance skin dose ≠ effective dose ≠ dose-area product. The 4–8× EOS-vs-CT claim derives from a pelvimetry protocol in pregnant women, not from spine deformity imaging, and CT dose is protocol-tunable. A dose comparison that does not name the metric and the protocol is not a comparison.
8. **BMD T-scores are not interchangeable.** AutoDensity T-scores are derived from EOSedge dual-energy AP spine L1–L4 with its own calibration; DXA T-scores use a different reference population. FDA's cleared IFU forbids substitution. Any thesis that treats EOSedge as a DXA replacement market is off-label by the label.
9. **The OASIS trial studies a different procedure.** OsteoAdapt SP's pivotal is anchored on **single-level TLIF** (with ALIF/LLIF expansion), a posterior procedure — not the PTP/lateral case mix driving ATEC's volume growth. A positive OASIS result does not automatically transfer to the cases ATEC actually does.
10. **Fusion is an endpoint with three definitions** — CT-defined bridging, plain-radiograph criteria, and "clinical" fusion — with materially different rates. rhBMP-2's history is precisely a history of endpoint and harms-reporting selection ([PMC3687771](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3687771/)).
11. **Guidance is being compared across two different bases.** The January outlook ($890M, ~17%) and the August guide ($882M, ~15%) are both "FY26 revenue." The delta is a real cut, not a rounding difference.
12. **Close vs intraday on the Q1 drop** (>20% vs 31.7%) — do not average; state which basis you are using.

## 6. WHAT COULD NOT BE FOUND

No silent caps. Each item with the instrument that resolves it.

1. **Biologics revenue, in dollars or as a % of revenue.** Absent from the 8-K press release, absent from the 10-Q. → *10-K revenue-disaggregation note, or IR directly.* Without it, "¾ of the RPC shortfall is biologics" cannot be sized.
2. **The definition of "case" and of "revenue per case."** → *IR / the metric definition ATEC uses internally; it is not in any filed document.*
3. **Whether the EOSedge–DXA agreement study met its endpoint** (NCT06659055). → *Registry results posting, a NASS/RSNA abstract, or a peer-reviewed publication.*
4. **SafeOp's actual sensitivity/specificity in ATEC's own hands** (NCT05648474). → *Results posting or publication. Until then the only number available is the 44%/97% literature figure, which is not ATEC-specific.*
5. **OASIS pivotal design, n, endpoints, sites, timeline.** → *Unwithholding of NCT06154005 (typically at FDA approval), an FDA IDE disclosure, or a Theradaptive investor deck.*
6. **OsteoAdapt Phase I/II fusion rates and adverse events across ~100 patients.** → *Peer-reviewed publication or conference abstract; none found.* Given rhBMP-2's publication-bias history, unpublished is materially different from unfavorable, but it is also materially different from favorable.
7. **Economics of the Theradaptive deal** (upfront $, milestone schedule, royalty rate). → *10-Q/10-K commitments note or the underlying agreement as a filed exhibit; not in the January PR.*
8. **Globus Medical v. Alphatec patent docket status.** → *PACER (D. Del.). CourtListener returned 403 this session.*
9. **Whether a securities class-action complaint has been filed** (vs six investigation announcements). → *Stanford SCAC case id=104965 — returned 403; or PACER.*
10. **Q2'25 adjusted EBITDA margin**, needed to verify the "+420bp YoY" the upstream stage asserts against the company's stated 16.8%. → *Q2'25 8-K Ex-99.1.*
11. **Q2'26 free cash flow in dollars, and the four-consecutive-quarters claim.** The PR I retrieved says "generated positive free cash flow" without a figure. → *10-Q cash-flow statement plus the prior three.*
12. **The EOS unit shortfall behind the Q1 statement** that ATEC "committed to a number of units" it "did not fulfill" — order book, backlog, whether units were cancelled or deferred. → *Q1'26 transcript in full, 10-Q revenue-recognition note, or IR.* This is the factual core of the integrity allegation and it is an **EOS capital-equipment** question, not a surgical one.
13. **Valence installed base and utilization.** Priced ~$500k with no contracted-utilization requirement (per trade press, vendor-sourced). → *IR; no disclosure exists.*
14. **Whether ATEC's biologics softness is company-specific or category-wide.** No competitor discloses biologics attach. → *Would require Medtronic/Globus segment detail that does not exist at this granularity; the closest instrument is a hospital GPO pricing survey, which I do not have.*

---

**Bottom line for the benches, stated as evidence and not as a position:** the *workflow* claim (single-position prone, shorter OR time and LOS) is verified at primary. The *clinical superiority* claim — safety, segmental correction, neuro protection — is **not** demonstrated at primary; the pooled data is null on complications and segmental lordosis, the neuromonitoring modality has 44% sensitivity, and the company's own confirmatory studies are past their completion dates with no results and stale registry records. The biologics remedy is an rhBMP-2 derivative on a PMA path whose pivotal record is withheld, in a class whose canonical prior failed independent reanalysis. That is the shape of the evidence. What it implies for price, size or timing is the court's to decide.
