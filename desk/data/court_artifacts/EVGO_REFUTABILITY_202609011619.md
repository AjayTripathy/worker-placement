**On the funding gate specifically (this is where red's REJECT was wrong):**

The premise "raise overhang at 5.2% long yields — dilution math sub-$1.30" is not supported by the filing. Per the 10-Q: DOE loan drawn **$226.1M with $409.0M still available at Treasury +~1.2%**, plus $71.1M drawn on a $300M Voyager facility — **~$630M of undrawn committed capacity** against cash+restricted of **$197.7M** ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1821159/000182115926000019/evgo-20260630.htm)). H1 operating cash use was −$41.9M and H1 capex $64.4M, so ~$200M/yr of total cash need is covered roughly 3x by undrawn facilities before the ATM is touched. There is **no going-concern language**. The $183.5M ATM is a real overhang — at $1.295 it would issue ~142M shares — but it is optional, not mechanical. The honest bear case is not "they must sell equity at $1.29"; it is **"the DOE facility gets cut again"** — and that risk is demonstrated, not hypothetical: the DOE loan was already amended **down from $1.25B to $750M in April 2026**.

**Cap-structure correction (both prior benches appear to have missed this):** EVGO is an Up-C. Class A 140,390,001 + Class B 172,800,000 = **~313M economic units**, so equity value at $1.295 is **~$405M**, not the ~$180M Class-A-only figure most screens show. EV ≈ $505M against a $415M FY26 revenue midpoint (~1.2x EV/S). Calling this "a $1.29 microcap" understates the enterprise by ~2x and is likely why the two benches disagreed on whether the discount was extreme.

**Benign, not a red flag:** the 2026-07-01 Form 25-NSE is Nasdaq delisting the **redeemable warrants**, which expired worthless on 7/1/2026 at an $11.50 strike ([25-NSE](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/EVGO/25-nse-e-vgo-inc-sec-filing-6521b31da9d2.html)). Not a common-stock listing action. Common at $1.295 is above the $1.00 Nasdaq minimum bid, but the 52w low is $1.27 — that cushion is 23%.
