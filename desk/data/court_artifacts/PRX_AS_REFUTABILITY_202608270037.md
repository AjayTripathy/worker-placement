**Why the operating leg is DAMAGE-ABSENT and the governance leg is not:** the narrative's testable prediction — "the non-Tencent portfolio is a marks-based value sink" — is refuted by *cash*: US$1.3bn Ecosystem aEBITDA and US$1.5bn FCF, all three regional ecosystems profitable, OLX 48% aEBITDA margin on US$992m revenue, iFood aEBITDA US$400m, PayU aEBITDA positive for the first time (all FY26 media release, primary). Those are not marks. But the discount is not priced off operating quality — it is priced off the control structure, and that structure is unchanged and unfixable by operations.

**The cannibalisation question, reconstructed (the one thing a court must nail):** 54% of the buyback is funded by *selling Tencent* — the asset you are buying exposure to. Weekly Prosus-leg repurchases are still running and are dated: 3–7 Aug 1,790,596 sh @ €41.62 (€74.5m); 10–14 Aug 2,069,018 sh @ €39.32 (€81.4m); 17–21 Aug 1,837,563 sh @ €37.57 (~€69m) → ~€3.9bn/yr ≈ 4.3% of the 2.11bn share count. Against ~35m Tencent shares/yr sold, look-through Tencent shares per PRX share go from ~0.953 to ~0.979 — **+2.7% (range +2% to +6% on input uncertainty)**. Directionally the machine *adds* Tencent per share; the cannibalisation critique fails. **This is a reconstruction, not a citation** — the exact prior-year share count and Tencent share count come from the FY26 annual report and are the first pull a court should make.

**COURT-WORTHY (damage-absent, ranked):**
1. **PRX.AS** — court-worthy only on the *operating/valuation* leg, not on the dislocation premise. At €80.32bn market cap versus a Tencent stake alone worth ~€100bn, the entire ecosystem (US$1.3bn aEBITDA, US$1.5bn FCF, growing 40–84%) is embedded at *less than zero*, while the per-share Tencent look-through is rising. The dispersion the market is ignoring is not "China governance" — it is that a cash-generating, consolidating operating business is being valued below nothing inside a wrapper whose discount has been stable, not widening.

**Two items I could not resolve, and they are sizing-decisive:** (1) the **non-Tencent NAV mark** — I used a ~€24bn placeholder; if the true marked value of iFood/JET/Despegar/OLX/PayU is materially lower, the discount is ~30% rather than ~35% and the "free ecosystem" claim weakens by most of its force; (2) the **SOTP tax perimeter** — whether Dutch participation exemption makes Tencent disposals effectively tax-free determines whether the discount is a tax artifact or pure control-stack friction. Both are red/blue questions, not arithmetic.
