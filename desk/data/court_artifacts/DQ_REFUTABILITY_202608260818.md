### Capital structure — resolved (the pack flagged it UNCHECKED)

The single fact that reframes the name: **the cash is not at the entity you'd be buying.**

- Liquid assets reconcile exactly to $1.92B: cash $555.3M + ST inv $250.0M + notes rec $71.7M + HTM $51.0M + fixed deposits $928.9M (<1y) + $65.9M (>1y) = **$1,922.8M**, against **zero debt** (total liabilities $478.0M, none of it borrowings).
- But **noncontrolling interests = $1,489.1M** of $5,863.1M total equity (**25.4%**). DQ owns ~**72.8%** of Xinjiang Daqo (688303.SS), where the operations and the cash live.
- Attributable liquid assets ≈ **$1.40–1.43B** vs market cap ~$0.92–0.96B → **~0.65x attributable net liquid, debt-free**. Cheap on its face; DQ also trades at **0.22x its own stated book** ($4,373.9M).

### Why STRUCTURAL rather than DAMAGE-ARRIVING

Three named, load-bearing kills — so the [clean-court minimum-starter default] does not apply here:

1. **The cash floor is being actively dismantled by management.** DQ has committed **RMB 6B (~$833M)** to an AI-data-center pivot (solid-state transformers/breakers, 800V DC), phase 1 **RMB 3B (~$417M)** already committed, $30–40M spent in 2026, first prototype end-2026, revenue not until 2028–30. That is ~60% of attributable liquid assets converting into an unproven business rather than returning to ADS holders. The net-cash thesis has a dated mechanism for its own destruction.
2. **Trapped and unclaimable.** PRC capital controls, no dividend, and the only buyback in H1 was **$7.8M of subsidiary stock bought from 688303 minorities** — capital returned at the subsidiary, not to the ADR. Per house doctrine this fails the USD-claimability test outright.
3. **Producing into a glut on a policy bet.** 87,077 MT produced vs 19,672 MT sold in H1. Management is explicitly refusing below-cost sales under the Aug 6 eight-producer pact and stacking inventory instead, into an industry overhang of **500–600k tons**. That is a levered bet on a cartel pledge holding.

### The anchor problem (this matters for the screen, not just DQ)

The event scores DQ's **−0.289 excess drawdown vs the *Technology* sector median**. DQ is not a technology or semiconductor company — it makes solar-grade polysilicon, and its price driver is China PV spot, not semi capex or AI. The excess drawdown is a **GICS taxonomy artifact**, not dispersion inside a shared-driver cohort. Per the mispricing-artifact doctrine, an edge is only real against a *same-event external* anchor.

The correct anchor exists and cuts the other way: **688303.SS carries a market cap of ~$5.71B (2026-08-21)**. DQ's 72.8% look-through = **~$4.16B**, versus DQ's own ~$0.92–0.96B — DQ trades at **~22% of its stake's A-share value**. That ~78% gap has persisted since the 2021 STAR listing, through every polysilicon cycle. The likelier read is not "the ADR is mispriced" but "**the A-share is expensive**" — a loss-making producer at ~1.0x book on policy hope. The ADR is arguably the rational price.
