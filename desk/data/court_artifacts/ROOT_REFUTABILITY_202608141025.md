## Root, Inc. (ROOT) — refutability triage

**PRINT PROXIMITY: 2026-11-04 (Q3'26) — yfinance-derived, UNCONFIRMED; no company PR names it and no 8-K in the EDGAR list schedules it. Prior-year Q3 landed 2025-11-05, so the date is plausible. That is ~57 trading days out, well beyond the 5-day window, so no pre-print reconstruction is triggered. The relevant print already happened: Q2'26 reported 2026-08-05 (7 trading days ago) and the stock fell ~10% on it.**

**CAUSE-CHECK (run first, per template).** This is not a sector event — it is idiosyncratic and fully identified. ROOT ran to a $104.47 52w high on a hard-market growth story, then the personal-auto cycle turned soft: incumbents cut price and raised ad spend. Root's response was to switch growth off. Q2'26 gross written premium **fell 1.9% YoY to $339.7M** while policies in force rose 6.2% to 483,921, and management guided year-end 2026 PIF to "relatively flat" vs ~482,000 at end-2025 — i.e. roughly zero net adds for the rest of the year ([Q2'26 shareholder letter, EX-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1788882/000178888226000060/q22026shareholderletter.htm)). A growth multiple met a management-guided growth stop. The -52% dd52 has a known, disclosed cause.

**The decisive thing the headlines missed.** Every summary read "net income +15%, combined ratio improved to 92.1%, profitable underwriting." The composition inverts that framing: sales & marketing fell $11.8M YoY ($37.1M → $25.3M), worth **3.2 pts of gross earned premium**, while the **gross loss ratio deteriorated 70bps (58.0% → 58.7%)**. The gross combined ratio improved 2.4 pts (94.3% → 91.9%). The marketing cut more than fully explains the improvement; underwriting went backwards. And that lever is spent — it cannot repeat as a YoY tailwind past Q2'27, and cutting further shrinks PIF.

Why the class is DAMAGE-ARRIVING rather than either endpoint, stated as the mechanism a court has to break: Q2's 92.1% net CR is earned on premium written mostly at *older, higher* rates. The rate reductions written in H1'26 earn through over H2'26–H1'27 against a loss ratio that is already drifting the wrong way. That is a mechanical headwind with a known lag and no offsetting expense lever remaining. The question is not whether it bites but by how much.

**COURT-WORTHY (damage-absent, ranked):**
None qualify as DAMAGE-ABSENT. ROOT is the sole member and it classifies DAMAGE-ARRIVING — the narrative's predicted topline damage is in the printed numbers and in management's own guide, so it cannot be scored as absent. It still escalates on information value, for the reason below.

**COURT-WORTHINESS ROOT: 7/10** — a court changes the sizing decision because the whole name reduces to one bounded, publicly-reconstructible question (does the H1-written −7.6% rate give-back push the 2027 earned combined ratio through 100?), and the answer flips ROOT between "~7.9x a durable ~30% ROE with a live buyback" and "a value trap re-rating toward book."

Supporting the score, for whoever picks up the court:

- **The hinge is bounded and the evidence is public pre-print.** State rate filings (SERFF) show exactly what rate Root has filed and taken by state — testing management's claim of ~3% remaining national pricing flexibility, which is an assertion, not a fact. Progressive reports combined ratio and PIF **monthly**, giving a real-time read on how deep the soft market goes. Industry frequency/severity trend is public. A court can resolve this without waiting for Nov 4.
- **Valuation sits exactly on the hinge.** Q2 annualized net income $101.6M against a ~$780–850M market cap (15.7M basic shares × $49.83; the pack's $797.9M implies a slightly different count/date) = **~7.9x annualized**. Stockholders' equity $327.9M vs $284.3M at 12/31/25 → **~2.4x book on ~31% annualized ROE**. That combination is only cheap if the CR holds. Note this is *annualized-Q2*, not TTM — I did not rebuild Q3'25–Q1'26.
- **Capital return is net-shrinking the count.** >$20M repurchased in Q2 against a $75M authorization, versus a diluted-to-basic gap of 17.1M vs 15.7M shares. Debt was refinanced into a $200M Huntington term loan at lower cost. A no-growth ROOT that buys back 2.5% of the cap per quarter is a genuinely different outcome from a melting one.
- **Two real bull options the tape may be discarding:** the next-gen predictive pricing model launches Q4'26 (prior model reportedly lifted LTV >20%; benefit lands in 2027), and the low-CAC channel mix shift is already happening — partnership/IA at 51% of new writings, Carvana past 200k cumulative policies, New Jersey taking the footprint to 37 states / >80% of US population.
- **What holds the score off 9–10:** the growth stop is management-disclosed, so there is no asymmetry there; there is no catalyst to race; and 2027 loss trend is genuinely hard to pin — a court could legitimately return "unresolvable until Q3/Q4 earn-in."

Two caveats on my own numbers, so the court does not inherit them as facts. GWP/PIF is a *proxy* for written rate, not a clean one — it is contaminated by new-vs-renewal mix, channel mix, and 6- vs 12-month policy terms, and some of the −7.6% is mix rather than rate. Separately, net premiums earned ($363.5M) are now ~99% of gross earned ($368.1M), meaning Root retains nearly all risk with little quota-share cushion; whether that leaves adequate statutory capacity is a court question I could not answer, because GAAP consolidated equity is not statutory surplus and I did not pull the subsidiary statutory filings.
