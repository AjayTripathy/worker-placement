JBGS never owned Wardman Tower; a subsidiary acted under a project-management agreement, and substantial completion predated JBGS's 2017 formation. If liability is apportioned, the equity math changes by multiples.
2. **The treble** — base damages were $118.7M, trebled under the DC CPPA. Stripping the treble on appeal is a distinct and more winnable question than overturning liability.
3. **Bond mechanics and covenant headroom** — [DC Super. Ct. Civ. R. 62](https://www.dccourts.gov/sites/default/files/rules-superior-court/DR%20Rule%2062.%20Stay%20of%20Proceedings%20to%20Enforce%20a%20Judgment.pdf) defaults to full judgment plus costs and interest, *but expressly lets the court "fix a different amount or order security other than the bond … for good cause."* Against $74.8M cash and a $526.2M revolver that is drawable only "subject to covenants," this is the binding near-term liquidity question — and it is the one the company itself flagged as potentially impacting liquidity.

Counterweight the court must price: even a clean legal outcome leaves a 12.4x-levered REIT with negative SSNOI and an uncovered dividend. The litigation is necessary but not sufficient for a re-rate.
