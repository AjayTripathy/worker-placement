## 1. MODE B FIRST — the decisive question, derived without the memo's framing

The triage's frame is "backlog duration is compressing, therefore agentic seat displacement is arriving." I set that aside and asked instead: *if seat displacement were happening at Workday, which disclosed number would break first, and can I still see it?*

The answer is the second one. **The two metrics that would show seat-level and duration-level damage were both removed from Workday's disclosure in the same filing, and a third caveat was removed one quarter ago — each removal landing exactly as the number it governs turned down.** That is the finding of this court. It is not a re-detection of disclosed bad news; it is a narrowing of the aperture through which the bad news would be visible.

**Why it is the load-bearing metric.** With 12-month, 24-month and total you can decompose backlog into three duration buckets. With only 12-month and total you cannot see the 12-to-24-month bucket — and that is the *first* bucket in which duration compression appears, because a customer shortening a 3-year renewal to 2 years moves dollars out of the far bucket before it touches the near one. Removing the 24-month figure removed the only outside-visible early-warning on exactly the mechanism the bear case alleges. While it was still disclosed, the decomposition ran:

### 1b. Net retention was retired in the same filing — and replaced with a metric that is blind to seats

| Filing | Net retention | Gross retention |
|---|---|---|
| FY23 10-K | "gross and net retention rates **over 95% and over 100%**, respectively" | same sentence |
| FY24 10-K | "gross and net retention rates **over 95% and over 100%**, respectively" | same sentence |
| FY25 10-K | **absent — zero occurrences of "net retention"** | formalised: **"gross revenue retention rate of approximately 98%"** |
| FY26 10-K | **absent — zero occurrences of "net retention"** | **"gross revenue retention rate of approximately 97%"** |

- Claim: *net retention disclosure was dropped and gross-only substituted.* | Source: FY23/FY24/FY25/FY26 10-K full-text scans, URLs above | Result: "net retention" count 1 / 1 / **0 / 0**; "gross revenue retention" count 0 / 0 / **5 / 5** | **Verdict: VERIFIED.**

**Why the substitute cannot fall when seats fall.** Workday's own definition, FY26 10-K: gross revenue retention *"takes into account recurring revenues lost to product or customer churn but **does not account for additional revenue earned from add-ons or net expansions, which include volume and price adjustments**."* Volume adjustment = worker count. **A customer that renews with 20% fewer workers registers 100% gross revenue retention.** The single retention metric Workday now discloses is, by construction, insensitive to the exact variable the agentic thesis predicts will fall. That is the definition of a masking channel, and it is not disclosed as a change in metric.

- Claim: *GRR excludes volume adjustments by Workday's own definition.* | Source: [FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781126000014/wday-20260131.htm), MD&A "Gross Revenue Retention Rate" | Result: exact quoted language above | **Verdict: VERIFIED.**
- Claim: *even the insensitive metric is falling.* | Source: same, FY25 vs FY26 10-K | Result: **98% → 97%**. On TTM subscription revenue of $9.13B, 1pt of gross churn ≈ **$91M/yr of outright customer loss** | **Verdict: VERIFIED.**

Paradox (closed Q3 FY26) and Sana (closed Q4 FY26) are **in** the Q1 FY27 backlog and **not** in the Q1 FY26 base. The y/y comparison the caveat existed to qualify — total backlog **+10.9%** — is the single most load-bearing number in the release, and the quarter in which that caveat became most material is the quarter it was removed. Organic total-backlog growth is therefore **below 10.9% and is not disclosed**.

- Claim: *organic total subscription revenue backlog growth is not disclosed anywhere.* | Source: Q1 FY27 EX-99.1 and [Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781126000026/wday-20260430.htm) full-text | Result: no organic backlog figure in either document | **Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE — scored as an open exposure, not a pass.**

**Mechanism, encoded.** Masking channel = *selective metric retirement* (three retirements, FY25 10-K × 2 and Q1 FY27 release × 1), each timed to the turn in the number it governs. Signal channel = the residual arithmetic that survives retirement — beyond-12-month backlog, derivable as total minus 12-month (§2). Signal-to-price latency = **long**, and Workday states the reason itself: FY26 10-K, *"a decline in new or renewed subscription contracts in any single quarter may not be reflected in our revenue results for that quarter but will negatively impact our revenue in future quarters."* Backlog leads revenue by roughly the contract term, ~3 years.

## STRONGEST KILL

**Workday deleted the two metrics that would show seat and duration damage, in the same filing, at the moment the damage began — and then deleted the caveat protecting the one number that survived.**

Not one omission but three, each dated to the turn it governs:

1. **24-month subscription revenue backlog** — disclosed FY23 and FY24 10-Ks, gone from the FY25 10-K (filed 2025-03-11) and the FY26 10-K. It is the only disclosure that isolates the 12-to-24-month bucket, which is where duration compression appears *first*. Two of three duration buckets have been dark ever since — and in the last period they were visible, the far bucket was compounding at 37%. It is now growing at 8.8%.
2. **Net retention rate** — disclosed "over 100%" in the FY23 and FY24 10-Ks, absent from FY25 and FY26, replaced by **gross** revenue retention, which by Workday's own written definition *"does not account for… volume and price adjustments."* Volume = workers. **The one retention metric Workday still publishes cannot fall when a customer renews with fewer seats.** That is not a disclosure choice that is neutral to the agentic thesis; it is precisely the substitution that makes the thesis untestable from outside. And even that metric slipped 98% → 97%.
3. **The acquisition caveat** — Q3 and Q4 FY26 both footnoted that backlog "includes the impact from the acquisitions of Paradox and Sana." Q1 FY27 dropped the note and does not mention Paradox once — in the quarter where those acquisitions are in the numerator, absent from the base, and attached to the release's most load-bearing number, total backlog **+10.9%**. Organic is lower and is disclosed nowhere.

Take the three together and the pattern is not deceleration — deceleration is disclosed and would be clean. It is that **the aperture through which an outsider could measure the deceleration has been narrowed three times in eighteen months, always in the direction that flatters, and never announced as a change in metric.** The marketed takeaway ("subscription revenue accelerating to 15.7%, margin raised to 30.5%, retention 97%") diverges from the data the company has stopped printing. That is takeaway-versus-data divergence, and it is the catch.

**Compounding it into a sizing decision:** you are being offered this at a 13-week high after a **+56.7%** six-week rally, 13 days before an unscheduled print, with implied vol at the **96th percentile** of the year. The dislocation the cohort screen flagged was priced between September 2025 and April 2026 — a −56% drawdown — and has been un-priced since June. There is no distressed entry left to take.

**What this kill is NOT.** It is not a claim that the business is breaking. Subscription revenue grew 14.3% last quarter, gross retention is 97%, backlog coverage of revenue is identical to two years ago, free cash flow is $2.8B and growing 27%, the share count is shrinking 5.9% a year, and 13–15x EV/FCF is a defensible multiple. **A long here fails on the setup and the disclosure integrity, not on the fundamentals.** I decline to dress a fair business in a broken one's clothes.

1. **Any one of the three retired disclosures is restored** at the Q2 FY27 print or the FY27 10-K — the 24-month backlog, net revenue retention, or an organic (ex-Paradox/Sana) backlog growth figure. Reinstating a metric published as recently as the FY24 10-K costs management nothing if the underlying is fine. Restoration voids the masking finding and I withdraw it in full; this is the single highest-information event available.
2. **Total subscription revenue backlog growth ≥13%** (≥$28.67B) at Q2 FY27, with the beyond-12-month bucket ≥12%. That breaks a four-quarter monotonic decay and refutes §2 outright.
3. **A price that restores the dislocation.** The whole setup objection is entry, not thesis. Back near the **$110–125** double-bottom, at 2.5–2.8x EV/revenue and ~9x EV/FCF, the same facts support a long: the disclosure narrowing is a discount-rate argument, not a solvency one, and the fundamentals in §3 and §7 are sound. **This is a price gate, not a business-risk gate** — and per the response taxonomy, valuation findings get a price gate and nothing else.
4. **FY27 subscription revenue guidance raised above $9.95B on organic strength**, with the margin guide *held* rather than raised — i.e. the company spending the upside on growth instead of converting a growth miss into a margin beat for the sixth consecutive time.
