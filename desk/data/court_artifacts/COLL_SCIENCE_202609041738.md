## SCIENCE BRIEF — COLL

**The technical question the case turns on:** for each of four formulation-IP franchises (a wax-matrix abuse-deterrent oxycodone, a pH-buffered bilayer buccal buprenorphine film, a pH-triggered delayed+extended-release methylphenidate, and a serdexmethylphenidate prodrug), does the *formulation* patent estate actually block a substitutable copy, and for how long — because in every case the molecule is old, off-patent, and the entire exclusivity rests on delivery-system claims and on private settlement dates that are not visible in the Orange Book.

**Correction to the upstream artifact, stated up front (details in §1 and §5):** the thesis treats BELBUCA as "28% of revenue re-dates 2032→2027 if [the '539 patent] falls." Two primary documents contradict the framing. (i) BDSI granted Teva a license to launch generic BELBUCA **on January 23, 2027** under a February 6, 2018 settlement — that date is already contracted and does not depend on any 2027 trial ([BDSI 8-K Ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001103021/000119312518033365/d533530dex991.htm)). (ii) The April 12, 2027 trial is an **infringement** trial over Alvogen's *reformulated* film, not a validity trial — '539 validity was already tried and upheld ([10-Q Note, Commitments & Contingencies](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1267565/000162828026053851/R23.htm)). Separately, the thesis's "~75% of revenue sits behind 2030-2037 patents" is inconsistent with the Orange Book: **every** JORNAY PM listed patent expires 2032-03-23, not 2037 ([OB, NDA 209311](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/ob/patent_info.cfm?Product_No=001&Appl_No=209311&Appl_type=N)).

## 2. THE MECHANISM

**Established science underneath all four franchises is old and unprotectable.** Oxycodone (1916), buprenorphine (initial US approval **1981**, per the BELBUCA label), tapentadol, and methylphenidate are all long off-patent as molecules. Nothing Collegium owns is a novel pharmacology. Every dollar of exclusivity is a *delivery-system* claim, and delivery-system claims are the class of patent most vulnerable to design-around, because the accused party only has to move a formulation parameter outside a numeric range while keeping the drug bioequivalent.

**XTAMPZA ER — abuse deterrence by physical chemistry, not by antagonist.** Oxycodone base is combined with myristic acid to form a fatty-acid salt (oxycodone myristate) and embedded in a hydrophobic wax matrix of beeswax, carnauba wax and stearoyl polyoxyl-32 glycerides (label §11). The deterrence mechanism is that milling or chewing the microspheres does not liberate a burst of freely soluble drug, and the lipophilic salt resists aqueous extraction. The label's data support that: crushing produces essentially no C_max rise (57.6 vs 62.3 ng/mL), where crushed IR oxycodone reaches 79.4; intranasally, crushed XTAMPZA gives C_max 29.8 vs 60.9 for crushed IR (§9.2, Tables 4–5). Human abuse-potential studies show statistically lower drug liking by both oral-chewed and intranasal routes. **The ceiling is explicit in the label**: this approach cannot deter the dominant abuse route — swallowing intact — and the label says abuse by injection, oral and nasal routes "is still possible." FDA's own ADF page says the same about the whole class. The historical failure mode of this class is not chemical, it is *epidemiological and commercial*: FDA has required post-market studies precisely because real-world abuse reduction has never been demonstrated, and payers have generally not paid a premium for the property. That failure mode is **class-inherent**, not implementation-specific.

A second, implementation-specific property cuts both ways: XTAMPZA is **not bioequivalent** to oxycodone ER tablets and has a food effect large enough that the label instructs consistent dosing with food (§12.3). That makes it non-substitutable at the pharmacy counter for OxyContin or its generics — a defense against generic-OxyContin price pressure — but it equally means an ANDA filer must match XTAMPZA's own unusual PK envelope, which is a genuine barrier to a copy.

**BELBUCA — a pH-gradient bilayer film, and the pH range *is* the patent.** BEMA is a bi-layer: a mucoadhesive layer carrying buprenorphine HCl and a backing layer, with the buffer excipients (citric acid, monobasic sodium phosphate, sodium hydroxide) setting the pH of each layer. Buprenorphine has pKa 8.5 (amine) and 10.0 (phenol); the buffered microenvironment governs the ionised/un-ionised ratio at the mucosal interface and therefore transmucosal flux. That is why the label reports absolute bioavailability of **46–65%** for a drug with poor oral bioavailability, and why drinking liquid during administration — especially low-pH cola — cuts exposure by 23–37%.

The '539 claim reads directly on that chemistry: mucoadhesive layer buffered to **pH 4.0–6.0**, backing layer buffered to **pH 4.0–4.8**. Alvogen's disclosed strategy is to change the **backing-layer pH** to fall outside 4.0–4.8 while remaining bioequivalent. This is the classic numeric-range design-around, and it is exactly what the April 12, 2027 trial adjudicates. Note two structural features benches should not get wrong: (i) claim 1 is a **method-of-treatment** claim, so the theory against a generic runs through induced infringement via the label, and the labeled indication is the only indication — a "skinny label" carve-out is not obviously available; (ii) claim 1 also carries a **steady-state C_max limitation of 0.156–0.364 ng/mL**, a numeric window that does not self-evidently span the marketed 75–900 mcg q12h dose ladder (see §4).

The pharmacology that actually drives BELBUCA's clinical position — partial mu agonism with a ceiling on respiratory depression, kappa antagonism, and **Schedule III** rather than II status — is a property of the *molecule*, not of Collegium's formulation. A generic buccal film inherits all of it. The moat is the film, not the ceiling effect.

**JORNAY PM — a timer, not a new drug.** DELEXIS is two coats over a methylphenidate core: an outer pH-responsive methacrylic acid copolymer Type B layer that suppresses release through the acidic stomach and the proximal small bowel, and an inner ethylcellulose extended-release layer. The result is the label's defining PK: ≤5% of drug available in the first 10 hours, median T_max **14.0 hours**. Dosed at 6:30–9:30 pm, the concentration peak lands on the next morning — the clinical problem it solves is pre-school/pre-work coverage before a morning dose could act. The known failure mode of pH-triggered enteric coats is variable gastric emptying and alcohol-induced coat failure; the label documents the latter directly (97% released in 2 h in 40% alcohol in vitro, **no in vivo study**) and instructs alcohol avoidance. The moat is 16 Orange Book patents all expiring **2032-03-23**.

**AZSTARYS — a prodrug timer.** Serdexmethylphenidate is d-methylphenidate conjugated so that conversion occurs distally in the GI tract, producing a delayed second exposure phase alongside the co-formulated immediate-release d-MPH. The prodrug is a genuine new chemical entity (NCE exclusivity ran to 2026-05-07), which is why its patent estate reaches 2037 while JORNAY's does not — this is the one franchise where the exclusivity rests on a molecule rather than a coating.

**NUCYNTA — the completed case.** Tapentadol's dual mu-agonist/norepinephrine-reuptake-inhibitor mechanism is real but is not protected in the IR presentation: the Orange Book lists **no patents at all** for NDA 022304. That, not any formulation failure, is why a third-party ANDA (Humanwell) was approved 2026-01-27 and why Collegium pre-empted with a Hikma AG. NUCYNTA ER retains Grünenthal patents to 2028/2029 — patents Collegium does not own, and whose licensor is now suing over the AG.

## 3. THE FIELD (as of 2026-09-04)

**Long-acting branded opioids.** FDA recognises exactly four branded ADF opioids: OxyContin (now Knoa Pharma, the Purdue successor substituted as plaintiff in July 2026), Hysingla ER, XTAMPZA ER, and RoxyBond, plus one ADF generic (hydrocodone bitartrate ER). This is a shrinking, litigated category with no new entrants; management's own characterisation is "pressure across the branded, long-acting opioid market." Demand-side: BELBUCA's growth (+9.6% Q2, formulary access for "an additional 9 million lives starting in Q4" per the call) is the one place the pain portfolio is expanding, and the driver is buprenorphine's Schedule III / ceiling-effect profile in a prescribing environment that penalises Schedule II full agonists — a *molecule* advantage a generic film will share from January 2027.

**Buccal buprenorphine specifically.** Three challengers are on record: Teva (settled, licensed 2027-01-23), Alvogen (original ANDA enjoined to Dec 2032; reformulated product now in a fresh infringement case, trial 2027-04-12), and Ascent (P-IV notice 2026-06-04, 30-month stay running). A fourth application exists — FDA issued a **tentative approval** letter for a buprenorphine buccal film under application 211594 in 2021 — but I could not read the applicant off the letter (see §6). No generic buccal buprenorphine film appears on FDA's first-generic list.

**ADHD — the field moved materially in the last four months, in both directions.**
- **Simtriyo (centanafadine), Otsuka — FDA approved 2026-07-24** for ADHD in **adults and pediatric patients ≥6 years weighing ≥20 kg**. Otsuka's own release describes it as "a first-in-class norepinephrine, dopamine, serotonin reuptake inhibitor (NDSRI) and CNS stimulant," supported by four Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials — adults NCT03605680 and NCT03605836 (AISRS), pediatric/adolescent NCT05428033 and NCT05257265 (ADHD-RS-5). Launch is pending DEA scheduling, "expected to be available later this year." Otsuka carries a large existing CNS commercial organisation. *Note a live source conflict: several trade outlets characterised Simtriyo as a nonstimulant; Otsuka's own release calls it a CNS stimulant pending DEA scheduling. Both are shown; the sponsor document governs.* ([otsuka-us.com](https://www.otsuka-us.com/otsuka-shares-fda-review-update-for-centanafadine); [fiercepharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-approves-otsuka-adhd-drug-simtriyo-teeing-its-next-major-cns-launch))
- **CTx-1301 (Cingulate) received a Complete Response Letter on 2026-06-01** against a 2026-05-31 PDUFA date, following a February 2026 inspection of its Gainesville, GA CDMO. This removes, for now, the one competitor built on the same premise as JORNAY PM — engineered dose timing across the day. Primary is Cingulate's 8-K; I read this from press coverage of it rather than the filing itself ([Psychiatric Times](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/fda-issues-crl-for-ctx-1301-for-treatment-of-adhd); [Cingulate NDA acceptance PR](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/10/14/3166151/0/en/fda-accepts-cingulate-s-new-drug-application-for-ctx-1301-in-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd-and-sets-a-may-31-2026-pdufa-date.html)).
- **Onyda XR (Tris)**, ER clonidine oral suspension, is the first liquid nonstimulant and the only nonstimulant with nighttime dosing — the closest thing to a direct claim on JORNAY PM's "dose at night, work in the morning" positioning, though in a different (nonstimulant, lower-efficacy) tier.
- **Supply side:** DEA's 2026 aggregate production quota rulemaking cites ~6.74% growth in 2024 medical use of Schedule II stimulants and projects ~8.94% for 2026 — the largest quota increase in a decade, with supply/demand equilibrium projected for late 2026–2027. Both JORNAY PM and AZSTARYS are Schedule II and therefore quota-constrained; Simtriyo's scheduling is not yet determined.

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS

1. **The thesis mixes a six-month growth rate against a quarterly decline rate.** "Xtampza −14.5% / Jornay +38.9%" pairs XTAMPZA's **Q2** decline (−14.4%) with JORNAY's **six-month** growth (+38.9%). Matched: Q2 is −14.4% vs +41.2%; six-month is −4.5% vs +38.9%. Any bench arguing about deceleration must pick one basis. ([R9.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1267565/000162828026053851/R9.htm))
2. **"Nucynta is 25.1% of revenue" is not the Q2 mix.** On Q2 2026 filed revenue the franchise (IR + ER + both AGs) is 35,215/199,878 = **17.6%**; BELBUCA is **28.9%**, JORNAY 23.0%, XTAMPZA 22.5%, AZSTARYS 6.5% on a ~7-week stub. A trailing-twelve-month basis will give a different, higher Nucynta share. State the basis.
3. **AZSTARYS's \$12.9M is ~7 weeks (May 12 – June 30), not a quarter.** Annualising it is wrong in both directions; the company's own FY guide is \$65–75M.
4. **AG revenue is not comparable to branded revenue.** The \$5.1M of AG revenue is a blend of product-material sale plus an *estimated* profit share, on a product netting ~10–15% (IR) / ~20–25% (ER) of branded net price. Unit erosion and revenue erosion are therefore not the same number, and the "estimated" profit share is a management accrual subject to true-up.
5. **The XTAMPZA −14% contains a ~\$2.4M prior-year rebate-timing benefit** — roughly 4.6 points of the 14.4-point decline is comparison artifact, not current-period demand. Neither the filing nor the call decomposes the remainder into volume vs net price.
6. **Gross-to-net regimes differ by product and are not disclosed per product** (only JORNAY PM's "mid-60%" was given). Comparing net-revenue trends across BELBUCA, XTAMPZA and JORNAY is comparing three different GTN regimes, not three demand curves. The six-month GTN rollforward is further distorted by \$45.9M of rebate liability *assumed in the acquisition* and by prior-period adjustments of −\$3.7M (rebates) and −\$6.4M (returns). ([R9.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1267565/000162828026053851/R9.htm))
7. **Orange Book expiry ≠ loss of exclusivity.** BELBUCA's OB date is 2032-12-21; the operative date is the **2027-01-23** contractual Teva entry. XTAMPZA's OB date is 2036-09-02; the operative date is **2033-09-02**. Both operative dates are in settlement agreements, not the Orange Book. Any patent-cliff model built off Orange Book dates alone will be wrong on the two largest pain products.
8. **The '539 C_max limitation and the label PK are different estimands.** Claim 1 recites a **steady-state** C_max window of 0.156–0.364 ng/mL. The label reports **single-dose** C_max (0.17 ng/mL at 75 mcg, 0.47 at 300 mcg, 1.43 at 1200 mcg) and states only that steady state is reached before the 6th dose with dose-proportional C_max — it gives no per-strength steady-state C_max table. Whether the claimed window covers the whole marketed 75–900 mcg q12h ladder therefore **cannot be resolved from the label**, and a bench should not assert it either way. (Claim 9, the second independent claim, was not retrieved.)
9. **Drug-liking scores are not cross-comparable between products.** XTAMPZA's intranasal E_max of 61.81 was measured against **crushed IR oxycodone** (82.72) in a 39-subject study with that study's own subject pool and dose equivalence. It is not comparable to OxyContin's or RoxyBond's published liking scores from different studies with different comparators, doses and populations.
10. **JORNAY PM dose is not exposure.** At an identical 54 mg dose, C_max is 11.6 (children), 7.2 (adolescents), **6.0 ng/mL (adults)**. Any argument about adult uptake that reasons from mg-dose or from pediatric trial results without the ~2x exposure gap is mismatched.
11. **TRx growth ≠ revenue growth.** JORNAY PM TRx +13.1% against revenue +41.2% in the same quarter. The gap is price/mix/GTN/channel and possibly inventory, not demand — and the same wedge, inverted, may be operating on XTAMPZA.
12. **Simtriyo's Phase 3 endpoints (AISRS in adults, ADHD-RS-5 in children) are not the endpoints in JORNAY PM's trials (SKAMP analog-classroom, ADHD-RS-IV, PREMB-R AM).** Cross-trial effect-size comparison between these products is not supportable; JORNAY's differentiating endpoints are explicitly *early-morning* measures with no counterpart in the Simtriyo program.

## 6. WHAT COULD NOT BE FOUND

1. **The volume-vs-net-price decomposition of XTAMPZA ER's decline** — the explicit question the upstream thesis asked me to settle. The 10-Q's MD&A revenue discussion did not render through the fetch tool (the document truncates before Item 2), and neither the press release nor the call gives the split beyond "branded LA opioid market pressure" plus the \$2.4M prior-year rebate-timing item. **Resolving instruments, in order of accessibility:** (a) the full 10-Q MD&A "Product revenues, net" narrative, which is public and normally states volume vs price; (b) **CMS Medicaid State Drug Utilization Data** (data.medicaid.gov), which publishes *units reimbursed* per NDC per quarter and is a free, primary, unit-level series — it lags roughly two quarters; (c) CMS Medicare Part D spending dashboards for dosage-unit and per-unit-cost trends; (d) IQVIA/Symphony TRx (vendor, paid). Until one of those is pulled, "rebate pressure vs volume loss" is **UNVERIFIABLE**, and no bench should build a kill on either reading.
2. **Whether Teva's BELBUCA ANDA holds FDA final approval.** Without it the 2027-01-23 licensed date is permissive, not operative. Resolved by the Drugs@FDA ANDA record for buprenorphine buccal film, or by FDA's first-generic list (which shows no such entry as of its 2026-09-01 update).
3. **The applicant behind application 211594** (buprenorphine buccal film, tentative approval 2021) and the basis for its tentative status. The FDA approval letter PDF has broken font embedding and could not be read; the Drugs@FDA overview page did not return record data. Resolved by the Drugs@FDA application record or the FOIA'd approval letter. Circumstantially the BDSI complaint named Chemo Research S.L., Insud Pharma and IntelGenx as buccal-film challengers — but I did not confirm that link and am not asserting it.
4. **Alvogen's reformulated product's own regulatory status** — whether it is a supplement to the enjoined ANDA or a new application, and whether it has tentative or final approval. Resolved by the Drugs@FDA ANDA record and the D. Del. docket.
5. **Claim 9 of US 9,901,539** (the second independent claim) and the prosecution history bearing on the backing-layer pH range. Resolved by the patent full text and the USPTO Patent Center file wrapper — this is the single most decisive unexamined document for the 2027-04-12 trial.
6. **The identity and terms of the asserted BELBUCA authorized-generic agreement.**
7. **Whether any Paragraph IV notice has been served on JORNAY PM or AZSTARYS.** The Q2 2026 contingencies note discloses none; absence in that note is evidence, but a notice served after 2026-08-06 would not yet appear. Resolved by FDA's Paragraph IV certifications list and by subsequent 8-Ks.
8. **Real-world abuse/overdose outcomes for XTAMPZA ER.** FDA's own ADF page — which I note is **last updated 2021-03-08 and is therefore stale as a statement of current agency thinking** — says the agency is still "prioritizing the need for data." Collegium's FDA-required post-marketing ADF studies were not located. Resolved by the FDA post-marketing requirements database and by the published Category 4 epidemiologic study reports.

If an adult trial exists outside the label, it would appear on ClinicalTrials.gov under the Ironshore sponsor record — not located.
