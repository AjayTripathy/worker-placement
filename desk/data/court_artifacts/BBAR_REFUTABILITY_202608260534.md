### Print-decisive reconstruction

The loudest bull claim is management's own dated pre-commitment on the Q1 call: NPLs *"stabilize during Q2,"* falling to ~5% or below by year-end. That claim is testable **before the print** from public BCRA data, because BBAR's deterioration is explicitly retail-card/consumer driven (commercial NPL is only 0.50%).

System household delinquency: **12.1% (April) → 12.7% (May 2026)** — the **19th consecutive monthly increase**, up from 2.5% at end-2024; personal loans 14.9% in April; non-bank fintech/wallet delinquency >32% ([Buenos Aires Herald on BCRA data](https://buenosairesherald.com/economics/household-debt-delinquency-hits-record-near-13-in-argentina); [BCRA Banking Report](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/en/publicaciones/banking-report-january-2026/)).

So through **two of Q2's three months, the consumer cycle had not stabilized** — it was still making new highs. BCRA VP Werning's "delinquency peaked in the second quarter" is a forward policymaker assertion, and even taken at face value it concerns the *rate of change*, not the level. **Pre-print read: BBAR's Q2 NPL most likely prints above 5.60%, contradicting the stabilization guide.**

The subtlety that keeps this from being an outright short: NPL is a *stock* and lags; the P&L driver is *cost of risk*, which already improved 8.11%→6.14% on tightened origination. Rising NPL with falling CoR is the bull path. **The single line that decides it: NPL × coverage.** Coverage is already 88.41% (below 100%). If NPL rises toward ~6% and they hold coverage, they must build reserves and the P&L takes it; if coverage slips further, that's a quality-of-earnings flag, not a beat.

**Bond-dominance floor (the structural gate):** equity earnings yield = ROE/(P/B) = **6.5% trailing / 7.5% on Q1-annualized** vs Argentine sovereign hard-currency yield of **~9.1%** (490bp + UST). The equity currently pays you *less than the sovereign* in the same jurisdiction, before bank credit and FX risk. Only the low-teens guide (→ ~11–12%) clears the floor — and Q1 delivered 8.3%. The entire long case rests on a guide the public consumer data is currently contradicting. For an ADR holder the peso pinned at the band ceiling is a direct USD-return headwind regardless of peso earnings.
