## BLUE BENCH — NET: **RED CASE UPHELD**

Upheld on the conclusion, **rebuilt on the reasoning, and strengthened on the bear side.** My assigned lead attack — the SBC double-count — **fails outright**: reconstructing owner earnings from actual share issuance and cash withholding, a wholly independent route, produces **$629.0M of true economic comp cost against red's $618.1M charge**, i.e. red was *lenient*, and the honest multiple is **20.2× / 4.94%**, not better than red's 19.8× / 5.05%. Red's central kill — *~20× owner earnings for a business whose acceleration is arithmetically capped at ~1pp over four years* — stands, and I could not move it.

What red got wrong does not rescue the long. The IAM "no price uplift" mechanism is **refuted** (+67%/+78% list, 5× minimum-contract floor), the PEG is a **units error** (1.50, not 2.67), the flip gate is **mis-calibrated 3:1 against the long** with **condition #4 already satisfied and unnoticed**, and red missed a genuine positive retention disclosure. But each of these repairs the *argument*, not the *price*: at $57.39 the market already pays for 4.82% perpetual growth, probability-weighted fair value is **+4.7%**, and red's own arithmetic ceiling on acceleration is the binding constraint.

Meanwhile the single most important thing either bench found is **against** the long, and neither bench had it before today: **Docusign deleted its IAM moat claim and added an LLM/homegrown-substitution risk paragraph in the same 10-K.** That raises terminal-value uncertainty precisely where the entire valuation lives — and there is **no quantified offset**, because "Docusign Identify" appears nowhere in the FY26 10-K and "Notary" appears once.

But the terminal risk must be named correctly, and here I also correct **my own** framing as well as red's. The **platform-bundling** version of the thesis — the one the cohort tag encodes — is **refuted on dated primary evidence**: Microsoft is not a bundler but a **distribution channel that ships Docusign inside SharePoint at $0 Microsoft charge** while metering its own simple-signature service at $2.00/request, gross margin is **flat at 79.4%** with the non-GAAP drift attributed to IAM hosting rather than discounting, and the only third-party "share decline" series is a **crawler-coverage artifact** I excluded rather than cite. The real, narrower exposure is **Adobe's unlimited-transaction bundle in Acrobat Standard/Pro** at the low end — bounded by the fact that KBA and phone authentication are withheld from that bundle, which is exactly Docusign's AES/QES and identity ground. So: **near-term damage is verifiably ABSENT; the open question is long-dated AI substitution, which the issuer itself now flags and nobody can size.**

Red's classification of **RP_FAIR / no overlay** is therefore close but slightly too comfortable. The correct label is **RP_UNCERTAIN: fairly priced, not impaired, against a terminal value the issuer has just told us is less defensible than it said a year ago — with no disclosed adjacency to offset it.**
