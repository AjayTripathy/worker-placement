## 0. Verdict up front

The triage's arithmetic is the whole bull case, and **the arithmetic is wrong**. Annualizing a
seasonal-peak quarter ($76.4M × 4 = $305.6M) against a company whose recast Q1-2026 continuing
Adjusted EBITDA was **$16.4M** overstates the run-rate by ~58%. The real FY26 figure is **~$193M**.
That converts "sub-2× EV/EBITDA" into ~2.8–3.3× — and on EBITDA less stock comp less capex, into
**7–8×** on a number falling ~35% per year.

Everything else in this document supports or bounds that finding. Tripadvisor is not dishonest —
every adverse datum is disclosed, several of them by management in plain language. It is simply
**cheaper-looking than it is, in a business melting faster than the multiple implies.**

**RECOMMEND: REJECT the long. No position. Re-look at the Q3-2026 print on one named metric.**

**Second finding, added on revision and co-equal in weight (§2.3a).** The "growth asset" has never
earned a real margin. On the basis it was **originally reported** — charged for the Tripadvisor
traffic it consumed — the experiences business earned **$0M of Adjusted EBITDA in FY2023 and $33M
(3.9%) in FY2024**. A 2025 segment recast removed a **~$135M/yr internal traffic charge** and lifted
the identical FY2024 revenue to **$79.1M (9.4%)** with **consolidated EBITDA unchanged**. And the
free traffic that justified dropping the charge is the very thing AI overviews are destroying — so
the margin is now being repaid in cash marketing spend. **The two segment declines are one
mechanism, not two.**

## 1. Mode B — first principles, no promoter framing

Before touching the pitch, the three questions that decide this name:

1. **Where does the profit actually come from?** Not from experiences. From the segment being
   abandoned.
2. **Is the decline cyclical or structural?** The company itself names the cause, and it is
   structural.
3. **Does the cash get to shareholders?** There is no authorization large enough for it to, and
   management's revealed preference is to buy high and not buy low.

All three resolve against the long. Detail follows.

### 2.3b The unifying mechanism (this is the whole thesis in one paragraph)

**Hotels & Other is not a separate melting asset sitting beside a growth asset. Hotels & Other *is*
the growth asset's demand input.** Brand Tripadvisor's free SEO traffic is what fed Viator; the 2025
recast simply stopped charging Experiences for it. Now AI overviews are destroying that free traffic
(#22–#24), so the demand must be bought in the open market instead — which is exactly, and only,
what the numbers show:

> Experiences marketing **56.2% → 61.3%** of segment revenue while revenue growth collapsed
> **13% → 3%**, at **$2.30 of incremental marketing per $1 of incremental revenue**.

And TRIP discloses this causal chain verbatim in the Q2-2026 10-Q:

> *"…declines in **free marketing channels, particularly SEO, and the resulting shift from free to
> paid marketing channels**."*
> *"Marketing costs increased… primarily driven by an increase in marketing costs in **Experiences,
> in order to drive revenue growth and increase market share**."*
> *"The decrease in adjusted EBITDA was primarily due to an **increase in paid marketing costs**."*

**So the two segment declines are one phenomenon, not two.** The recast front-loaded a margin whose
economic underpinning — free internal traffic — is being destroyed by the same force that is killing
Hotels & Other. Experiences is now giving that margin back in cash marketing spend, and will keep
giving it back for as long as the SEO channel decays. **This is why the melt cannot be modelled as a
declining annuity plus an independent growth stub — the stub is downstream of the annuity.**

## 5. Honesty-alpha verdict (takeaway-vs-data, not datum-disclosure)

**Revised from my first pass.** My initial read was "CLEAN on the numbers, mild framing flag." The
segment-recast finding (§2.3a) does not support that, and I am correcting it.

**On disclosure of adverse facts: TRIP is candid, and gets credit for it.** The AI-overview
causation, the segment EBITDA declines, the free→paid channel shift, the zero buyback, the
goodwill-impairment warning naming the specific reporting unit at risk, and the macro one-offs are
all disclosed in plain language. Per the standing doctrine (*re-detecting honestly disclosed bad
news is not alpha*), none of that earns an EXCLUDE.

**But there is a real takeaway-vs-data divergence, and it is structural, not rhetorical.** The
marketed takeaway is that experiences is a ~10%-margin business scaling toward operating leverage —
the stated basis for reorganising the entire company around it. The data underneath says the
**same revenue** produced **3.9%** on the basis it was originally reported, and that the entire
+5.5pp of "margin expansion" came from ceasing to charge the segment a **~$135M/yr internal traffic
fee**, with **consolidated EBITDA unchanged**. That is a **presentation-driven improvement presented
inside a strategy narrative about operational improvement.**

Two things make this ELEVATE-grade rather than boilerplate re-segmentation:
1. **The reallocation runs in the direction of the strategy being sold.** Profit moved *from* the
   segment being divested/wound down *to* the segment the equity story is built on, in the same
   quarter the company renamed itself "experiences-led."
2. **The economic basis for the reallocation is being destroyed as it is booked.** The internal
   charge was dropped because Tripadvisor traffic was treated as a shared asset — precisely as that
   traffic began collapsing under AI overviews. The margin was recognised on a subsidy that is
   disappearing, and the cash cost is now reappearing in the marketing line (§2.3b).

This is legitimate under GAAP segment reporting — segments follow how the CODM manages the business,
and nothing here is a misstatement. **The finding is not fraud; it is that the marketed takeaway
diverges from what the same company's own prior presentation of the same period showed.** That is
exactly the boundary the doctrine defines, and it is the reason this file's conviction is 8 rather
than 6.

**Secondary framing flag (mild).** The CEO's *"reshaping the Company around experiences – the
largest, most durable growth category in travel"* sits above a table showing Experiences at **12% of
H1 profit, −52% y/y, with a Q1 EBITDA loss**. Datum disclosed in the same release → **review_flag**,
not a masking finding.

### 5.1 Mechanism encoding — masking channel × signal channel × latency

**Masking channel #1 — *reader-side seasonal-peak annualization of an un-guided metric*.** With no
forward guidance (#54), the only anchor is the last printed quarter. Q2 is a **4.7× seasonal peak
over Q1** ($76.4M vs $16.4M). Annualizing it yields $305M against a true $193M — a **58%
overstatement** that converts ~2.8× into ~1.7×. Critically, **the issuer performs no manipulation**;
the mask is arithmetic performed *by the reader*, and it is enabled by the discontinued-ops recast,
which strips the historical quarterly base a reader would otherwise use to check seasonality.

**Masking channel #2 — Adjusted EBITDA with SBC at 38% of the metric and no buyback offset.** $71M/yr
of stock comp is added back while share count creeps **+4.3%/yr** with zero repurchase. Invisible in
EV/EBITDA; fatal in per-share terms.

**Masking channel #3 — a restructuring program landing inside the comparison.** $85M of savings
absorb ~40% of the gross decline, so the reported y/y understates the underlying melt.

**Masking channel #4 — *segment redefinition that reallocates profit toward the segment the equity
story is built on*, with consolidated EBITDA unchanged.** Removing a ~$135M/yr internal traffic
charge moved ~$46M of FY2024 EBITDA from the melting segment to the growth segment, lifting the
"growth" margin 3.9% → 9.4% on identical revenue (§2.3a). **This is the highest-value channel in
this file because it is invisible in every current-basis comparison** — the recast is applied to all
prior periods, so a reader looking only at the 10-K's own y/y tables can never see it. **It is
detectable only by diffing the current filing against the pre-recast release** (here: Q2-2026 8-K
vs. Q4-2024 8-K).

**Signal channel.** All three are defeated by **10-Q Note 13** (segment expense table: marketing by
segment) plus **the H1 column** (from which Q1 = H1 − Q2 is derivable). Neither the marketing ratios
nor the H1 segment split appears in the press-release highlights.

**Signal-to-price latency.** **~0 days** for the headline — the 8-K moved the stock −25.5% in the
same session. But **~1–2 quarters** for the segment-mix truth: the H1 Experiences −52%, the
88%-of-profit-from-Hotels fact, and the negative incremental marketing ROI all require Note 13,
filed the same day but absent from the narrative and still absent from the framing. **That latency
gap argues the stock is not yet finished de-rating — it does not argue it is a buy.**

### 5.2 Generalizable rule for the knowledge graph

> **When a company (a) omits or withdraws forward guidance and (b) simultaneously reclassifies a
> segment into discontinued operations, "annualize the last quarter" becomes maximally wrong** —
> because the recast removes the historical quarterly base a reader would use to sanity-check
> seasonality, while the missing guidance removes the alternative anchor. **Always derive
> Q1 = H1 − Q2 from the recast columns, and derive the H1-to-FY ratio from the prior year's recast
> segment totals, before annualizing anything.**

This is precisely the error that produced the 1.7× headline and the 7/10 court-worthiness score.
It generalizes to every post-divestiture stub in the deep-value and GARP verticals.

### 5.2b Third rule — **diff the segment definition, don't just read the recast**

> **When a company redefines reportable segments, always pull the LAST pre-recast earnings release
> and diff the same fiscal year on both bases.** A recast is applied retroactively to all prior
> periods, so a margin improvement created purely by reallocation is **invisible in every
> current-basis comparison** the company publishes — including its own y/y tables. The diagnostic is
> the **intersegment revenue/eliminations line**: if it collapses across the redefinition (here
> $(135)M → ~$(4)M), profit has moved between segments without any change in consolidated earnings.
> Then ask the decisive question: **does the reallocation flatter the segment the equity story is
> built on, and is the economic basis for it stable?** Here the answer was yes and no respectively —
> which is the finding.

**Recall-floor implication:** this check should fire automatically on any name where (a) segment
definitions changed within the last 8 quarters **and** (b) a segment-level margin is load-bearing in
the thesis. It would have fired on TRIP at triage and pre-empted the 7/10 score.

### 5.3 Second rule — activist-instrument conversion

> **An activist converting physical shares into cash-settled, non-convertible, fixed-maturity total
> return swaps immediately after signing a standstill is a DE-ESCALATION signal, not a continuation
> of pressure.** It surrenders votes, drops the 5% reporting threshold, frees capital, and — via the
> swap maturity — installs a **hard, dated terminal on the economic exposure**. Read the maturity
> date as the activist's own stated horizon. Here: **October 2027**, with a standstill covering
> everything until ~Q1-2027, and a blended cost basis (**~$12.4–13.4**) **above** the current price.
