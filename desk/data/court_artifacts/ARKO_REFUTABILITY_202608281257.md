## The structural finding (this is what a court should adjudicate, not the consumer narrative)

ARKO carved out and IPO'd **ARKO Petroleum Corp (Nasdaq: APC)** on 2026-02-13 at $18.00 ([closing PR](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/17/3239193/0/en/ARKO-Corp-and-ARKO-Petroleum-Corp-Announce-Closing-of-ARKO-Petroleum-Corp-s-Initial-Public-Offering.html)) and retains **35,000,000 Class B shares = 73.6% economic / 93.3% voting**.

- APC close 2026-08-27: **$18.50**, 47.57M shares, $880M cap → ARKO's stake marks at **$647.5M = $5.77/ARKO share**
- ARKO close 2026-08-27: **$4.46**, 112.19M shares → **$500.4M cap**
- **Implied stub for 1,057 c-stores + corporate: −$147M (−$1.31/share)**

Three things cut *against* trading this, and they're why the score isn't higher:

1. **The anchor is related-party, not external.** APC's earnings run partly through an exclusive long-term Fuel Distribution Agreement with ARKO retail at *rack + a fixed adder*. Per the mispricing-artifact rule, an edge is only real against a same-event **external** anchor — a controlled sub with contracted intercompany flow isn't one. (The fixed-adder structure does make APC's stream more contractual than discretionary, which is the strongest bull point.)
2. **No dated catalyst.** Kotler on the call: *"There is really no reason for us to issue equity or to sell equity at that level."* Class B is **not listed or traded**, and I could **not verify it converts into Class A** — if it doesn't, the $647.5M mark isn't claimable. A price gate here would be a beta-carrier self-trigger.
3. **Leverage is rising by design.** Management guides post-USPP leverage to **3.0–3.5x** vs ~1.7x today — roughly doubling net debt to buy $30M of EBITDA for $205M cash + $30M stock (~7.8x) while its own stub trades negative.

**What I could not verify** (SEC.gov returned 403 to every fetch; no Bash in this session, so no full-fingerprint retry): the APC-vs-GPM **debt split**, **Class B convertibility**, and H1 consolidated OCF — so I could **not** complete the instructed "strip net new borrowing out of headline FCF" test. Segment EBITDA and the related-party note were also absent from the IR-mirrored 10-Q excerpt. Also flagging a source conflation I rejected: several aggregators attribute APC's Q2 ($12.2M NI / $39.8M EBITDA / $10.4M OCF) to ARKO — ARKO's were $9.4M / $72.0M.
