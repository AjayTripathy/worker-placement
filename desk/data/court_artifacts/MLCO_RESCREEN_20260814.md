**DISPOSITION: STAY FLAT — and RETIRE the pre-staged dislocation-buy plan rather than re-date it.**
0 of the 3 pre-staged gates cleared. More important than the gate arithmetic: the print refuted the
*premise* the pre-staged underwrite rested on. The plan assumed the Q2 miss would be optical — bad

thing you buy on a gap-down.

## 2. What the print actually said — the volume/hold decomposition

Gross gaming revenue is *win*, and win is volume × hold. Hold is luck; volume is the business. The
court's whole pre-staged case was that Q2 would be a hold accident. Restating in volume terms:

**Macau mass-market table drop (the hold-free demand measure), US$M**

| Property | Q2-26 drop | Q2-25 drop | Change | Hold % 26 vs 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| City of Dreams | 1,750.0 | 1,750.0 | **0.0%** | 29.8% vs 30.5% |
| Studio City | 884.1 | 958.2 | **−7.7%** | 36.3% vs 34.0% |
| Altira | 134.0 | 119.0 | +12.6% | 22.0% vs 21.3% |
| **Melco Macau total** | **2,768.1** | **2,827.2** | **−2.1%** | — |

**The same measure at the two named competitors, same quarter, from their own quarterly filings:**

| Operator | Q2-26 | Q2-25 | Change | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Las Vegas Sands — Macao non-rolling chip drop (5 properties) | 7,188 | 6,251 | **+15.0%** | LVS Form 10-Q filed 2026-07-24 |
| Wynn — Macau mass table drop (Wynn Palace + Wynn Macau) | 3,651.9 | 3,461.8 | **+5.5%** | Wynn Form 10-Q filed 2026-08-06 |
| **Peers combined** | 10,840 | 9,713 | **+11.6%** | |
| **Melco Macau** | 2,768.1 | 2,827.2 | **−2.1%** | Melco Q2 release |

**A 13.7-point relative gap, measured on a metric hold cannot touch.** This is the finding of the
re-screen. The 8/11 blue bench was right that Q2 was a hold lottery across the sector — Wynn Palace's
mass hold ran 29.7% against 22.3% a year ago, which is what drove its beat — but blue inferred from
that a *symmetric* MLCO binary. The realized draw was not symmetric: Melco lost mass volume while the
market gained it.

**Rolling chip — this is where the optical miss is real.** City of Dreams' rolling win rate printed
2.71% against the company's own stated expected range of 2.85%–3.15%, versus 3.93% (far above range)
a year ago. Against the 3.00% midpoint that is a $15.0M drag this year against a $51.1M gift last
year — a $66.0M year-over-year swing in gross win. Manila's rolling hold swung the other way, +$8.9M.
Macau mass hold was a small net positive, +$9.0M.

**Bridging hold luck to the earnings line:**

- Macau gross-win hold luck: **−$57.0M** → after 39% Macau gaming tax and levies ≈ **−$34.8M** of EBITDA
- Manila gross-win hold luck: **+$8.9M** → ≈ **+$4.9M** of EBITDA
- Net year-over-year hold effect: **≈ −$30M**

Adjusted Property EBITDA printed **$303.8M against $377.7M**, down $73.9M or 19.6%. So hold explains
about **40%** of the decline. Normalizing hold, Q2 would still have been roughly **$334M, down ~11.6%**.
The remaining **$44M is real** — volume, mix, and cost.

Corroborating the same story elsewhere in the release: Studio City non-gaming revenue fell 17.4% and
Manila's fell 13.7%; Studio City's table count was unchanged at 253 and it still lost 7.7% of drop.
Group general and administrative expense rose to $166.2M from $158.5M on 5.7% lower revenue.

**The genuine bright spots, stated fairly.** Gaming-machine handle across Macau rose 13.9%
(City of Dreams +26.3% on a 33% larger machine count, 819 vs 616) — Melco is winning slot share where
it has added capacity. Cyprus revenue rose 13.4% with EBITDA up 60%. Manila EBITDA rose 8.9%. City of
Dreams runs 99% occupancy at a $224 average daily rate, which is consistent with a bull argument that
its flat mass drop is *room-constrained* rather than demand-lost, and the REM hotel begins a phased
opening in Q3. That argument is available and it is not silly. It does not extend to Studio City,
which lost mass volume with unchanged capacity, and it is a thesis for a future quarter, not a reason
to buy this one.

## 3. The buyback — what was actually executed inside the quarter

The release states: "During the period from April 1, 2026 to August 12, 2026, Melco Resorts
repurchased approximately 22.4 million ADSs (representing approximately 67.1 million ordinary shares)
from the open market at an aggregate purchase price of approximately US$120.6 million."

That window is 4½ months spanning two quarters. Reconstructing the quarter from the audited-form
balance-sheet treasury line across the three consecutive releases:

| Date | Treasury shares | Treasury at cost |
|---|---|---|
| 12/31/2025 | 179,484,916 | $356.835M |
| 3/31/2026 | 186,665,728 | $369.989M |
| 6/30/2026 | 210,808,192 | $409.835M |

- Q1 2026 movement: +7,180,812 ordinary shares, **+$13.2M** (the Q1 release separately disclosed $13.8M through 29 April — consistent)
- Q2 2026 movement: +24,142,464 ordinary shares, **+$39.8M**
- Therefore **Q2 executed repurchases ≈ $40M**, and at the outside no more than the $53.0M of total first-half treasury cost. **The $100M gate fails by roughly 2×.**
- The residual implies roughly **$81M bought between 1 July and 12 August** — a genuine post-quarter acceleration into the weakness, at an average around $5.30–5.50.

Two caveats I will not paper over. First, the treasury line can be contaminated by share-plan
forfeitures and issuances; the implied Q2 average of $4.95 per ADS sits below the quarter's traded
range, which is evidence some contamination exists. That is why I quote a $40–53M band rather than a
point. Second, the direction of the finding is unaffected: no reading of these balance sheets supports
$100M inside Q2.

The authorization itself is real and large: a new $500M program approved 30 April 2026 on top of the
2024 program, $589.6M remaining as of 12 August — about 28% of the $2.13bn market capitalization. The
company is using it. It is also worth noting the mechanical consequence red flagged in the original
court: every share retired raises the controlling holder's percentage of the remaining float, which is
collateral-enhancing for the pledged block.

## 5. Mode-B finding: the $393M that left the company, and how it was booked

This is the item neither bench had, and it is the largest single cash movement of the quarter.

**The transaction.** On 30 April 2026 a Melco Resorts subsidiary agreed to buy from its controlling
parent a newly incorporated British Virgin Islands holding company owning the group's trademarks —
"Melco," "Morpheus," and related marks — for **US$375.0 million in cash**, $300M at signing and $75M
at completion, which occurred **8 May 2026**. A further **$17.3–18.0M** was payable for licence-fee
receivables covering January–April 2026. Total cash out: **$392.3–393.0M**.

**The reconciliation — this is where the primary record is decisive.** Additional paid-in capital went
from **$2,995,669k at 31 March** to **$2,602,843k at 30 June**, a decline of **$392,826k**, landing
inside the disclosed $392,300–393,000k consideration range. Over the same period **intangible assets,
net *fell*** ($270,903k at year-end to $266,991k at 30 June). So **no asset was recognized at all**:
under common-control accounting the target's carrying value was effectively nil, and the entire
consideration was charged straight to equity. That also closes the cash bridge — without this outflow
the movement from $1,023M to $913M of cash cannot be reconciled against first-half EBITDA, the $303M
of net new debt, $197.5M of capital expenditure and $53M of buyback.

**Scale.** $392.8M is **1.29× the quarter's Adjusted Property EBITDA**, **3.17× the quarter's capital
expenditure**, and **$1.03 per ADS — 18.4% of the current share price** — paid by a company carrying
$7.05bn of debt at roughly 4.4× net leverage with earnings declining.

**Honesty grade — and I am going to be careful here, because the reflex is to over-call this.**

*Disclosed, and disclosed properly.* The transaction, its $375M price, the signing/closing structure,
the unanimous approval by the **disinterested members of the Audit and Risk Committee**, and the
engagement of an independent valuation firm were all set out in Melco's **own Q1 press release dated
30 April 2026**, and the parent's Hong Kong announcement was filed as a 6-K the same day. Under our
own doctrine — grade takeaway-versus-data divergence, not datum disclosure — **the existence and
governance of this transaction are CLEAN.** It does not qualify as concealment and I am not calling it
that.

*What is nonetheless a legitimate finding, independent of disclosure:*

1. **The price mechanism.** The $375M was set "with reference to... the expected license fee income
   receivable" — and the licence fee in question, paid by Melco Resorts to its parent under an
   agreement that began 1 January 2024, ran **$6.0M in FY2024 and $32.7M in FY2025, a 5.5× step-up in
   the year before the sale**. The buyer's own payment history, set by the seller, was the valuation
   input. $375M against the FY25 fee is 11.5×, an 8.3% cash-on-cash return.
2. **Where the money went.** The parent's stated use of proceeds is repayment of its own bank debt —
   the parent whose subsidiary has 677.4M Melco Resorts shares pledged against a $1bn facility. This
   is the pledge being serviced by the listed subsidiary's minorities. It is the *other* resolution
   path for a stressed control pledge, and it is the one that actually occurred: not a forced sale,
   an intra-group extraction.
3. **The Q2 presentation.** The Q2 release's "Financial Position and Capital Expenditures" section
   walks through revolver drawdowns, a maturity extension, an incremental facility, a notes issuance,
   a partial redemption, liquidity and capital expenditure — and omits the largest discretionary cash
   item of the quarter, which completed inside it. The datum was disclosed a quarter earlier, so this
   is a presentation choice rather than a misstatement, but a reader working only from the Q2 release
   cannot reconcile the cash and will not know the equity charge happened.
4. **The liquidity headline.** "Available liquidity... approximately US$2.80 billion," up from $2.36bn
   at Q1. The definition given is correct and includes undrawn revolvers. The *driver* is the
   9 June incremental commitment of $821M — new borrowing capacity, not cash generation. Cash fell
   $110M in the half and gross debt rose $303M.

*The fair counter, which I hold:* the purchase **eliminates a ~$32.7M-per-year related-party royalty**
with roughly eight years left to run at a fee the parent could keep re-setting. That is worth about
2.4% of annualized property EBITDA going forward and, more valuably, it permanently closes a channel
through which value could have been transferred repeatedly. Expensive, arguably over-priced off a
freshly-inflated fee, but it plugs a leak. Reasonable people can call this deal fair; nobody should
call it immaterial.

## 7. Where the name stands now

At $5.61: 380.2M ADS = **$2.13bn** market capitalization; net debt **$6.01bn**; enterprise value
**$8.15bn**. On annualized first-half figures that is **5.95× Adjusted Property EBITDA / 6.53× Adjusted
EBITDA**, with **4.4–4.8× net leverage**. Red's equity-torque claim verifies: a 10% move in EBITDA is
about **38% of the equity**. This remains a highly geared instrument on a business whose Macau mass
volumes are currently going the wrong way relative to its two largest competitors.

**Recommendation: STAY FLAT. Retire the pre-staged underwrite; do not re-date it.** Per the response
taxonomy the two live findings are business-risk (mass-share loss) and governance (the related-party
extraction channel with a live control pledge). Neither maps to a price gate — both map to size, and
the size is currently zero. There is therefore **no price at which the 8/11 plan re-arms as written**.

**A fresh court is warranted only on a new thesis**, and it would have to be built on share recovery
rather than hold normalization. The specific evidence that thesis would need, all of which is
observable before the Q3 print:

1. **Melco Macau mass drop growing again, and closing the gap to peers.** This is the single decisive
   number. Q3 releases: Sands China and LVS late October, Wynn early November, Melco mid-November.
2. **REM's phased opening actually delivering rooms in Q3**, with City of Dreams table drop responding.
   The capacity-constraint defence stands or falls here.
3. **Studio City stabilizing.** It has no capacity excuse: unchanged tables, −7.7% drop, −17.4% non-gaming.
4. **The pledge quantified.** Melco International's half-year accounts, due around late August, should
   show the facility balance after the $375M paydown. If the Credit Facility is materially reduced, the
   reflexive tail that made this name unownable shrinks and the size cap can loosen.
5. **No further related-party purchases.** A second extraction from the listed entity toward the parent's
   debt is a kill trigger, not a discount.

### Detector note for the graph

The 8/11 court marked **common-control merger accounting NOT-FIRED** on both benches ("no such absorption
in the filing inventory"). That was wrong — a common-control acquisition had been announced on 2026-04-30
in a 6-K and in Melco's own Q1 release, and completed 8 May 2026. It did not change the 8/11 verdict, but
it is a recall miss on a detector that was in the dispatch set and should be graded as one.

The existing `control_pledge_coverage_tracker` candidate should gain a **second fire condition**: a
material related-party cash transfer *from* the listed subsidiary *to* the pledgor's group while a
control pledge is live. Coverage tracking assumes the pledge resolves through the tape. Here it resolved
through the subsidiary's balance sheet instead — same mechanism, opposite direction, and invisible to a
price-only tracker.
