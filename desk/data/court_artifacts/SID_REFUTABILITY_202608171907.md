**Why STRUCTURAL and not DAMAGE-ABSENT.** The operating narrative partly fails on this company: steel volumes 1,182kt (+5.9% QoQ, +16.7% YoY), domestic 825kt (+9.9% YoY), steel EBITDA R$636.3M at a 10.5% margin vs R$393M in 1Q26 — margin back to double digits on March-2026 antidumping measures cutting import penetration. But that is not what is being discounted. Mining is where damage is arriving (unit net revenue **US$49.09/t, -21.5% QoQ, -5.4% YoY**; mining EBITDA R$929.7M vs R$1,374M in 1Q26, -32%; freight a -R$479M EBITDA hit), and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin actually **fell** 0.5pp QoQ and 0.1pp YoY (23.4%) despite the +4.8% EBITDA "beat." The discount is priced off the balance sheet, and the balance sheet moved the wrong way in the very print that beat.

**Decisive reconstruction (the metric the headline hides).** CSN reports *"Free Cash Flow was positive by R$808.1 million... a turnaround."* Its own waterfall shows how: Adjusted EBITDA 2,773 → jointly-controlled (354) → **working capital +934** → capex (1,414) → financial result (1,549) → tax (73) → others (535) → TSLA (495) = **Adjusted Free Cash Flow −714**. The +808 headline is then reached by adding **borrowings +6,918 against amortization (3,932) = +R$2,986M of net new debt**, less iron-ore prepayment (1,271), buyback (129), FX (64). So the headline "positive FCF" is a cash-balance movement that **includes net new borrowing**, and the pre-financing figure is negative. Strip the non-repeatable working-capital release (inventory drawdown; CMIN inventory already at a five-year low, 2,739kt) and sustaining Adjusted FCF is **≈ −R$1,648M for the quarter — roughly the entire market cap on an annualized basis.**

**The honest counterweight (why it is not a zero).** Refinancing access is demonstrably open, not closed: US$1.2B bridge loan signed mid-April, exchange offer end-July extending US$1.3B of 2028 notes by two years at 77.49% participation, R$6,918M of new borrowings drawn in the quarter, average maturity 42.4 months. Liquidity R$15,898M covers 2026 (R$4,487M) plus 2027 (R$7,579M) maturities with ~R$3.8B to spare — but that spare is fully consumed by the ~R$4B of P15 capex management says is still outstanding through end-2027. The buffer exists and is exactly one refinancing window deep.
