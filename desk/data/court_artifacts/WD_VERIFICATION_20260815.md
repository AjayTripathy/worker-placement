# MODE B — THE FIRST-PRINCIPLES PASS (leading, per doctrine)

Stripping the memo framing, the decisive questions are not the ones the court argued.

**Q1. If privatization were the risk, what security would carry it?** FNMA/FMCC/FNMAS/FMCKJ,
not WD. Those securities have de-rated ~60%/~42% since October 2025. If WD were short the
release, WD should have *rallied* into that de-rating. It fell alongside. The sign is wrong.

**Q2. Is "release without an explicit guarantee" even the right binary?** No — and this is the
single most important thing the court record missed. Under the PSPA, Treasury's **remaining
funding commitment is $113.9B to Fannie and $140.2B to Freddie — $254.1B combined**, and it
survives any release that does not terminate the Purchase Agreement. Terminating the PSPA
means Treasury walks away from **$380.8B of senior preferred liquidation preference** and
79.9% warrants. No administration does that for free. The realistic administrative release
therefore *retains a quarter-trillion-dollar Treasury backstop*. "Release without a
guarantee" as a clean binary is close to a strawman, and variant (a)'s damage compresses
accordingly.

**Q3. Which way does the clock actually run?** Against release, monotonically. The senior
preferred liquidation preference accretes with net worth every quarter: Fannie $230.5B
(3/31/26) → $234.2B (6/30/26) → **$238.0B (9/30/26, already declared)**; Freddie $135.1B →
$146.6B → **$150.4B**. That is **+$7.6B per quarter, combined, of additional write-down a
release must clear.** The court framed a Democratic House as an *accelerant* on the bad
branch. The mechanism runs the other way: **every quarter of inaction makes the arithmetic of
release strictly harder, and the structure is self-reinforcing against the very outcome the
thesis fears.**

**Q4. What is actually wrong with this company?** Credit, mix, and the servicing strip — all
disclosed, none political. Defaulted loans went 0.07% → 0.23% → **0.28%** of the at-risk book
in eighteen months. Q2'26 provision of **$20.97M exceeded all of FY2025 ($9.59M)**. Brokered
volume — which generates *no* MSR and *no* servicing strip — is now **58–61% of volume vs
54–55% a year ago**. The agency MSR rate fell **1.14% → 0.95%**; the weighted-average
servicing fee ground from **24.2bps → 23.4bps**. And the tell: **the buyback stopped after
Q1'26**, with $61.7M of a $75M authorization unused and zero program purchases in Q2, while
unrestricted cash fell $299.3M → $160.9M.
