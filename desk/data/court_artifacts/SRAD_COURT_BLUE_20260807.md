# KILL 2 — RIGHTS PAYABLES AS DEBT · **OVERTURNED**

Red's own words: *"This is the strongest kill."* It is the weakest. The adjustment is made on one side of the balance sheet only.

## 2.1 The symmetry test — decisive

**Sportradar's adjusted EBITDA is already charged with the full amortization of the capitalized sport rights.** From the non-IFRS definition (Q2 6-K, and identically in the FY2025 20-F):

> *"'Adjusted EBITDA' represents earnings for the period adjusted for finance income and finance costs, income tax expense or benefit, depreciation and amortization **(excluding amortization of capitalized sport rights licenses)**…"*
>
> *"…our presentation of Adjusted EBITDA reflects the full costs of our sport rights licenses. Management believes that, **by including amortization of sport rights in its calculation of Adjusted EBITDA**, the result is a financial metric that is both more meaningful and comparable…"*

The magnitude (Q2 6-K, sport rights expense table):

| in €'000 | Q2-26 | Q2-25 | H1-26 | H1-25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-capitalized sport rights expenses | 37,533 | 31,685 | 71,701 | 64,016 |
| **Amortization of capitalized sport rights** | **100,219** | 74,509 | **188,344** | 146,208 |
| **Total sport rights expenses** | **137,752** | 106,194 | **260,045** | 210,224 |

FY2025 capitalized-rights amortization was **€270.2M** (20-F cash-flow statement) / €272.6M (Note 13). Applying the FY2025 H1→FY ratio (1.848x) to H1-26's €188.3M gives **FY2026E ≈ €348M**.

**If you put the rights obligation in the numerator, you cannot leave the amortization of the asset it funded in the denominator. That is the same cost counted twice.** The consistent frames:

| Frame | EV | ÷ FY26E EBITDA | Multiple | Coherent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Conventional** (triage, vendors, screens) | €3,073M | €364M | **8.4x** | yes |
| **B. Red — liability added, amortization left as a charge** | €4,523M | €364M | 12.4x | **no** |
| **C. Red "fully loaded" — liability added, amortization AND accretion charged** | €4,523M | €276M | 16.4x | **no** |
| **D. Debt-like, done symmetrically** — liability in EV, rights amortization added back (the IFRS-16 lease analogy) | €4,523M | €712M | **6.4x** | yes |
| **E. Asset-netted** — liability added net of the €1,466.7M rights intangible | €3,136M | €364M | **8.6x** | yes |
| **F. Operating, with accretion charged** — liability stays out of EV, but the €80.6M/yr accretion is deducted from EBITDA as true rights cost | €3,073M | €276M | **11.1x** | **yes — this is the honest bear case** |

Frame **C is a triple count**: the obligation sits in EV, its amortization is charged to EBITDA, *and* its accretion is subtracted from EBITDA. Interest is below EBITDA by definition — that is the "I". No recognized framework produces 16.4x.

**Red had the right denominator and attached it to the wrong numerator.** The €276M figure in frame C is legitimate — it is the honest treatment of the accretion that escapes to finance costs. But it belongs with the *conventional* EV, not the rights-inflated one. **The coherent version of red's own "fully loaded" case is frame F at 11.1x, not 16.4x.** Blue concedes frame F is defensible and that the triage's unqualified 8.4x was too generous by roughly a third.

**The defensible range is therefore 6.4x – 11.1x. Red's 12.4x and 16.4x both sit outside it**, and the symmetric debt-like treatment red itself proposed lands at **6.4x — cheaper than the 8.4x it set out to destroy.**

## 2.6 The lender test — secured creditors do not treat these as debt

The strongest external authority available, and it is contractual. RCF amendment 6-K, 2026-04-30 — **eight days after the short reports** (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836470/000110465926053115/tm2613145d1_6k.htm):

- Commitments **increased** €220.0M → €250.0M; maturity **extended** to 2031-05-20; per the Q2 release, *"significantly reducing undrawn and drawn borrowing fees."*
- Margin ratchet on **Senior Secured Net Leverage Ratio**: ≤1.00:1 → 1.50%; >3.00:1 → 2.25%.
- *"The applicable margin for the RCF is currently **1.50% per annum**"* — the **lowest tier, ≤1.00:1**.
- Springing financial covenant: senior secured net leverage not to exceed **6.50:1**.

€1.45BN of rights payables against €364M of EBITDA is ~4.0x. Sportradar prices at the ≤1.0x tier. **Secured lenders, with full diligence, a security package over the borrower's assets, and the short reports already in the public domain, expanded the facility, extended it five years and cut the price.**

**Blue states the caveat against itself:** the tested ratio is *senior secured* net leverage, and the rights payables are unsecured trade payables — so they would fall outside that specific definition on seniority grounds alone, independent of whether anyone considers them debt. The ratchet is therefore **suggestive, not dispositive**. What is not merely suggestive is the **commercial** decision: no lender expands commitments, extends to 2031 and reduces both drawn and undrawn fees eight days after a 123-page fraud allegation if it reads the borrower as carrying €1.45BN of concealed interest-bearing leverage. That is a market test red did not run, and it points the other way.

## 2.7 External convention — no agency, no vendor, no peer treats these as debt

The coordinator asked whether any rating agency or peer treatment capitalizes rights payables as debt. **The answer is no, and the single most decisive fact is that S&P rates Sportradar itself.**

**S&P rates SRAD 'BB', UPGRADED from 'BB−' on 2025-11-26**, with downside sensitivity set at *"adjusted debt to EBITDA above 3x"* — https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3486372. On SRAD's reported debt, leverage is ~0.2x gross and net cash. **Include the rights payables and it is ~4.3x gross / ~3.1x net — already past S&P's own downgrade trigger at the exact moment S&P upgraded.** S&P's adjusted debt for this issuer demonstrably excludes the rights payables. That is not an analogy; it is the agency's arithmetic on the name in question.

The criteria say so explicitly (*Corporate Methodology: Ratios and Adjustments*, ¶10–11, https://www.maalot.co.il/Publications/MT20190402125127.PDF):

> *"**We do not consider a company's future commitments to purchase goods or services it has not received as akin to debt. This is because these are executory contracts…**"*
>
> *"The adjusted debt figure **excludes obligations, such as accounts payable and other accrued liabilities, because we regard them as trade credit.**"*

SRAD's rights payables sit *literally inside Trade payables*. S&P's media guidance goes further: film and programming rights *"are capitalized and amortized to income… **However, we view these items as operating**."* Moody's standard-adjustments methodology returns **zero** hits for programming, film, sports rights or take-or-pay. Fitch's Appendix 1 adjustment list carries no content item.

**The Netflix analogue settles it arithmetically.** Netflix carries $24.04B of content obligations, **$18.4B of it explicitly off balance sheet** (FY2025 10-K Note 9). Moody's upgraded Netflix to A3 on 2025-05-01 against a *"debt/EBITDA ~2.0x or lower, downgrade above 2.5x"* frame. Bonds-only leverage at that date ≈ **1.45x**; bonds-plus-content ≈ **3.61x** — a full turn past the *downgrade* trigger, at the moment of an *upgrade*. Only the bonds-only reading is possible. Content enters via liquidity and FCF, never via debt.

**And the peer comparison inverts.** Genius Sports (GENI, CIK 1834489, US GAAP) **expenses rights as executory contracts** — no capitalized asset, no payable, no accretion. Its entire rights book is off balance sheet in the commitments note: *"future minimum commitments under the Company's data rights license agreements accounted for as executory contracts… **Total $1,512,682**"* (thousands) — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1834489/000119312526110749/geni-20251231.htm.

| FY2025 | GENI (US GAAP) | SRAD (IFRS) |
|---|---|---|
| Rights obligation **on** balance sheet | **$0** | **€1,529.2M** |
| Rights obligation **off** balance sheet | **$1,512.7M** | — |
| Capitalized rights asset | ~$0 | €1,466.7M |
| Adj. EBITDA treatment of rights | adds back $160.5M stock comp **including equity-classified awards issued to suppliers** | **keeps the full €270.2M rights amortization in** |

**Callisto's headline comp — "SRAD 14.6x vs Genius Sports 7.2x" — is therefore broken twice over.** GENI carries an economically identical ~$1.5BN obligation that no screen sees, and GENI's adjusted EBITDA strips out the equity-paid slice of its rights cost while SRAD's deliberately does not. Applying the prosecutor's own rule consistently to GENI would add ~$1.2–1.3BN to its EV against ~$136M of adjusted EBITDA — roughly **nine turns**. **Sportradar is being punished for the more transparent presentation.**

Vendor practice confirms the convention: stockanalysis.com shows SRAD **Total Debt $69.7M, EV $3.54BN, EV/EBITDA 7.11x** — and that $69.7M is the loans-and-borrowings line, which per 20-F Note 15.2 is **100% IFRS-16 lease liabilities** (bank principal repayments in FY2025: €0).

## 2.8 The "two independent routes converge on 16x" claim is false independence

Red: *"Two methodologically independent routes converge on ~16x."*

They are not independent and they do not measure the same thing. The 16.0x is **P/FCF on equity value** (€3,263M ÷ €204M) — **no rights payable is added to the numerator at all.**

And Sportradar's FCF captures the rights burden **on both legs**:
- **Principal** — FCF is defined as *"net cash from operating activities adjusted for payments for lease liabilities, acquisition of property and equipment, and **acquisition of intangible assets**"*; the €223.4M of FY2025 rights payments ran through investing and is deducted.
- **Interest** — FY2025 **interest paid of €85.6M ran through operating cash flow**, i.e. it is already inside net cash from operations before the FCF deduction. That €85.6M is ~29% of adjusted EBITDA.

**So free cash flow is burdened by the rights principal *and* the accretion that frame F charges — there is nothing left to deduct.** 16.0x P/FCF is therefore a fully-loaded check on the CONVENTIONAL frame, and what it says is that a 19–21% constant-currency grower trades at 16x all-in free cash flow. Red concedes this is "fair." Two different bases landing on the same number is a coincidence, not corroboration — and the one that is actually fully loaded is the one that looks cheap.

FY2025 delivered €167.2M of FCF at 56% conversion; H1-2026 delivered €103M, **+23% YoY**.

Corroborating behaviour: H1-2026 FCF €103M, **+23% YoY**; €217M of buybacks in H1 alone; **26 million shares / $422M repurchased through 2026-07-31**, ~8% of the share count, under a $1BN authorization. That is not how a €1.45BN-levered issuer behaves, and no lender objected.

**KILL 2 — OVERTURNED.** Two things survive and blue concedes both:

1. A naive screen computing net debt as (loans and borrowings − cash) does understate a €1.45BN obligation, and the triage should not have said "net cash" unqualified. **That is a labelling correction.**
2. **~€80.6M/yr of genuine rights cost does escape adjusted EBITDA** into finance costs. Charging it (frame F) gives **11.1x FY26E**, and that is a legitimate bear number the triage did not have. **The triage's 8.4x was too generous by roughly a third.**

But neither rescues the kill as filed. **The defensible range is 6.4x–11.1x; red's 12.4x and 16.4x are outside it**, produced only by pairing a debt-inclusive numerator with an operating-basis denominator — a combination used by no rating agency (including S&P *on this issuer*), no vendor, no lender, and neither company's own non-GAAP framework.

## 3.3 The divergence — **REFUTED**. Red truncated the rebuttal.

**Sportradar affirmatively told investors the disclosure had changed, in the sentence red cut.** The "have emphasized" present perfect refers to the historical filings — accurately. ## 3.4 Chronology, and one concession red should have made
