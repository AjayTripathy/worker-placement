## PRINT-DECISIVE RECONSTRUCTION (public pre-print only)

**Loudest bull claim:** *"World Cup is a tier-1 event; Q2 accelerates GMS to the 8–10% FY guide."*

1. **GMS is dollars, not tickets — and WC resale ASPs collapsed.** All-knockout average get-in fell **26% in seven days ($2,930 → $2,165)**; Round-of-32 fell **39% ($2,040 → $1,245)**; individual matches −68% to −72% (Germany-Paraguay −68% to $466; Switzerland-Algeria −72% to $443). FIFA dumped supply — available tickets jumped **1,774 → 10,547 in a single day** ([Newsweek, late June 2026](https://www.newsweek.com/world-cup-ticket-prices-plunge-as-knockout-stage-begins-12134662)). Record volume with halved ASPs is not record GMS.
2. **The calendar splits the quarter favorably.** Group stage (Jun 11–~Jun 27, peak pricing) lands in **Q2**; the knockout price collapse and the run to the Jul 19 final land in **Q3**. So Q2 GMS should print *well* — the danger is the Q3/FY guide, not the quarter.
3. **Cancellations mechanically cut reported GMS.** StubHub's own GMS definition excludes "event cancellations or expected cancellations of the initial transaction on our platform" ([Q1'26 release, p.8](https://s204.q4cdn.com/138970181/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/Q1-2026-StubHub-Earnings-Release-Final.pdf)). The ghost-ticket voids — StubHub blamed FIFA transfer failures ([NPR, 2026-06-26](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/26/nx-s1-5871397/world-cup-tickets-stubhub-resale-controversy)) — subtract from Q2 GMS *and* add make-good/refund cost to opex, with a Paxton probe opened 2026-07-03 ([Texas OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-investigates-stubhub-failing-provide-fifa-world-cup-tickets-fans-have)).
4. **The rights holder took the resale rent on the biggest event of the year.** FIFA ran its own Official Resale Marketplace at **15% buyer + 15% seller = ~30% take**, above StubHub's ~20%. That is the structural datum management will be asked about, and it is not in any reported number yet.
5. **FCF trap — do not extrapolate Q1.** Q1 FCF of $290.6M was **94% float**: $273.6M of the $298.4M operating cash flow was the change in *payments due to buyers and sellers*. Ex-float FCF was **~$17M**. Read the balance-sheet float line ($1,111.7M at 3/31 vs $845.9M at 12/31) at 4:05pm — a build means forward bookings held; a drawdown means demand rolled.

**Loudest bear claim:** *"price caps gut the take rate."* Pre-print resolution: **regulation cannot touch FY26 at all.** Enacted = DC (10%, effective **Jan 1, 2027**, still pending mayoral signature + 30-day congressional review), Maine, Vermont, Ontario. Pending and unpassed = CA AB1720 (parked on the Senate Appropriations **suspense file** Aug 3, Dept. of Finance opposed on cost, committee analysis noted no empirical basis for the 10% figure), plus NY/MA/NC. Citi's Bazinet frames the tail: ~20% of sales capped at ~15% → **~−$95M EBITDA**, −23% of the $410M guide ([Motley Fool, 2026-07-17](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/17/why-stubhub-stock-plummeted-by-13-this-week/)). **Asymmetry to exploit:** a FY26 guide cut on Aug 12 would be a clean read on the *non-regulatory* case (demand/World Cup pricing), because caps can't reach these numbers.

**PRE-PRINT POSITION: FLAT** — the reconstruction *bounds* the print but doesn't call it (Q2 catches group-stage GMS at peak ASPs, while the guide has to absorb the knockout collapse), and there's no valuation cushion to pay for the wait: charging the ~$150M/yr SBC and ~$124M/yr cash interest against the $410M adj-EBITDA guide leaves ~$105M of real pre-tax owner earnings on a $3.28B cap, a **~3.2% yield at 10.6x EV/EBITDA** — this is a no-edge call, not a bearish one.
