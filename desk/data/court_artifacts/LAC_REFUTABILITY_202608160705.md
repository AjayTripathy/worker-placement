**The instrument is the disclosure.** LAC reported ~$1.3B total cash/restricted cash at 6/30/26 ($530.3M of it trapped at the Thacker Pass JV level), $1.209B of a $2.23B DOE facility already advanced (3rd advance $342M on 6/3/26), and ~$1.02B of DOE capacity still undrawn. A company in that position does **not** issue a VWAP-reset convertible to Yorkville — conversion at the *lower* of $4.56 fixed or **95% of the lowest daily VWAP over the preceding 5 trading days**, floor $1.63 (reducible to $0.65), coupon stepping 5%→7.5%→15% on floor-breach / registration-unavailability / exchange-cap exhaustion (8-K 2026-08-13, acc. 0001193125-26-349161, Item 1.01, quoted in the evidence pack). That is a distress-priced instrument bolted onto a balance sheet management describes as strong. The gap it fills is the one the DOE loan structurally cannot: project-restricted advances don't fund corporate overhead or LAC's own JV equity contributions.

**Reflexivity, quantified (my computation from the disclosed terms + NYSE 19.99% standard — the exchange cap number is *not* disclosed in the 8-K):** at ~$3.21 conversion (95% of a $3.38 VWAP) the $150M prints ~46.7M shares = **12.9% of current o/s**. At the $1.63 floor it needs ~92.0M shares, which exceeds a ~72.6M-share 19.99% cap — i.e. below roughly **$2.07 average conversion the cap binds**, forcing cash repayment (capped at $35M while Orion's $195M notes are outstanding) or a shareholder vote, and tripping the 15% coupon. Every conversion sells into the tape, lowering the 5-day VWAP that sets the next conversion. Add ~18.3M DOE penny warrants ($0.01 strike, 10-year) and $181.5M of unused ATM capacity ($68.5M of $250M drawn).
