## SCIENCE BRIEF — TMDX

**The technical question the case turns on:** whether normothermic *ex vivo* machine perfusion (OCS) is a durable clinical improvement over cold storage that justifies a ~$100–140k per-case device+logistics charge — or a transitional technology whose two largest end-markets are being taken by cheaper substitutes (in-donor NRP for hearts, hypothermic oxygenated perfusion for livers) whose evidence bases matured in 2026.

Note on scope: I take no position and recommend nothing. Where the upstream thesis contains a factual error I say so with the primary that corrects it (row T-5 below).

## 2. THE MECHANISM

**Established science.** Static cold storage (SCS) suppresses metabolism to ~10% of baseline but does not stop it. Ischemia depletes ATP; reperfusion then drives a mitochondrial burst of reactive oxygen species (succinate accumulation → reverse electron transport at complex I). The organ-specific ceiling: hearts tolerate ~4h of cold cross-clamp, lungs ~6–8h, livers ~8–12h — beyond which graft dysfunction rises steeply. This is not contested and it is the physical basis of every perfusion product on the market.

**The three ways to attack it, which is the whole competitive map:**

1. **Normothermic *ex vivo* perfusion (NMP)** — OCS, OrganOx metra, TorEx. Keep the organ at 37°C, metabolically active, on oxygenated blood. Two distinct value propositions: (a) *time extension*, and (b) *viability assessment* — the organ is functioning, so lactate clearance, bile production, vascular resistance can be measured before the recipient is committed.
2. **Hypothermic oxygenated perfusion (HOPE)** — VitaSmart, XVIVO Heart Assist. Stay cold, but oxygenate. Mechanistically this targets the succinate/ROS pathway directly: it recharges the mitochondrial electron transport chain *before* warm reperfusion. It does **not** give you assessment, and gives less time extension. It is dramatically simpler and cheaper.
3. **Normothermic regional perfusion (NRP)** — restore circulation *in the donor* after death declaration using an ECMO circuit, with the cerebral vessels ligated. This resuscitates all organs simultaneously, in situ, before procurement, using **commodity ECMO hardware that requires no PMA**.

**Where TransMedics' novel claim sits.** OCS is the only *portable, blood-based, normothermic, beating-heart / ventilated-lung* platform with PMA across three organs. That is real and unmatched. The company's incremental claim — that perfusion improves outcomes rather than merely enabling logistics — is supported for **liver ischemic biliary complications at 12 months** (C-2, the strongest single result in the file) and for **lung PGD3** (C-6), and is *unsupported at any timepoint for survival* (C-3, C-8).

**Class-inherent vs implementation-specific failure modes.** Two named failures matter:

- **The Chapman trial (Ann Surg 2023, N=383 randomized / 266 transplanted, 14 US centers, OrganOx metra):** continuous NMP did **not** beat cold storage on early allograft dysfunction — 20.6% vs 23.7%, not significant. Benefit appeared only in exploratory as-treated subgroups (DCD: 22.8% vs 44.6%; highest donor-risk quartile: 19.2% vs 33.3%). ([PubMed 37389552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37389552/); commentary: [PMC11634420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11634420/)). This is a *different device* than OCS — see trap M-3 — but it establishes that the NMP class benefit is **risk-stratum-dependent, not universal**. A trial enrolling low-risk donors (median DRI 1.6) finds nothing.
- **The LungFX pivotal (CLES study, United Therapeutics / Lung Bioengineering):** FDA granted **PMA on 2026-06-29** even though "the 12-month survival rate did not meet the pre-specified performance goal" and "rates of 12-month overall mortality and CEC-adjudicated lung graft-related mortality were **higher** in EVLP subjects" ([UTHR IR, 2026-06-29](https://ir.unither.com/press-releases/2026/06-29-2026-113020795)). Class-inherent lesson: in this field FDA has approved on utilization/access grounds where survival endpoints missed. "FDA-approved" therefore carries much less outcome information here than in drugs.

**The ceiling.** The binding constraint is not device performance — it is the donor pool and the surgeons' willingness to pay per case. There were 49,065 US transplants in 2025 across all organs; the thoracic + liver addressable base is roughly 4,600 hearts + ~10,000 livers + ~3,000 lungs. *(Derived, mine: at V-4's stated 36% liver penetration and ~10,000 US liver transplants, ~3,600 OCS liver cases carrying $461M implies ~$128k of revenue per liver case including logistics. Treat as an order-of-magnitude derivation; the company does not publish per-organ case counts.)*

## 3. THE FIELD — as of 2026-09-04

**Heart — the substitute is NRP, and it is not a device company.**
- Dec 2019–Mar 2024: **595 NRP vs 625 DPP** heart transplants — near parity, from a standing start ([JTCVS/ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002252232401122X)). By *center* count NRP is already the more widely adopted technique: 2019–2024, **54/61 centers (88.5%) used NRP** vs 50/61 (82%) DPP, 43/61 (70.5%) used both.
- NRP uses standard ECMO circuits; there is **no PMA, no per-case device charge, and no third-party logistics fee**. Reported advantages: significantly lower distance traveled and lower out-of-body time.
- Independent outcome comparison is genuinely **mixed and unsettled**: propensity-matched SRTR shows no 1-year difference (C-10); an unmatched UNOS analysis shows a large 3-year gap against OCS (C-9). Do not treat either as settled.
- The counterweight, and it is real: **NRP has a live ethical challenge** to the dead-donor rule. The American College of Physicians has issued a Statement of Concern and subsequent ethics guidance raising objections to thoracoabdominal NRP; OPTN's Ethics Committee published a white paper on it. ([ACP ethics guidance](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/new-acp-paper-provides-ethical-guidance-amid-controversies-and-changing-practices-in-organ); [OPTN Ethics NRP white paper, Dec 2023](https://optn.transplant.hrsa.gov/media/143lxeha/ethics_nrp_wp_dec23.pdf)). OCS/DPP has no equivalent ethical objection — it is the ethically conservative option. Any bench arguing NRP displacement must price this.
- **XVIVO Heart Assist Transport (HOPE for hearts):** PRESERVE trial **met its pre-specified primary endpoints**, late-breaking data announced **2026-04-22**; company "intends to submit… as part of the PMA process later this year"; FDA granted **additional CAP enrollment of 60 transplants in August 2026** while 1-year follow-up accrues. Still investigational. **One demand-side risk that cuts against the whole category:** the July 2025 HRSA investigation into DCD practice found that of 351 authorized-but-not-completed donation cases, **103 (29.3%) showed concerning features**, with **at least 28 patients possibly not deceased** when procurement was initiated; HHS announced systemic reform and the House Energy & Commerce Committee opened an investigation. Public NYT coverage (2025-07-20) triggered measurable donor-registry withdrawals. ([HHS press room](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hrsa-to-reform-organ-transplant-system.html); [E&C](https://energycommerce.house.gov/posts/house-committee-on-energy-and-commerce-is-continuing-to-investigate-concerning-practices-within-our-nation-s-organ-procurement-and-transplant-system)). DCD volume did *not* fall through 2025 (V-2). Whether 2026 DCD volume was affected is unresolved (§6).

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS

**M-1 — The 3-year "60.7% vs 83.3%" number is the most likely bad kill in this file.** The Heart Surgery Forum analysis (C-9) is retrospective UNOS, OCS n=507 vs NRP n=265, and its **abstract discloses neither propensity matching nor confidence intervals nor pairwise p-values**. Selection is the obvious confounder: DPP/OCS is chosen for *long-distance, longer-out-of-body* recoveries (NRP's own literature reports significantly shorter distance and out-of-body time), so the OCS cohort is systematically the harder cohort. A propensity-matched analysis of the same question found **no 1-year difference** (C-10). Do not present these two as agreeing or average them; they are different designs answering the same question with opposite implications.

**M-2 — Non-inferiority is not superiority, and 6 months is not 3 years.** The DCD Heart trial's estimand was *6-month risk-adjusted survival, non-inferiority margin*. Reading it as evidence of long-term durability is a category error in both directions.

**M-3 — Chapman ≠ PROTECT.** Both are US liver NMP RCTs; the null one used **OrganOx metra**, the positive one used **OCS Liver**. They also differ in primary endpoint definition and donor risk (Chapman median DRI 1.6). Anyone using Chapman to refute PROTECT, or PROTECT to validate metra, is comparing devices, populations and endpoints simultaneously.

**M-4 — The two liver labels are not the same population, and TransMedics' is broader.** OCS Liver: DCD **≤55 years, ≤30 min WIT**. OrganOx metra: DCD **≤40 years, ≤20 min WIT**. Both cap macrosteatosis at ≤15%. A "both are FDA approved for DCD" comparison silently equates a wider indication with a narrower one.

**M-5 — "Approved" carries different evidentiary weight per device.** OCS Liver: PMA on a positive 300-patient RCT. VitaSmart: **De Novo** (a different pathway) on a 219-patient RCT. LungFX: **PMA despite missing its performance goal with numerically worse 12-month mortality**. Ranking these by regulatory status alone inverts the evidence.

**M-6 — MAUDE is a numerator without a denominator.** The failure reports in C-12 are real and specific, but MAUDE is passive-surveillance, is not a rate, and the only denominator on offer is the manufacturer's own "<0.2%" (C-11). Neither a bull nor a bear case can be built on MAUDE counts alone; the Scorpion petition's under-reporting allegation (T-5b) is precisely a claim that the numerator is understated, and it is unresolved at FDA.

**M-7 — The company's per-organ revenue is not product revenue.** Q2-26: liver ~$148M + heart ~$33M + lung ~$2M = **$183M**, against **transplant product revenue of $111M** and total revenue of $190M. The organ split therefore bundles logistics/service. Any "device ASP" or "per-case device revenue" derived from the organ split will be inflated by roughly the logistics load.

**M-8 — Two terminated TransMedics trials are benign; do not build a kill on them.** **NCT04194437 (OCS Liver DCD, n=9)** — registry whyStopped: *"Enrollment in Liver DCD was stopped as a result of the FDA approval for the OCS Liver System."* **NCT03343535 (EXPAND II Lung, n=46)** — *"Enrollment in EXPAND II was stopped June 24, 2019 as a result of the FDA approval for the EXPAND indication."* Both terminations are administrative consequences of approval, not safety or futility stops.

**M-9 — "Utilization rate 89%" is not an outcome.** It is the fraction of *perfused* DCD hearts that were transplanted (90/101). It says nothing about survival and cannot be compared to a survival percentage.

**M-10 — The ENHANCE Part B control arm is a sponsor device.** If the CHOPS IDE amendment is granted, the "static cold storage" comparator will be TransMedics' own investigational active-cooling device rather than conventional phase-change cold storage. Whichever way the trial reads out, the comparator is not the standard of care that clinicians actually use today, and a superiority result would be against CHOPS specifically.

**M-11 — DPP ≠ OCS in registry data.** UNOS/SRTR cannot reliably identify which perfusion device was used; the SRTR-based study explicitly notes the registry "poorly captures organ storage/transport practices" and that misclassification is possible. "DPP" is a proxy for OCS in the US, not a measurement of it.

## 6. WHAT COULD NOT BE FOUND

No silent caps. Each of these is a question I could not close, with the instrument that would close it:

1. **Any controlled ≥3-year survival comparison for OCS in any organ.** The only 3-year data is the unmatched registry analysis (C-9). *Resolved by:* results posting for [NCT05741723](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05741723) (primary completion 2026-10) and [NCT05047068](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05047068), or an SRTR analysis with a perfusion-modality flag.
2. **Exact long-term EXPAND Lung numbers** (Loor et al, eClinicalMedicine 2025;85:103334). Lancet and PMC both returned 403. *Resolved by:* institutional full-text access or the ISHLT 2026 presentation.
3. **The National Organ Perfusion Registry AJT 2025 paper's exact N, device mix (OCS vs metra), and outcome deltas** — AJT returned 403. This is the best available independent real-world liver comparison and its device split matters enormously. *Resolved by:* full text of [AJT S1600-6135(25)00784-1](https://www.amjtransplant.org/article/S1600-6135(25)00784-1/fulltext) or the newer [Clin Transplant 2026;40(9):e70664](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42672047/).
4. **The Scorpion petition's full text and appendix** — regulations.gov and its CDN both returned 403 to me; I have its substance only through secondary summary and Scorpion's own account. *Resolved by:* direct download of FDA-2025-P-0182-0001 attachment_1.pdf, plus TransMedics'/Covington's rebuttal comments (FDA-2025-P-0182-0005).
5. **Whether FDA has issued a final citizen-petition response since the docket was last updated.** Absence of a document in a docket is not proof of absence of a decision. *Resolved by:* a direct regulations.gov docket check on the day of use, or a TMDX 8-K.
6. **What fraction of real-world OCS Liver cases fall outside the PMA label criteria** (donor >55y, WIT >30 min, macrosteatosis >15%). This is the petition's core factual allegation and I could neither confirm nor refute it. *Resolved by:* post-approval registry results ([NCT05074160](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05074160), [NCT05940857](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05940857)) or the FDA Post-Approval Studies database entry.
7. **The cause of heart's deceleration to +6% y/y.** Management gave none and was not asked about NRP (V-6). NRP substitution is a *hypothesis consistent with* the field data, not an established cause. *Resolved by:* OPTN/SRTR 2026 DCD-heart procurement-technique splits, or a direct question on the Q3-26 call.
8. **Per-case cost of OCS vs NRP vs HOPE.** No primary source publishes list prices. Every "cost-effective alternative" claim in the NRP literature is qualitative. *Resolved by:* an OPO or center contract, or a published cost-effectiveness analysis with disclosed unit costs.
9. **2026 year-to-date OPTN counts by organ, and whether the July 2025 DCD scandal dented 2026 DCD volume.** 2025 data show continued DCD growth; 2026 data were not retrievable. *Resolved by:* the OPTN national data portal.
10. **NRP's US share in 2025–2026.** Best available is Dec 2019–Mar 2024 (595 NRP / 625 DPP). The trend direction is clear; the current level is not. *Resolved by:* an updated UNOS procurement-technique analysis.
11. **Whether PMA supplements broadened the OCS Heart DBD indication beyond the >4h cross-clamp language.** The PMA summary record shows the original IFU and lists supplements S001–S009 without per-supplement IFU text. *Resolved by:* the supplement approval orders / current OCS Heart labeling in the FDA cdrh_docs library.
