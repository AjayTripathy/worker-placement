## Finding B — non-deductible parent comp → issuer tax leakage: **PARTIAL**

Confirmed verbatim: Q2-26 income tax expense **$11.091M** against a **−$23.473M** pre-tax
loss (effective rate −47.3%), attributed by management "primarily due to non-deductible
compensation expense related to Series A Interests." The desk's "no Legence cash leaves"
framing was incomplete — the aggregators settle the awards ("solely responsible … cannot
direct Legence Holdings to settle or declare distributions for such purpose", confirmed
verbatim), but the NON-DEDUCTIBILITY creates a real issuer-level permanent difference.

Bound on it: H1-26 net income tax was a **$2.29M BENEFIT** (Q1 carried a ~$13.4M benefit),
so "$11.1M/quarter leak" is NOT a run-rate — it's lumpy with the comp remeasurement. The
structural point stands: while parent comp remeasures upward, LGN's effective tax rate
carries a persistent penalty. Watch item, not a kill.
**Consequence: add to the LGN forward instrumentation — effective-rate vs statutory gap
each quarter; escalate if the permanent-difference drag exceeds ~$15M/half.**

## Finding C — sponsor stake "staleness" (mandate ~47% vs court 28.8%): **REFUTED**

The 13G/A (amendment 3, Blackstone group, filed 2026-08-07, event date 2026-06-30 —
POST the Q2 secondary) shows total Blackstone beneficial ownership of **50,647,086 shares
= 46.9%**: Legence Parent ML holds 178,571 Class A + 31,171,134 exchangeable B units;
Legence Parent II ML holds 19,297,381 Class A DIRECTLY. The mandate's 46.9% is exact and
current — it is Blackstone's own number.

The app court made two errors: (1) it took the 28.8% NCI percentage from the 10-Q equity
note (the B-unit slice only: 31.17M / 108.05M) as the sponsor's economic stake, missing
the 19.3M directly-held Class A that stayed with Blackstone through the secondary
restructuring; (2) the "13G 2026-07-29" it cited as sponsor evidence is **BlackRock's**
passive institutional filing, not Blackstone. Entity-resolution failure
(BlackRock ≠ Blackstone) — feed to the app-court improvement queue.
**Consequence: the sponsor-overhang risk is as LARGE as the desk mandate assumed (46.9%
still to distribute), and each future exchange grows the TRA liability (Finding A). The
overhang + TRA coupling is a single joint risk, not two.**
