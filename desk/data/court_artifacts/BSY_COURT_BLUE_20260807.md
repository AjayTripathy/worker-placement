## LEAD FINDING (Mode B, derived independently of both benches)

**Red identified a real-time sensor for its own hazard, then declined to read it.**

Red's kill #2 establishes — correctly — that E365 is a daily-usage meter recognized on actual consumption within the quarter. Red's kill #7 then argues NRR cannot test AI seat-compression because NRR is a last-twelve-month metric that lags the hazard by up to 12 months.

These two kills are mutually destructive. If E365 revenue is recognized on **daily user access within a calendar quarter** (FY25 10-K Note 3: *"E365 revenues are recognized based upon usage incurred by the account. Usage is primarily defined as distinct user access on a daily basis"*), then **E365 revenue is a zero-to-one-quarter-latency direct measurement of whether infrastructure engineers are still opening the applications.** Bentley discloses it every quarter in the revenue-disaggregation note.

The instrument reads:

| E365 revenue | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | Q2'25 | Q2'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $ thousands | 411,025 | 517,997 | 614,306 | 150,166 | **168,730** |
| YoY | — | +26.0% | +18.6% | — | **+12.4%** |

E365 is **46% of total ARR** (FY25 10-K Key Business Metrics; 45% FY24, 41% FY23). The daily user-day meter that red says "falls in-quarter when engineers stop opening the app" grew **+12.4% YoY in the quarter ended June 30, 2026** — versus total revenue +12.8%. Four-plus quarters into the AI-displacement narrative, on a metric with no reporting lag, usage is expanding roughly in line with the company.

This is the microstructure contribution and it survives the whole argument: **masking channel** = LTM dollar-based NRR (12-month lag, what the market watches); **signal channel** = E365 revenue in the quarterly disaggregation note (≤1 quarter lag, what almost nobody watches); **latency edge ≈ 3 quarters**. Red built the hazard model and handed over the tripwire in the same paragraph.

## FINDING RED MISSED — reported against my own side

Red never located the strongest bear fact in the filings, and I will not bury it because it favors the other bench.

**Bentley has almost no contracted backlog.** Q2'26 10-Q, Remaining Performance Obligations: *"As of June 30, 2026, amounts allocated to these remaining performance obligations are **$280,641**, of which the Company expects to recognize approximately **94%** over the next 12 months."* FY25: **$296,654**, 94% inside 12 months. That is **~19% of one year's revenue**, front-loaded — and it **declined** sequentially ($296.7M → $280.6M) while revenue grew. FY25 10-K Note 3 is explicit that *"the term of E365 subscriptions aligns with calendar quarters,"* revenue is usage-recognized rather than ratable, and CSS deposits *"may roll over to future periods **or are refundable**."*

The honest structural conclusion: **BSY's revenue durability is behavioral, not contractual.** It rests on 99% account retention, 109% NRR, 93% recurring revenue and a $496M prepaid deposit balance — not on committed backlog. That is a legitimate reason to size this position smaller than a comparably-growing multi-year-committed SaaS name. It is a *sizing* input under the response taxonomy (business-risk → size cut), not a rejection: quarterly floors bound the per-quarter downside, and 99% account retention has held through two full cycles of this concern.

Also confirmed against red: **"IIJA" / "Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act" / "Bipartisan Infrastructure" appear zero times** in the FY25 10-K, FY24 10-K, or Q2'26 10-Q (raw-HTML verified). Red's anti-finding stands and I adopt it — the infrastructure-stimulus leg of the bull case is **not** supported by BSY's own filings. What the filings do support is a diversified, sector-countercyclical demand base: 52% engineering/construction contractors, 48% infrastructure owner-operators, ~42,000 accounts in 189 countries, **no account >2% of revenue**, and sectors described as *"typically countercyclical to one another."* Q2'26 growth was *"led once again by the Resources sector, followed by Public Works / Utilities, including from the electric grid."*

**Unverified, flagged rather than filled:** I could not complete the independent labour-market work (BLS NAICS-5413 engineering-services employment, ACEC/ENR shortage data, AECOM/Jacobs/Tetra Tech headcount direction) — subagent capacity was saturated by concurrent sessions. The steelman of "infrastructure engineers are not AI-displaceable" therefore rests here on BSY's own E365 meter (+12.4%) and on management's *"close the engineering capacity gap"* framing (Q3'25 Ex-99.1) — **not** on independent employment data. Treat the vertical-differentiation argument as *supported but not independently verified*. This is a coverage gap, not a clean bill.
