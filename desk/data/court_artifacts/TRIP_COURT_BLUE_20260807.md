Both bridges close to the dollar: Experiences revenue unchanged, expenses −46 → EBITDA +46. Hotels
revenue −131, expenses −85 → EBITDA −46. **Consolidated ex-TheFork: 301+33 = 334 = 255+79. Identical.**

**The decisive line RED never computed.** Note *external* revenue is **unchanged in both segments** —
the Tripadvisor point-of-sale experiences revenue was already inside Viator's external line; what the
old presentation did was charge Viator an internal affiliate commission for it. The recast dropped that
$131M charge **and simultaneously pushed the actual direct costs of that demand into Experiences**:

| | FY2023 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Internal transfer price removed | **$130M** | **$131M** |
| Actual direct third-party cost moved *in* (marketing + personnel + COS) | **$97M** | **$85M** |
| Transfer price ÷ actual cost | **1.34×** | **1.54×** |
| Net EBITDA reallocation | $33M | $46M |

*(FY2024: $69M of marketing and $15M of personnel left Hotels & Other; $62M net of marketing and $15M of
personnel arrived in Experiences, plus $1M COS. FY2023: $80M marketing + $16M personnel + $1M G&A.)*

**Finding.** The old basis did not charge the experiences business "for the traffic it consumed" — it
charged it a transfer price **1.3–1.5× the actual cost of that traffic**. The recast substituted cost
causation for an internal markup, which under ASC 280 is the *more* economically honest presentation,
not the less. RED's inference — "never demonstrated a real margin once charged for its own demand" —
inverts which of the two presentations is the distorted one. **REFUTED.**

**Answering the court's question directly** ("if Viator had to buy that traffic externally, what is the
true standalone cost?"): the primary record now bounds it twice, and both bounds land in the same place.
The *accounting* answer is $85M/yr (FY2024 actual direct cost). The *observed market* answer is the
2026 replacement spend — Experiences marketing is running **+$18.6M in Q2 / +$31.0M in H1**, i.e. a
$62–74M annualized step-up, while Experiences bookings still grew +5% (Q2) / +7% (H1). **Both routes say
~$70–85M/yr, not $131M — and decisively not RED's implied "no cost at all."** RED's Viator haircut is
sized off a number ~55–90% too large.

## 3. The macro one-offs, quantified

The issuer's language, verbatim (10-Q 2026-08-06, MD&A, "Macro-Environment Headwinds"):

> "During the first half of 2026, specifically beginning in late February and throughout March, the
> conflict in the Middle East, severe flooding in Hawaii and civil unrest in Mexico, two key destination
> markets, as well as weather-related events and travel conditions throughout May and June within Europe
> and the U.S., had an adverse impact on our Experiences business. **Although Experiences showed growth
> across all reported measures through February**, as a result of these macro-events, during the first
> half of 2026, we did observe a negative impact on booking volumes, gross booking value, and revenue, as
> well as an increase in cancellation rates across all points of sale in our Experiences segment."

**The sentence RED did not quote, and it cuts against the steelman:**

> "**We also expect that heightened geopolitical tensions and conflicts, including evolving events in the
> Middle East, may continue to have an impact on the travel industry and, as a result, continue to impact
> our financial results in the second half of the year.**"

So management attributes the Experiences weakness to macro **and** tells you the macro does not clear in
H2. The steelman survives on *cause* and dies on *timing*. That is an adverse fact RED missed while
arguing the same side.

**Quantification.** Bookings — the tightest disclosed series (0.1M granularity) — decompose as:

| | Q1-2026 | Q2-2026 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experience bookings growth | **~+9.4%** (derived, H1 12.1M − Q2 6.5M) | **+5.0%** | +16% |
| GBV growth | ~+11% (coarse, $0.1B rounding) | **+3%** | +13% |

The Q1 print corroborates "growth across all reported measures through February." But the honest
allocation is this: **of the $50.7M H1 continuing-EBITDA decline, $38.5M (76%) is Hotels & Other, which
is attributed to SEO/AI-overviews and NOT to macro.** Experiences accounts for $12.2M. Even crediting
the entire Experiences decline to macro — generous, since marketing inflation is in there too — **the
one-offs explain at most ~20–25% of the −$50.7M.** RED's B8 rebuttal (iii) is right: macro does not
touch Hotels, and Hotels is three-quarters of the damage. Directly measured cancellation refunds were
only **$4.6M (H1-26) vs $3.2M (H1-25)**, a $1.4M delta — small, though it captures only deferred-revenue
refunds, not bookings that never converted.

**Ruling: the macro steelman is real but bounded, and it does not rescue the thesis. UPHELD for RED.**

## 4. The Airbnb undercut

*Note: RED's declared coverage gap (GetYourGuide / Klook financials UNVERIFIABLE, web budget exhausted)

RED's own sourcing already contains the rebuttal, and I re-pulled it from the primary rather than

> "Our revenue consists of service fees, net of incentives and refunds, charged to our customers. For
> stays, service fees… vary based on factors specific to the booking… **For experiences and services, we

*http://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1559720/000155972026000004/abnb-20251231.htm*

Airbnb discloses **no** Experiences revenue, GBV, or booking-dollar line anywhere in the 10-K — the

attention threat, not yet a revenue threat** — which RED itself concedes in #18b ("take-rate threat, not
yet a disclosed revenue threat"). Note also the framing in Airbnb's own competition section: it lists
*"online platforms offering experiences and activities, such as **Viator**, GetYourGuide, and Klook"* as
one bullet among six categories of competitor — Airbnb is the **entrant** to Viator's category, not the
incumbent being displaced.

### 4.1 A correction against BLUE's own first draft — the category defence FAILS

My initial rebuttal was that Airbnb Experiences is host-led activity inventory (cooking classes, walking
tours) and therefore a different category from Viator's attraction-admission and skip-the-line ticket
business. **I dispatched that question to primary observation and it came back against me. I am
withdrawing the argument.**

Airbnb now sells operator-supplied, ticket-only, timed-entry attraction admission — the exact Viator SKU.
Live on 2026-08-07: **"Orsay Museum Entrance Ticket Only"**, $28, under a page section headed
*"Ticket highlights"*, supplied not by an individual local host but by a tour operator ("Paris Access…
*We host unique Parisian experiences for you, offering premium tours and activities*"), with reviews
dated the same day (https://www.airbnb.com/experiences/6710659). Also observed: *"Vatican Museums &
Sistine Chapel: Skip-Line Ticket"* $64, *"Colosseum: Skip the Line Ticket & Audio Guide"* $51
(*"timed-entry tickets"*), Borghese Gallery skip-the-line, Versailles fast entry, "Disneyland® Paris:
1 Day 2 Parks with Transport" $237, and multi-hour coach excursions (Pompeii/Amalfi from Rome). The
search UI now carries a dedicated **"Tickets"** filter.

Two corroborating disclosure tells, both from Airbnb's own filings, that RED did not surface and that
**strengthen RED's leg**: Airbnb's competitor list **named** Viator/GetYourGuide/Klook in the FY2023
10-K, **removed all names** in the FY2024 10-K, and **restored Viator, GetYourGuide and Klook** in the
FY2025 10-K; and the headline metric was renamed from "Nights and **Experiences** Booked" to "Nights and
**Seats** Booked." That arc is consistent with a dormant category being deliberately revived and
re-pointed at Viator. It also sits awkwardly against Airbnb's own May-2025 relaunch positioning, which
explicitly disclaimed exactly this product: *"Too often you end up in a big group, following a tour
guide with a megaphone, doing activities that feel like you're checking a box."* Fifteen months later
they are selling ticket-only Musée d'Orsay admission.

### 4.2 What survives — the threat is real in kind, unquantified and small in degree

The rebuttal that does hold is **scale and monetization**, and it holds firmly:

1. **The +80% is off an undisclosed base, and every absolute number available says that base is tiny.**
   The only absolute supply figures Airbnb has ever published are category-level, from its 2026 Summer
   Release: *"more than 3,000 landmark experiences"* and *"more than 2,500 food culture experiences"* —
   its two self-described most in-demand categories. Viator advertises **"400,000+ travel experiences"**
   and lists **1,651 products against the Colosseum alone**. Live city counts: Airbnb Rome **871**,
   Paris **1,151**; Viator caps its Rome display at "1,000+" before attraction-level drill-down.
2. **Equal GBV would not be equal revenue.** Host-fee-only versus Viator's merchant-of-record commission.
   This is the leg of RED's #18b that is correct and it is a genuine long-run take-rate threat.
3. **Airbnb's own filings still say the category is immaterial**: *"Substantially all of the bookings on
   our platform to date have come from nights"*; one operating segment, revenue disaggregated by
   **geography only**, no product-line split in the FY2025 10-K or the Q2-2026 10-Q.
4. **The disclosure asymmetry is the tell.** In the same Q2-2026 letter Airbnb quantifies hotel
   materiality — *"While hotels still represent a single-digit percentage of nights booked"* — and
   pointedly declines to quantify Experiences, offering only "thousands of new," "nearly 80%," and
   "accelerating." A company that quantifies when the number helps, and does not when it does not.
5. **Viator's own point of sale is still growing.** The 10-Q attributes H1 bookings growth to *"growth on
   Viator and third-party points of sale"* — the opposite of share loss where Airbnb would take it.

**Ruling: WEAKENED — but less than I first argued, and RED's category premise is better than RED
itself established.** Anyone claiming Airbnb is not in Viator's category is now wrong on the product
facts. Anyone claiming +80% means Airbnb is taking Viator's market is extrapolating from a base Airbnb
refuses to disclose. This is a **credible directional threat of undisclosed and probably currently
immaterial magnitude** — a reason to hold the Experiences multiple down, not a reason to reject.
