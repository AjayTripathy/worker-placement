## BLUE BENCH — CORRECTION TO RULINGS D AND (vi)

The completed disclosure trace returned a fuller record than my own pass. Two of my rulings were wrong and I reverse them.

**RULING D — I over-corrected. Partially reinstated.**

My narrow findings stand: "National Crime Agency" is **0 in FY23, FY24, FY25 and FY26** annual reports (all four obtained complete, page-verified), so red's "scrubbed from the FY26 AR" is still **REFUTED as framed** — it was never there. And the sensitivity-caveat deletion is still explained by the provision being settled.

But red was prosecuting the wrong year, and so was I. The real finding is **FY25**:

- The NCA notified Braemar in **late January 2025** — *before* the 28-Feb-2025 balance-sheet date (company's own 11-Jun-2025 RNS).
- FY25 accounts were approved 28-May-2025 with Note 7.4 (p.145) stating: *"There were no other adjusting or significant non-adjusting events between the reporting date and the date these Financial Statements were authorised for issue."*
- **Fourteen days later** the company announced, flagged as **inside information under MAR**, that the NCA had been granted an account freezing order and that it had been on notice since January.

If it was price-sensitive on 11-Jun, it was material in February. That is a takeaway-vs-data divergence in audited accounts — **CONFIRMED**, and it is a better finding than red's.

Compounding it: FY25 said *"No significant further costs are expected in FY26"*; costs then **doubled** (£185k→£381k), and FY26 neither repeats the assurance nor acknowledges the miss (cumulative £3.2M). A FY26-only reader cannot see that a law-enforcement freeze existed.

**Revised ruling: SUSTAINED in substance, on different evidence.** Not fatal — £2.0M provision, fully paid, no solvency or going-concern implication on a £73.7M company. But it is a management-narrative discount, and it bites precisely where this thesis is exposed: the bull case requires trusting management's "in line" against a peer's "materially ahead."
