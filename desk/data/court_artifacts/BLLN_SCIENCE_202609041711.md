## 0. THE TECHNICAL QUESTION

**Whether QCT molecular counting resolves fetal allele *dosage* with enough precision at early gestational ages to make single-gene cfDNA screening a *primary* screen — because that claim is (a) the mechanism behind ~86% of revenue, (b) the entire premise of the 130-gene expansion, and (c) the one claim that two national payers and ACOG independently decline to endorse today.**

A second, separable question rides underneath it: whether the Q2 sequential ASP decline that the upstream stage read as *price erosion* is instead a revenue-recognition timing artifact. **It is** — the 10-Q settles it arithmetically, and the correction cuts in both directions. See §4.1, which I put first among the traps because the upstream artifact rests a conclusion on it.

## 2. THE MECHANISM

### 2.1 What is established science

Cell-free DNA in maternal plasma is a mixture: ~85–95% maternal (leukocyte-derived), the remainder placental. Counting molecules at a locus to infer fetal genotype is textbook — the **relative haplotype dosage** principle (Lo et al., 2010). Aneuploidy NIPT is the mature commercial expression of it and is guideline-endorsed. Nothing about BLLN's aneuploidy screen is scientifically contested.

### 2.2 Where the novel claim sits

For a **recessive** condition, the mother is a heterozygous carrier. Her own cfDNA contributes mutant and wild-type alleles at exactly 1:1. The fetal contribution perturbs that ratio, and **the size of the perturbation is bounded by the fetal fraction**:

- Fetus wild-type/wild-type → mutant fraction ≈ 0.5 − FF/2
- Fetus heterozygous → mutant fraction ≈ 0.5
- Fetus homozygous mutant → mutant fraction ≈ 0.5 + FF/2

At a fetal fraction of 8%, the entire dynamic range separating "unaffected" from "affected" is **±4 percentage points around 0.5**. That is the whole assay. QCT's contribution is that spiked synthetic counting templates let you measure that ratio with counting statistics rather than a signal-intensity estimate — a genuine advance, and it is what makes the claim non-frivolous.

**The physical ceiling is fetal fraction, and fetal fraction is a function of gestational age.** It rises from roughly 4–10% at 9–11 weeks toward 10–20% by 18–20 weeks. Below ~4% the ratio shift is smaller than achievable precision and the result is either a no-call or, worse, a call toward the null — **a false negative.**

This is not a theoretical concern. In the one peer-reviewed head-to-head case available ([AJP Reports 2026;16(2):e90–e92](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13095397/)), Unity was drawn at **11 weeks** and returned decreased risk; Natera Fetal Focus was drawn at **19 weeks** and returned high risk; the fetus was **homozygous F508del**. Both parents were known F508del carriers and there was a prior affected child — the highest-prior case imaginable. The company's stated limitation ("rare homozygous variants in *CFTR*") does not cover it: F508del is >60% of US *CFTR* alleles, and a homozygous fetus produces the *largest* available ratio shift. A miss here is a failure of the counting mechanism at low fetal fraction, not a panel-coverage gap.

**The commercial incentive runs against the physics.** The product is marketed for draws "as early as nine weeks." Earlier draw = more clinical utility = more addressable volume = lower fetal fraction = lower sensitivity. Benches should treat gestational-age-at-draw as the primary suspected effect modifier and note that **no published Unity performance stratified by gestational age or fetal fraction was located this session** (§6).

### 2.3 Class-inherent vs. implementation-specific failure modes

| Failure mode | Class-inherent or implementation? | Named precedent |
|---|---|---|
| Low fetal fraction → false negative | **Class-inherent** to all cfDNA prenatal testing | Natera's own Q2-26 relaunch of Panorama was explicitly to address "the low fetal fraction gap" ([Natera Q2-26 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/natera-ntra-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)) — a competitor conceding the class limit |
| Confined placental mosaicism → discordance | **Class-inherent** (cfDNA is placental, not fetal) | Long-documented for aneuploidy NIPT; applies to any cfDNA-derived fetal inference |
| Paternal variant not on panel → residual risk survives a "low risk" result | **Implementation** — direct consequence of the no-paternal-sample design | Unity computes fetal risk from maternal dosage + a *population* paternal prior; the AJP authors' recommendation to counsel on limitations follows from this |
| "100% sensitivity" marketing outrunning the data | **Implementation** | The historical analogue is the 2014–2022 microdeletion-NIPT episode, where marketed detection rates far exceeded realized PPV; ACOG/ACMG response was to withhold endorsement — the same posture now applied to single-gene NIPT (C1/C3/C5) |
| Clonal hematopoiesis → false-positive somatic calls in liquid CGP | **Class-inherent** to all plasma CGP; *amplified* by pushing LOD down | DNMT3A/TET2/ASXL1/**TP53**/ATM/CHEK2 are canonical CH genes and are on essentially every CGP panel ([PMC8572768](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8572768/)) |

### 2.4 Reconciliation: do the 13 CF false negatives fit the 94.4% sensitivity?

Yes — and this matters, because it means the false negatives are **the assay performing to spec**, not a manufacturing excursion.

US CF incidence is ~1 in 3,500 live births; carrier-couple arithmetic in a predominantly non-Hispanic-white cohort gives roughly 1 in 2,500. Across ~650,000 pregnancies that implies **~186–260 expected CF-affected pregnancies**. Thirteen misses is a **5.0%–7.0% miss rate → 93.0%–95.0% sensitivity**, bracketing the 94.4% figure in the Green Journal paper.

The finding is therefore not "the test broke." It is that **the test's true sensitivity has been ~94% throughout, while "100% sensitivity" was in physician-facing marketing.** Those are different claims with different consequences, and benches should not conflate them.

## 4. MEASUREMENT TRAPS

### 4.1 ⚠️ The upstream artifact's central finding does not survive the 10-Q — and the correction is two-sided

The upstream stage concluded: *"volume still compounds but price per test is rolling over,"* built on blended ASP falling ~$571 → $551 (−3.5%) and sequential revenue of +0.9%. **Both inputs are contaminated by prior-period revenue true-ups, which the 10-Q quantifies.**

First, the ASP *definition* — reconstructed and confirmed. "Overall ASP" is **(prenatal + oncology) ÷ tests delivered**; it **excludes** clinical-trial-support revenue:

- Q2-26: (94.2 + 13.7) ÷ 196,000 = **$550.5** → reported $551 ✓
- H1-26: (190.8 + 24.4) ÷ 384,000 = **$560.4** → reported $561 ✓
- Q1-26 (derived): 107.2 ÷ 188,000 = **$570.2** → the upstream's ~$571 is correct ✓

Now apply D1. The 10-Q discloses revenue from performance obligations satisfied in **prior** periods: **$2.8M in Q2-26 and $12.0M in H1-26 ⇒ $9.2M in Q1-26** — a **−$6.4M sequential swing** in non-current-period revenue.

| Metric | As reported | **Current-period basis** |
|---|---|---|
| Q1-26 revenue | $108.4M | **$99.2M** |
| Q2-26 revenue | $109.4M | **$106.6M** |
| **Sequential growth** | **+0.9%** | **+7.5%** |
| Q1-26 ASP | $570 | **$521** |
| Q2-26 ASP | $551 | **$536** |
| **Sequential ASP** | **−$20 (−3.5%)** | **+$15 (+2.9%)** |

My reconstruction yields **+$14.9**; management said "+$15." **Management's characterization is verified, not spin.** The "price per test is rolling over" claim is refuted by the company's own ASC 606 disclosure.

Pushing it into the segment that matters: the true-ups arose from 2025 claims, and 2025 revenue was ~90%+ prenatal, so they are prenatal-dominant. Under a proportional split, and again under an all-prenatal split:

- Reported prenatal revenue **fell −2.5% q/q** ($96.6M → $94.2M). *This is the sharper version of the upstream finding, and it is also an artifact.*
- Current-period prenatal revenue **grew +3.9% to +4.6% q/q**.
- Implied **prenatal ASP was approximately flat** (±$2 across oncology-ASP assumptions from $1,500 to $3,000), on prenatal volume up ~3.4–3.7%.

**And the correction cuts the other way on the guide.** H1 revenue of $217.8M contained $12.0M of prior-period true-up. The H2 requirement is unchanged at $232.2–247.2M, but the base it must be compared against is **$205.8M**, not $217.8M:

- Upstream's stated hurdle: **+6.6% to +13.5%** half-on-half
- Corrected, current-period hurdle: **+12.8% to +20.1%**
- Even allowing H2 true-ups to continue at the Q2 run-rate (~$5.6M): **+10.1% to +17.4%**

**Net for the benches: the mechanism the upstream named is wrong, and correcting it makes the guidance hurdle roughly twice as demanding as stated.** A bench that kills on "ASP erosion" is killing on an artifact. A bench that defends on "the guide is conservative" is defending against a hurdle that is larger than the upstream document says.

### 4.2 Do not compare 94.4% to 96% — the intervals swamp the gap

BLLN's 94.4% is almost certainly **17/18** affected pregnancies (Wilson 95% CI ≈ **74%–99%**). Natera's 96% is **24/25** (Wilson 95% CI ≈ **80%–99%**). Any claim that either test is more sensitive than the other is unsupported by these data. Additional non-comparabilities:

- **Outcome ascertainment differs**: Natera states confirmatory diagnostic testing on *all* positive *and negative* cases; BLLN's release says outcomes "collected" for 98.6%. Unconfirmed negatives systematically **inflate** apparent sensitivity and NPV. This is the single most important asymmetry in §3.1.
- **Specificity denominators differ**: BLLN's 99.5% is across 2,212 carriers; Natera reports both a 94.2% *observed* specificity in a 193-sample enriched cohort and a 98% *population-weighted* specificity across 294. Observed and population-weighted specificity are different estimands; do not place them in the same column mentally.
- **Different gene sets**: 4 conditions (BLLN pivotal) vs. 21 genes (Natera). Sensitivity is not gene-transferable.

### 4.3 "51% more actionable alterations" is not a sensitivity result

The claim is that most of the excess calls sit **below the comparator assays' 95% LOD**. Calls below a comparator's LOD are, by construction, unconfirmable by that comparator — so the comparison cannot distinguish **true low-VAF ctDNA** from **clonal hematopoiesis**, the canonical false-positive source in plasma CGP (DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1, **TP53**, ATM, CHEK2 — [PMC8572768](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8572768/)). The resolving instrument is **paired white-blood-cell sequencing** or a validated CH classifier, and whether Northstar Select performs either was not established this session (§6). A bench should not treat "51% more alterations" as either a strength or a weakness without that fact.

### 4.4 The 130-gene number is not comparable to Natera's 21

**Carrier-screening panel size and cfDNA-fetal-risk panel size are different quantities.** Unity's architecture is: maternal carrier screening → cfDNA fetal risk *when the mother is a carrier*. A 130-gene carrier panel does not entail 130-gene fetal-risk capability, and I could not establish the fetal-risk gene count (A9). Natera's "21 genes" is explicitly a **fetal-risk** count with per-gene performance data. **Comparing 130 to 21 is very likely comparing a carrier panel to a fetal-risk panel.** Note also management's own economic guidance: *"we do not expect much of a difference in the economics with respect to the 130-gene panel… in terms of reimbursement or the COGS"* — a panel that quadruples the addressable ACMG-Tier-3 conversation without changing per-test price.

### 4.5 Tissue-of-origin accuracy figures are almost never the same metric

Origin's **87% top-1 / 91% top-3** is measured on a cohort of 1,578 patients where a cancer diagnosis already exists. CUPiD's headline "**TOO accuracy 96.8%**" is conditioned on **84.6% sensitivity** — i.e., accuracy *among cases the classifier detected*, a materially easier denominator ([Nat Commun 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-47195-7)). GRAIL's Cancer Signal Origin is measured in an asymptomatic *screening* population, a third estimand. **Number of classes also differs** (CUPiD: 29 classes; Origin: unspecified). Any cross-vendor TOO comparison built on these numbers is invalid.

### 4.6 Tissue-free vs. tumor-informed is a specificity/logistics trade, not a quality ranking

Northstar Response (tissue-free methylation) needs no tumor tissue and no bespoke panel build — faster to first result, usable when tissue is exhausted. RaDaR ST (tumor-informed, 48 patient-specific variants from WES) buys specificity by anchoring on variants pre-verified as tumor-derived, at the cost of tissue requirement and turnaround. These are legitimately different products; a bench arguing one "beats" the other on a single metric is comparing across estimands.

### 4.7 Unit and source hygiene

- **Transcripts are unreliable here.** Two independent errors found: prenatal revenue "$95.8M" vs. the filings' $94.2M (D4), and "Unity **Fetal Antigen** Screen expanded to 130 genes" (Fetal Antigen covers 6–7 antigens; the 130-gene expansion is **Fetal Risk**). Quote the 8-K and 10-Q.
- **Unity Confirm's "100% concordance" is 16 of 16.** The 95% CI on 16/16 extends below 80%. It is a feasibility result described as a validation.
- **Northstar Origin's data has not been presented.** The release says "submitted for presentation." A guided readout for an unpresented dataset is a flag, not a result.
