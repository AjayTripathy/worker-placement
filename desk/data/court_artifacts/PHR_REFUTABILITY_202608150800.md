## The channel error in the priced narrative

The FDA's proposed rule (RIN 0910-AJ14) eliminates the "adequate provision" option for **broadcast** DTC ads — HHS/FDA concede it would make TV ads prohibitively long and expensive ([Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/fda-proposes-to-eliminate-adequate-provision-standard-for-dtc-broadcast-advertising)). Killing broadcast economics **redirects** pharma promotional dollars toward measurable non-broadcast channels — point-of-care being the purest one. On the *rule*, PHR is a share-gainer, not a victim.

What actually hit PHR is the other prong: ~100 cease-and-desist letters plus thousands of untitled letters ([FDA, 2025-09-09](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-crackdown-deceptive-drug-advertising)), which lengthens legal review and shortens brand commitment windows. Management's own words are a *visibility* symptom, not a demand symptom: "shorter visibility into spending commitments," clients "committing lower spend levels for the second half," and explicitly "we do not believe these developments are signaling a structural shift in demand." A budgeting-cycle effect and a demand-destruction effect look identical for two quarters and diverge completely thereafter. That divergence is what a court would have to price.
