## VERDICT (up front)

**RECOMMEND: REJECT the long. Conviction 8/10.**

The triage asked whether this is the "honest-cut-oversold" profile — the AGL-inverse. It is not. It is the
opposite: **an honest-disclosure company whose marketed takeaway diverges materially from its own data**,
and a triage that got captured by the company's chosen framing.

The triage's entire refutation rested on one metric — new student starts +10.9% — which is the metric the
press-release **headline** foregrounds. That metric has been structurally decoupled from revenue by a program
mix shift that management itself blames for 30% of the guidance cut. And the "13% EBITDA cut" the triage
graded severity on is the **smallest** of the six numbers management moved.

Three findings kill the long. Each is from a primary source, and each was available to the triage.

1. **The "investment timing" defense is refuted by the company's own ex-investment metric.** Baseline
   Adjusted EBITDA — which strips out 100% of new-campus and new-program spend *by construction* — was cut
   from ~$156M to ~$135M (−12.5%), and now implies **+1.7% growth on +7.3% revenue**, an **83bp margin
   contraction**. If the cut were investment phasing, this number holds by definition. It didn't.
2. **The drawdown is not disproportionate — it decomposes almost exactly.** −12.9% numbers × −24.6%
   multiple = **−34.3%** versus the observed **−34.2%**. The multiple de-rate is *earned*: this is UTI's
   first guidance cut after beating in FY24 and FY25, and a 20× EV/EBITDA beat-and-raise multiple does not
   survive the loss of beat-and-raise status.
3. **After −34% it is not cheap, and it is not washed out.** 15.0× EV/EBITDA and 46× EPS on the cut guide,
   with **negative** free cash flow. On trailing EBITDA it is **more expensive today (12.1×) than it was
   twelve months ago (10.9×)**. It is +6.7% YTD and 31% above its own 52-week low of $21.29.

The business is real, demand at the UTI brand is genuinely strong, and ~70% of the cut is a specific,
identified, already-remediated staffing failure. This is **not a short**. It is a **price and framing
rejection at $27.88**.

# MODE B — FIRST PRINCIPLES (led, per doctrine)

*Derived with no promoter framing, hunting disconfirming evidence.*

## B1. The refuting metric the triage chose is contaminated by the cut it was used to refute

The triage nominated **total new student starts** as the metric that refutes a demand break. Management
nominated the same metric — it is the press-release headline:

> "Total New Student Start Growth Exceeded Expectations Driven by Strong Demand and Continued Momentum
> Across New Campuses, Reinforcing Confidence in Long-Term North Star Targets"
> — 8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-08-05

A "start" is a unit of enrollment, not a unit of revenue. Its revenue content is program length. On the
Q3 call, CFO Bruce Schuman told Eric Wold (Texas Capital) that the difference between the growing programs
and the shrinking ones is exactly that: **skilled-trades programs run ~9 months; auto/diesel run ~51 weeks.**
And CEO Jerome Grant told Griffin Boss (B. Riley) the shift is genuine and accelerating — *"if traditionally
5% of the students picked going into one of the skilled trades... that is no longer that way."*

A start migrating from auto/diesel to skilled trades is counted at par while carrying roughly **a quarter less
revenue**. That is precisely what the data shows:

| Metric (Q3 FY26 y/y) | Value |
|---|---|
| Total new student starts | **+10.9%** |
| Average full-time active students | **+5.8%** |
| Revenue | **+7.2%** |
| FY26 starts guide (midpoint) | **−0.5%** |
| FY26 revenue guide (midpoint) | **−1.5%** |

Starts and revenue have **decoupled**, and the guide cut revenue while holding starts. Management attributes
**~30% of the $20M baseline EBITDA cut to "the mix piece"** (Schuman, to Steven Frankel/Rosenblatt).

**So the metric the triage used to refute the cut is itself a named cause of the cut.** This is framing
capture: the triage adopted the issuer's chosen headline metric as its independent control. Starts growth is
now a **degraded predictor** of UTI revenue, and mix is **permanent** — it is a change in what students want
to study, not a timing item.

*Verdict: the triage's refutation does not survive contact with the segment and program-length data.*

## B2. The company's own ex-investment metric refutes the company's own "timing" defense

This is the decisive finding.

Management's defense (CFO Schuman, 8-K Ex-99.1):
> "Importantly, these adjustments reflect largely **timing** and, to a lesser degree, mix considerations,
> rather than a change in the underlying demand environment."

UTI reports a metric called **Baseline Adjusted EBITDA**, defined in its own deck as *"Reported Adj EBITDA
excluding growth investments,"* where growth investments are *"expenses specifically associated with opening
new campuses and programs."* This metric exists **precisely to isolate the base business from investment
phasing**. If the cut were timing, baseline holds by construction.

Verified from primary sources (all figures the company's own):

| | Revenue | Baseline Adj. EBITDA | Baseline margin | Growth inv. | Reported Adj. EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 actual | $835.6M | **$132.8M** | **15.89%** | $(6.3)M | $126.5M |
| FY2026E — original plan | ~$910M | **~$156M** | **17.14%** | $(40)M | $114–119M |
| FY2026E — revised | $896.5M | **~$135M** | **15.06%** | $(35)M | $100–103M |
| FY2029E — target | $1,200M+ | **~$270M** | **22.50%** | $(50)M | ~$220M |

Sources: FY25/FY26E/FY29E bridge — Q3 FY26 investor presentation, verbatim slide strings `"132.8 ($6.3) $126.5"`
and `"~$135.0 ~($35.0) ~$101.5"` and `"Approaching $270 ... Approaching $220"`
(https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1261654/000126165426000015/q3fy2026investorpresenta.htm).
Original FY26 baseline ~$156M — Q1 FY26 8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-02-04
(https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1261654/000126165426000003/exhibit991-q12026earningsp.htm):
*"baseline adjusted EBITDA of approximately $156 million... inclusive of approximately $40 million of growth investments."*

**What this proves:**

- Baseline Adjusted EBITDA was cut **−12.5%** — statistically identical to the −12.9% reported cut. **The
  base business was marked down just as hard as the reported number.**
- Growth investment spend went **DOWN**, $40M → $35M. So the cut is not "we invested more."
- Baseline EBITDA now grows **+1.7%** on **+7.3%** revenue. **Baseline margin contracts 83bp.** The base
  business, with every dollar of new-campus cost already removed, generated **no operating leverage at all**.
- Versus the original plan (+125bp expansion), that is a **208bp swing in the ex-investment margin**.

**And the CFO's own bridge attributes none of it to investment timing.** To Steven Frankel (Rosenblatt):

> "The adjusted EBITDA numbers we had had in our guide originally was north of $155. We're now saying that
> baseline EBITDA is going to be about $135... That **$20 million delta, 70%, roughly, is directly related to
> the auto-diesel high school starts miss**... About **30% is the mix piece**."

~$14M operating shortfall + ~$6M permanent mix. **$0 investment timing.** The word "timing" in the CFO's
written quote is not supported by the CFO's own spoken bridge.

*Verdict: **REFUTED.** The marketed takeaway ("largely timing") diverges from the company's own segregated data.*

## B3. The "costs unwind as campuses fill" bull case is contradicted by the company's own plan

The natural long thesis is: growth investment is front-loaded, campuses fill, the drag rolls off, margin
snaps back. **UTI's own deck says the opposite.** The growth-investment layer does not unwind — it *grows*:

**$6.3M (FY25) → $35M (FY26E) → ~$50M (FY29E)** — a further **+43% from here**.

The FY29 margin recovery is not built on the drag disappearing. It is built on **baseline EBITDA doubling**
($135M → ~$270M) so that a *larger* drag becomes proportionally smaller. That requires baseline margin to
expand from 15.06% to 22.50% — **+744bp over three years** — in a year when baseline margin just **contracted
83bp**. The FY29 revenue build the company discloses is *"~3% Same Store Volume + 2-3% Tuition Increases +
~5% Growth Initiatives,"* i.e. roughly half the growth is new campuses, which is why the investment layer is
structurally permanent.

*Verdict: the bull's "investment timing" mechanism is **not the company's own mechanism**. Anyone underwriting
"the costs roll off" is underwriting something management has not guided.*

## B9. Enrollment quality — the deterioration the narrative does not mention

Priority (5) asked what the market may know that the filing does not say. It is in the filing — in the cash
flow statement, not the narrative:

| 9M ended 6/30 | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Provision for credit losses** | **$22.403M** | **$15.063M** | **+48.7%** |
| Revenue | $661.153M | $613.174M | +7.8% |
| Provision as % of revenue | **3.39%** | **2.46%** | **+93bp** |
| Student receivables (incl. notes + retail installment contracts) | $104.524M | $98.815M *(9/30/25)* | +5.8% |

**The provision grew 48.7% while the receivable book grew 5.8% and revenue grew 7.8%.** Annualized provision
against gross student receivables moved from **~20.3% to ~28.6%**. Charge-off *intensity per dollar of
receivable* is rising sharply — the incremental cohort is materially less creditworthy, which is the
signature of buying enrollment growth with in-house credit.

Corroborating at the segment line: UTI's **"student related"** expense rose **+60.7%** ($7.671M → $12.326M)
in a quarter when UTI average active students rose only **+4.0%**. That gap is not volume.

*This is disclosed but unmentioned in the release narrative, the CEO quote, the CFO quote, and the deck
highlights. It is the cleanest single indicator that the celebrated starts growth carries lower quality.*

## B10. Concorde — 37% of revenue, and its demand is breaking

The "strong demand" claim is segment-blind. Split it:

| Q3 FY26 y/y | UTI segment | Concorde segment |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $138.0M, **+5.0%** | $80.9M, **+11.1%** |
| New student starts | 3,491, **+23.4%** | 2,851, **−1.4%** |
| Average active students | 14,767, **+4.0%** | 10,364, **+8.5%** |
| Advertising | $16.243M, +8.2% | $9.696M, **+28.7%** |
| **Advertising per start** | **$5,305 → $4,653 (−12.3%)** | **$2,605 → $3,401 (+30.5%)** |
| Leads (per Q3 call) | **+15%** | **+22%** |
| Adj. EBITDA | $14.284M, **−41.1%** | $6.400M, +95.7% |

**Concorde generated +22% more leads and produced 1.4% fewer starts, while paying 30.5% more advertising per
start.** Lead-to-start conversion fell roughly **19% per lead**. That is a demand-quality break in the
healthcare/allied-health business — 37% of consolidated revenue — and it is nowhere in the "demand is intact"
narrative. The prior-year comp makes it worse, not better: Concorde starts grew **+9.1%** in Q3 FY25, so this
is a genuine inflection, not a hard comp.

Note the honest counterweight: **UTI-brand marketing efficiency genuinely improved** (−12.3% cost per start),
and UTI-brand demand is real. The demand story is true for 63% of revenue and false for 37%.

# CONVICTION & RECOMMENDATION

**CONVICTION: 8/10 — REJECT the long at $27.88.**

**Why 8 and not 10.** Three things genuinely cut against me and I will not paper over them:
- **Management's track record is real.** They beat in FY24 and FY25, including revenue $20.6M above the high
  end. This is not a serial disappointer, and first-cut de-rates in credible operators do sometimes overshoot.
- **~70% of the cut has a specific, identified, cheap, already-executed fix** (admissions reps ~140 → ~170).
  If it converts in Q1 FY27, FY27 could recover most of the $14M.
- **The balance sheet is not distressed** (net cash, 77% of capex discretionary growth spend, ~$72M
  maintenance FCF if the OCF guide holds), and the new-campus playbook is genuinely working — Atlanta opened
  ~30% ahead of plan.

**Why 8 and not 5.** The kill does not depend on any of the above being wrong:
- The ex-investment margin is contracting (**−83bp**) with every dollar of growth investment already removed.
  The staffing fix does not address this.
- The investment drag **grows** to $50M by FY29 on the company's own plan — the bull's mechanism is not
  management's mechanism.
- ~30% of the cut is **permanent mix**, and management says the shift is accelerating.
- **LINC raised guidance three ways and grew EBITDA +85% through the same campus drag in the same window.**
  The structural excuse is gone; what remains is an execution gap.
- The drawdown is **fully explained** (−34.3% modeled vs. −34.2% actual). There is no dislocation.
- **After the crash it is more expensive on trailing EBITDA than it was a year ago** (12.1× vs. 10.9×), it is
  **up 6.7% YTD**, free cash flow is **negative**, and it sits at a premium to every profitable peer.
- Enrollment quality is deteriorating (**provision +48.7%**) and Concorde's conversion is breaking (**−19%
  per lead**) — neither disclosed in the narrative, both in the filings.
- **Concorde is losing starts in the fastest-growing occupational market in the portfolio** (medical
  assistants +12%). A demand tailwind you cannot convert is a competitive-position problem, not a timing one.

**This is a REJECT, not a short.** The demand is real, the disclosure is complete, and the fix is plausible.
There is simply nothing to buy: you are paying a growth multiple, in cash-burn, for a business whose
ex-investment earnings power just went sideways, on the first broken promise from a team whose multiple was
built on keeping them.

## Reconciliation to the triage

The triage was right to flag this 7/10 and right that demand did not break at the UTI brand. It made two
correctable errors, both worth encoding:

1. **Framing capture.** It adopted the issuer's headline metric (starts) as its independent refuting control.
   The recall-floor lesson: **a refuting metric must be chosen adversarially — never the one the press-release
   headline leads with.** Test the candidate metric for contamination by the cause of the cut before nominating it.
2. **Severity graded on the wrong line.** It sized the event on Adjusted EBITDA (−12.9%), which was the
   *smallest* of six guidance moves, and **never surfaced the FCF sign flip (−$32.5M) or the −46.5% y/y EPS
   decline**. Standing fix: **grade a guidance cut on the full guidance table — every line, at midpoints,
   including cash — and always compute the y/y change, not just the guide-to-guide change.** A −12.9%
   guide-to-guide EBITDA cut concealed a −46.5% y/y EPS collapse.

Both are matcher-level fixes, not judgment calls, and should be wired into the velocity-triage template.
