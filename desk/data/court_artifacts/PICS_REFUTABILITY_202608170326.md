### Print-decisive reconstruction — the flow/stock contradiction

The loudest bull claim is **"cost of risk is stable at 3.7%, guided 3.7–3.9%."** The loudest bear claim is **"the credit models are deficient and formation is running ahead of provisioning"** — which is also the litigation's premise. Both are resolvable from the **June 2 print alone**; no Q2 data is needed.

Cost of risk is a **flow** ratio (quarterly provision ÷ average portfolio). NPL 90+ and Stage 3 are **stock** ratios. On a book compounding 17% q/q, a flat flow ratio is mechanically easy to hold — the denominator inflates underneath it. Rebuilding the stock series from disclosed figures:

| | Q4-25 | Q1-26 | implied |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit portfolio | ~R$23.9B | R$28.0B | **+17% q/q** |
| NPL 90+ ratio | 7.2% | 8.9% | +1.7pp |
| NPL 90+ **absolute** | ~R$1.72B | ~R$2.49B | **+45% q/q** |
| Provision expense (CoR 3.7% × avg book) | ~R$0.81B | ~R$0.96B | **+18% q/q** |

**Bad loans grew at 2.6× the rate the book grew; provisions grew at exactly the rate the book grew.** That gap is the entire bear case, and the "stable 3.7% cost of risk" is the denominator concealing it. Stage 3 stock sits at **12.7% of the portfolio** — while management guides NPL 90+ to *"low teens"* by year-end, i.e. **the company itself is guiding the damage to continue** for two more quarters.

Second leg — the guide is *already* conceding it. Q2 guides managerial revenue **+13%** and NII **+12%** but gross profit only **+5%**. Roughly R$414M of incremental revenue converts to ~R$55M of incremental gross profit; ~87% of the growth is absorbed below the revenue line. The headline "flat cost of risk" and the guided gross-profit compression are telling opposite stories about the same book.

**Pre-committed Q2 gate** (derived from management's own stated path: 8.9% → low teens over three quarters ⇒ ~+1.2pp/qtr):
- **NPL 90+ ≤ 9.5%** with Stage 2+3 coverage ≥ 64% → the Q1 jump was the R$590M reclassification washing through; damage contained; thesis reopens.
- **NPL 90+ ≥ 10.5%** (or Stage 3 ≥ 14%) with cost of risk still "in guide" → flat CoR is denominator-driven, provisioning is behind formation, and the litigation premise gets **forward** confirmation. Value trap; discount correct.

Third leg — **the June 2 tape precedent**. The Q1 headline beat: shares rose **+4.79% aftermarket to $11.60**. Within 48 hours the stock traded **under $9.00** (52-wk low 8.32 per pack). The market popped the headline and then did the credit math. Whatever pops on Aug 24 is not the trade; the T+2 reversal is the risk.

### Two findings that change the sizing math (not the classification)

**1. The Section 11 tail is bounded and small — the litigation is not the reason to stay away.** Shares traceable to the registration statement cap at **22,857,143 @ $19.00**. Maximum statutory damages = 22.9M × ($19.00 − value), i.e. **~$173M at $11.42, ~$244M at the $8.32 low** — and negative-causation is a strong defense here, since a comparable Brazilian credit-cycle de-rate hit Nubank ~−33% over the same window. At typical Section 11 settlement rates (~2–10% of estimated damages), realistic exposure is **~$5–25M ≈ 0.3–1.7% of market cap**, before D&O and underwriter contribution. The market has taken ~40%. **The legal liability is bounded; the epistemic taint is not** — and it is the taint, not the dollars, that forces FLAT. That distinction is precisely what a court adjudicates.

**2. Screen-data defects (two, both worth harvesting).**
- **Stale mcap.** The event's `mcap $1,380,107,333` back-solves to **$10.67/share** (= 19.95 × (1 − 0.465)), the 2026-08-12 blob_sweep price. The pack tape is $11.42. The `mcap` and `dd52` fields in a screen row are snapped at sweep time; the tape block is not. Never let them share a sentence.
