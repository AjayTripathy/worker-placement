## Print-decisive reconstruction (pre-print filings only)

**The loudest claim** is management's, reaffirmed on the Q1 call (2026-05-15): Accelsius "exit[s] December 2026 with positive operating cash flow, implying a revenue run rate of approximately $100 million" — clarified as "**by year-end we'll be running at $8 million or $9 million of revenue a month.**"

**The arithmetic that claim requires.** Accelsius did **$1.3M** of revenue in Q1 2026 (93% of the $1.4M consolidated). Getting to ~$26M in Q4 needs roughly Q2 $4M → Q3 $11M → Q4 $26M: a **~20x ramp in three quarters** of physically shipped two-phase direct-to-chip hardware.

**What pre-print public data resolves it.** Hardware revenue is preceded by a working-capital build, and that build was already visible — or not — on the 3/31/2026 balance sheet ([10-Q, innv-20260331.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002001557/000200155726000115/innv-20260331.htm)):

| line | 12/31/2025 | 3/31/2026 | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory, net | $1,604k | **$1,562k** | down |
| Contract liabilities (deferred rev) | $947k | **$275k** | **−71%** |
| Accounts receivable | $1,094k | $840k | down |
| Accelsius PP&E invested in Q1 | — | $0.8M | negligible |

Every working-capital line that must lead a 20x hardware ramp moved the **wrong way**. Blue-team outs, stated fairly: management said explicitly that larger customers "probably won't" pay deposits, which weakens deferred revenue as a tell for this mix; and a contract-manufacture/drop-ship model would suppress on-balance-sheet inventory. Those are real, but they don't cover three independent lines plus $0.8M of capex moving together.

**So the decisive Q2 line item is not the revenue print — a back-half-weighted ramp is consistent with a small Q2 — it is the 6/30/2026 inventory and contract-liability balances.** Threshold: inventory must be up multiples off $1.56M (order $10M+) and/or contract liabilities materially rebuilt, alongside Accelsius Q2 revenue ≥ ~$4M, for the December $8–9M/month exit to remain arithmetically live. If inventory is still ~$2M on 8/13, the run-rate target is dead on arrival regardless of call commentary.

**Second pre-print resolvable: the liquidity bridge.** $55.4M unrestricted + $5.0M restricted at 3/31 (the $5.0M is a WTI Facility covenant minimum), plus **$11.9M drawn on the Yorkville SEPA after quarter-end** (disclosed on the May 15 call), against −$34.0M quarterly OCF → implied ~$33–39M unrestricted at 6/30 before further SEPA draws, and roughly $5–15M by 9/30 against $19.8M of debt due within twelve months and $6M of 5.0% convertible debentures maturing **9/15/2026**. Management's "we do not anticipate the need to raise significant capital for the foreseeable future" fails on its own Q1 arithmetic inside ~2 quarters. The January raise cleared at **$3.50** on a 7% best-efforts fee ([424B5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2001557/000114036126001155/ny20061800x2_424b5.htm)); the stock is $3.37, i.e. **below the last clearing price**, with an open SEPA that sells into weakness. Share count went 55.2M (6/30/25) → 80.1M (3/31/26), +45% in nine months.

**PRE-PRINT POSITION: FLAT.** No position is held, the pre-print working-capital evidence points against the ramp claim rather than toward the dispersion trade, and the balance sheet makes a dilutive raise near-certain within two quarters into a stock already trading under its last deal price — there is no asymmetry worth owning into a two-sided binary I can't resolve.
