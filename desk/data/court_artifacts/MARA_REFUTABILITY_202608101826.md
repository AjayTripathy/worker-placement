**Three findings that the "35,577 BTC / $2.1B treasury" headline hides:**

1. **The treasury is now mostly encumbered.** 4,742 BTC loaned + 4,528 pledged at Q2-end, plus **18,750 BTC pledged on Aug 4** (post-quarter) — that pledge alone is ~53% of the stack, up to ~79% if the pools are additive (overlap is plausible: the Coinbase facility refinanced an existing $150M line). Unencumbered BTC is ~7,557 coins ≈ $491M.

2. **Zero-coupon debt was swapped for cash-pay leverage.** The converts are all zero-coupon and deeply OTM — 2030 $632.5M @ $25.91, 2031 $291.6M @ $34.58, 2032 $950M @ $20.26 (stock $9.415, so 2.2–3.7× away: these will **not** convert, they must be repaid in cash). Against that, the new $750M BTC-backed facilities carry **~7.5% (Coinbase, Fed midpoint +3.875%) and 7.65% fixed (Two Prime, matures 2028-08-03)** — roughly **$57M/yr of new cash interest** at a company whose mining gross margin is negative.

3. **The margin-call trigger is undisclosed.** Collateral was ~1.6× principal at closing. MARA has **not disclosed the maintenance ratio**, so the BTC price forcing lender liquidation is unobservable to us. At a conventional 1.25–1.4× maintenance, the trigger sits near **$50,000–$56,000 BTC** — a 23–30% drawdown, well inside one quarter of BTC variance.

**In MARA's favor, stated fairly:** the 29% treasury decline was *not* a distress sale. It was a Q1-2026 deleveraging trade — 15,133 BTC sold at ~$72,600 to retire $1.0B face of converts at a 7–12% discount, capturing ~$88.1M ([MARA IR, 2026-03-26](https://ir.mara.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1418/mara-holdings-inc-announces-1-0-billion-repurchase-of-0-00-convertible-senior-notes-due-2030-and-2031-and-sale-of-15133-bitcoin)). Selling near the highs to buy back your own paper at a discount is good capital allocation. Holdings were roughly **flat QoQ** (35,303 → 35,577), and BTC has since recovered to $65,003 from the $58,524 Q2 mark, partially reversing the $343M unrealized loss.
