## THE TWO FINDINGS THAT DECIDE THE CASE

**A. The quarterly receivable sequence refutes the "structural AR build."** Reconstructed from three separate primary filings (Q2 10-Q three-month column, Q3 10-Q three-month column, 8-K FY column). The four quarters sum **exactly** to the reported FY figure, so the reconstruction is arithmetically closed:

| FY26 quarter | AR change ($K) | Direction |
|---|---:|---|
| Q1 (Sep-25) | **+241,361** | release — cash IN |
| Q2 (Dec-25) | −367,390 | build |
| Q3 (Mar-26) | **+4,260** | flat |
| Q4 (Jun-26) | −362,696 | build |
| **FY26** | **−484,465** | ✓ ties to 8-K |

Atlassian invoices enterprise renewals into Q2 and Q4 and **collects in Q1 and Q3**. Q1 FY26 released $241.4M — *more than the entire Q4 FY25 build of $136.1M*. **75% of the full-year build landed in Q4 alone.** This is a fiscal-year-end invoicing spike on a demonstrated release cycle, not a monotonic structural build.

**B. The FY25 control document destroys the honesty catch.** The red's designated "honesty catch" was that the FY26 release states FCF with no prior-year comparison while every other metric carries one. The FY25 release — a year when FCF *grew* and the comparison would have flattered them — uses the identical template:

> **FY25:** "Free Cash Flow: Cash flow from operations was $1,460.4 million and free cash flow was $1,415.5 million for fiscal year 2025. Free cash flow margin for fiscal year 2025 was 27%."
> **FY26:** "Free Cash Flow: Cash flow from operations was $1,353 million and free cash flow was $1,319 million for fiscal year 2026. Free cash flow margin for fiscal year 2026 was 20%."

Word-for-word the same construction. The FCF bullet has **never** carried a prior-year clause. The "omission" is a standing template convention, not a FY26 concealment choice. Further, the prior year **is** given — both years side by side — in the non-GAAP reconciliation table of the same release (FY26 $1,319,075 vs FY25 $1,415,543), and the letter states the quarter explicitly: *"Free cash flow of $475 million increased 32% y/y."*

## WHAT THE RED GOT RIGHT THAT ITS OWN CASE UNDER-USED

The red **withdrew** its "FY27 operating income declines 7%" reading as probably wrong. Arithmetically it was right ($7,427M × 25.0% = $1,857M vs FY26 $1,996.5M = −7%). It was right to withdraw it, but for a reason it never found: the decline is fully explained by two *disclosed* items (the 4pp DC-EOL benefit rolling off, the 3pp SBC-to-cash conversion), and the company states outright that *"after adjusting for these impacts, we anticipate our non-GAAP operating margin in FY27 will reflect an increase as compared to FY26."*

Counterweight the blue bench concedes: the red's sandbag defence ("FY26 guided 18%, delivered 26%") is weaker than either bench stated, because FY26's initial guide predated the DX and BCNY closings — part of that 8pp beat was bought, not sandbagged. Organic beat was nearer ~6pp.
