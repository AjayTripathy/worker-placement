## COURT-WORTHY (damage-absent, ranked)

**None qualify as damage-absent.** KEEL does not make this list: the narrative's predicted damage — revenue collapse with no contracted replacement — is *in the printed numbers* (rev −51% y/y and headed to ~zero as the 1,861 BTC is sold; OCF −$117.6M H1). Promoting it to damage-absent on the strength of the balance sheet alone would be exactly the error the R2.3 rule exists to prevent.

It is nonetheless court-worthy on **dispersion**, which is a separate question:

- **Bull-side dispersion:** EV ≈ $2.4B (mcap ~$2.17B + $1.03B LT debt − $0.77B cash) against ~574MW of hard-secured capacity (Panther Creek 350 / Sharon 110 / Sherbrooke 96 / Moses Lake 18) → **~$4.2M per secured MW**, a steep discount to where contracted peers mark. Power interconnect is the binding constraint in 2026 and KEEL holds real ESAs.
- **Bear-side dispersion (the kill):** secured ESA ≠ permitted ≠ built ≠ leased. Sharon awaits final environmental permits, Sherbrooke awaits provincial approval, Scrubgrass's 1.3GW is *still a utility application*. At industry build costs, $819M funds roughly 60–100MW of the 850 — so the headline capacity is an **option on future capital markets**, not an asset, and the [S-3ASR automatic shelf](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1812477/000121390026041047/ea0285203-s3asr_keelinfra.htm) makes equity issuance at $3.51 available on any day. Six-plus quarters of zero revenue against ~$100M/yr cash SG&A plus capex.

That gap — $4.2M/MW on paper vs. a possibly-unfundable buildout — is wide enough that adjudicating it moves the sizing decision from "no position" to "starter" or to "avoid," which is what court-worthiness measures.
