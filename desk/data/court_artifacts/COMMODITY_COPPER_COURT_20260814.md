## 2. STRUCTURE — the decisive finding, and it cuts against the US expression

The two exchanges are telling **opposite stories**, and it is the *curve shape*, not the price
level, where the distortion now lives.

**LME — severe physical tightness, squeeze conditions** (mining.com, 2026-08-14):
- Cash-to-3-month backwardation **$434/t — a five-year high**
- Aug-over-Sep one-month spread up to **$370/t — widest since the 2021 squeeze** that forced LME intervention
- Stocks fell for a **42nd consecutive day to 204,975 t — longest draw since 2014**
- **Nearly half** of what remains is already earmarked for withdrawal

**COMEX — contango, i.e. comfortable supply** (IBKR, close 2026-08-14):

| Expiry | $/lb | vs front | annualised |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sep-26 | 6.6015 | — | — |

| Dec-28 | 7.3330 | +11.08% | +4.8% |

**Mechanism — why this matters more than anything else in this report.** The physical tightness
signal is expressed as *backwardation on LME*. The instrument this book can actually buy —
COMEX futures, or CPER which holds them — is in **~5-6%/yr contango**. A US investor buying the
US-listed vehicle to express "copper is squeezed" is on the wrong end of the trade: they **pay
carry while the squeeze pays out in LME spreads they cannot access.** The tightness is real and
this book's rails structurally cannot harvest it.

**And the tightness has a known expiry.** Both drivers of the LME draw are *flows, not consumption*:
1. Metal shipping to the US **ahead of prospective refined-copper tariffs**
2. Metal shipping to China, where smelters are cutting runs on feedstock shortage after the
   **DRC concentrate export ban**

Driver (1) is inventory **relocation**, not disappearance — global metal is moving from LME sheds
to US warehouses. This is the 2025 pattern repeating. In 2025 the tariff threat drove the COMEX
premium to a reported 25-30%, refined copper was then exempted, the premium collapsed, and copper
fell from $5.03 (Jun-25) to $4.33 (Jul-25) — **-14% in a month**, verified on the tape. A squeeze
built on front-running a scheduled tariff resolves when the tariff resolves. That is a dated,
knowable reversal risk sitting underneath an all-time-high price.

Driver (2), the DRC export ban, is a genuine supply shock. That part is real tightness.

The dominant event is a **reserve downgrade at Sasa — the zinc-lead asset**, not copper. The
secondary event is an acquisition that converts a high-yield cash-return story into a growth story,
which de-rates the income holder base.

**Why it cannot be the copper position:**
- **Sasa is zinc-lead.** Zinc and lead have not rallied with copper.
- **Kounrad, the copper asset, is a melting ice cube.** It is a dump-leach SX-EW operation
  reprocessing *historic waste dumps*: 13,311 t Cu in 2025, **~85,000 t recoverable remaining** at
  end-2024, life "to at least 2034" — roughly 6-7 years. You **cannot expand a dump leach**; the
  dumps are a fixed inventory. It structurally cannot lever a copper up-cycle.
- The 8.22% dividend yield is **trailing** and may be stale given the reserve cut and the pivot to
  acquisition-led growth. **UNVERIFIED — confirm against the interims.**

**Dated resolver:** "Notice of Interim Results" was posted 2026-08-07, so **H1-2026 interims are
imminent (late Aug / Sep 2026)** — that is the event that reprices the dividend and the Sasa
guidance. Do not add ahead of it.

**Action:** reclassify CAML out of "copper" in the book taxonomy; treat as a zinc-lead special
situation with a finite copper byproduct. **Do not add to it to express a copper view** — that was
the implicit plan in the brief and it is a category error. A hold/trim decision belongs to its own
review keyed to the interims, not to this court.

## 10. BOTTOM LINE

**Score 3/10.** Not zero: the LME physical tightness is real, the DRC supply shock is real, and
copper metal does diversify at the margin. But:

1. **It is not ballast.** -64% in 2008, -54% through 2011-16 while equities rose. Ballast that
   halves in a cyclical downturn is not ballast.
2. **It does not hedge the AI exposure — it concentrates it.** Miners are up on 5% of AI-break days;
   copper's correlation to the AI complex is at a three-year maximum right now.
3. **The book already owns +0.15 copper beta** through its EM, JP/EU-value and consumer cyclicals.
4. **There is no edge at this price.** All-time high, +47% in 12 months, and the forward already
   prices +11% more by 2028.
5. **The tightness is unharvestable on these rails** — backwardation on LME, contango on COMEX, and
   this book can only buy COMEX.

**Do nothing on copper.** Keep SGOV and PHYS as the actual ballast. If the AI-break call needs a
hedge, the data points at duration, not copper.

**The one thing to act on is CAML.L** — reclassify it out of the copper sleeve, and give it its own
review against the imminent interims.
