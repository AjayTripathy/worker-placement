## KILL 1a — the 24-month backlog was never retired. This is a search artifact.

The red's §1a is the load-bearing pillar of its strongest kill: *"FY25 10-K — NO, zero occurrences of '24-month'"*, and therefore *"the 12–24mo and >24mo buckets are no longer computable from public disclosure."*

| Filing | Disclosed sentence | 24-mo figure |
|---|---|---|
| [FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781125000056/wday-20250131.htm) | "approximately $7.6 billion and **$13.6 billion** … over the next 12 and **24 months**" | $13.6B |
| [FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781126000014/wday-20260131.htm) | "approximately $8.8 billion and **$15.8 billion** … over the next 12 and **24 months**" | $15.8B |
| [Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781126000026/wday-20260430.htm) | "approximately $8.8 billion and **$15.6 billion** … over the next 12 and **24 months**" | $15.6B |

- Claim: *the 24-month backlog was retired.* | Method: full-text fetch of all three filings, searched `24-month`, `24 months`, `next 12 and 24 months` | Authority: SEC-filed 10-K/10-Q, ASC 606 "Transaction Price Allocated to the Remaining Performance Obligations" note | Finding: `24-month` returns **0 hits in all three** — including filings that plainly disclose the figure. `next 12 and 24 months` returns **1 hit in each**. | **REFUTED.**

What actually happened is a **demotion from the MD&A key-metrics table to the ASC 606 note** — a change in prominence, not in disclosure. The consequence the red drew from it is therefore also false: the 12–24mo and >24mo buckets are fully computable, and I computed them for every quarter the data has existed:

**Against the red:** the metric it says was deleted *to conceal* the long-duration decay is the metric that **documents** the long-duration decay, published by Workday every quarter, in an audited filing, for eleven straight quarters as the number fell from +48.6% to +5.2%. **Against me:** the decay is worse than the red's own headline. Beyond-24-month growth is +5.2%.

**Direction of travel, also missed:** the 12-month metric did not exist before Q3 FY24. ## KILL 1b — net retention was a two-year clause, and what replaced it discloses more

The red presents FY23/FY24 "over 95% and over 100%" as an established disclosure that was withdrawn. I pulled the full history back to FY21:

| 10-K | Retention disclosure |
|---|---|
| [FY2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781121000020/wday-20210131.htm) | **none** |
| [FY2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781122000030/wday-20220131.htm) | "**gross retention over 95%**" — gross only, no net |
| [FY2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781123000024/wday-20230131.htm) | "gross and net retention rates over 95% and over 100%" |
| [FY2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781124000044/wday-20240131.htm) | identical sentence |
| [FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781125000056/wday-20250131.htm) | **"Gross Revenue Retention Rate"** — own MD&A heading, **98%**, full stated methodology |
| [FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781126000014/wday-20260131.htm) | same construct, **97%** |

- Claim: *net retention was an established metric that Workday withdrew.* | Method: full-text fetch of six consecutive 10-Ks, searched `gross and net retention`, `net retention`, `over 95` | Authority: SEC-filed 10-Ks | Finding: net retention appears in **exactly two** filings, FY23 and FY24. It was never a named key metric, never a number, never headlined — it was a subordinate clause inside a revenue-variance paragraph. **Gross-only is the convention Workday used before it (FY22) and after it (FY25–26).** | **REFUTED.**

Five points finish this kill:

1. **"Over 100%" could not fall.** It is a qualitative floor, not a measurement. It was printed unchanged for two years and would have kept printing unchanged through any deterioration short of crossing a round number. What replaced it is a **specific number with a published definition** — strictly more falsifiable.
2. **The replacement then fell, and Workday printed the fall.** 98% → 97%. A substitution engineered to conceal deterioration does not select a metric that visibly declines in the very next filing. This is the anti-masking tell.
3. **It was not sprung in the 10-K — it was phased through the quarterlies seven months earlier, in plain sight.** Q2 FY25 10-Q (2024-08-28) dropped the 24-month table row and printed the last NRR mention; the **Financial Analyst Day deck of 2024-09-17** formally defined GRR in its "Other Business Metrics" appendix; the Q3 FY25 10-Q (2024-12-05) carried **GRR 98% with the full definition** — three months before the FY25 10-K. The 10-K was the *fourth* consecutive filing/event with the new metric set. The red's four-row annual table makes a staged public rollout look like a silent swap between 10-Ks.
4. **The same filing ADDED an expansion disclosure that lets you reconstruct net retention.** FY25 and FY26 10-Ks each carry a sentence absent from FY24: *"Approximately 60% of the increase in subscription services revenues was attributable to expansion [within] our customers that existed as of the beginning of the [prior year]… the remaining 40%… to customers added after."* Existing-customer revenue growing ~$650–670M/yr on a ~$7–8B base implies **NRR of roughly 108%** — still above 100%. The expansion axis the red says was retired is **reconstructable from a disclosure introduced in the very filing that allegedly retired it**.
5. **The same filing also NAMED the seat headwind.** *"reduced growth in headcount level commitments upon renewals of existing customers"* — **absent from FY23 and FY24, present from the FY25 10-K onward** and retained in the FY26 10-K and Q1 FY27 10-Q. This is MD&A statement-of-experienced-fact, not a boilerplate risk factor. **Workday wrote the deterioration the red says it was concealing into the MD&A of the filing the red says concealed it.**

**Correction to my own filing — I made the red's error.** My first draft argued that "users under contract" was a brand-new metric in FY25, on the strength of a zero-hit grep for that exact phrase in FY21–FY24. That is the same string-form artifact I convict the red of above: the FY24 10-K discloses the identical concept as *"licensed by more than 65 million users."* The metric was **rephrased and updated, not introduced**, and I withdraw the point. The count itself runs 65M (FY24) → 70M (FY25) → 75M (FY26) → **80M+ (Q1 FY27 release)** — volunteered quarterly, growing throughout the window the seat-compression thesis covers. Points 4 and 5 above carry the argument that point 3 of my draft was reaching for, and they carry it better.

**Where the red is still right, and I concede it:** Workday's own definition states GRR *"does not account for additional revenue earned from add-ons or net expansions, which include volume and price adjustments."* A customer renewing with 20% fewer workers does register ~100% GRR. That is a real analytical limitation. But Workday **writes the limitation into the filing itself** — the red's own quote is Workday's own sentence. A disclosed limitation is not a masking channel; and the seat variable it cannot capture is separately disclosed as users-under-contract. Peers are no better: 0/6 disclose GRR at all, and the loss-side metrics the seat-based incumbents do publish (CRM attrition ~8%, NOW renewal rate 98%) are gross-shaped and carry the identical blindness.

**Net on kill 1:** one metric demoted from table to footnote but still published; one metric upgraded from qualitative clause to defined number that then declined and was printed; one seat metric added that never previously existed. The FY25 10-K disclosed **more**, not less. The "aperture narrowed three times in eighteen months, always in the direction that flatters" finding is **OVERTURNED**.

## KILL 3 — my lumpiness attack FAILS. Reporting it against myself.

I was tasked to test whether the beyond-12-month halving is 3-year renewal-cycle oscillation, by checking FY23/FY24 for the same pattern. **It is not, and there is no prior episode.**

**Beyond-12-month y/y, every quarter it has been computable (11 consecutive):**
> +35.7 → +30.8 → +27.4 → +23.3 → +22.7 → +21.8 → **+20.7** → +18.1 → +16.7 → +10.6 → **+8.8**

Strictly monotonic. **Not one re-acceleration quarter in the metric's entire history.** Beyond-24-month shows a single inverted-U across 17 quarters — one turning point in Jul-2023, then eleven straight quarters down. A 3-year echo would require re-acceleration around FY27Q2; that has not happened and is unobservable to date. **The hypothesis is untested, not confirmed. Kill 3 is UPHELD and strengthened against me.**

**But the mechanism is not what the cohort thesis says it is.** Decomposing total backlog into its two arithmetic drivers:

| Quarter | 12-mo growth | implied duration | duration y/y | total growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY26 | 15.6% | 3.228 | +3.0% | 19.1% |
| Q2 FY26 | 16.4% | 3.208 | +1.0% | 17.6% |
| Q3 FY26 | 17.6% | 3.164 | −0.6% | 17.0% |
| Q4 FY26 | 15.8% | 3.181 | −3.1% | 12.2% |
| **Q1 FY27** | **15.5%** | **3.099** | **−4.0%** | **10.9%** |

**12-month backlog growth is flat at 15.5–17.6% across the entire window.** One hundred percent of the total-backlog deceleration is the **duration** term — contract length — not volume. A customer renewing for 2 years instead of 3 at unchanged ARR produces identical revenue, identical 12-month backlog, and a smaller far bucket. Revenue impact is zero unless they fail to renew, and gross retention of 97% says they renew.

So the decay measures **contract term, i.e. buyer willingness to commit long in an uncertain AI environment**. That is a real and legitimate negative — it is a confidence signal and it compresses visibility. It is *not* seat destruction, and the red's own §2 concedes the magnitude: duration 3.28 → 3.10, about **two months off a 3.2-year contract**.

**And the timing destroys the cohort's causal story.** The turn is **Jul-2023** — eleven quarters ago, roughly two years before "agentic seat displacement" entered the narrative, and fifteen months before the AI-crash cohort tag was applied. Combined with the red's own item 15 (Workday named "lower customer headcount growth" in [May 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327811/000132781124000089/wday-04302024x991.htm)), the deceleration is a long-running, fully-disclosed, pre-AI normalisation. **Whatever is driving it, it is not a 2026 discovery, and the market has had eleven quarters and a 56% drawdown to price it.**
