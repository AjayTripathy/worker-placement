## 0. The two numbers that decide the case

The triage's headline is *"net new ARR $27M → $49M, +81% y/y."* Both numbers are correct. The
inference drawn from them is not, for two independent reasons that compound.

**(a) UiPath's own guidance, printed four lines below the headline, says the improvement stops
immediately.**

| | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ARR at 2026-01-31 (FY26 close) | $1,853M | [Q4 FY26 EX-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472226000007/path-2026131xex991.htm) |
| ARR at 2026-04-30 (Q1 FY27) | $1,901.2M | [Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472226000041/path-20260430.htm) |
| Q1 FY27 net new ARR (actual) | **$48.2M** | derived; company rounds to $49M |
| FY27 ARR guide, midpoint | $2,060.5M | [Q1 FY27 EX-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472226000037/path-2026430xex991.htm) |
| **FY27 full-year net new ARR implied** | **$207.5M** | guide midpoint − FY26 close |
| **Implied Q2–Q4 FY27 net new ARR** | **$159.3M** | $207.5M − $48.2M |
| Actual Q2–Q4 FY26 net new ARR | **$160M** | $31M + $59M + $70M, per each quarter's EX-99.1 |
| **Implied y/y growth, remaining 9 months** | **−0.4%** | |

And the next quarter specifically: Q2 FY27 ARR is guided to $1.929–1.934B. Midpoint minus the
$1,901.2M just reported = **$30.3M of net new ARR, versus $31M actually delivered in Q2 FY26 —
down 2.3% y/y.**

So UiPath's own guide says: one quarter of +81%, then nine months of zero. The entire full-year
net-new-ARR growth of +11.6% *is* the Q1 step. There is no inflection in the forward numbers — the
company did not guide one, and the triage's "durable or one-quarter artifact?" question is answered
by the company itself, in the same press release, in the negative.

**(b) A $190M acquisition closed on day 5 of the quarter that produced the +81%, and its ARR
contribution is nowhere disclosed.**

UiPath acquired **WorkFusion, Inc. on 2026-02-05** — five days into the quarter ended 2026-04-30.
Total consideration **$190.0M** ($160.0M cash + $30.0M contingent). Provisional intangibles $85.0M,
weighted-average life ~6 years. ([Q1 FY27 10-Q, Note 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472226000041/path-20260430.htm))

I verified the size from the intangibles roll-forward rather than taking the note at face value:

| Intangible, gross | 2026-01-31 | 2026-04-30 | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer relationships | $20,910k | $60,696k | **+$39,786k** |
| Developed technology | $37,716k | $82,241k | **+$44,525k** |
| | | **Sum** | **$84,311k** ≈ the $85.0M allocated to WorkFusion |

WorkFusion sells AI agents for financial-crime compliance to banks — a recurring subscription book,
and it brought a **$39.8M customer-relationship asset with a 6-year life**.

Now the mechanical point. UiPath's ARR definition — *"annualized invoiced amounts per solution SKU
from subscription licenses and maintenance and support obligations"* — contains **no exclusion for
acquired contracts**. WorkFusion's subscription ARR is therefore inside the $1,901.2M, and inside
the $48.2M of net new ARR. UiPath discloses no organic/inorganic split. The word **"organic" appears
zero times in the Q1 FY27 press release.**

The arithmetic then corners them:

| If UiPath paid… | implied WorkFusion ARR | organic Q1 FY27 net new ARR | vs. $27M in Q1 FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9.0× ARR | $21M | $27M | **flat — no improvement at all** |
| 6.0× ARR | $32M | $16M | −41% |
| 4.0× ARR | $48M | $0M | −100% |
| 3.1× ARR (*UiPath's own multiple*) | $61M | **−$13M** | negative |

**The break-even is 9.0× ARR.** For the +81% to be even partly organic, UiPath must have paid more
than nine times ARR for a distressed 2011-vintage intelligent-automation vendor — while its own
stock trades at **3.11× ARR**. That is not a plausible price. At any realistic multiple, organic
net new ARR in Q1 FY27 was flat to negative against the collapsed prior-year quarter.

This is corroborated, not contradicted, by (a): WorkFusion is already in the Q1 ARR base, so the Q2
guide is a nearly clean organic read — and it is **flat-to-down y/y**. The two findings are the same
finding seen from two sides.

## 1. Mode B first — what I would ask if no one had handed me a memo

### 1.1 The land motion is dead, and has been for three years

This is the finding the DBNR framing cannot see, and it is the strongest structural fact in the file.

UiPath's **total customer count**, disclosed only in Item 1 of the 10-K — never in any press release:

| As of | Total customers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-01-31 | ~10,800 | [FY24 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472224000011/path-20240131.htm) |
| 2024-01-31 | ~10,830 | FY24 10-K / [FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472225000007/path-20250131.htm) |
| 2025-01-31 | ~10,753 | FY25 10-K / [FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734722/000173472226000012/path-20260131.htm) |
| 2026-01-31 | **~10,747** | FY26 10-K |

**Three years. Net −53 customers.** Zero logo growth since January 2023 — and the 2026 figure
*includes* the customers acquired with Peak AI (closed 2025-03-07), so organically it is worse.
The FY27 count will likewise be propped up by WorkFusion's acquired logos.

Meanwhile the top of the base is being upsold hard: customers with ARR ≥$100k went 2,054 → 2,292 →
2,565; customers ≥$1M went 317 → 357; and revenue concentration in the ≥$100k cohort rose from 87%
to **89%** (FY26 10-K).

That is a closed system. ARR has grown from ~$1.19B to $1.90B entirely by selling more to a static,
very slightly shrinking set of accounts, while the expansion rate doing that work decays.

### 1.2 Therefore: DBNR is the wrong instrument, and the triage graded with it

UiPath's own definition is explicit — dollar-based net retention starts with *"the ARR from the
cohort of all customers as of 12 months prior"* and **"does not include ARR from new customers in
the current period."**

Agentic substitution, if it is happening, shows up as **(a) failure to win new logos** and **(b)
attrition of the long tail** — and DBNR excludes (a) by construction and dilutes (b) into a
surviving-cohort average. It measures only how much more the survivors bought. **A company can lose
the entire new-deployment decision to competitors for three straight years and still print a rising
DBNR**, provided its installed robot fleets renew and expand.

That is precisely UiPath's pattern: flat logos, upsold core, DBNR the only metric moving up. Grading
PATH **DAMAGE-ABSENT on DBNR is grading with an instrument that is blind to the failure mode being
tested.** The metric that is sensitive to it — total customer count — is flat-to-down for three
years and appears in no press release.

Two readings the "inflected up" framing suppresses:

- **The Q1 comparison is a round trip, not growth.** Q1 net new ARR: $45M (FY24) → $44M (FY25) →
  $27M (FY26) → $49M (FY27). Against three years ago, that is **+9% — on an ARR base 52% larger.**
  As a share of opening ARR it went 3.7% → 3.0% → 1.6% → **2.6%**: the "recovery" restores about
  half the lost sales productivity, and leaves it a third below FY24.
- **Annual net new ARR has gone nowhere in two years.** FY25 $202M → FY26 $186M (−8%) → FY27 guided
  ~$207.5M. That is **+2.7% over two full years — and it includes two acquisitions.**

DBNR 109 is a two-point bounce inside a 13-point structural decline, measured against a year-ago
base (108) that was itself the worst in company history. It is not an inflection; it is the second
derivative of a decline briefly turning positive.

### 1.4 The invoiced backlog actually shrank

This one I found by decomposing rather than reading the headline, and it cuts against the most
bullish number in the file.

| Remaining performance obligations | 2025-04-30 | 2026-04-30 | y/y |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Billed** (invoiced, collectible) | $672.0M | **$658.2M** | **−2.1%** |
| **Unbilled** (committed, not yet invoiced) | $559.3M | $755.0M | **+35.0%** |
| Total RPO | $1,231.3M | $1,413.2M | **+14.8%** |

*(Both 10-Qs, RPO note.)*

**I concede the headline: total RPO +14.8% is a genuine positive and it is above ARR growth.** But
**100% of that growth is uninvoiced.** The hard, billed, cash-collectible portion of the backlog
*declined 2.1% y/y* while ARR grew 12% and revenue grew 17%. Unbilled RPO is the soft half — it
depends on contract duration and on customers honouring commitments not yet invoiced. Accounts
receivable also fell from $488.3M to $300.0M sequentially and deferred revenue fell $603.7M →
$572.1M. The direction of the cash-backed measures is not the direction of the headline.

### 1.5 Revenue +17% is a recognition artifact, and management guides it away next quarter

Revenue grew 17.3% while ARR grew 12%. That five-point gap is not demand. UiPath's own revenue
policy: term licences are **"recognized at the point in time at which the customer is able to use
and benefit from the software, generally upon delivery."** Revenue is therefore a function of *when
contracts start and renew*, not of run-rate — which is exactly why ARR, not revenue, is the metric
UiPath itself calls *"the key performance metric we use in managing our business."*

Q1 FY27 revenue mix: licences $149.3M (+16.4%), subscription services $252.9M (+16.4%),
**professional services $16.2M (+46.5%)** — after three consecutive quarters of *declines* (−4%,
−13%, −14%). A services line that falls double digits for a year and then jumps 47% is an
acquisition fingerprint, not a demand signal.

And the guide:

| | Q1 FY27 actual | Q2 FY27 guide (mid) | FY27 guide (mid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $418.4M, **+17.3%** | $397.5M, **+9.8%** | $1,778.5M, **+10.4%** |
| Sequential | — | **−5.0% q/q** | — |
| Non-GAAP operating income | $92M | ~$75M (**−18% q/q**) | $430M |

Management guides a **7.5-point deceleration in revenue growth in the very next quarter**, with
revenue and profit both down sequentially. The +17% is the least durable number in the release.

## 3. The structural case — built only from UiPath's own words

I could not pull third-party competitive data this session. So I make the structural argument
entirely from UiPath's own SEC-filed exhibits, which is the more defensible ground anyway.

**Value is migrating out of the product and into partners' models — UiPath says so itself.** From
the Q1 FY27 release's own business highlights: UiPath's Intelligent Xtraction and Processing product
is now on Google Cloud Marketplace and **"Gemini will become the default third-party model for new
IXP projects."** Document understanding was one of UiPath's highest-value attach products; its
intelligence is now supplied by Google by default. "UiPath for Coding Agents" positions the platform
as a wrapper that makes *other vendors'* coding agents deployable. The other headline relationships
in the same release — Salesforce AgentExchange, Microsoft Defender/Sentinel, Databricks, Deloitte
Ascend — are each a partner that ships its own agent and orchestration layer and has every incentive
to own it. The 10-K risk factors confirm the dependence: *"We rely on our channel partners,
including our strategic alliances, to generate a substantial amount of our revenue."*

**Category flight, in filed documents, within eight weeks.** An 8-K exhibit dated 2026-03-25
describes UiPath as *"a global leader in agentic automation."* The 8-K exhibit dated 2026-05-28
describes it as *"a global leader in business orchestration and automaton"* [sic — the typo is in
the filed exhibit]. That is the third self-relabel in roughly three years: RPA → AI-powered
automation → agentic automation → business orchestration. Companies whose category is being taken
from them rename the category.

**Governance offers no correction mechanism.** Class B carries **35 votes per share**. 64,691k Class
B shares command ~2.26B votes against 455.8M Class A votes — roughly **83% of voting power**. Daniel
Dines is **both CEO and Executive Chairman**. The sunset triggers only if Class B falls below 20% of
its IPO-date level, or six months after Dines's death or incapacity. Two years of failed growth
execution carries no governance consequence.

I am explicit that I have **not** verified the competitive displacement quantitatively. The claim I
am making is narrower and fully sourced: *UiPath's own filings describe a company moving up-stack
into thin orchestration, dependent on partners who compete with it, relabelling its category, with
no new logos for three years.* Whether Microsoft or open-source frameworks specifically are the
cause is UNVERIFIABLE this session.

## 5. Does the derate correctly price terminal-value compression? Yes.

**Capital structure** (10-Q balance sheet, 2026-04-30): Class A 455,762k + Class B 64,691k =
**520,453k shares outstanding**. Cash $632.2M + restricted $1.5M + marketable securities $675.0M +
non-current securities $108.5M = **$1,415.2M**. **No debt of any kind.** Contingent consideration
$30.0M. Operating leases $302.7M undiscounted.

At $14.09: **market cap $7,333M; enterprise value ≈ $5,918M.**

| Multiple | |
|---|---|
| EV / ARR ($1,901M) | **3.11×** |
| EV / FY27E revenue ($1,778.5M) | **3.33×** |
| EV / FY27E non-GAAP operating income ($430M) | **13.8×** |
| EV / FY26 adjusted FCF ($372M) | 15.9× |
| **EV / (FCF − stock comp)** | **≈ 31.6×** |

That last line is the honest one. Stock compensation ran $53.3M in Q1 (≈$213M annualized, **12.0% of
FY27E revenue**). It is a real cost, and UiPath spends roughly its entire free cash flow buying
stock back to contain it — $243.8M of repurchases in Q1 alone. Strip it and the business trades at
**~32× cash economics while its own guidance implies zero growth in incremental ARR for nine
months.** The FY27 "24% non-GAAP operating margin" is roughly **9–10% on a GAAP basis** after stock
comp and the now-larger intangible amortization.

**Growth is also being bought outright.** Peak AI $38.1M (FY26) + WorkFusion $190.0M (FY27) =
**$228.1M of acquisitions in fifteen months**, against FY26 organic net new ARR of $186M. Cash and
securities fell from $1.69B to $1.42B in a single quarter as buybacks and M&A outran a $132M
operating cash flow.

**Is the −29% derate sufficient?** If organic ARR growth converges toward 6–8% — which is what flat
logos plus a DBNR gliding from 109 delivers — the correct comparison set is mature software at
**2.0–2.5× EV/revenue**. On FY27E revenue that is EV $3.56–4.45B, plus $1.42B net cash, over 520.5M
shares = **$9.57–11.27 per share, or −20% to −32% from here.**

That band contains the 52-week low of $9.205 — a level the market printed on 2026-05-20 and has
since left, +53%. **The market has already visited fair value and walked away from it.** The derate
has not overshot; the recent rally has.

## 6. Honesty grade

Nothing here is hidden in the sense of being absent from the filings. WorkFusion is in the
acquisition note; the customer count is in Item 1 of the 10-K; the billed/unbilled RPO split is in
the RPO note; the guide is in the release. Under our standing rule, *disclosed bad news is clean*
and re-detecting it is not alpha.

But the grade is on **takeaway-versus-data divergence**, and there is real divergence:

- The release markets *"Net new ARR of $49 million"* and *"Dollar based net retention rate of 109
  percent"* as evidence of momentum, while **never disclosing that a $190M acquisition closed on day
  5 of the quarter.** WorkFusion appears in the release exactly twice — in a business-highlights
  bullet, as *"a UiPath company"* that won an industry award. It is never identified as an
  acquisition and its contribution is never quantified. **"Organic" appears zero times.**
- The marketed takeaway (*we have inflected*) is contradicted by the company's **own guidance
  printed in the same document** — nine months of zero net-new-ARR growth.
- **Total customer count — the one metric that would show the land motion is dead — has never
  appeared in a press release.** It lives in a partners paragraph in Item 1 of the 10-K.

That is a genuine masking pattern: **the metric that flatters (DBNR, which excludes new customers by
construction) is marketed quarterly; the metric that indicts (logo count) is filed annually and
never marketed; and the step-change is delivered by an acquisition the release does not mention.**
Not fraud — everything is findable, and I found it in an afternoon from primary documents. But it
**ELEVATES on takeaway divergence**, and it means the DAMAGE-ABSENT grade was assigned to a
presentation rather than to a business.

**Mechanism, for the knowledge graph:**
*Masking channel* — acquisition-contaminated run-rate metric (undisclosed inorganic ARR inside
reported net new ARR) combined with metric selection (a retention statistic structurally blind to
logo loss is marketed quarterly; the logo count is buried annually).
*Signal channel* — the intangibles roll-forward in the 10-Q, which sizes the acquired customer book
independently of anything management chooses to say; and the forward ARR guide in the same release,
which reveals the run-rate the headline conceals.
*Signal-to-price latency* — **~1 week** (10-Q filed 2026-06-04, seven days after the 2026-05-28
release that produced a +11.8% gap). The market paid for the headline before the document that
sizes the acquisition was filed. That is the exploitable gap, and it recurs every quarter.

## 7. What I concede to the blue team

I will not overstate this case. Four things genuinely favour the long:

1. **The balance sheet is excellent.** $1.42B of cash and securities, **zero debt**, positive
   operating cash flow every quarter, FY26 adjusted FCF $372M. There is no solvency or funding
   question here at all. My brief was asked to test "cash burn vs. net cash" — **there is no burn.**
2. **The share count is genuinely shrinking.** Weighted basic shares fell 548.5M → 523.6M, **−4.5%
   y/y**. The buyback more than offsets stock compensation. This is not a dilution story.
3. **Cost execution under Dines is real.** First full-year GAAP profitability in company history,
   first Q1 GAAP profitability, non-GAAP operating margin guided from ~17% to ~24%, the FY25
   restructuring completed and not repeated. That is verified and it is creditable.
4. **Total RPO +14.8% is above ARR growth** and I cannot fully explain it away as acquisition and
   duration. It is the single strongest number the blue team has, and my billed-versus-unbilled
   decomposition weakens it rather than destroying it.

A business with net cash, a shrinking share count, real free cash flow and 24% non-GAAP margins at
13.8× EV/EBIT is **not a short.** My verdict is a rejection of the *long*, not a bear case for
borrow.

---

## STRONGEST KILL

**The +81% is a one-quarter, acquisition-contaminated base effect that UiPath's own guidance says
ends immediately — and underneath it, the company has not added a net customer in three years.**

Three facts, each from a primary document, that fit together with no interpretation required:

1. A **$190M acquisition (WorkFusion) closed on day 5** of the quarter that produced the +81%; its
   ARR is mechanically inside the reported net new ARR under UiPath's own ARR definition; the
   contribution is **undisclosed**; and the break-even that would preserve *any* organic improvement
   requires UiPath to have paid **more than 9× ARR** while its own stock trades at **3.11× ARR**.
2. UiPath's **own FY27 guide implies −0.4% net-new-ARR growth for the remaining nine months** and
   **−2.3% for Q2 specifically** — a nearly clean organic read, since WorkFusion is already in the
   base. The inflection is not in the forward numbers because management did not put one there.
3. **Total customers: ~10,800 → ~10,747 across three years**, including acquired logos. The land
   motion is dead. DBNR — which by its own definition *"does not include ARR from new customers"* —
   cannot see this, which is exactly why it is the metric being marketed.

The triage graded the most conceptually agent-disrupted name in the cohort DAMAGE-ABSENT using the
one metric that is structurally incapable of detecting the damage in question.

**And the trade fails independently of all of it:** the stock is at **98.9% of its 26-week high**,
**+53% off the May low**, **+24% over 52 weeks**, having risen **38% in nine sessions on 3–4× volume
with no SEC filing since 2026-06-29** — and it already gapped **+11.8%** on the very print the
thesis rests on. There is no dislocation left to buy.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

Falsifiable, dated, and mostly resolvable at the **Q2 FY27 print (est. 2026-09-03)**:

1. **WorkFusion's acquired ARR is disclosed at under ~$15M** (on the Q2 call or in the FY27 10-K).
   That would put organic Q1 net new ARR at $33M+, a genuine +22% y/y, and the inflection becomes
   real. *This is the single cleanest test and it is the one I most want answered.*
2. **Q2 FY27 net new ARR prints at $40M or better** (vs. the $30.3M the guide implies and $31M
   a year ago) **and** the FY27 ARR guide is raised above **$2.09B**. That breaks the
   flat-for-nine-months arithmetic, which is the spine of my case.
3. **Total customer count at 2027-01-31 breaks above ~11,000 excluding WorkFusion's acquired
   logos.** That refutes the dead-land-motion finding, which is my core structural claim. A count
   that rises *only* by roughly WorkFusion's book confirms it instead.
4. **Billed RPO returns to growth above +10% y/y** at Q2, showing the −2.1% decline was invoicing
   timing rather than backlog quality.
5. **Price: at $10.50 or below (≈2.4× EV/ARR)** the terminal-compression scenario is substantially
   paid for and net cash is ~19% of the market cap. At that level this stops being a rejection and
   becomes a fairly-paid-risk starter — carry and balance sheet, with no growth edge claimed.

Things that would **not** move me: another quarter of DBNR up (wrong instrument); revenue
outperformance (recognition timing); non-GAAP margin beats (already conceded and already priced);
new partnership announcements with Microsoft, Salesforce, Google or Databricks (each is a partner
that competes for the same layer).

## CONVICTION: 8 / 10 in the REJECT

High, because the two load-bearing findings require no judgment: the guide arithmetic is UiPath's
own number, and the three-year customer count is UiPath's own disclosure. The WorkFusion break-even
corners the organic question from the third side, and the tape independently kills the entry.

Held below 9 for four honest reasons: **WorkFusion's ARR is an inference I bounded, not a number I
read**; **total RPO +14.8% is a real positive I only partially defused**; **the balance sheet and
buyback are genuinely good**; and **I could not verify the competitive-displacement case with any
third-party data this session** — that argument rests entirely on UiPath's own language, which is
suggestive rather than dispositive.
