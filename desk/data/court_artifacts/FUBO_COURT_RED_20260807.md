## 1. MODE B FIRST — the questions the pitch framing hid

I derived these before reading the triage's claim list. Every one of them is more decisive than the
squeeze-out question the triage flagged.

### B-1. Whose subscribers are they, and what does Fubo actually own?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim (derived)** | The "5.75M Total North America Subscribers" headline is a Fubo asset. |
| **Method** | Read the Commercial Agreement summary in the 10-Q MD&A and Note 1 |
| **Authority** | 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-06-30, filed 2026-08-05 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1484769/000162828026053535/fubo-20260630.htm |
| **Result** | **REFUTED.** |

The 10-Q states, in terms:

> "Hulu or its affiliates **continue to own and operate the Hulu and Disney platforms** on which the Hulu
> Live Service is distributed and **will exclusively sell and administer subscriptions** to the Hulu Live
> Service, as well as each add-on thereto, and **retain subscription revenue**."

What Hulu/Disney keeps on the Hulu+Live TV leg: the platform, the customer relationship, subscription
billing, **the subscription revenue**, the "Live TV" brand (licensed *to* Fubo, for which Fubo pays a
**1.0% brand license fee** on Hulu Live revenue), exclusive ad sales (**15% agency fee**), and all
marketing execution (Fubo funds the budget and pays Hulu a further **10% marketing support fee**).

What Fubo owns on that leg: **the carriage agreements.** The contributed asset was defined as "certain
assets related to the business of **negotiating and administering carriage agreements**."

Size of the leg: 3Q26 related-party revenue $1,067.851M of $1,481.714M total = **72.1%**. Fubo's own
subscription revenue was $300.4M, implying roughly **1.2–1.5M genuinely-Fubo subscribers of the 5.75M
marketed** — i.e. **~75–79% of the headline subscriber count belongs to Hulu.** The 10-Q concedes the
concentration in its own words: "Revenue from third parties … was 27.9% … The Company's revenues are
therefore subject to significant concentration risk."

**Exclusivity lock — Fubo is contractually forbidden from going around Disney.** The closing 8-K's
description of the HL Commercial Services Agreement:

> "HL granted to Hulu the right, license and obligation to distribute the HL DMVPD Service via the Hulu
> platform that is currently branded as 'Hulu' (**and any Disney-branded successor or replacement
> platform**) on a wholesale basis, and **HL will not otherwise distribute the HL DMVPD Service, nor any
> programming outside of the HL DMVPD Service**, in each case, during the term."

Fubo may not take those ~4.3M subscribers direct, may not distribute the service on any competing
platform, and may not monetise that programming anywhere else — for five years. The parenthetical "**and
any Disney-branded successor or replacement platform**" is the Disney+ migration written into the contract
at signing, a year before the CEO letter mentioned it.

**And Disney, not Fubo, sets the retail price.** From Fubo's own risk factors: "If Hulu and its affiliates
prioritize other platforms or content over the Hulu Live Service, do not market or promote the Hulu Live
Service appropriately, **do not set pricing that maximizes the value of the Hulu Live Business** … our
revenues could be negatively impacted." Fubo has no pricing power over 72% of its revenue.

**Terminal clock:** "The Commercial Agreement provides for an **initial term of five years, renewable for
an additional five-year term by mutual agreement**." Initial term runs to ~2030-10-29. Renewal requires
Disney's consent. Fubo concedes it may not even keep the customers: "**There is also uncertainty regarding
how the expiration of the commercial services agreement would impact our subscribers and data**,
particularly if Newco were to cease being a subsidiary of Hulu." Meanwhile the incoming CEO's own
shareholder letter flags "**Disney's progress towards fully integrating Hulu into Disney+, including the
planned Live TV integration**" — the successor product is being built on Disney's side of the wall, and
the charter's corporate-opportunity waiver (§2 below) means Disney owes Fubo nothing when it lands.

**The agreement itself is not in the public record.** The closing 8-K exhibit index (Item 9.01) lists
10.1 Registration Rights, 10.2 Tax Receivables, 10.3 Newco LLC Agreement, 10.4 Stockholders Agreement —
**the HL Commercial Services Agreement and the Hulu Brand License Agreement are described but not filed.**
The contract governing 72% of revenue and ~80% of the FY28 EBITDA bridge cannot be read by an outside
investor. That is claim #14 below, and **UNVERIFIABLE is not clean.**

### B-2. What is the FY28 ≥$300M target actually made of?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | FY2028 Adjusted EBITDA target of at least $300M (reaffirmed 2026-08-05). |
| **Method** | Derive the contractual step-up from the disclosed wholesale-fee schedule and the disclosed related-party revenue |
| **Authority** | 10-Q Note 2 (revenue recognition) + MD&A; 8-K/Ex-99.1 shareholder letter, 2026-08-05 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1484769/000149315226036098/ex99-1.htm |
| **Result** | **VERIFIED as to existence — but ~80% of the bridge is a Disney-granted price schedule, not operating performance.** This is the single most important undisclosed-by-framing fact in the name. |

The disclosed schedule: Hulu pays Fubo a wholesale fee equal to **95% of the Hulu Live Business's carriage
fee expenses in CY2025–CY2026, escalating to 97.5% in CY2027 and 99% in CY2028 and thereafter.**

Derivation (method shown so it can be checked):
- 3Q26 related-party revenue $1,067.851M = 95% of Hulu Live carriage cost
- ⇒ implied Hulu Live carriage cost ≈ **$1,124.1M/quarter (~$4.50B/yr)**
- Cross-check: total subscriber-related expense 3Q26 = $816.867M (third-party) + $547.820M (related-party) = $1,364.687M; $1,124.1M of Hulu Live carriage leaves ~$240M for Fubo Services' own content against $300.4M of Fubo subscription revenue — an ~80% content-cost ratio, normal for a vMVPD. Per-sub: $1,124.1M ÷ 4.3M Hulu Live subs ÷ 3 months ≈ **$87/sub/month** carriage. The derivation holds.

Step-up value at constant carriage (fiscal year ends ~Sept 30; the fee schedule is on CALENDAR years, so
each fiscal year straddles two rates):
- 95% → 97.5% = +2.5pp ≈ **+$112M/yr**
- 95% → 99% = +4.0pp ≈ **+$180M/yr**
- **FY2027** (Oct-2026 → Sep-2027) = 3 months at 95% + 9 months at 97.5% ⇒ **≈ +$84M** vs baseline
- **FY2028** (Oct-2027 → Sep-2028) = 3 months at 97.5% + 9 months at 99% ⇒ **≈ +$163M** vs baseline

Bridge: FY26 guide midpoint **$95M** + **$163M contractual** = **$258M**, against a "≥$300M" target.
**$163M of the required $205M increase = 80% of the FY26→FY28 EBITDA bridge is a related-party price
schedule the controller wrote.** Only ~$42M has to come from the business.

Why this is the mechanism and not a footnote: **the controller set the clock, and the controller's
own constraints expire immediately before the clock strikes.** See §2.

## 2. THE STUB — governance, verified clause by clause

Every item below is from the operative Delaware charter filed as Exhibit 3.4 to the closing 8-K
(2025-10-30) — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1484769/000149315225020163/ex3-4.htm — the
closing 8-K itself, the Disney/Hulu Schedule 13D, and the 2026 proxy.

### Structure (Up-C), verified
- Fubo Operations LLC ("Newco") holds the whole business. **Hulu, LLC holds 78,992,518 Newco Units +
  78,992,518 Class B shares; FuboTV Inc. holds Newco Units equal to its Class A count.**
- Class B is **expressly non-economic** (Charter §4.02(e): "Shares of Class B Common Stock shall be deemed
  to be a non-economic interest in the Corporation").
- **Disney/Hulu = 72.34% of common outstanding** as of 2026-07-31 (78,992,518 of 109,196,795). The 13D/A
  filed 2026-03-20 reports **72.9%** on the then-outstanding basis; the "70%" universally quoted is the
  *fully-diluted* figure at closing.
- Hulu's Newco Units are redeemable for Class A **or, at Fubo's option, cash**, with Hulu able to retract
  if Fubo elects cash — hence **redeemable NCI of $1,825.5M in mezzanine**, remeasured to redemption value
  each period (down $528.4M in the March quarter).

### The minority protections that exist
1. **Class A class vote** on any charter amendment that adversely alters Class A powers/preferences, and
   on non-proportional splits/reclassifications (§4.02(d), (f)). Real, but narrow.
2. **Two "Unaffiliated Independent Designees"** (Daniel Leff — Lead Independent Director — and Ignacio
   Figueras) on a nine-member board.
3. **Hulu voting covenant** (Stockholders Agreement): while Hulu holds ≥50%, it votes for board-nominated
   directors and in line with board recommendations.
4. **24-month lockup** on Hulu's stake.

### What each of those is worth — and when it dies

- **Board composition is 6-of-9 Hulu-designated before you count the CEO.** Charter §7.03(b): five "Hulu
  Affiliated Designees" + one "Hulu Independent Designee" + two Unaffiliated Independent Designees + the
  CEO. Elected 2026-07-28: Bird, Headley, Lygopoulos, OConnell, Taff, Warbrooke (Hulu Designees); Leff,
  Figueras (Unaffiliated). Ninth seat: **Alisa Bowen, appointed CEO 2026-07-10 and to the board 2026-07-29
  — President, Disney+ from Sept 2022 until July 2026.** Seven of nine seats are Disney-designated or
  Disney-derived.
- **The founder was removed.** 8-K filed 2026-07-09: "**On July 9, 2026, David Gandler's employment as
  Chief Executive Officer of the Company terminated**… entitled to receive the severance benefits …
  for a termination … **without 'cause' … within 24 months following a 'change in control'**… Mr.
  Gandler also resigned from the Board … the Board has withdrawn his nomination." Eight months after
  closing, the only director with a founder's stake in the minority's outcome is gone, on
  change-of-control severance, replaced by Disney's streaming president.
- **Both remaining protections expire on the same day: 2027-10-29.** The Stockholders Agreement's
  undertaking to keep the Unaffiliated Independent Designees in place runs **"until the second anniversary
  of the Closing."** The **lockup on Hulu's shares runs "for 24 months following the completion of the
  Transactions."** Closing was 2025-10-29.
- **DGCL §203 is affirmatively waived.** Charter Article XI §11.01: "**The Corporation shall not be
  governed by Section 203 of the DGCL.**" No three-year moratorium; no 66⅔% disinterested-share vote gate
  on a business combination with an interested stockholder.
- **Action by written consent is permitted.** Charter Article X §10.01: prior to the Majority Sunset Date,
  any stockholder action "may be effected without a meeting, without prior notice and without a vote" by
  consent of holders of the minimum votes required. **At 72.34%, Disney can approve a long-form merger by
  written consent.** The minority receives an information statement and an appraisal notice, not a vote.
- **No standstill exists anywhere.** The Schedule 13D Item 4 expressly reserves the right to acquire
  "securities or additional securities of the Issuer, through **open market purchases**, private
  agreements or otherwise," and to pursue "business combinations or other extraordinary corporate
  transactions."
- **Corporate opportunity is fully renounced.** Charter Article XIII §13.01: no Hulu Group Entity, and no
  dual-hatted director or officer, "shall have or be under any fiduciary duty … to refer any corporate
  opportunity" to Fubo, or to refrain from "engaging in the same or similar activities or lines of
  business" or "doing business with any client, customer or vendor" of Fubo. **Disney may build the
  successor product and owes Fubo nothing.**
- **The charter contemplates Disney buying a competitor.** §7.03(e) and the definitions define a
  "Competing DMVPD Transaction" as an acquisition of a target that "operated a Competing DMVPD" and
  "derived **less than 50% of its LTM EBITDA** from operating such Competing DMVPD." The only consequence
  is that Hulu loses board-majority designation rights 12 months later **if it has not divested by then.**
  The controller reserved, on the face of the charter, the right to acquire a competitor to the company
  it controls.
- **Tax Receivables Agreement — a permanent leak, but NOT the acceleration trap I first alleged.**
  Fubo must pay Hulu the lesser of 70% / Hulu's Newco ownership of the benefit realized from **Fubo's own
  historic NOLs**, plus **70%** of basis-step-up and other TRA-attributable benefits, and the 10-Q concedes
  payments "could be made … **significantly in excess of 70% of the actual cash tax savings**." $0 accrued
  at 6/30/26. That is a real, permanent transfer of minority value to the controller.

  **My change-of-control claim is WITHDRAWN on the primary text.** I opened this leg on the 8-K's summary
  of the early-termination mechanic. Reading the filed agreement itself — Exhibit 10.2, TRA §4.2(a) —
  refutes it verbatim:

  > "For the avoidance of doubt, **Fubo shall not be required to make an Early Termination Payment in
  > connection with a change of control of, or other similar transaction by, Fubo**, a material breach of
  > this Agreement by Fubo or a bankruptcy or other insolvency event of Fubo."

  There is **no** change-of-control acceleration. Early termination is purely voluntary and, under
  §4.1(a), requires **"the written approval of the Audit Committee"** — a 100%-independent committee per
  the proxy. That is a genuine, working minority protection, and I record it as such. Note also that the
  TRA runs to **Hulu itself**, so in a squeeze-out Disney would sit on both sides of it — it is not a
  deal-blocker and not a hidden liability ahead of the Class A holder. **VERDICT: CONSISTENT.**
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1484769/000149315225020163/ex10-2.htm

## 4. THE SQUEEZE-OUT — mechanics and the window

**Disney cannot use the short-form route.** DGCL §253/§267 require 90%; Disney is at 72.34%. The realistic
paths are (a) a §251 long-form merger approved by Disney's own written consent under Charter Art. X
§10.01, or (b) a tender offer followed by a §251(h) back-end. Either way appraisal under §262 should be
available because the consideration is cash (the §262 market-out exception does not apply to cash).

> **Sourcing flag.** The 90% short-form threshold, the §251(h) mechanics and the §262 cash carve-out are
> standard Delaware law asserted from general knowledge, not bound to primary text in this pass. None of
> the §6 kill depends on them.

### 4a. What amended DGCL §144 actually requires — and my prior framing corrected

Verified against the statute as in force (8 Del. C. §144, Delaware Code, subchapter IV).
**This matters because Fubo converted from a Florida to a Delaware corporation on 2025-10-29 — after the
March-2025 SB 21 amendments took effect.**

- **Ordinary controller transactions — §144(b): EITHER/OR.** Safe harbor on (b)(1) approval by a committee
  of **two or more disinterested directors**, **OR** (b)(2) an "informed, uncoerced, affirmative vote of a
  majority of the votes cast by the disinterested stockholders," **OR** (b)(3) fairness.
- **GOING-PRIVATE transactions — §144(c)(1): BOTH ARE REQUIRED.** The safe harbor demands committee
  approval per (b)(1) **AND** disinterested-stockholder approval per (b)(2). §144(c)(2) leaves the
  alternative of defending on fairness.
- **"Going private transaction" — §144(e)(6)(a):** for a public company, a **Rule 13e-3 transaction**. A
  Disney cash-out of the Class A minority is squarely inside this.
- **"Controlling stockholder" — §144(e)(2):** majority of voting power, or contractual right to elect a
  majority of directors, or **≥⅓ of voting power plus managerial authority.** Disney satisfies all three.
- **§144(d)(5):** controller exculpation from monetary damages except loyalty breaches, bad faith /
  intentional misconduct, or an improper personal benefit.

**I overstated this in my first pass and correct it here.** SB 21 relaxed the *MFW* dual-protection
requirement for controller transactions **generally**, but for a **going-private** deal it did the
opposite of relaxing — **it codified MFW's both-conditions structure into statute.** So Disney cannot get
business-judgment deference on a squeeze-out with a special committee alone. That is a **stronger**
minority protection than I implied, and it is the single most important correction in this report.

Two qualifications that keep it from being a bull case on its own:
1. **The controller can simply decline the safe harbor and defend on fairness** — and controllers do. See
   the MSG Networks and Chindata precedents in §4b, where the controller expressly refused any
   majority-of-the-minority condition.
2. **The §144(b)(2) test is "a majority of the votes CAST by the disinterested stockholders," not of
   shares outstanding.** With **10,042,528 broker non-votes — ~33% of the Class A count** — turning out at
   Fubo's last meeting, the disinterested electorate is thin and passive. A majority-of-the-minority
   condition here is far more achievable for Disney than the headline makes it sound, which cuts toward
   Disney conceding it and paying the associated premium rather than fighting on fairness.

**The dated window falls out of the documents, and it is uncomfortably clean:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2025-10-29 | Closing. Lockup starts; independent-designee undertaking starts. |
| CY2027 (from Jan 2027) | Wholesale fee steps 95% → **97.5%** (≈ +$112M/yr) |
| **2027-10-29** | **Lockup expires AND the Unaffiliated Independent Designee undertaking expires — same day** |
| CY2028 (from Jan 2028) | Wholesale fee steps → **99%** (≈ +$180M/yr vs baseline) |
| FY2028 (Oct-27 → Sep-28) | The "≥$300M" year |

**Disney's optimal squeeze-out window is roughly Nov-2027 → Jan-2028:** after its own contractual
constraints lapse, before the step-up it granted lands in the reported numbers, striking the price on
FY2026/FY2027 depressed EBITDA. It would be buying, at a premium to a depressed print, an earnings stream
whose principal driver is a schedule it wrote and controls.

There is no standstill preventing open-market accumulation in the meantime, §203 is waived, and — see §5 —
Delaware's 2025 §144 amendments materially reduced the litigation cost of a controller take-private
relative to the old MFW regime.

target raises the price Disney must pay only if Disney is still buying after it prints. Missing it wipes

## 6. STRONGEST KILL

**Fubo is not a 30% economic interest in a streaming business. It is a 27.7% interest in Disney's carriage
procurement desk, on a five-year contract, whose margin schedule Disney wrote, whose customers Disney
owns and bills, whose successor product Disney is openly building, and whose two remaining minority
protections both expire on 2027-10-29 — three months before the contractual earnings step-up that is 80%
of the target the stock is being marketed on.**

**The kill is emphatically NOT "Disney might squeeze you out."** The precedent work in §4b changed my
mind on that: controllers essentially never bid below market, 89.5% raise their opening price, and the
median final premium is ~+42%. A squeeze-out is closer to a **floor** than a risk.

The kill is that **every lever that could make the stub worth more is held by the counterparty who would
be buying it — and if that counterparty simply does nothing, there is no floor at all.** The calendar
aligns those levers against the minority with a precision that is difficult to read as accidental:

1. Disney sets the wholesale fee (95% → 97.5% → 99%) and therefore sets Fubo's EBITDA almost directly.
2. Disney owns the ~75% of subscribers that generate 72% of Fubo's revenue, keeps their subscription
   revenue, and can end the whole arrangement by declining to renew in 2030.
3. Disney has renounced any duty to refer opportunities or refrain from competing, and the charter
   expressly anticipates Disney buying a competing vMVPD.
4. Disney is the marginal lender ($145M Disney Note).
5. Disney's designees hold 6 of 9 board seats; the seventh is its own former Disney+ President; the
   founder was removed on change-of-control severance in July 2026.
6. §203 is waived, written consent is available at 72.34%, and there is no standstill.
7. The one number that could force a higher take-out price — FY28 ≥$300M — is unreconciled, unaccountable,
   and simultaneously the assumption holding up $2.6B of goodwill on a 3.6% cushion.

And the price does not compensate for the *no-bid* path: **~11x EV/EBITDA — ~21x ex-SBC — on the FY26
number that actually exists**, with 1.3% EBITDA margins, subs −7% from peak, advertising revenue down YoY
in a World Cup quarter, negative FY26 free cash flow by the company's own guidance, a Q4 guided to roughly
zero, and $2.6B of goodwill standing on a 3.6% cushion. **The triage graded that business DAMAGE-ABSENT.
It is not. It is deteriorating on every operating metric it discloses, and the reverse split is what made
that invisible.**

**The refined thesis, stated plainly:** the squeeze-out is a ~$12.90 soft floor with maybe 40–60%
probability of ever arriving; the no-bid path is an unfloored decline in a business whose economics its
counterparty controls; and the run-to-FY28 upside (~$18–20) requires Disney to leave $163M/yr of
contractual value with a minority it has every tool and every incentive to buy out first.

## 8. CONVICTION AND RECOMMENDATION

**CONVICTION: 7/10 against the long as an equity position — DOWNGRADED from 8/10 during this court.**

I opened at 8 and the verification moved me down. Three of my bear legs weakened or failed on primary
sources, and I will not hold a score my own evidence no longer supports:

1. **TRA change-of-control acceleration — REFUTED** by TRA §4.2(a). It does not exist, and Audit Committee
   approval is required for any voluntary termination. That was a real leg and it is gone.
2. **"SB 21 makes a low-ball cheaper" — PARTLY REFUTED.** §144(c)(1) requires *both* a disinterested
   committee *and* a majority-of-disinterested-votes for a going-private. Delaware codified MFW here.
3. **"Squeeze-out is the risk" — REFUTED by the base rates.** Median final premium +41.9%, median bump
   +7.5%, 89.5% re-priced up, none re-priced down. A bid is closer to a floor than a threat.

Also withheld in the bulls' favour: at $9.08 the market has *already* applied a severe controller discount
(3.6x FY28), so a meaningful part of my thesis is priced; the conditioning read says this is a
**DISCOVERED**, heavily-traded name, not an overlooked one; and the FY28 step-up is genuinely
**contractual** — reneging is precisely the act that would strip Disney of any deference.

**What holds at 7, and why it is still a reject:** the case no longer rests on the squeeze-out. It rests
on (i) what Fubo actually owns — carriage contracts, on a five-year term, with distribution exclusivity to
the controller's platform and no pricing power; (ii) the operating deterioration the reverse split hid —
EBITDA −54% in two quarters, subs −7% from peak, advertising −0.5% YoY *in a World Cup quarter*, negative
FY26 FCF, $2.6B of goodwill on a 3.6% cushion; and (iii) the fact that the **no-bid path has no floor**
while the bid path caps out near $12.90.

**RECOMMENDATION: REJECT the long. Do not initiate an equity position.**

Reasoning under the response taxonomy: this is **not** a valuation finding (which would gate on price) and
**not** a timing finding (which would gate on tranching). It is a **business/structure-risk finding** — the
asset's cash flows, pricing, distribution and terminal value are all contractually controlled by the
counterparty most likely to buy it. The correct response to structure risk is a **size cut**, and the
correct size here is zero: the minority has no mechanism to convert being right about the business into
being paid, and the business itself is getting worse.

*Note for the blue team, since 7/10 is within argument range:* the strongest blue case is **not** "it's
cheap." It is that the +42% precedent premium and the contractual FY28 step-up together give a
defined-floor / open-upside payoff from a heavily-discounted DISCOVERED price. I would answer that the
floor requires Disney to *choose* to bid, which it has done zero of to date (13D/A, no purchases), and
that no precedent premium protects you on the fourth row of the §4b table.

**Two dissents I will defend, in case the blue team wants them:**
- **If the desk wants exposure, the instrument is convexity, not stock.** IV at 78.7% is *below* 30-day
  realised of 81.3%, IV percentile is near the floor, and a controller bid is a gap event that the options
  surface is not pricing. Long-dated calls struck above the likely low-ball, sized as a lottery, express

  no-premium-selling rule.
- **The genuinely interesting security here is the 2029 convertible senior secured note**, not the equity:
  7.5% cash / 10% PIK, secured, $177.5M against ~$1.08B of EV, marked at $175.1M (~98.6), with a **par

## 9. WHAT THIS ENCODES FOR THE GRAPH

**Masking channel:** *denominator-and-base substitution under a controller.* Four independent instances in
one release — subs measured off the seasonal trough rather than the peak; a "raise" that moves only the
floor of a range 98% already banked; a YoY net-loss improvement against a carve-out comparative that
excludes the acquired business (reverse-acquisition accounting); and advertising narrated as momentum
while the number falls 0.5%. None of it is false. All of it is framing.

**Signal channel:** *the governing documents, not the print.* The decisive facts here were in Exhibit 3.4
to a closing 8-K (§203 waiver, written consent, corporate-opportunity renunciation, "Competing DMVPD"),
in a revenue-recognition footnote (the 95/97.5/99% schedule), and in an Item 5.07 vote table (44%
withheld). None of them appear in any earnings summary. **Standing rule: for any controlled company,
read the charter and the closing 8-K exhibits before reading a single quarter.**

**Signal-to-price latency:** *long and calendar-pinned.* The step-up schedule and the two expiry dates were
public from 2025-10-30 and have not been priced in ten months. The catalyst set is dated and public:
