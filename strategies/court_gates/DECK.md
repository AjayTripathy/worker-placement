# Court Gates — thesis deck

**Bucket:** idiosyncratic · **Edge:** EDGE · **Author:** pitch-bot · **As of:** 2026-09-12

Prices below are live IBKR snapshots pulled 2026-09-12; company identities were resolved
against IBKR primary US listings, not a data vendor. Every figure here traces to that pull
or is labelled an estimate.

## Thesis
"Court Gates" is not one trade. It is the set of single names that cleared an adversarial
red/blue diligence court before they were allowed into a book. The name comes from the gate,
not from a shared macro view.

The origin read was a **consumer sub-cluster**: sell-side and headlines called the category
"cooling," while the demand channel said otherwise. In that read the **promotional rack is the
single most informative witness for the bear side** — if a brand starts discounting harder
week over week, real demand is softening and the disconfirming-story wins. So the rack is
watched weekly as the explicit falsifier, not as color.

Where the market has repriced these names hard on the "cooling" story and the channel does
**not** confirm the cooling, the repricing is the edge. Each position resolves on its own
catalyst, which is why the sleeve is tagged idiosyncratic rather than a sector bet.

So the decisive first-principles finding: **only ONON is a clean promo-rack read, with BGSI

generalized to a ten-name court-cleared basket. That is a legitimate way to run an
idiosyncratic sleeve — the common factor is "survived the gate," not "same catalyst" — but it
must be said out loud, because the single falsifier does not cover eight of ten names. Each of
those eight needs its own falsifier, listed below, or it should not be in the book.

A second finding worth stating: six of ten names are down hard year-to-date
(ONON -41%, HUBS -44%, BGSI -43%, COLL -51%, FNF -19%, FIGR -14%), while three are up
(ARX +21%, TSSI +27%, BELFB +48%). The sleeve therefore mixes "buy the disconfirmed dip"
with "ride the confirmed winner." Those are different trades with different risk; do not let
the winners' momentum launder the dip-buys' thesis.

## Mechanism / what resolves it, and the falsifier
- **Consumer cluster (ONON, BGSI):** channel/velocity data holds while the tape prices
  cooling → gap closes on the next print that confirms demand. **Falsifier:** the weekly
  promo rack deepens (wider, longer, earlier markdowns) — that is demand softening, exit.
- **Every other name:** resolves on its own catalyst, and each carries its own named
  falsifier (see kill triggers). If a name has no live, dated falsifier, it does not belong
  in the sleeve.

## Risks & kill triggers
- **Composite thesis risk.** The one-line thesis covers ~2 of 10 names. Kill any name that
  lacks its own live, dated falsifier — do not carry it on the sleeve's reputation.
- **Promo-rack falsifier (ONON, BGSI):** deepening weekly markdowns → the bear was right,
  exit.
- **HUBS:** further net-revenue-retention deceleration or seat contraction → thesis is a
  falling knife, not a reset.
- **COLL:** patent/generic or formulary loss on the lead franchise → cash-flow thesis breaks.
- **FNF:** rates back up / housing-volume air-pocket → title revenue rolls over.
- **TSSI:** the funding-inversion / customer-concentration triggers in the next 10-Q; a single
  hyperscaler cancellation. Winner, but concentrated.
- **BELFB / ARX:** valuation risk — up 48% / 21% YTD; a starter that becomes a chase is the
  error. No adds without a pullback and a fresh pack.
- **FIGR / LFTO:** recent IPOs — lock-up expiries, thin float, no multi-cycle track record.
  Highest variance in the sleeve; keep starter-only.
- **Basket correlation risk.** Tagged idiosyncratic, but six names are high-beta and down big
  together — in a drawdown they can move as one. The "idiosyncratic" label is a claim about
  catalysts, not a guarantee of low correlation.
- **Cap-structure check owed** on any name before an EV/valuation call (warrants/preferred),
  per desk doctrine — not yet completed for the newer listings here.

## What could make this wrong
- The channel read is stale or cherry-picked and the "cooling" story is simply correct — the
  promo rack is the test precisely because this is the most likely way to be wrong on ONON.
- "Court-cleared" is doing too much work: a gate that passed ten heterogeneous names may be
  too permissive. If the sleeve's win rate is not better than a random idiosyncratic basket,
  the gate is not the edge.
- The winners (TSSI/BELFB/ARX) carry the sleeve's return and mask dip-buys that quietly fail.
  Attribute P&L per name, not per sleeve.

## How it fits goals & scenarios
- **Goal fit:** growth / opportunistic capital only. Not for spending or liquidity-floor
  goals — these are volatile single names, several recent IPOs.
- **Scenario behavior:** high-beta into a market drawdown (roughly S&P beta ~1.0, with a
  consumer-discretionary and AI-hardware tilt — estimated, not regressed). Helps in a
  risk-on tape where the "cooling" narratives unwind; hurts in a broad de-rating where the
  down-YTD names keep de-rating. Position it as satellite risk sized small, with a human in
  the loop on every T1.
