# Deep Value Thesis1 — DFIN print-to-software crossover

**Bucket:** cyclical_value · **Edge:** RP_FAIR · **Author:** pitch-bot · **As of:** 2026-09-12

*Single name: DFIN (Donnelley Financial Solutions). Concentration capped at 20% of the sleeve.*

## Thesis

What the tape shows: a shrinking financial-printing company. Consolidated revenue has fallen every year since the 2021 capital-markets boom — $993.3M (FY21) → $833.6M (FY22) → $797.2M (FY23) → $781.9M (FY24) → $767.0M (FY25) [EDGAR XBRL, 10-K]. Reported FY25 net income dropped to $32.4M from $92.4M, which makes the trailing P/E look punitive.

What is underneath: about 47% of net sales is now software — Venue (virtual data room), ActiveDisclosure and Arc Suite (compliance software) — and that software line grew ~8.7% to $358.4M in FY25, a record, with Venue and ActiveDisclosure each up ~20% and the compliance-software basket up ~16% [DFIN FY25 results, 8-K/press release]. Management targets a 60% software mix. The FY25 earnings drop is largely a one-time, non-cash pension-settlement charge (~$82.8M) rather than an operating collapse; normalized earnings run around ~11x P/E and EV/EBITDA in the ~6x range [SignalOS DFIN dossier, 2026-06].

The bet: as software crosses further toward 60% of sales, the market grants a software-like multiple to a business it currently prices as declining print. While that recognition is slow, normalized free cash flow carries the position at roughly 10%/yr. This is explicitly a **fair-price, paid-to-wait** position (RP_FAIR), not a cheap-vs-value mispricing.

## Mechanism / what resolves it — and the falsifier

**Convergence mechanism.** Two curves cross: software (growing ~high-single to ~20% on its leading products) versus print/compliance-services (declining). Once software is the clear majority of sales and the pension noise is out of the trailing numbers, the normalized ~11x P/E and ~10% FCF yield become visible without an adjustment footnote, and the multiple re-rates. Timing of the >50% software mark is plausibly 2026–2027; the company's own 60% target is a longer horizon than the thesis's "~Q3-2027" crossover date, so treat Q3-2027 as the optimistic end of the window, not a committed date.

**Falsifier (what would prove this wrong).** Software growth is the entire thesis. If software net-sales growth decelerates to low-single digits for two consecutive quarters, the crossover engine has stalled and the re-rate does not come. Equally, if the growth turns out to be **Venue deal-cycle beta wearing a SaaS costume** — i.e., Venue rises only with M&A/IPO volume and shows no net-new-logo or seat expansion — then "software crossover" is really "capital-markets cycle," and it re-derates on the next deal trough.

## Risks & kill triggers

- **Venue is deal-cycle beta, not ARR.** Venue (virtual data room) is the biggest software growth driver but its usage tracks M&A/IPO activity. A soft deal environment stalls the fastest-growing "software" line. This is the single most important risk and the reason the pack sits in **cyclical_value**, not growth.
- **The recurring base is quietly eroding.** DFIN's "recurring and reoccurring" basket (~80% of revenue, includes Venue) actually *decreased ~1.6%* in FY25 even as software grew — durable compliance/services recurring revenue is shrinking while deal-linked software carries the top line. Watch whether the genuinely subscription piece (ActiveDisclosure + Arc Suite) can keep mid-teens growth.
- **Mix can cross while the pie shrinks.** Software can reach 60% simply because print falls faster; a re-rate is not guaranteed on a still-declining consolidated top line.
- **Balance sheet drift.** Long-term debt rose to $165.5M (FY25) from $124.7M (FY24) [EDGAR]. Rising leverage while buyback pauses would undercut the carry story.
- **Kill triggers:** (1) software net-sales growth < ~mid-single-digits for two straight quarters; (2) evidence Venue growth is purely deal-volume with no logo/seat expansion; (3) consolidated revenue decline accelerating past ~5%/yr; (4) normalized FCF yield compressing below ~6%, or debt rising further with no buyback; (5) multiple re-rates before fundamentals confirm — take the gift and exit, thesis "worked" for the wrong reason.

## Bear case (state it plainly)

DFIN is a secularly shrinking financial-printing and compliance-services company whose most exciting growth line is really M&A-cycle sensitivity dressed as SaaS. The genuinely subscription-like compliance software is smaller and the total recurring basket fell in FY25. You would be paying a fair price today (~$48 vs ~$45–50 fair) for a re-rate that may arrive late, may not arrive at all if deal volume stays soft, and could be given back on the next capital-markets trough. The honest upside here is the ~10% carry plus optionality on recognition — not a deep discount to intrinsic value.

## How it fits goals & scenarios

- **Goal fit:** long-horizon compounding (retirement / wealth accumulation) funded by the ~10% normalized FCF carry, not by near-term spending — the payoff is patience-dependent.
- **Scenario behavior:** *helps* in a recovering capital-markets / active-deal environment (Venue and re-rate both benefit) and in any environment where the pension-noise clears and normalized earnings become visible. *Hurts* in a prolonged deal drought (Venue stalls) and in a sharp small-cap / risk-off drawdown (small-cap beta ~0.6). Rate sensitivity is mildly negative — higher rates suppress deal activity that Venue depends on. Best held as a capped, single-name compounding sleeve, sized to the carry and watched against the software-growth falsifier.
