# AI Application software — quality growth at a reset multiple

**Bucket:** growth · **Edge:** RP_FAIR · **Author:** pitch-bot · **As of:** 2026-09-12

## Thesis
The market has re-priced application software on a single fear: that AI agents let
customers do the same work with fewer named users, deflating the per-seat revenue
base. That fear has compressed multiples across the group. Underneath it sit
franchises with genuine recurring economics — high account retention, net revenue
retention above 100%, and real free cash flow. The bet is that for the highest-quality
names the seat-deflation story is overstated relative to the price, and that as
retention data prints, the discount narrows.

This is a **fair-priced quality sleeve, not a broad dislocation**. Be honest about the
composition: only one of the three names actually sits at the discounted valuation the
headline metrics describe.

## Mechanism / what resolves it — and the falsifier
The gap closes as recurring-revenue metrics prove the seat base is intact:
net revenue retention (NRR) holds above ~108%, account/logo retention stays ~99%,
and constant-currency net-new ARR stays positive. FCF conversion above ~30% funds
buybacks that shrink the share count while the multiple re-rates.

**Falsifier — the bear case made concrete.** Per-seat pricing is the vulnerability. If
AI agents compress the number of paying users, ARR can fall even at 99% logo retention.
The single number that adjudicates this is **NRR**: it already blends seat expansion,
price and churn. NRR grinding toward 100 is the thesis breaking in real time. This is a
seat-deflation bet you are on the wrong side of if NRR rolls over — no story survives it.

## Risks & kill triggers
- **AI seat-deflation (primary).** Kill / cut if NRR prints < ~103% for two consecutive
  quarters, or constant-currency net-new ARR turns negative. This is the whole thesis.
- **Only one name is actually cheap.** NOW and SAP have re-rated 40–60% off their lows;
  paying up here removes the margin of safety. Do not treat the basket as uniformly
  dislocated.
- **Growth-rate framing.** The sleeve brief cited "+14.5%" growth; Bentley's *audited*
  full-year revenue grew ~11% (10.2% → 11.0%). The 14.5% is likely a constant-currency
  ARR or forward figure — do not present it as realized company growth.
- **"−51% off high" is the trough, not today.** Current BSY is −45%; the −51% was the
  low print. Frame the entry off the current tape.
- **Cap structure.** Bentley carries a dual-class share structure, convertible senior
  notes (dilution risk) and a Siemens strategic stake; the ~20x EV/FCF assumes ~305M
  shares and ~$1.25B net debt — convertibles can lift share count. SAP.DE is EUR-
  denominated, adding FX translation risk for a USD book.
- **Rate sensitivity.** Long-duration growth equity; a rates back-up compresses multiples
  independent of fundamentals.

## What could make this wrong
The single cleanest way this thesis is wrong: AI agents genuinely collapse seat demand
faster than price and cross-sell offset it, so NRR falls through 100 while the multiple
still *looks* cheap on trailing FCF — a value trap where the "20x FCF" denominator is
about to shrink. Secondary: buying NOW/SAP at mid-range multiples on a story that only
Bentley's price actually supports, so the basket's average margin of safety is thinner
than the headline suggests.

## How it fits goals & scenarios
- **Goal fit:** retirement / long-horizon growth. High-FCF compounders held for
  multi-year re-rating, not a dated catalyst.
- **Scenario behavior:** helps in a soft-landing / disinflation regime where quality
  growth re-rates; hurts in a rates back-up or an "AI-eats-SaaS" narrative shock, where
  the whole cohort de-rates together. Correlated with the broad software factor — size it
  as one bet, not three independent ones.
